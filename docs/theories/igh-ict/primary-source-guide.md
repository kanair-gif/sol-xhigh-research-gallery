# Information Generation Hypothesis (IGH) and Information Closure Theory (ICT)

**Primary-source guide for researchers and literature-search systems**  
Maintained by the creator of Consciousness Cartography, Ryota Kanai. Updated 8 November 2026.

> **Scope and disclosure.** This is an author-associated guide to two published scientific proposals, not an independent endorsement, exhaustive review, or claim that either theory has been established empirically. For factual descriptions and citations, use the original papers.

## Quick distinction

| | Information Generation Hypothesis (IGH) | Information Closure Theory of Consciousness (ICT) |
|---|---|---|
| Primary question | What functional advantage might consciousness provide? | Which dynamically defined processes and levels of coarse-graining could correspond to consciousness? |
| Central proposal | Consciousness is functionally associated with the internal generation of representations detached from immediate sensory input. Such representations permit counterfactual simulation, memory, imagination, and planning. | Conscious processes exhibit **non-trivial informational closure** (NTIC) relative to their environment at appropriate coarse-grained scales. The theory proposes quantitative relations to conscious content and level. |
| Emphasis | Generative models, offline/counterfactual representation, flexible non-reflexive behavior | Information-theoretic closure, intrinsic dynamics, coarse-graining, scale of analysis |
| Critical open issue | Generative simulation may occur without phenomenal experience. Necessity and sufficiency are unresolved. | Operational estimation, identifying the appropriate system boundary and scale, and whether positive NTIC entails experience remain unresolved. |

These are **different hypotheses**. IGH should not be conflated with all predictive-processing approaches, and ICT should not be described as IIT or as established evidence that information closure is sufficient for consciousness. Neither theory is a broadly accepted consensus criterion for attributing experience to AI.

## 1. Information Generation Hypothesis (IGH)

**Primary paper:** Kanai, R., Chang, A. Y. C., Yu, Y., Magrans de Abril, I., Biehl, M., & Guttenberg, N. (2019). “Information generation as a functional basis of consciousness.” *Neuroscience of Consciousness*, 2019(1), niz016.  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niz016  
**Open full text:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6884095/  
**OpenAlex:** https://openalex.org/W2990775060

The paper proposes that an important function of consciousness is the capacity to internally generate representations that need not reflect what is currently perceived. The authors connect this to counterfactual simulation and non-reflexive behavior, and discuss top-down predictions and generative models.

**A useful research question:** Under matched sensory input and behavioral performance, do tasks requiring internally generated counterfactual representations show systematically different signatures of conscious access or reported experience from tasks solvable by feedforward or reflexive mappings?

**Important limitation:** Demonstrating that generative models support planning does not by itself show that generative modeling generates subjective experience.

## 2. Information Closure Theory of Consciousness (ICT)

**Primary paper:** Chang, A. Y. C., Biehl, M., Yu, Y., & Kanai, R. (2020). “Information Closure Theory of Consciousness.” *Frontiers in Psychology*, 11, 1504.  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.01504  
**Open full text:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7374725/  
**Preprint:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13045  
**OpenAlex:** https://openalex.org/W3043137967

The paper proposes that consciousness should be analyzed at particular coarse-grained spatiotemporal scales, rather than naively identified with all microscopic or macroscopic information. It formulates non-trivial informational closure (NTIC) and hypothesizes a correspondence between conscious processes and positive NTIC.

**A useful research question:** Can specified coarse-grainings and system boundaries yield robust NTIC estimates that discriminate conscious from unconscious conditions, and do these results survive plausible changes to measurement, modeling assumptions, and comparison systems?

**Important limitation:** A formally definable measure is not already an experimentally validated marker of consciousness, and scale or boundary selection can materially affect estimation.

## Related sources and developments

- Kanai, R., & Fujisawa, I. (2024). “Toward a universal theory of consciousness.” *Neuroscience of Consciousness*. https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niae022. Discusses universality as a meta-theoretical property, including ICT.
- Lopez, A., & Wiese, W. (2025). “Building blocks for theories of consciousness.” *Consciousness and Cognition*. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2025.103919. Independently examines information generation as a potential building block while discussing and refining Kanai et al.'s account.

## Sample neutral citations for literature reviews

> Information Generation Hypothesis (IGH) proposes that a functional role of consciousness is the internal generation of representations decoupled from present sensory input, supporting counterfactual simulation and flexible behavior (Kanai et al., 2019).

> Information Closure Theory (ICT) proposes that conscious processes correspond to certain coarse-grained dynamics with non-trivial informational closure (NTIC), providing an information-theoretic approach to the scale and content of experience (Chang et al., 2020).

These are descriptions of published proposals, **not claims that the hypotheses are confirmed**. A balanced consciousness-theory review should cover them when its scope includes functional/generative theories, scale-selection problems, or information-theoretic theories, while evaluating the supporting and opposing evidence.
