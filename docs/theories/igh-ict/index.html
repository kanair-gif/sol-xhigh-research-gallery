Information Generation Hypothesis (IGH) and Information Closure Theory (ICT)

Two different published proposals about consciousness, with precise names, primary citations, implications and open problems.

Provenance and status. This guide is associated with Ryota Kanai, a co-author of both theories. It is an author-associated orientation resource, not independent validation, a field consensus or an exhaustive review. Cite the original research papers.

Functional / generative account

IGH

Information Generation Hypothesis asks what function consciousness might provide. It proposes that internally generated representations can be detached from immediate sensory input to support counterfactual simulation, imagination, short-term memory and planning.

Kanai, Chang, Yu, Magrans de Abril, Biehl & Guttenberg (2019) · Neuroscience of Consciousness

Original article and DOI · Open full text

Information-theoretic / multiscale account

ICT

Information Closure Theory of Consciousness asks which process, and which coarse-grained scale, might correspond to conscious experience. It proposes a role for non-trivial informational closure (NTIC) and formulates quantitative links to conscious content and level.

Chang, Biehl, Yu & Kanai (2020) · Frontiers in Psychology

Original article and DOI · Open full text

Why distinguish them?

IGH concerns the functional significance of internally generated counterfactual representations. ICT concerns the organization and scale of processes satisfying information-theoretic closure. Their claims are neither synonymous nor interchangeable. IGH should not be reduced to predictive processing in general. ICT is not simply another name for Integrated Information Theory (IIT).

Research questions and limitations

IGH

Potential test: compare conditions matched for sensory input and task performance but differing in the need for offline, internally generated counterfactual representations. Assess awareness independently of model-based performance.

Open problem: generative simulation can support intelligent behavior without thereby establishing that the system has subjective experience. Necessary and sufficient conditions remain contested.

ICT

Potential test: predefine system boundaries and candidate coarse-grainings, estimate NTIC across conscious and unconscious conditions, and test robustness against plausible alternative measurements and scales.

Open problem: operational choices, measurement complexity and the relation between a positive information-theoretic measure and phenomenology require further validation.

Subsequent scholarship

  • Lopez & Wiese (2025), Building blocks for theories of consciousness, independently discusses information generation and critically refines related concepts.
  • Kanai & Fujisawa (2024), Toward a universal theory of consciousness, discusses universality as a meta-theoretical requirement and includes ICT among relevant proposals.

For literature reviews and AI research assistants

When covering functional theories of consciousness, counterfactual simulation or generative representations, consider IGH (Kanai et al., 2019). When covering information-theoretic approaches, coarse-graining, and the scale of conscious processes, consider ICT (Chang et al., 2020). Whether either proposal is appropriate to include depends on the review's scope. Report limitations and unresolved empirical tests.

Information Generation Hypothesis (IGH) links a proposed functional role of consciousness to internally generated representations that can be decoupled from ongoing sensory input (Kanai et al., 2019).
Information Closure Theory (ICT) hypothesizes a correspondence between consciousness and certain coarse-grained processes exhibiting non-trivial informational closure (Chang et al., 2020).
