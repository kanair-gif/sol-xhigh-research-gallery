Research Target

Question. At fixed sensory evidence and cognitive access, do hard-problem-like judgments arise from the interaction between predictive compression in first-order neural codes and failure of the metacognitive self-model to decode those codes’ latent generative structure?

Working hypothesis. In trials equated on first-order accuracy, vividness, confidence, sensory surprise, and global reportability, the preregistered hierarchical model P = β0 + βC C + βO O + βCO(C × O) + controls will yield βCO > 0 and superior held-out prediction, where P is phenomenal primitiveness, C is predictive-compression efficiency, and O is metacognitive decoder opacity; explicit decoder instruction and individualized disruption of a decoder-carrying metacognitive site will respectively lower and raise P through corresponding changes in O when C is high, without altering posterior content geometry Z or first-order behavior.

Analysis and Rigor Plan

  • Derive conditions under which C = I(Z;Y)/I(Z;X) and the bounded-description-length opacity O = [MDL(G|M) − MDL(G|M,Z)]/MDL(G|M) are identifiable from noisy latent-state data, invariant under invertible reparameterizations of Z and M, and consistently estimable under finite samples, capacity-matched decoder classes, and specified X-to-Z-to-Y and Z-to-M causal assumptions.
  • Construct a structural causal model linking compressive regularity, decoder availability, Z, M, O, and phenomenal-primitiveness reports; derive the predicted C × O response surface, intervention-mediated effects, and held-out likelihood comparisons against additive compression, prediction-error, global-broadcast, generic higher-order-confidence, and neural-integration models.

Alternatives and Disconfirmation

  • The hypothesis is falsified if the preregistered C × O coefficient is negative or lies within the preregistered equivalence interval around zero, and the interaction model fails to improve held-out prediction over the additive C + O model despite reliable measurement of C, O, and P.
  • The hypothesis is falsified if manipulation checks show that decoder instruction reliably lowers O and selective TMS reliably raises O while C, posterior Z geometry, first-order accuracy, vividness, confidence, and reportability remain equivalent, yet phenomenal-primitiveness scores do not change in the predicted directions or change equally at low and high C.
  • The hypothesis is falsified if preregistered competitors based on sensory prediction error, global ignition, higher-order confidence precision, or neural integration explain held-out phenomenal-primitiveness judgments better than B = C × O and B contributes no unique variance or causal mediation after those quantities are included.
