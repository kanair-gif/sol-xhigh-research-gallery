Research Target

Question. Does the signed arrival-time map of a metastable posterior-cortical–hippocampal wavefront causally determine the subjective temporal relation between distributed item representations when activation magnitude, undirected coherence, and global recruitment are held constant?

Working hypothesis. During individually thresholded two-item temporal-order judgments, if a closed-loop induced wavefront lasting 150–400 ms produces a measured arrival lag Δτ = τΓ(B) − τΓ(A) of 10–40 ms between mapped item ensembles, then the psychometric point of subjective simultaneity, defined as tB − tA, will equal Δτ with a hierarchical slope between 0.8 and 1.2 and cross-validated R² of at least 0.50, reversing sign under exact time reversal of the wavefront; power-matched standing-wave and phase-scrambled controls will show no such lag-to-percept mapping.

Analysis and Rigor Plan

  • Derive existence and finite-lifetime stability conditions for traveling-front solutions of τu∂tu = −u + ∫W(x,y)f(u(y,t))dy + g(x) + I(x,t) on coupled posterior-cortical and hippocampal manifolds, where g(x) is an excitability gradient; compute the front-arrival map τΓ(x), its adjoint phase-response modes, and controls that reverse its signed gradient while preserving local spectra and coherence magnitudes.
  • Define the temporal-binding operator BΓ[z](s) = Σi wi zi(s + τΓ(xi)) and derive its behavioral prediction tB − tA = τΓ(B) − τΓ(A); establish identifiability against scalar ignition, stationary synchrony, and centralized-clock models using time-reversal invariants, hierarchical psychometric inference, causal mediation, and cross-validated representational-geometry comparisons.

Alternatives and Disconfirmation

  • After independently verifying a 10–40 ms reversal of Δτ and satisfying the preregistered nuisance-equivalence bounds, the forward-to-reverse change in the point of subjective simultaneity is less than one quarter of the predicted change 2Δτ, has the opposite sign, or yields a lag-to-PSE slope whose 95% confidence interval excludes 0.8.
  • Power-matched standing-wave or phase-scrambled stimulation shifts temporal-order judgments as strongly and systematically as signed traveling-wave stimulation, or unsigned power, coherence magnitude, item decodability, or late global recruitment explains the behavioral effect while signed arrival lag contributes no out-of-sample predictive information.
  • Transforming neural activity into the co-moving coordinate t − τΓ(x) fails to improve cross-validated event-level representational similarity over physical time, stationary synchrony, and ignition models, and trial-wise Δτ fails to mediate the directional-stimulation effect despite reliable decoding of both item representations and wavefront direction.
