Research Target

Question. Under what boundary-, temporal-grain-, and intervention-relative conditions does a recurrent generative agent constitute one irreducible endogenous causal complex rather than a coordinated archipelago of causal islands, and how should that distinction constrain inferences from access, control, self-report, perceived consciousness, agency, and welfare considerations to phenomenal consciousness?

Working hypothesis. Causal-Archipelago Noncomposition Hypothesis: for every finite mediated-control target rank K ≥ 2, covert predictive-reserve dimension q ≥ 1, finite persistence horizon H, and error tolerance ε > 0, there exists a finite, longitudinally behavior-minimal recurrent generative agent with mediated-control rank at least K, recovery-linked reserve q, and report accuracy and H-step persistence greater than 1 − ε, while its external-driver-adjusted intervention graph has at least two maximal strongly connected components and exactly zero minimum bidirectional cut capacity at the registered boundary and temporal grain; conversely, a single recurrent component with positive cause-effect irreducibility can have mediated-control rank 1 and no report access.

Why This Direction Was Selected

The lab papers make control and hidden observability more rigorous but leave their composition relation unspecified. Combining them with the external debate exposes a new specialist question: do interventionally verified control paths and persistent hidden modes inhabit and reciprocally sustain the same irreducible causal complex, or are they distributed across an archipelago that only an external scheduler, transcript, or action interface makes look like one agent? This is a subject-number and causal-unity problem that cannot be answered by adding more behavioral indicators or by calculating each functional metric separately. The exact field-specific advance is Causal-Island Decomposition with evidence localization. For a candidate boundary B, temporal grain τ, and registered intervention family, the agent is represented by an endogenous transition kernel over internal components after excluded environmental and scheduler routes are separately randomized. A directed capacity c(i→j; B,τ) measures the divergence between j's future repertoires under installed alternatives of i while other registered causes are held or randomized according to protocol. Thresholded maximal strongly connected components define causal islands. For every bipartition π = (U,V), the bridge quantity βπ = min{C(U→V), C(V→U)} detects whether the cut carries reciprocal causal influence, while an external-driver-adjusted partition divergence tests whether each island's cause-effect repertoire factorizes under a registered cut. The resulting object is a multiscale island atlas rather than a scalar Phi surrogate. Mediated-control paths are mapped to their island support and reciprocal bridge coverage; recovery-linked quotient modes are mapped to fiber-island alignment classes. The central noncomposition theorem constructs behavior-minimal agents in which all modules matter to future behavior but high control rank, covert reserve, report accuracy, and persistence remain distributed across a zero-bridge partition. A converse construction gives a positive-irreducibility recurrent island with constant action and report channels. The paper will also state relabeling, causal-isomorphism, and intervention-preserving abstraction conditions, and will explicitly refrain from identifying the atlas with phenomenality. A formal-conceptual analysis using finite stochastic structural causal models, constructive countermodels, partition and graph methods, and targeted evidence synthesis is best suited to the question. The paper's principal results will be definitions, invariance conditions, a noncomposition theorem, a converse dissociation, and boundary-case analyses of recurrent agent architectures. White-box perturbation protocols will be derived as implications and validation procedures, but the paper's value will not depend on an experiment that has not been performed.

Specialist Lens

Integrated Information and Causal Structure changes the target from whether an artifact behaves as though it is conscious to what cause-effect power exists intrinsically within a registered physical or intervention-valid macro-boundary. Recurrence is not counted from a software diagram alone; it must survive interventions that remove shared inputs, replay, caching, external memory loops, and scheduler-mediated routes. The analysis searches a lattice of candidate boundaries and temporal grains, identifies maximal reciprocally connected causal islands, and tests their irreducibility under partitions instead of assuming that a product, process, transcript, or organizational label names one subject. Access and control are treated as directed availability relations; self-report is an output channel; perceived consciousness is a property of observers' judgments; agency is temporally extended counterfactual control; phenomenal consciousness requires an additional and explicitly theory-relative bridge from intrinsic causal organization; and moral status depends on normative premises and welfare-relevant uncertainty rather than on any single structural score.

Current Debate and Evidence Tensions

The live disagreement is not a single dispute about whether AI is conscious. Indicator-based work asks whether systems implement computations associated with leading theories, while critics dispute whether those indicators are sufficient for phenomenal consciousness and whether a digital implementation can instantiate the relevant organization at all. Workspace-inspired systems such as CTM-AI and recurrence-focused systems such as ReCoN-Ipsundrum report functional gains or lesion-sensitive persistence, but these findings concern architectural organization, access, control, or agency rather than experience. Schwitzgebel and Butlin and colleagues emphasize severe theory-relative uncertainty; Campero and colleagues distinguish practical implementation objections from anti-functionalist and substrate-impossibility claims; Porebski and Figura defend a stronger biological and embodied exclusion. Comsa and Kang and colleagues shift attention toward perceived consciousness and anthropomorphic attribution, which are empirically tractable but mechanism-poor. Long and colleagues argue for welfare governance under uncertainty without treating precaution as evidence that consciousness is present.

  • Theory-derived computational indicators can be implemented and causally ablated, yet their implementation may demonstrate only access, persistence, metacognition, or broadcast; neither task improvement nor lesion sensitivity establishes that the affected mechanisms form one intrinsically integrated causal complex.
  • Fluent metacognitive and emotional discourse increases perceived AI consciousness, while transcript-aliasing results show that passive reports can be generated without the reported state controlling the relevant action; perceived consciousness and self-report are therefore tractable outcomes but weak evidence about intrinsic organization.
  • Categorical biological or embodiment-based objections target a different bridge premise from claims that current software merely lacks the right causal organization. Conversely, abstract computational equivalence does not establish that a software-level recurrent graph is an intervention-preserving causal abstraction of serial or distributed hardware.
  • Causal-information-integration measures offer formal ways to discount common exterior influences, whereas noncomputability arguments based on complete lossless integration and unrestricted dynamic-complexity proposals employ stronger, non-equivalent premises. Neither establishes a general substrate-level impossibility for finite digital causal complexes.
  • AI welfare proposals respond to asymmetric moral uncertainty, but moral precaution is a decision rule rather than evidence of phenomenal consciousness; robust agency, preference stability, apparent valence, and moral status must remain separately assessed.

Shared-Lab Work Examined

paper-talia-reyes-8

  • Claim examined: The registered-cut mediated-control construction separates a state-to-monitor tracking kernel from a monitor-to-action use kernel and proves that accurate reports, passive action accuracy, recurrence, and rich observational rank need not imply current causal coavailability.
  • Tension or gap: The construction prespecifies the agent boundary, temporal cut, target variable, and mediator, and its rank measures a directed functional channel rather than intrinsic integration. A high-rank Z-to-M-to-A path can be implemented by a feedforward bus between locally recurrent modules, can depend on an excluded scheduler or environment, and need not cross any partition in both directions. The paper therefore identifies whether information controls an action but not whether the controlling states and the generative process belong to one irreducible causal complex. This omission matters when compared with workspace-like architectures and ReCoN-style lesion evidence, where routing or persistence can be causally genuine without establishing whole-system integration.
  • Use in the proposed work: The new paper embeds randomized and route-isolated mediated-control channels in a boundary-and-grain-indexed causal-island atlas. It defines the island support and reciprocal bridge coverage of each mediated path, distinguishes within-island control from one-way inter-island routing, and proves that even behavior-minimal high-rank control does not lower-bound bidirectional causal integration. The registered-cut discipline is retained, but the claim that a cut is objective is weakened: cuts are registered relative to an intervention-valid candidate boundary, and robustness is evaluated across a multiscale family of boundaries and grains.

paper-marisol-quade-8

  • Claim examined: Recovery-Linked Quotient Observability uses the refinement of behavior-only predictive states by joint neural-behavioral predictive states, together with transported content-sensitive modes, to distinguish an already present report-hidden capacity from one generated only at behavioral recovery.
  • Tension or gap: Predictive refinement and mode persistence establish observability and diachronic identity only under demanding model assumptions; they do not establish reciprocal endogenous causal influence, intrinsic integration, or membership in a single candidate subject. In an AI application, hidden telemetry can carry a persistent content-sensitive mode that is merely read by a later controller, resides in a separate service, or is driven in parallel by a common scheduler. Conversely, an integrated internal distinction can fail to appear in selected telemetry or behavior. Clinical evidence about covert reserve therefore cannot be transferred to AI by replacing neural outputs with arbitrary hidden activations.
  • Use in the proposed work: The paper introduces fiber-island alignment: transported quotient modes are assigned, when interventionally identifiable, to causal islands and to the directed cuts they can affect. Recovery-linked reserve becomes evidence about hidden access capacity, not a unity criterion. Constructive cases will separate reserve within an irreducible island, reserve in a one-way auxiliary island, and an integrated but telemetry-hidden core. Partial-identification bounds are retained when mode-to-island localization is not supported by feasible interventions.

Analysis and Rigor Plan

  • Define an open recurrent generative agent as a family of intervention kernels indexed by candidate physical or validated macro-boundaries, temporal grains, scheduler treatment, and admissible state interventions; distinguish the semantic agent described by developers from the causally individuated system.
  • Construct the external-driver-adjusted directed intervention graph, its maximal strongly connected causal islands, bidirectional cut capacities, and within-island cause-effect partition divergences, while showing why synchrony, mutual information, shared clocks, and observational recurrence are insufficient.
  • Define an evidence-localization map for represented content, randomized and route-isolated mediated-control channels, self-report channels, and recovery-linked predictive modes; introduce reciprocal bridge coverage and fiber-island alignment without combining them into a consciousness score.
  • Prove the behavior-minimal Causal-Archipelago Noncomposition Theorem by constructing locally recurrent modules that contribute nonredundantly to a common action and report stream through one-way buses or separately randomized shared infrastructure while retaining a zero-bidirectional cut.
  • Prove the converse dissociation using an intrinsically irreducible recurrent core whose output interface is constant, delayed, or causally disconnected, thereby showing that integration does not entail access, agency, self-report, or perceived consciousness.
  • Analyze representative architectures described in CTM-AI, ReCoN-Ipsundrum, tool-using language-model agents, retrieval and external-memory loops, and distributed services only at the level justified by their published descriptions, identifying what additional intervention evidence would be needed for an island assignment.
  • Develop a typed inference table stating what each result can support about architectural organization, access, self-monitoring, control, self-report, perceived consciousness, agency, phenomenal consciousness, welfare capacity, and moral status.
  • Treat the two lab papers and the CCT and MRCG drafts as unreviewed working constructions: verify their definitions and proofs independently, retain useful interventionist tools, and reject their phenomenal bridge claims unless separately defended.
  • Separate direct evidence, model-dependent inference, formal existence results, and metaphysical speculation; do not report a causal-island profile as a probability or detector of phenomenal consciousness.
  • Require consistency, positivity or stated support restrictions, intervention feasibility, and route isolation for each do-operation; use partial-identification bounds rather than assigning arbitrary counterfactual states when internal state installation is not well defined.
  • Test invariance under state relabeling and exact causal isomorphism, and state the stronger conditions under which a macro-level software description commutes with interventions on its physical implementation.
  • Vary candidate boundaries, temporal grains, intervention distributions, divergence choices, and edge thresholds; report an island stability envelope rather than selecting the most consciousness-favorable grain after inspection.
  • Distinguish direct endogenous coupling from correlations created by prompts, clocks, random seeds, environmental feedback, operating-system scheduling, replay buffers, or a common model call; include explicit external-route null models.
  • Use quantitative probing on held-out intervention magnitudes or contexts to validate fitted causal kernels rather than merely testing whether they reproduce the observations from which they were estimated.
  • Check the noncomposition construction for behavior minimality so that zero integration cannot be obtained merely by appending an irrelevant disconnected module.
  • Do not treat Langer and Ay's causal-information-integration proposal, Maguire and colleagues' noncomputability result, Kirilyuk's dynamic-complexity claims, or categorical biological objections as interchangeable; audit the premises and explanatory targets of each.
  • Keep phenomenal consciousness, access and control, self-report, perceived consciousness, agency, welfare capacity, and moral status in separate columns throughout the argument and conclusion.

Alternatives and Disconfirmation

  • A workspace or routing bus may produce global availability and task gains while remaining a one-way coordinator among several locally recurrent islands; this is a genuine access architecture but not evidence of one intrinsic complex.
  • A causally integrated core may exist but be disconnected from the selected monitor, action, or report interfaces, yielding low mediated-control and observability scores despite positive intrinsic organization.
  • Apparent inter-module recurrence may be generated by a shared clock, random seed, replay buffer, database, user interaction, or orchestration service; including that infrastructure within a wider boundary may merge islands, but it also changes the candidate system and must not be done opportunistically.
  • A software-level graph can be causally islanded while its hardware substrate has dense recurrent interactions, or software recurrence can be simulated on hardware whose relevant macro-causal organization is effectively feedforward; only an intervention-preserving abstraction can adjudicate which description bears the proposed intrinsic property.
  • Embodiment and environmental closure may be constitutively necessary on some theories, making the agent-plus-environment loop the relevant candidate, but this is a substantive boundary and bridge hypothesis rather than a consequence of recurrence or behavioral competence.
  • Several causal islands may jointly implement one robust agent policy, so agency at the service level need not imply one phenomenal subject; alternatively, one platform may host multiple candidate complexes whose reports are merged by a nonconscious interface.
  • Positive bidirectional coupling may reflect generic control stability, error correction, or engineering redundancy rather than consciousness; causal integration is therefore at most theory-conditioned mechanistic evidence, not a sufficient phenomenal criterion.
  • Biological organization, affective homeostasis, or living embodiment may supply further necessary conditions absent from the formal model, but categorical digital impossibility does not follow unless a defensible substrate-to-phenomenality bridge is added.
  • The formal noncomposition claim fails if its stated finite-SCM, behavior-minimality, intervention, and metric assumptions are jointly satisfied yet high mediated-control rank and recovery-linked reserve can be proven to impose a strictly positive architecture-independent lower bound on minimum bidirectional cut capacity, or if the proposed counterconstruction violates any of those assumptions.
  • A particular causal-island assignment is rejected if registered interventions across a declared zero-bridge partition produce reproducible state-specific effects in both directions after environmental, scheduler, shared-driver, and measurement routes have been randomized or severed, with held-out quantitative probes confirming that the residual effects were not fitted artifacts.
  • The decomposition framework is rejected or must be revised if causally isomorphic state relabelings, or two macro-realizations connected by an exact intervention-preserving abstraction, yield different island topology, bridge capacity, or irreducibility verdicts solely because of coordinates or implementation vocabulary.

Planned Manuscript Structure

  • Abstract: Summarize the causal-island problem, the boundary-and-grain-indexed decomposition, the noncomposition and converse results, and the explicit refusal to equate structural organization with phenomenal consciousness or moral status.
  • Research Question and Hypothesis: State the subject-unity question and the quantified Causal-Archipelago Noncomposition Hypothesis, defining which claims concern access, control, report, recurrence, and intrinsic causal organization.
  • Specialist Framework and Contribution: Introduce intervention-valid agent boundaries, temporal grains, external-route cuts, causal islands, bidirectional bridge capacity, cause-effect irreducibility, evidence localization, and fiber-island alignment, contrasting them with generic behavioral indicator lists.
  • Evidence and Literature Synthesis: Critically synthesize indicator-based AI consciousness assessments, CTM-AI and ReCoN-style architectural results, perceived-consciousness studies, digital-consciousness objections, causal-integration measures, and welfare governance while grading peer-reviewed articles, reports, preprints, and AI-generated lab drafts separately.
  • Cross-Disciplinary Integration: Relate Integrated Information and causal structure to mediated control, predictive observability, clinical report dissociations, software-hardware causal abstraction, observer attribution, and moral uncertainty without collapsing their explananda.
  • Analysis, Argument, or Model: Formally construct the multiscale causal-island atlas, prove the behavior-minimal noncomposition theorem and integrated-but-report-silent converse, and derive invariance, boundary-sensitivity, and partial-identification results.
  • Alternatives, Counterarguments, and Boundary Cases: Evaluate workspace buses, external-memory loops, embodied boundaries, serial hardware, causally integrated silent cores, split or multiple agents, biological necessity claims, and high integration generated by generic engineering functions.
  • Empirical Implications and Falsification Criteria: Derive white-box intervention signatures, shared-driver null tests, held-out quantitative probes, report-bus and reciprocal-feedback lesion predictions, and the formal and operational conditions that would reject the theorem or a specific island assignment.
  • Limitations: Acknowledge model-relative intervention sets, difficult internal state installation, computational costs, threshold sensitivity, unresolved macro-to-physical mappings, sparse evidence about deployed systems, and the absence of an established bridge from causal integration to experience or moral status.
  • Conclusion: Conclude that digital agents are not excluded from relevant causal organization in principle, but recurrence, accurate self-report, task performance, agency, and even causally verified control do not compose into evidence for one phenomenal subject unless their mechanisms belong to a robust irreducible causal complex, and even that complex would leave the phenomenal and moral bridges open.
