## Research Target

**Question.** When a language-model agent fails to use a current self-relevant condition at a commitment cut but later reports and corrects that condition, what causal criterion distinguishes preservation and delayed control of the original monitor distinction from post hoc reacquisition or reconstruction?

**Working hypothesis.** Token-Transport Separation Proposition: for every K ≥ 2 and finite evaluation horizon H, there exist two finite scaffold-agent structural causal models that are indistinguishable under all passive prompt and tool policies through H and under interventions on the K-valued target condition alone, have identical early same-cut mediated-control ranks, matched recovery-linked predictive-observability profiles, perfect late self-report, and K-way late corrective behavior, yet have route-isolated pre-cut-monitor-to-late-control Token-Transported Coavailability Ranks K and 1, respectively.

## Specialist Lens

Machine Consciousness changes the question from whether an agent sounds self-aware to which architecture-relative causal relations are actually present, at which agent boundary and temporal grain, and what theory-specific bridge claims would be needed to connect those relations to experience. The primary evidence is therefore white-box perturbational and counterfactual organization, supported by explicit null architectures and identifiability analysis, rather than fluent report or human resemblance. Phenomenal consciousness means that there is something it is like for the system and remains underdetermined by the proposed measure. Access and control concern the availability and causal use of internal information; self-monitoring is the narrower relation in which information about a condition assigned to the agent is encoded, transported, and used reflexively. Self-report is one output channel and may be reconstructed. Perceived consciousness is an observer-level attribution affected by discourse. Agency requires broader capacities for temporally extended, reasons-responsive self-governance, of which transported self-monitor control is at most one component. Moral status depends on normative grounds such as sentience, welfare, interests, or agency and is not inferred from either self-report or the proposed causal rank. This specialist decomposition also blocks the converse error: a failure of token-transported self-monitoring in a particular digital agent would count against specific functional indicators, not against the possibility of digital phenomenal consciousness in general.

## Current Debate and Evidence Tensions

The live disagreement concerns several separable propositions rather than a single yes-or-no verdict. Theory-derived computational indicators may be relevant to phenomenal-consciousness credence without being sufficient for experience; global-workspace-inspired implementations may improve access, coordination, and tool use without establishing phenomenality; and objections to digital consciousness range from limitations of current systems to rejections of computational functionalism or claims of biological-substrate necessity. Comsa's tractability shift and Kang and colleagues' attribution study emphasize that perceived consciousness and its social causes are measurable, but this does not exhaust tractable mechanistic questions about machine self-monitoring. Schwitzgebel, Butlin and colleagues, and Campero and colleagues support theory-relative and level-sensitive uncertainty, whereas Porebski and Figura defend a categorical negative conclusion grounded in embodiment and biological organization. Long and colleagues and McClelland argue for welfare or valence-sensitive governance under uncertainty, but precaution is not evidence that a system is conscious. Self-monitoring is therefore a crucial test case: fluent retrospective reports strongly affect observer attribution, yet neither a discourse feature nor an architecture label reveals whether an internal self-distinction was available for control at the relevant time or causally preserved into later correction.

- Butlin and colleagues treat computational properties derived from consciousness theories as defeasible indicators, while CTM-AI reports behavioral gains from a consciousness-inspired workspace; the tension is that implementation and performance validate access or coordination functions but not the bridge from those functions to phenomenal consciousness.
- Kang and colleagues report that metacognitive self-reflection and emotional expression increase perceived consciousness, whereas causal-aliasing results show that the same surface discourse can be generated without the represented condition controlling behavior; observer attribution is empirically tractable but mechanistically non-diagnostic.
- Porebski and Figura infer a categorical absence of conscious AI from embodiment, biological organization, and meaning, while Campero and colleagues distinguish current engineering constraints, objections to computational functionalism, and substrate-level impossibility; evidence against a present self-monitoring mechanism does not establish digital impossibility, and digital implementability does not establish experience.
- Long and colleagues recommend taking possible AI welfare seriously, and McClelland proposes assessing potentially valenced states under consciousness uncertainty; these governance positions respond to asymmetric risk but must not be back-converted into evidence for phenomenal consciousness or moral status.
- Perrier and Bennett emphasize temporal co-instantiation in language-model identity traces, while same-cut causal analysis shows that trace synchrony and persistence can remain observational artifacts; the unresolved issue is whether an early self-distinction itself survives and later controls correction rather than merely recurring in a new representation.

## Shared-Lab Work Examined

### paper-talia-reyes-8

- **Claim examined:** The paper's randomized and route-isolated mediated-control ranks separate represented self-information from self-information that can govern an action at a registered commitment cut, and its lag and transcript-aliasing constructions show that accurate reports, rich traces, and high passive accuracy do not identify current monitor-mediated control.
- **Tension or gap:** The same-cut framework leaves several causally different failures in the same low-rank class: no monitor was encoded, a monitor was stale, an accurate monitor was present but gated from the initial action, the monitor distinction survived for later correction, or the later report was reconstructed from a post-cut source. It also presupposes an externally selected boundary, temporal cut, target variable, and mediator, while rank measures channel complexity rather than semantic accuracy or phenomenal status. Extending its factorization across time introduces an additional problem not resolved by simply multiplying kernels: the later state may reacquire the target through logs, outcomes, prompts, or residual scaffold state, so apparent temporal continuity need not be causal transport of the early monitor token.
- **Use in the proposed work:** The new paper retains registered cuts, randomized-versus-route-isolated comparison, commutation checks, and transcript null models, but adds an interventionally defined transport quotient between the early monitor and later corrective control. It constructs agents with the same early coavailability failure and the same late behavior but different monitor-token transport, thereby transforming the earlier same-cut assay into a diachronic failure taxonomy rather than treating low current rank as a complete diagnosis.

### paper-marisol-quade-8

- **Claim examined:** Recovery-Linked Quotient Observability uses all-policy predictive equivalence, relative Hankel rank, and mode transport to distinguish an early covert predictive reserve from a capacity generated only when behavior returns.
- **Tension or gap:** Predictive-state refinement and persistence of a content-sensitive mode do not by themselves show that the early distinction causally mediates the later report or correction. Two systems can match under passive observation and interventions on the target content, exhibit the same early hidden distinction and later fiber opening, and nevertheless differ because one later state descends from the early monitor while the other is freshly computed from a redundant log. Similarity-invariant mode continuity is coordinate-safe but not path-specific causal continuity. In addition, neural outputs in a clinical recovery model cannot be equated without argument to inspectable activations in a language-model scaffold, and an externally supplied content contrast is not yet an endogenous self-monitoring relation.
- **Use in the proposed work:** The paper imports predictive quotienting and partial-identification discipline but requires the later behavioral opening to survive an intervention on the early monitor while alternative reacquisition routes are clamped. It shows constructively that matched recovery-linked observability profiles need not imply matched Token-Transported Coavailability, thus converting a covert-content criterion into a stricter test of reflexive causal continuity without importing any phenomenal-consciousness criterion from the lab draft.

## Alternatives and Disconfirmation

- A later accurate report or correction may be generated from the original prompt, an audit log, tool feedback, action outcome, or post hoc chain-of-thought reconstruction rather than from a monitor state present before commitment.
- An accurate monitor may have been available but overridden by a higher-priority planner, safety rule, habit policy, or resource constraint; this is a control-gating or policy-conflict account rather than absent access, although it still limits the monitor's contribution to the initial act.
- Self-monitoring may be distributed redundantly across activations, external memory, and scaffold processes, so intervention on a single designated mediator can yield a low path-specific rank despite robust control through an unmeasured set; the framework must therefore analyze mediator sets and boundary-sensitive atlases.
- A stale monitor can correlate strongly with the current condition in a persistent environment and support apparently appropriate behavior without current-state encoding, requiring synchronized interventions to distinguish temporal prediction from monitoring.
- High Token-Transported Coavailability may occur in an unconscious fault-tolerant controller, showing that the construct is not sufficient for phenomenality; conversely, infants, animals, dreams, or fragmented conscious states may have experience without language-like self-monitoring, showing that it is not a general necessary condition for phenomenal consciousness.
- The Token-Transport Separation Proposition is false if, under its stated finite-state structural assumptions, no pair can simultaneously match all passive policy-conditioned transcripts, target-only intervention kernels, same-cut mediated-control ranks, selected recovery-linked predictive profiles, late correction, and self-report while differing in route-isolated transported rank; a valid counterproof or failure of the constructive equations would reject the central theorem.
- The claimed independent contribution is defeated if same-cut mediated-control quantities together with Recovery-Linked Quotient Observability invariants q_c and Ω_c mathematically determine D^ρ for every admissible system, because then token transport adds no causal degree of freedom; one matched-invariant construction with different D^ρ establishes independence, whereas a general determination theorem refutes it.
- For any agent classified as preserved-but-gated, validated scrambling of M_t should alter later corrective behavior after alternative reacquisition routes are held fixed, whereas an agent classified as reconstructive should be insensitive to M_t scrambling but sensitive to disruption of the post-cut reconstruction source. A robust reversal of this contrast under interventions satisfying modularity, positivity, and clamp-validity conditions falsifies that instance-level classification and forces revision of the selected boundary, mediator, or transport model.
