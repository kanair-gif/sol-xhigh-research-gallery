Research Target

Question. Does feedback-specific metabolic expenditure during conscious access to a first item impose an exponentially recovering, layer-specific refractory constraint on conscious access to a second item?

Working hypothesis. In near-threshold two-target tasks, second-target access will obey logit P(A₂ = 1) = α + βᵀX − γC_R1 exp(−Δt/τ_R), with γ > 0, where C_R1 is the first target's ATP-equivalent expenditure attributable to feedback-recipient cortical compartments, Δt is target separation, X contains preregistered sensory, behavioral, arousal, representational-overlap, and criticality controls, and τ_R is measured independently from post-access metabolic recovery; selective suppression of local astrocytic glycogen mobilization will increase τ_R, whereas L-lactate rescue will restore τ_R and short-lag access without improving early feedforward decoding of the second target.

Analysis and Rigor Plan

  • Construct a stochastic resource-recovery model with feedback-compartment reserve B_R governed by dB_R/dt = (B_0 − B_R)/τ_R − κu_R(t), where u_R is content-specific recurrent synaptic demand; derive the exponential lag-cost law, its limiting cases, and predictions for blink depth, recovery slope, and interactions between first-target cost and target lag.
  • Develop an identifiable multimodal state-space estimator that separates feedforward and feedback-attributable energetic costs by combining laminar metabolic signals, directed connectivity, and representational similarity; constrain τ_R with independently measured recovery data and compare held-out predictions against fixed-capacity workspace, recurrence-only, predictive-error, and critical-branching models.

Alternatives and Disconfirmation

  • At matched first-target accuracy and recurrent peak strength, second-target feedforward decodability, prestimulus arousal, representational overlap, and network criticality, C_R1 has no positive held-out effect on short-lag second-target suppression, operationalized as γ ≤ 0 or a preregistered Bayes factor of at least 10 favoring the null.
  • The behavioral recovery constant inferred from second-target access falls outside the preregistered uncertainty interval of the independently measured feedback-compartment metabolic recovery constant τ_R in both the human transfer analysis and the causal primate experiment.
  • After metabolic sensors verify that feedback-layer suppression of astrocytic glycogen mobilization prolongs recovery and L-lactate restores it, the second-target access curve does not shift in the predicted directions, or any shift is fully accounted for by changes in early feedforward decoding, global arousal, motor reporting, or criticality.
