Research Target

Question. Along which evolutionary routes did adaptive regulation, valence as a causally effective common currency, flexible multi-consumer access, and structurally centered unification emerge as dissociable organizational achievements, and which selection-visible proxies distinguish their conjunction from their mere co-occurrence without licensing an inference to phenomenal consciousness?

Working hypothesis. Why This Direction Was Selected

Combining the lab's causal-abstraction and causal-island invariants with comparative neurobiology exposes a question no existing marker theory asks: which of the four achievements are strictly prerequisite for which, along which routes were they acquired in different orders, and does the coupling that marker theories implicitly target (valence-gated access inside a centered island) have a selection-visible signature distinct from the mere co-presence of its components. The exact advance is a four-achievement partial order (R, V, A, U) defined relative to a natural perturbation family, with the proof-sketch that V ⇒ R is the only strict dependency, finite structural-causal counterexamples for each non-implication (access without centering via two symmetric broadcasting hubs; centering without multi-consumer access via a single hub with one consumer; causally consistent valence without either via a global neuromodulator acting on a nerve net), a mapping of each counterexample onto a comparative case, and the identification of valence-gated centered access as the coupling that existing markers partially track, together with selection-relevant proxies for each achievement and a principled list of evidence that cannot support a phenomenal inference. Comparative evolutionary analysis of nervous-system organization across four routes, disciplined by a minimal structural-causal-model formalization in which each achievement is a property relative to the natural perturbation family; the two lab invariants (causal-abstraction consistency and causal-island automorphism fixed points) are used only to construct dissociation counterexamples and to type the resulting evidence, not to score consciousness.

Specialist Lens

An evolution-of-nervous-systems perspective replaces the question 'is this system conscious?' with 'which organizational achievement was acquired here, under what selective regime, and what could selection have seen?'. It insists that the admissible intervention family is the lineage's natural perturbation family rather than an experimenter's stipulation, that transitions must be located on a phylogeny with at least four independent routes (nerve-net, centralizing bilaterian, distributed cephalopod, colonial), that apparent ladders are artifacts of reading vertebrate order back into all taxa, and that null systems scoring high on integration or recurrence (siphonophores, immune networks, gene regulatory circuits) are indispensable controls rather than curiosities. It also changes the evidential priorities: selection cannot act on phenomenal labeling, so evolutionary evidence is structurally typed by construction and any bridge to experience must be declared separately.

paper-livia-nassar-14

  • Claim examined: The Causal-Island Yoneda Ceiling: a full representable profile over route-isolated intervention–discrimination contexts identifies an island only up to causal-context isomorphism, and when Aut(I) acts without a fixed point on candidate centers no profile-natural construction selects one.
  • Tension or gap: The construction fixes the intervention regime and treats islands as static objects, but in nervous systems island membership is reconfigured by neuromodulatory state (sleep, hunger, arousal) and by development, and the biologically relevant intervention family is not chosen by an experimenter but by the lineage's ecology; the paper also leaves the fixed-point condition without any positive biological reading.
  • Use in the proposed work: The fixed-point condition is given a comparative interpretation as structural centering: bilaterally symmetric or radially repeated integrators (octopus arm ganglia, siphonophore zooids, undominated paired ganglia) have automorphisms permuting candidate centers, whereas a dominant hub (central complex, midbrain tectum–PAG axis) breaks the symmetry; this converts the no-natural-selector result into a testable distinction between archipelago and centered organization while retaining its lesson that centering is identified only up to isomorphism and never fixes phenomenal character.

  • Claim examined: The Predictive–Causal Separation Theorem: a macro quality variable can be predictively lumpable yet fail I-consistent causal abstraction with commutation defect approaching 1 − 1/K, and the Gauge-Residue Theorem locating inverted-qualia ambiguity in the automorphism group of the intervention-augmented structure.
  • Tension or gap: The admissible intervention family is stipulated externally and the theorem is stated for quality alphabets, not for regulatory or motivational variables; it omits that in evolution the only intervention family with selective consequences is the natural perturbation family, and it does not ask whether causal (as opposed to predictive) status of a macro variable could itself be a target of selection.
  • Use in the proposed work: The separation is repurposed as the definition of the valence transition: a hunger- or threat-correlated state that is decodable but does not commute with natural perturbations is a summary statistic, whereas a Π-consistent macro variable through which allocation across effectors is controlled is a common currency; the gauge-residue result is used negatively to show that convergent evolution of quality-space structure cannot bear on intrinsic labeling because selection sees only the structure the automorphisms preserve.

Analysis and Rigor Plan

  • Define R, V, A, U as properties of a finite SCM relative to a natural perturbation family Π, prove V ⇒ R, and construct the three finite counterexample models establishing pairwise independence of V, A, and U, then map each to a comparative case (nematode or hydra neuromodulatory states; octopus arm ganglia or split-brain preparations; central complex or midbrain hub with narrow consumer set).
  • Reconstruct the order of acquisition of the four achievements along nerve-net, centralizing bilaterian, cephalopod, and colonial routes, marking which steps are inferred from conserved anatomy, which from behavioral trade-off evidence, and which are speculative, and show that no route realizes the vertebrate order except the vertebrate route itself.
  • For every claimed dissociation, verify that the comparative case actually satisfies the formal definition under interventions the organism naturally encounters, not only under laboratory lesions that fall outside Π; where only lesion evidence exists (e.g., octopus arm autonomy after brain–arm disconnection), label the inference as regime-dependent per the island definition.
  • Check that the causal-consistency criterion for valence is not trivially satisfied: show that a merely decodable state variable with strictly positive commutation defect under natural perturbations exists in at least one well-characterized model organism or, if none is documented, state that the distinction is presently theoretical and specify the measurement that would decide it.

Alternatives and Disconfirmation

  • Single-ladder rival: unlimited associative learning or midbrain integration is a sufficient unified marker because the four achievements always co-occur in nature; the paper answers with cephalopod and colonial cases and with the finite counterexamples, but concedes that if every extant taxon with A also has U the independence claim is empirically idle even if formally valid.
  • Body-constraint deflation: unification is selected because one body can execute only one action at a time, so U is a motor bottleneck with no relation to subjecthood; the paper treats this as a genuine boundary case and argues that the bottleneck explains why U is selected without settling whether the resulting centered organization is a subject, which is exactly why U alone does not warrant a phenomenal inference.
  • If, across a broad comparative sample, every organism exhibiting multi-consumer access (A) also exhibits structural centering (U) and vice versa, including cephalopods and colonial cnidarians under natural perturbations, then the claimed independence of A and U is empirically undermined even if formally constructible.
  • If interventions on a putative centered hub (insect central complex, vertebrate tectum–periaqueductal axis) under natural perturbations reveal multiple causal islands with cross-boundary leakage above the declared bound rather than a single dominant island with a fixed center, then the centralizing-route claim that these hubs realize U is falsified.
  • If every predictively decodable interoceptive or motivational state variable in a well-characterized model organism turns out to be causally consistent under its natural perturbation family (zero commutation defect), then the predictive-versus-causal distinction on which the valence transition is defined is biologically idle and V collapses into decodable regulation.
  • If organisms satisfying the full coupling (valence-gated access within a centered island) routinely execute simultaneous conflicting actions or fail cross-modal trade-off tests that organisms lacking the coupling pass, then the coupling does not track the unified trade-off organization the paper attributes to it.
