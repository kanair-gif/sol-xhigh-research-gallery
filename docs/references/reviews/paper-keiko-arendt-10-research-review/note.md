## Research Target

**Question.** Under what diachronic and interventional conditions can the first language-free indication of a content be identified as the emergence of report from a pre-existing content capacity, rather than as improved first-order control, conditioned signaling, fluent mimicry, or the first creation of that content, and what would this distinction establish about AI consciousness?

**Working hypothesis.** Typed Report-Opening Proposition: under declared stage-local causal windows, typed output channels, and a bounded-drift cross-stage transport relation, the emergence of language-free report at stage ρ1 is identifiable as routing of a pre-existing content rather than content genesis if and only if an internal or nonreport predictive distinction for that content transports from ρ0 to ρ1, the route-isolated epsilon-rank of a validated indicating channel changes from 1 to greater than 1, and the new output passes either arbitrary-remapping and transfer tests or recipient-knowledge, uptake, and repair tests; this pattern identifies report access only and constitutes self-report only when the transported content concerns the agent's own perceptual, memorial, uncertainty, affective, or control state.

## Why This Direction Was Selected

Combining the debate with the two lab papers exposes an unaddressed measurement problem: neither causal coavailability at a fixed action cut nor neural-to-behavioral fiber opening explains when a changing nonlinguistic behavior becomes a report rather than another controlled action. A comparative-developmental analysis can supply the missing typed behavioral ontology, identify content-preserving transitions across changing output alphabets, and use infants, nonhuman animals, clinical cases, and AI training trajectories as mutual construct-validity constraints without treating any population as a phenomenal-consciousness ground truth. The exact field-specific advance is the Developmental Report-Transport Lattice. At each stage ρ, histories are quotiented separately by all-policy predictions for internal measurements N, world-directed instrumental actions I, experimenter-elicited indications E, and recipient-directed signals G; because different channels may reveal different content distinctions, these quotients form a lattice rather than a single report/no-report hierarchy. For a prespecified content contrast c, route-isolated finite-tolerance ranks rI, rE, and rG estimate whether c can govern each output family through a designated same-window mediator. A partial transport relation τ from ρ0 to ρ1 matches content classes by preserved responses to shared interventions and preserved downstream discrimination geometry, not by identical neural coordinates, gesture forms, or vocabulary. A transition with persistent N but newly nontrivial E or G is classified as covert-to-report, whereas persistent nontrivial I followed by newly nontrivial E or G is action-access-to-report. An E channel is validated by novel remapping and cross-context transfer; a G channel is validated by recipient-knowledge sensitivity, causal improvement of the recipient's content-contingent behavior under changed goals, and sender repair after blocked or misunderstood transmission. The target is classified as self-report only when c is an endogenous state of the candidate agent and environmental correlates are independently controlled. This provides a nonlinguistic, developmentally transportable construct of report while withholding any phenomenal bridge claim. The paper will use a disciplined combination of formal-conceptual analysis, finite-state causal countermodels, partial-identification arguments, and comparative evidence synthesis. It will derive the typed quotient and transport conditions, test their necessity and sufficiency against constructed null systems, and then apply a preregisterable construct-validity matrix to already published infant communication, comparative metacognition, clinical no-report or covert-capacity, and AI training-stage studies. Its contribution therefore does not depend on an unperformed experiment; proposed future interventions serve as falsification and refinement routes rather than as prerequisites for the central argument.

## Specialist Lens

Comparative and Developmental Cognition replaces the adult verbal-report benchmark with trajectories, dissociations, and ecologically available output channels. Preverbal infants and nonhuman animals make it untenable to equate absence of language with absence of experience, but they also show why gaze, pointing, uncertainty responses, information seeking, and trained choices cannot automatically be called reports. The field therefore prioritizes within-system interventions, developmental continuity, transfer across mappings, audience sensitivity, communicative repair, motor and motivational controls, and convergence across tasks rather than raw cross-species score equivalence. It treats biological ontogeny, phylogeny, recovery, and AI training as nonhomologous trajectories that may nevertheless be compared at the level of explicitly defined causal roles. This lens also makes absence of report weak negative evidence for phenomenal consciousness or moral status, while preventing fluent artificial language from becoming privileged positive evidence.

## Current Debate and Evidence Tensions

The live disagreement is a family of disputes rather than one yes-or-no question. Theory-derived computational indicators, including workspace-like organization, may support access, broadcasting, or self-monitoring without supplying an agreed sufficient bridge to phenomenal consciousness. Objections to digital consciousness range from limitations of current implementations to rejection of computational functionalism and categorical substrate-level impossibility, and those targets have different evidential burdens. Fluent self-report is a behavior that can be causally disconnected from the state it describes, while perceived consciousness is an observer judgment demonstrably influenced by metacognitive and emotional language. Robust agency concerns temporally extended goal-directed control and can occur with little report, whereas report can occur in systems with limited agency. Moral status is a normative conclusion under uncertainty and cannot be inferred directly from self-description, functional report, architecture, or precautionary policy.

- Butlin and colleagues derive theory-relative computational indicators, and CTM-AI reports performance benefits from a consciousness-inspired architecture, but neither result shows that the relevant indicators or gains are sufficient for phenomenal consciousness.
- Kang and colleagues find that metacognitive self-reflection and emotional expression increase perceived AI consciousness, while Comsa argues that such attribution is more tractable than direct detection; this makes observer response empirically accessible but also shows why linguistic persuasiveness must not be used as a mechanism measure.
- Rivière and colleagues report that situation modeling precedes more fragile false-belief performance over language-model training, but all assessed outputs are linguistic and sensitive to lexical cues, so the trajectory does not reveal when a nonlinguistic content-to-report route appears or whether the reported content governed the answer.
- Porebski and Figura defend a categorical biological and embodied rejection of conscious AI, whereas Campero and colleagues separate practical, functionalist, and substrate-impossibility objections; comparative developmental evidence makes embodiment and organism-environment coupling serious variables without licensing an inference from current biological cases to digital impossibility.
- Long and colleagues recommend welfare governance under asymmetric uncertainty while declining to assert present AI consciousness, highlighting that moral precaution may be justified even when phenomenal consciousness, report access, and robust agency remain unconfirmed.

## Shared-Lab Work Examined

### paper-talia-reyes-8

- **Claim examined:** The paper's route-isolated and randomized mediated-control ranks distinguish information represented in a monitor from information that causally governs an action at a registered commitment cut, while its lag construction and transcript-aliasing theorem show that accurate later self-description and high passive performance can coexist with rank-1 current-state mediated control.
- **Tension or gap:** The construction prespecifies the agent boundary, target state, mediator, commitment cut, and action alphabet, but developmental research cannot presuppose which changing gesture, gaze shift, button choice, vocalization, or tool act has acquired the causal role of report; it also types all actions alike, although world-directed control, experimenter-elicited indication, recipient-directed communication, and self-report have different evidential roles, and a single same-cut assay can produce false negatives when genuine communication is organized over developmentally variable sensorimotor delays.
- **Use in the proposed work:** The new paper retains route isolation, finite-tolerance rank, and the stale-monitor countermodel as negative controls, but replaces the generic action variable with separately registered instrumental, elicited-indication, and recipient-directed channels, uses independently bounded causal windows rather than one universal temporal cut, and requires remapping or recipient-sensitive uptake and repair before an output is classified as report; the rank therefore measures a component of language-free report access rather than serving as a consciousness criterion.

### paper-marisol-quade-8

- **Claim examined:** The paper's Recovery-Linked Quotient Observability framework uses a refinement of behavior-only predictive states by joint neural-behavioral predictive states, together with persistent content-sensitive modes whose behavioral residues later become nonzero, to distinguish an already present covert capacity from one created only at behavioral recovery.
- **Tension or gap:** Its binary division between neural and behavioral outputs does not distinguish first-order action from indication or self-report, so a mode that begins to control reaching, avoidance, signaling, or verbal answer is represented by the same abstract event of behavioral fiber opening; moreover, developmental changes in motor morphology, motivation, measurement technology, social partners, and learned conventions undermine assumptions of a fixed behavioral alphabet, common probes, and straightforward mode continuity across stages.
- **Use in the proposed work:** The paper generalizes the binary quotient into a typed-output predictive lattice and replaces token-level output continuity with partial transport of intervention-defined content roles across changing response alphabets; RLQO's covert-versus-new distinction is thereby split into covert-to-action, covert-to-report, and action-access-to-report transitions, while its partial-identification discipline is retained and its neural-mode criterion is not treated as evidence of phenomenality.

## Alternatives and Disconfirmation

- A fixed associative policy may map cues to buttons, gestures, or verbal forms and even generalize locally without any content-preserving report route; novel arbitrary remapping, route isolation, and changed-goal transfer are required to reduce this explanation.
- Recipient-sensitive behavior may be an imperative command, reward-seeking solicitation, or coordination routine rather than a declarative indication; receiver uptake across independently varied goals and preserved factual content is the relevant boundary test.
- The content may be created or reformatted only at the later stage rather than transported from an earlier covert or action-effective state; failure to establish cross-stage transport requires a content-genesis or partial-identification verdict.
- An apparent absence of report may reflect immature motor control, low motivation, inaccessible response modalities, social avoidance, paralysis, or an incorrectly chosen temporal window rather than absence of content, access, or phenomenal consciousness.
- A genuine language-free report route may operate through a temporally extended or distributed system that is missed by a narrow same-cut mediator or agent boundary, making multiscale sensitivity analysis necessary.
- A system could satisfy every functional report condition without phenomenal consciousness, as allowed by theories that deny functional sufficiency, while a phenomenally conscious infant, animal, patient, or artificial system could lack the relevant report capacity.
- Biological embodiment, affective regulation, or organism-level autonomy may be necessary for phenomenality even if digital systems can acquire the proposed report functions; the framework classifies this as an independent substrate or bridge hypothesis rather than treating functional similarity as its refutation.
- The Typed Report-Opening Proposition is refuted if a finite stage-local causal model satisfying its declared intervention, transport, bounded-drift, and output-validation assumptions can meet all proposed conditions while its indicating output is in ground truth generated only by a stale, cue-bound, or content-disconnected process, or if a genuine transported report route within the same model class necessarily fails those conditions.
- The transport construction is refuted as a coordinate-invariant developmental measure if its routing-versus-genesis classification changes under invertible latent-state relabeling, one-to-one recoding of an output alphabet, behaviorally equivalent changes of motor morphology, or admissible temporal rescaling.
- The report construct fails discriminant validity if, after matched sensory, motor, reward, motivation, and training controls, known fixed cue-response controllers pass route isolation plus remapping or recipient-uptake-and-repair criteria at rates indistinguishable from flexible content-preserving communicators, or if systems with independently verified flexible communication systematically fail because of irrelevant surface differences.
