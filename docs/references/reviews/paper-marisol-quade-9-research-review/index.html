Specialist Lens

A generic AI-consciousness survey typically compares architectures, reports, and benchmark behavior at one time and treats failure to report as weak negative evidence. Clinical Consciousness instead begins from structured dissociation: sensory processing, content representation, global or flexible access, policy commitment, motor execution, language production, memory-based retrospective report, and arousal can fail or recover separately. The model therefore includes a recovery stage ρ, externally controlled content C_ρ, a content-reachable predictive state Z_ρ, recorded neural or implementation telemetry N_ρ, a candidate access mediator M_ρ, a central commitment A*_ρ, a downstream motor or report gate G_ρ, overt behavior B_ρ, and delayed self-report Y_ρ. It treats prognosis of later recovery as distinct from diagnosis of current covert control and treats both as distinct from phenomenal consciousness. Clinical countermodels include aphasia, corticospinal disconnection, fluctuating arousal, sensory reserve without flexible access, memory reconstruction, stale monitoring, and newly recruited compensatory pathways. In a patient, organismal continuity and lesion anatomy may support—but cannot prove—a downstream-gate interpretation; in an AI system, a version update or scaffold may replace the relevant route and may not preserve token identity, mediator semantics, temporal grain, or agent boundary. The target vector is consequently disaggregated into representation, architectural access, current causal control, self-report, perceived consciousness, long-horizon agency, phenomenal consciousness, and moral-status judgment. Failure to identify one coordinate is not evidence that the others are absent, and uncertainty about a patient's covert control cannot diminish the patient's independent moral and legal status.

Current Debate and Evidence Tensions

The live disagreement concerns several non-equivalent targets. Theory-derived computational indicators and global-workspace-inspired architectures may establish architectural organization, broadcast-like access, self-monitoring, or improved control without establishing phenomenal consciousness. Fluent self-report and emotional or metacognitive language can increase perceived consciousness, but observer attribution is an output of a human-AI interaction rather than a measurement of experience. Objections to digital consciousness range from limitations of current implementations to rejections of computational functionalism and categorical substrate-level impossibility claims; these positions do not have the same logical force. Welfare proposals address moral decisions under uncertainty about sentience, valence, and agency, but precautionary thresholds are not evidence that consciousness is present. Most of the retrieved AI sources are preprints or reports, so they map uncertainty and proposed indicators rather than settle the phenomenal question.

  • Butlin and colleagues derive theory-relative computational indicators, while Schwitzgebel emphasizes disagreement among the theories from which those indicators are drawn; implementability therefore does not establish theory-independent sufficiency for phenomenality.
  • Yu and colleagues report functional gains from a Conscious Turing Machine-inspired architecture, but those gains concern access, multimodal coordination, tool use, and agentic performance rather than phenomenal consciousness.
  • Comsa argues that perceived AI consciousness is more tractable than direct detection, and Kang and colleagues report that discourse features alter perceived consciousness; this tractability also demonstrates why observer attribution must not be used as an internal-mechanism variable.
  • Campero and colleagues separate practical, functionalist, and strict-impossibility objections, whereas Porebski and Figura defend a categorical biological and embodied conclusion; the latter requires substrate and bridge premises not supplied by behavioral underdetermination alone.
  • Long and colleagues recommend taking possible AI welfare seriously without asserting present consciousness, while valence-focused proposals offer conditional governance proxies; moral precaution changes a decision rule under asymmetric loss but does not increase the evidential likelihood of phenomenal consciousness by itself.
  • Clinical prognostic models can predict later recovery from functional networks and clinical characteristics, yet prediction of outcome does not identify whether a patient currently has content-specific access, current decision control, or experience.

Analysis and Rigor Plan

  • Define a stage-indexed finite structural causal model in which the predictive quotient, same-cut mediator, central commitment, overt output gate, delayed report, and recovery-stage transition are separate mathematical objects.
  • Define the sharp retrospective causal-coavailability interval over an external-intervention compatibility class and characterize when its endpoints remain [1, K], become nontrivially bounded, or collapse to a point.
  • Prove the approximate transport inequality from row-wise total-variation contraction and derive the corresponding lower bound for approximate stochastic nonnegative rank under bijective content and action registration.
  • Construct one-defect-at-a-time necessity examples for content transport, mediator transport, commitment-cut registration, route completeness, and the no-new-upstream-route assumption.
  • Derive robustness conditions in terms of rank margins and simultaneous confidence sets, and prove an NP-hardness corollary for exact interval-endpoint evaluation by restriction to exact stochastic nonnegative rank.
  • For every K ≥ 2 and every j in {1, …, K}, explicitly construct a two-stage model with fixed all-policy external neural-behavioral-report kernels, fixed positive q_c and Ω_c, fixed late rank K, and early mediated-control rank j.
  • Prove that the closed-output-gate and newly-created-route systems preserve the same observable recovery law and the same transported content poles or predictive-state fibers despite differing in early causal control.
  • Prove max_z TV((R_0P_A)_z, (P_ZR_1)_z) ≤ δ_Q + δ_K and then prove rank_{+,TV}^ε(R_0) ≥ rank_{+,TV}^{ε+δ_Q+δ_K}(R_1), carefully stating where bijectivity, route isolation, and common commitment semantics are used.
  • Show by counterexample that dropping any one of mediator transport, action-cut transport, or no-new-route completeness can reduce early rank to 1 while leaving the remaining recovery signatures unchanged.
  • Prove the phenomenal-bridge underdetermination result as a conservative model expansion rather than treating philosophical zombies or substrate independence as empirical facts.
  • Gate-recovery versus route-growth pair: both systems contain the same persistent content-sensitive neural mode and later exact command response; in the gate model M_0 and A_0 already depend on content but G_0 blocks overt output, whereas in the route-growth model M_0 and A_0 are content-independent and a new Z-to-M-to-A* route is installed at recovery.
  • Stale-monitor and replay null: a persistent or post-action memory yields accurate delayed report and high perceived consciousness, while the monitor available at the registered current commitment cut is stale or causally disconnected, giving current control rank 1.
  • AI architectural null: a global-workspace-inspired scaffold broadcasts labels and improves benchmark performance, but a separate controller selects actions and the broadcast is read only by a report generator; an update later connects the broadcast to control, reproducing apparent recovery without backdating earlier access.
  • Stage-mixture null: apparent gradual fiber opening is generated by changing mixtures of arousal or task-engaged states rather than transport of one individual's content-control mode, showing why mixture stability is a boundary condition rather than a consciousness assumption.

Alternatives and Disconfirmation

  • The transport theorem is formally falsified by an exact finite set of stochastic matrices satisfying the declared bijective registrations, route-isolation assumptions, and defects δ_Q and δ_K but violating either the total-variation channel bound or the approximate-rank inequality.
  • The sharp no-go is falsified if the stated external all-policy law together with q_c, Ω_c, and the late route-isolated channel determines an early rank greater than 1 in every compatible finite SCM without adding an unacknowledged internal-intervention, continuity, or no-new-route assumption.
  • An applied claim of inherited covert control is rejected when the relevant patient or AI trajectory fails its prespecified boundary conditions—for example, content labels cannot be aligned, the mediator branch is absent early, the commitment cut changes meaning, stage mixtures are unresolved, or a newly formed bypass exceeds the allowed commutation defect—even if recovery and later report are genuine.

  • Countermodel or Null System: Develop the gate-recovery, route-growth, stale-monitor, report-replay, AI scaffold, and stage-mixture systems and show exactly which observables and invariants they share.
  • Empirical Boundary Conditions and Falsification Criteria: State content registration, temporal-cut, route-completeness, state-preparation, transport, mixture-stability, and tolerance conditions, using existing or future data only to shrink the formal compatibility class rather than to supply the paper's main result.

  • Conclusion: Conclude that later recovery can backdate early content-guided control only through an explicit and testable continuity certificate, while self-report, perceived consciousness, agency, phenomenality, and moral status remain distinct conclusions.
