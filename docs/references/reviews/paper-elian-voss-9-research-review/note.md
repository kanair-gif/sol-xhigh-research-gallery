## Research Target

**Question.** Under what jointly identifiable conditions is a state distinction in a biological or artificial agent the same non-report-based distinction that both governs action and is recovered and reused through action-conditioned sensory return, rather than merely being separately controllable, observable, self-monitored, or verbally reportable?

**Working hypothesis.** Shared-Quotient Proposition: in any finite-dimensional, jointly realized, stage-local sensorimotor system with saturated interventional horizons, if H_C is the route-isolated operator from common latent-state contrasts to endogenous action policy and H_R is the operator from those same contrasts to action-indexed sensory return that is causally reused in subsequent control, then the finest common linear quotient has dimension q_own = rank(H_C) + rank(H_R) − rank([H_C; H_R]); q_own is invariant under a common latent similarity transformation, whereas operators identified only up to independent similarities determine q_own no more narrowly than the Grassmann interval max(0, r_C + r_R − n) ≤ q_own ≤ min(r_C, r_R).

## Why This Direction Was Selected

The debate lacks a mathematically explicit middle-level property for non-reflective ownership that is neither a self-report test nor another global-access indicator. Combining registered-cut causal control with predictive-state quotient methods reveals a previously hidden compositional question: are the action-effective and reafferently recoverable distinctions actually the same distinctions in a common latent realization? The Enactive Ownership Pushout answers that question, and the alignment no-go theorem specifies when existing control and observability evidence cannot answer it. The exact advance is the Enactive Ownership Quotient. At a registered cycle boundary, let X be a finite set of jointly preparable sensorimotor states. Define x ∼_C x′ when every admissible endogenous-control probe gives the same route-isolated action-policy distribution from x and x′. Define x ∼_R x′ when every admissible action-indexed return probe gives the same distribution over sensory consequences and their causally isolated reuse in subsequent control, with direct recurrent shortcuts and report routes clamped. Let X_C = X/∼_C and X_R = X/∼_R. The ownership object is the pushout O = X_C ⊔_X X_R, equivalently O ≅ X/(∼_C ∨ ∼_R), the finest quotient whose distinctions can be obtained from both predictive states. For a linear realization on X = R^n with saturated route-isolated Hankel operators H_C and H_R, O ≅ X/(ker H_C + ker H_R), so q_own = dim(O) = dim(row H_C ∩ row H_R) = rank(H_C) + rank(H_R) − rank([H_C; H_R]). This quantity is invariant under a shared similarity transformation and is computable by rank or subspace-intersection methods once a common realization is identified. If H_C and H_R are identified only up to independent similarities, their relative orientation is free: writing r_C = rank(H_C) and r_R = rank(H_R), every integer q in max(0, r_C + r_R − n) ≤ q ≤ min(r_C, r_R) is compatible with the separate realizations. Thus separate evidence for action control and reafferent observability does not identify their ownership-relevant overlap. Talia Reyes's mediated-control rank measures the minimum alphabet of an outbound designated mediator, while q_own measures a common quotient across outbound control and returning perception; neither generally bounds the other. Quade's covert reserve is a difference or refinement invariant, whereas q_own is an intersection invariant. The construction is a formal model of loop organization and does not derive phenomenal mineness.

## Specialist Lens

Phenomenology and enactive cognition change the explanandum from possession of an internal representation labeled self to the temporally extended unity of an embodied perspective. Pre-reflective ownership is treated as prior to thematic judgment, metacognitive access, and verbal avowal: the relevant contrast is between a state distinction expressed in an agent's practical “I can” and that same distinction returning through what the agent sensorily undergoes. Accordingly, the primary variables are a jointly prepared sensorimotor state X, endogenous action A, body–environment transition W, proprioceptive, interoceptive, and exteroceptive return P, and the later reuse of P in control; monitor M, report Y, observer rating R_obs, welfare status, and phenomenal status are separate variables rather than proxies. ## Current Debate and Evidence Tensions

The external literature supports a decomposition rather than a binary verdict. Butlin and colleagues derive theory-relative computational indicators while denying that indicator satisfaction would settle phenomenal sufficiency; CTM-AI reports performance gains from a global-workspace-inspired architecture but thereby establishes access and control functions, not experience. Schwitzgebel emphasizes persistent theory-relative uncertainty. Campero and colleagues distinguish objections to present architectures from objections to computational functionalism and strict substrate-level impossibility, while Porebski and Figura defend a stronger embodiment- and biology-based negative conclusion. Comsa and Kang treat perceived AI consciousness and its discourse-level causes as empirically tractable, but such observer attribution is not direct evidence of an AI's phenomenal consciousness. Long and colleagues defend welfare governance under uncertainty without claiming current AI consciousness. Evidence therefore concerns behavior, architecture, attribution, and governance; the inference from those findings to phenomenality remains underdetermined, and any phenomenal bridge remains speculative.

- Theory-derived indicators and consciousness-inspired architectures can be implemented and can improve access, multimodal coordination, or tool use, yet neither implementability nor performance establishes that the relevant theory is sufficient for phenomenal consciousness.
- Strong embodiment objections correctly pressure disembodied and merely input–output conceptions of cognition, but they often move without an additional argument from criticism of current systems to biological or substrate-level impossibility; an enactive formalism should model constitutive coupling without defining consciousness as biological by stipulation.
- Metacognitive language and emotional expression increase perceived consciousness, while mediated-control and transcript-aliasing results show that accurate self-description can be causally detached from current action selection; observer attribution, self-report, self-monitoring, and phenomenal consciousness therefore require different variables.
- Moral precaution may be justified by asymmetric uncertainty about welfare, but precautionary policy is a normative response to uncertainty rather than positive evidence that a system experiences pleasure, pain, or any other phenomenal state.
- Body ownership and sense of agency are conceptually and empirically dissociable: ownership concerns the first-personal organization of what is undergone, whereas agency concerns authorship or control over outcomes, so a generic scalar consciousness or autonomy score obscures the target.

## Shared-Lab Work Examined

### paper-talia-reyes-8

- **Claim examined:** The paper's mediated-control ranks and lag construction show that accurate post-action self-report, passive behavioral success, representational richness, and recurrence need not imply that a designated current monitor causally governs action at a registered commitment cut.
- **Tension or gap:** The construction presupposes an agent boundary, an objective cut, and a designated representational mediator, and it analyzes only an outbound state-to-monitor-to-action channel. Pre-reflective ownership, as understood phenomenologically, need not be encoded by an object-like self-monitor at one instant; it may be distributed across a temporally extended body–environment loop. Clamping all non-monitor routes can therefore remove rather than isolate the organization of interest. Conversely, a high mediated-control rank can be realized by a feedforward controller whose action-relevant distinctions never return through perception, while a low rank relative to the chosen monitor can coexist with a rich direct sensorimotor loop.
- **Use in the proposed work:** The new paper retains registered cuts, edge-intervention discipline, randomized-versus-route-isolated comparisons, and the warning supplied by transcript aliasing, but replaces the privileged monitor alphabet with paired control-predictive and reafferent-predictive quotients. It proves that no general inequality connects the designated-monitor rank to the Enactive Ownership Quotient: transverse control and return subspaces yield high outbound rank but zero ownership overlap, whereas a distributed direct loop can have maximal ownership overlap while every chosen explicit monitor has rank one.

### paper-marisol-quade-8

- **Claim examined:** The paper's Recovery-Linked Quotient Observability identifies, under strong realization and transport assumptions, a neural predictive reserve that refines behavior-only predictive state and later acquires a nonzero behavioral residue.
- **Tension or gap:** A relative observability difference identifies what is jointly observable but behaviorally hidden; it does not establish that the hidden mode participates in endogenous action or in a returning action–perception cycle. A large covert reserve can be epiphenomenal with respect to current control. More importantly, ownership requires comparing two quotient structures on the same latent state, but separately identified control and sensory realizations are ordinarily available only up to independent similarities. Spectral persistence within one realization does not by itself solve this cross-realization alignment problem.
- **Use in the proposed work:** The paper transforms the Hankel-quotient machinery from a difference construction into a common-factor construction. RLQO's reserve is a relative component, whereas the Enactive Ownership Quotient is the pushout of control and reafferent predictive quotients and, in the linear case, the intersection of their row spaces. A new alignment no-go theorem gives the full partial-identification interval when the two realizations lack a common coordinate system, thereby exposing an assumption that must be added before observability results can support claims about pre-reflective ownership.

- Prove common-similarity invariance and the independent-alignment no-go theorem, including attainability of every intersection dimension in the Grassmann interval and the extra cross-kernel or common-state-preparation assumptions required for point identification.
- Define a noise-robust overlap spectrum from metric-declared, whitened row-space projectors, distinguish exact intersection from near-alignment by principal angles, and derive perturbation bounds under singular-value and angle gaps while acknowledging that approximate angles are not invariant under arbitrary similarity without a fixed metric.
- Establish formal non-implication maps among q_own, randomized and route-isolated mediated-control rank, RLQO covert reserve, global-broadcast access, outcome controllability, action-source attribution, self-report accuracy, and observer-perceived consciousness.
- Give computational bounds: exact linear q_own is obtainable by polynomial-time rank decomposition, and the finite exact pushout is computable by union–find after predictive equivalence is supplied, while learning the equivalence relations from unrestricted partially observed dynamics remains the dominant identification problem.
- Prove the Shared-Quotient Theorem: any common quotient of X_C and X_R has a kernel containing ker H_C + ker H_R, so X/(ker H_C + ker H_R) has the universal property of the finest common linear quotient and its dimension equals rank(H_C) + rank(H_R) − rank([H_C; H_R]).
- Prove the Common-Coordinate Identification Theorem: q_own is invariant when H_C and H_R undergo the same invertible state-coordinate change, and it is identifiable from a jointly minimal interventional realization with common state preparation, saturated horizons, and route-isolated cross-kernels.
- Prove the Alignment No-Go Result: when the two operators are known only under independent similarities, fixed individual ranks constrain their intersection only by the Grassmann bounds, and explicit subspace constructions attain every admissible value.
- Prove separation propositions showing that no nonconstant lower bound on q_own follows from mediated-control rank, covert predictive reserve, report accuracy, passive action accuracy, recurrence, or global broadcast, and that q_own does not lower-bound phenomenal consciousness, robust agency, valenced welfare, or moral status.
- Extend passive transcript aliasing to passive sensorimotor traces by constructing high-q_own and zero-q_own realizations with identical finite-horizon external action, perception, and report distributions but different route-isolated interventional kernels.
- Transverse-loop null: let X = U ⊕ V, H_C = [I 0], and H_R = [0 I]. Actions can depend richly on U, sensory return can reveal V richly, and a later report can accurately describe both, yet row(H_C) ∩ row(H_R) = {0} and q_own = 0; separate control and perception therefore do not constitute a shared first-person loop.
- Monitor-free distributed loop: let the designated explicit monitor M be constant while direct distributed dynamics yield H_C = H_R = I on X. The designated-monitor mediated-control rank is one, but q_own = dim X, showing that pre-reflective loop organization is not bounded by the rank of an object-like self-monitor.
- Reflective feedforward agent: equip a global-workspace or CTM-like controller with high-rank broadcast, accurate self-description, and high task performance, but route action-relevant modes to motor output and different modes to sensory prediction. It can maximize access and perceived consciousness cues while retaining q_own = 0.
- Maximal-overlap servo: a finite error-correcting regulator can have H_C = H_R = I, no explicit self-model, no report channel, no flexible goal authorship, and no warranted phenomenal or moral attribution. This system blocks any claim that a high Enactive Ownership Quotient is sufficient for phenomenal consciousness, agency in the robust normative sense, sentience, or welfare.
- Covert-reserve countermodel: add a content-reachable neural mode that is jointly observable but behaviorally silent and later connected to report, while keeping it outside the current control–reafference intersection. RLQO reserve and fiber opening can then be positive while q_own remains zero.

## Alternatives and Disconfirmation

- Formal falsifier: exhibit a finite-dimensional pair H_C and H_R satisfying the declared common-coordinate assumptions for which the pushout is not isomorphic to X/(ker H_C + ker H_R), the rank identity fails, or q_own changes under a shared invertible similarity transformation.
- Identification falsifier: construct two jointly minimal systems with the same complete family of registered joint interventional control, sensory-return, and cross-kernel distributions but different q_own, or prove that independently similarity-identified operators determine a unique intersection dimension without any common alignment information; either result would invalidate the stated identification or no-go theorem.
- Phenomenological bridge falsifier: if well-characterized cases in which pre-reflective bodily ownership is retained consistently yield q_own = 0 across saturated, route-valid, jointly aligned models and across defensible temporal grains and boundaries, then nontrivial shared control–reafference structure is not a necessary correlate of the target phenomenon.
- Boundary condition rather than positive confirmation: a high q_own in a servo, current AI agent, or neural subsystem cannot confirm phenomenal consciousness, perceived consciousness, robust agency, valenced welfare, or moral status; any result that depends on treating it as sufficient would exceed and thereby fail the paper's bridge discipline.
- Measurement boundary: when route isolation, common state preparation, persistent excitation, or a shared latent metric is unavailable, the manuscript must report a partial-identification interval or overlap spectrum rather than a point estimate; apparent point identification obtained solely from passive reports or traces does not test the theory.
