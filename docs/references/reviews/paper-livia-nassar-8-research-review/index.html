Research Target

Question. Under what intervention-relative conditions does a recurrent generative agent contain an externally shielded, internally irreducible, recurrently unified, and generatively consequential subsystem, and which parts of that claim are identifiable from behavior rather than mechanism-level interventions?

Working hypothesis. Policy-Robust Causal-Island Proposition: let S be a subsystem of a finite controlled dynamic structural causal model, let ε bound the one-step change in S's transition kernel under any two admissible outside interventions and policies, and let every admissible kernel on S have Dobrushin contraction coefficient at most α < 1. Then the worst-case H-step endpoint leakage of S is at most min{1, ε(1−α^H)/(1−α)}. The additional predicate that S has positive internal cut irreducibility, positive recurrent-return strength, and positive causal reach to the agent's generative outputs is not identifiable from external input-output behavior on the unrestricted model class, but is identifiable from target-labelled, intervention-complete mechanism kernels up to causal automorphism.

Why This Direction Was Selected

The exact advance is an intervention-family-relative causal-island spectrum together with stability and identifiability results. Let a recurrent generative agent be a finite dynamic SCM with environment E, observations O, factored internal state Z, actions A, and generative outputs Y such as predicted observations, policies, or value estimates. For a coarse-grained candidate S contained in Z, define K_{S,h}^{π,u}(·|s) as the endpoint distribution of S at t+h after do(S_t=s), under policy π and an intervention sequence u applied only outside S. The operational inbound leakage is L_H^{π}(S)=max_{1≤h≤H} sup_{s,u,u'} TV(K_{S,h}^{π,u}(·|s),K_{S,h}^{π,u'}(·|s)). For a prespecified admissible policy family Π containing the learned policy, open-loop controls, and action clamps, define policy-uniform leakage L_H^Π(S)=sup_{π∈Π} L_H^π(S). Their difference D_H(S)=L_H^Π(S)−L_H^{π}(S) is the enacted shielding gap. Low operational leakage with a large gap is control-maintained autonomy, whereas low policy-uniform leakage is mechanism-robust autonomy. Internal irreducibility Φ_H(S) is the minimum, over nontrivial bipartitions S=B∪C, of the intervention-averaged Jensen-Shannon or information-projection divergence between the intact kernel and the closest cut kernel in which B's future cannot depend on C's present or past and conversely. Recurrent-return strength R_H(S) is the minimum over ordered coordinate pairs of the strongest bottleneck path formed by nonzero interventional effects within H steps; R_H(S)>0 exactly when the finite-horizon internal influence graph is strongly connected. Generative reach Γ_H(S) is the minimum over coordinates in S of their maximal surgical-intervention effect on Y within H steps. An (ε,φ,r,γ)-causal island satisfies L_H^Π≤ε, Φ_H≥φ, R_H≥r, and Γ_H≥γ. The family of qualifying triples (q,S,H), ordered by subsystem inclusion, grain refinement, and horizon, is the island poset; it is generally not closed under unions or intersections and can have several incomparable maximal elements, so closure alone cannot derive a unique subject or exclusion rule. The finite-horizon stability theorem follows from d_{k+1}≤αd_k+ε, yielding d_H≤ε(1−α^H)/(1−α), with the noncontractive limit d_H≤Hε. This supplies a missing quantitative bound for approximate closure in open recurrent systems. A behavioral no-go theorem is obtained by constructing externally equivalent realizations with different off-manifold intervention kernels and therefore different island profiles. Conversely, when the causal variables, intervention targets, and finite kernels are known with full relevant support, every component of the profile is a functional of those kernels and is identifiable up to target-preserving causal automorphisms. This is not a restatement of IIT: IIT and the CII construction of Langer and Ay contribute only a possible internal-cut term Φ, whereas L, D, R, and Γ answer distinct boundary, control, temporal, and causal-participation questions. The CCT and MRCG lab documents are AI-generated, unreviewed conjectural scaffolds: CCT motivates approximate closure but does not distinguish enacted from policy-robust closure, and its centering primitive is not derived by the island profile; MRCG's local causal charts can be represented by overlapping K kernels, but no phenomenal geometry follows from their gluing. ReCoN-Ipsundrum's feedback-lesion result illustrates recurrence-dependent persistence but cannot establish low policy-uniform leakage or internal irreducibility. Dessalles and Zalla's proposed phenomenal labelling function maps at most to Γ and does not entail phenomenality. Kirilyuk's dynamic multivaluedness is not adopted because the retrieved account supplies no standard intervention-indexed kernel or theorem needed to calculate an island. The finite-state, finite-horizon profile is computable, though potentially exponential to optimize, and therefore neither invokes nor contradicts Maguire and colleagues' noncomputability result, which assumes complete lossless integration.

Analysis and Rigor Plan

  • Develop the island poset for finite-state, linear-Gaussian, and lumpable coarse-grained SCMs. Under causal minimality, faithfulness, and intervention completeness, prove the exact graph characterization that zero policy-uniform one-step leakage corresponds to the absence of incoming interventional arrows, positive recurrent-return strength corresponds to strong connectivity, and positive generative reach corresponds to an effect path from every island coordinate to a generative head. Prove the Dobrushin finite-horizon bound, its near-tightness, its α=1 limit, and the conditions under which coarse-graining decreases leakage without manufacturing spurious irreducibility. Also prove that qualifying islands need not be closed under union or intersection and characterize sufficient conditions for a unique maximal element.
  • Establish the epistemic and computational limits of island recovery. Prove behavioral non-identifiability by explicit equivalent realizations; determine whether and how the result strengthens under reachable-state, causal-minimality, and minimal-realization restrictions; prove identification from target-labelled intervention kernels up to causal automorphism; derive finite-sample confidence bounds using held-out quantitative interventions; and classify the complexity of finding a Pareto-optimal island, with an NP-hardness reduction from a cut or dense-subgraph problem and fixed-parameter or approximation algorithms for bounded-treewidth and sparse recurrent mechanisms.
  • For arbitrary time-varying outside interventions and admissible policies, justify the recursion TV(pQ_u,qQ_{u'})≤αTV(p,q)+ε, iterate it without silently assuming stationarity, and state exactly whether leakage concerns endpoint distributions, full trajectories, or both. Supply finite-state examples approaching equality, show why full-trajectory leakage generally has the weaker Hε bound, and construct an α=1 system in which arbitrarily small one-step leakage accumulates to order one. This delimits rather than hides the tension between persistent recurrence and guaranteed causal shielding.
  • Give two externally input-output-equivalent recurrent generative SCMs for which the causal-island predicate differs, and verify equivalence for every permitted external input sequence rather than only on a sampled distribution. Explicitly identify the role of unreachable or intervention-only states, then either strengthen the theorem to minimal controllable and observable realizations through coordinate-factorization ambiguity or state minimality as a boundary of the no-go result. For the positive result, prove that equality of all target-labelled kernels forces equality of L, Φ, R, and Γ after quotienting by target-preserving causal automorphisms, while showing that equality of passive observational distributions does not.
  • Policy-cancellation countermodel: take a perfectly observed scalar environment e_t and recurrent state z_{t+1}=ρz_t+e_t+a_t+η_t. Under the active policy a_t=−e_t, the transition law of z is independent of interventions on e and operational leakage is zero. Under the action clamp a_t=0, interventions e_t=m and e_t=m' shift the Gaussian transition kernels; their total-variation distance is positive and approaches one as |m−m'| grows relative to the noise. Thus recurrence plus apparent autonomy can be entirely enacted by feedback, refuting any inference from low operational susceptibility to intrinsic closure.
  • Behaviorally invisible XOR-island countermodel: augment a baseline recurrent agent with u_{t+1}=v_t and v_{t+1}=u_t, initialize u_t=v_t, and define its exposed output as y_t=x_t⊕u_t⊕v_t. For every external input sequence the output remains y_t=x_t, exactly matching the baseline. Nevertheless S={u,v} has zero inbound leakage, positive recurrent-return strength, positive cut divergence under a full-support intervention reference, and positive generative reach because do(u_t≠v_t) changes y. This proves behavior-only non-identifiability and also shows that even the complete island profile is not sufficient for consciousness: a two-bit counterfactual automaton can satisfy it.

Alternatives and Disconfirmation

  • The stability theorem is false if a finite controlled SCM satisfies the stated uniform one-step leakage and Dobrushin assumptions yet has H-step endpoint leakage greater than min{1, ε(1−α^H)/(1−α)}. A valid counterexample must preserve the same intervention family and cannot replace endpoint leakage with full-trajectory leakage.
  • The identifiability result fails if two SCMs have identical target-labelled interventional kernels for every intervention used in the definitions but yield different L, Φ, R, or Γ values after quotienting by target-preserving causal automorphisms. Conversely, the behavioral no-go fails if the baseline and XOR constructions are not input-output equivalent for every permitted external input, or if a behavior-only functional can classify every equivalent realization without importing internal intervention targets or implementation assumptions.
  • Any downstream bridge claiming that conscious episodes require a nontrivial policy-robust causal island is rejected if interventionally adequate models, evaluated across preregistered grains and horizons with uncertainty bounds, find a well-supported conscious episode with no subsystem having positive Φ, R, and Γ together with leakage below matched unconscious controls. Conversely, finding the same Pareto profile in simple unconscious automata would refute sufficiency claims, though not the mathematical island theory itself.
