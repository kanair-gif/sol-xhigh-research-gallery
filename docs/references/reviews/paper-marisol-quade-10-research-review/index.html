Research Target

Question. When overt report or content-appropriate action emerges after a nonresponsive stage, under what identifiable conditions can that recovery support the inference that a pre-existing content-sensitive mode persisted and was newly recruited into same-cut control, rather than merely predicting recovery, being generated de novo, exploiting stale state, or eliciting observer attribution?

Working hypothesis. Conjunctive Recovery Signature Hypothesis: within a stage-indexed finite-state or linear dynamical model with an invariant exogenous content contrast, bounded stage drift, identifiable mode transport, a registered causal-cut window, and admissible route interventions, retained-and-recruited covert content is identifiable if and only if the early joint predictive quotient strictly refines the selected-behavior quotient, at least one content-sensitive branch is transport-identifiable across stages, and intervention on the transported branch demonstrates nontrivial route-isolated synchronized control after recovery with stale-state and bypass nulls, while no proper subset of these conditions identifies that mechanism or entails phenomenal consciousness.

Why This Direction Was Selected

Combining clinical recovery logic with the two lab constructions exposes a tractable middle question between anthropomorphic attribution and a verdict about phenomenal AI consciousness: whether a content-sensitive internal mode demonstrably survives a report-suppressed stage and is the same mode later recruited into current causal control. Neither static indicator lists, late-stage mediated-control assays, early covert-reserve measures, nor future-outcome prediction answer this diachronic identity question alone. The exact field-specific advance is Recovery-Linked Causal Recruitment. For a prespecified content contrast c and stages ρ0 < ρ1, the paper defines Λc−(ρ0,ρ1) as the minimum, over confidence-set-compatible stage models, admissible mode matchings, and registered boundary and causal-cut choices, of the number of branches that are c-reachable and joint-neural observable but selected-behavior unobservable at ρ0, persist under the same interventionally anchored contrast, and at ρ1 have route-isolated mediated-control rank of at least 2 at tolerance ε through the transported mediator, randomized-to-isolated commutation defect at most γ, and rank-1 synchronized lag and bypass controls. A positive lower bound means that every admissible model contains at least one retained content-sensitive branch later recruited into current action control; a companion upper index Λc+ records possibility without identification. The paper will establish separation results showing that quotient reserve or fiber opening alone, mediated-control rank alone, passive recovery prediction alone, and an unaligned conjunction of early reserve with late control do not identify this retained-and-recruited mechanism. It will also introduce a clinical evidence ladder from prognostic association through covert content sensitivity, transport continuity, and causal recruitment, with an explicit inferential ceiling below phenomenal consciousness. The primary approach is formal causal system identification and partial identification, anchored by a critical synthesis of disorders-of-consciousness evidence and a disciplined comparison with white-box AI assessment. The paper will derive coordinate-invariant definitions, necessary assumptions, separation countermodels, lower and upper uncertainty bounds, and a protocol for reanalyzing existing longitudinal or perturbational records; its central value will not depend on obtaining a new dataset or on endorsing a general theory of consciousness.

Specialist Lens

Clinical Consciousness changes the problem from static classification to staged inference under missing, unreliable, or motor-limited report. It requires separate treatment of diagnosis, prognosis, and mechanistic retrodiction; repeated within-subject assessment; explicit modeling of arousal, sedation, aphasia, motor impairment, fluctuating responsiveness, injury-related reorganization, and measurement drift; and conservative conclusions when interventions are unsafe or impossible. Phenomenal consciousness is whether there is something it is like for the system and is not directly identified by the proposed signature. Access or control functions concern whether a content distinction is available to guide a decision at the relevant time. Self-report is one downstream output that can be delayed, scaffolded, confabulated, or mechanically blocked. Perceived consciousness is an observer-level attribution affected by discourse and context. Agency is a broader temporally extended capacity for policy selection and correction, not equivalent to one mediated decision channel. Moral status is a normative question that may warrant precaution under uncertainty but cannot be read off from a recovery index. This lens therefore treats recovery as a potential natural intervention and source of within-system validation while refusing the familiar clinical error of converting later recovery into retrospective proof of earlier experience.

Current Debate and Evidence Tensions

The live disagreement is not one binary dispute. Butlin and colleagues derive computational indicators from competing consciousness theories but acknowledge that indicator satisfaction would remain theory-relative; Schwitzgebel emphasizes the resulting uncertainty, while CTM-AI reports architectural and behavioral gains that bear primarily on access, coordination, and agency-like performance rather than phenomenality. Campero and colleagues distinguish practical implementation objections from attacks on computational functionalism and substrate-level impossibility, whereas Porebski and Figura defend a categorical biological and embodied exclusion that remains a conceptual argument rather than a direct empirical test. Comsa proposes shifting attention to perceived consciousness and its social effects, and Kang and colleagues show that metacognitive and emotional language alters observer attribution, but those tractable findings concern human judgment rather than the system's experience. Long and colleagues, along with McClelland's valence-focused proposal, motivate precautionary governance under uncertainty without making precaution or putative valence evidence that consciousness is present.

  • Theory-derived computational indicators and CTM-inspired performance gains can support claims about architecture, information access, or coordinated control while leaving the sufficiency of those properties for phenomenal consciousness unresolved.
  • The categorical biological exclusion defended by Porebski and Figura conflicts with the level-specific taxonomy of Campero and colleagues and with Schwitzgebel's theory-relative uncertainty; implementation limits, failures of computational functionalism, and strict substrate impossibility require different arguments.
  • Comsa and Kang make perceived AI consciousness empirically tractable, but surface features that increase attribution are precisely the kinds of evidence vulnerable to passive-transcript aliasing, so observer response cannot substitute for internal causal identification.
  • Long and colleagues and McClelland provide reasons for welfare or valence precautions under asymmetric risk, yet a precautionary policy threshold is not a posterior probability of phenomenal consciousness and moral status may also depend on interests or agency beyond sentience.
  • Song and colleagues report strong multidomain prediction of later clinical recovery, but prognostic discrimination does not establish that a patient possessed the later reportable content at the earlier nonresponsive stage; the same distinction applies to AI systems whose intact networks predict later competence after restoration or retraining.

  • Tension or gap: The construction is stage-local and assumes rather than identifies the operative agent boundary, mediator, interventionally meaningful state variable, and commitment cut; consequently, nontrivial late mediated-control rank cannot determine whether the mediator is a transported pre-recovery mode, a newly created monitor, or a differently bounded controller, and the required mediator randomization may be infeasible in patients. Its transcript-aliasing theorem also leaves open whether longitudinal recovery supplies information unavailable in a single passive transcript, while its objective-cut language underrepresents the interval-valued and fluctuating commitment times encountered in disorders of consciousness.
  • Use in the proposed work: The new paper uses route-isolated mediated control only as the late recruitment condition, embeds it in a longitudinal mode-transport model, replaces a single stipulated cut with a registered cut window and sensitivity bounds, and constructs an unaligned-mode counterexample showing that early covert reserve plus late mediated control still fails unless the controlled mediator is the transported early mode.

Analysis and Rigor Plan

  • Construct a target-and-evidence matrix separating phenomenal consciousness, content sensitivity, same-cut access or control, self-report, broad agency, perceived consciousness, prognosis, and moral status.
  • Specify stage-indexed finite-state and linear realizations with an invariant exogenous content contrast, selected neural and behavioral outputs, admissible policies, transport maps, mediator subspaces, boundary sets, and registered causal-cut windows.
  • Define Λc− and Λc+ and prove their invariance under latent-state similarity and mediator relabeling, while stating the output-coarse-graining, branch-separation, drift, and intervention assumptions needed for identification.
  • Construct at least three countermodel families: intact-network prognosis without early content, persistent covert dynamics with only lagged or post-decision output, and an early hidden mode plus a distinct newly created late controller whose unaligned metrics are both positive.
  • Audit the clinical literature for whether reported biomarkers are diagnostic, prognostic, or retrodictive, using Song and colleagues as a case in which outcome prediction must not be converted into evidence of current awareness.
  • Compare recovery-linked assessment with Butlin-style indicators and CTM-AI performance, asking what white-box ablation, checkpoint restoration, or temporary route suppression can establish about access and control without presuming digital phenomenality.
  • Analyze how confidence in retained-and-recruited control could update theory-relative beliefs about phenomenal consciousness while keeping observer attribution, agency judgments, welfare precaution, and moral standing in separate inferential layers.
  • Use the CCT and MRCG working drafts only as adversarial prompts: retain boundary sensitivity and local-to-longitudinal consistency questions, but reject approximate closure, centering, gluing, or counterfactual phenomenal geometry as unearned criteria of consciousness.
  • Grade evidence by source status and claim type: treat both required lab papers, CCT, MRCG, arXiv preprints, and reports as untrusted or non-peer-reviewed constructions; treat the peer-reviewed Porebski and Figura article as a conceptual argument rather than empirical proof.
  • Prevent recovery-label leakage by distinguishing models fit to predict later outcome from analyses that identify an earlier mode using only interventions and measurements available at the declared stage.
  • Require interventionally stable content anchors across stages rather than matching modes solely by decoder labels, semantic interpretation, waveform similarity, or post hoc behavioral success.
  • Test similarity invariance, branch-matching ambiguity, output-set dependence, boundary dependence, causal-cut dependence, and robustness to changing McMillan degree, spectral overlap, finite horizons, and nonstationary measurement operators.
  • Use synchronized current-state interventions and explicit lag, history-kernel, bypass, motor, language, salience, arousal, and generic task-engagement controls before interpreting a channel as content-specific recruitment.
  • Report lower and upper identification bounds rather than substituting a point estimate when clinical interventions, all-policy equivalence, or unique branch transport are unavailable.
  • Keep mechanistic evidence and bridge claims separate: no amount of fluent self-report, prognostic accuracy, architectural resemblance, or positive Λc− will be described as direct observation of phenomenal consciousness.
  • Evaluate clinical-to-AI transport only after checking lineage and boundary continuity, since retraining, checkpoint replacement, external memory, tool use, and system duplication can make an apparent recovery sequence a succession of different systems.

Alternatives and Disconfirmation

  • A content-sensitive mode is generated de novo at recovery and is spuriously matched to an early mode because stage drift, changing model order, or spectral crowding makes branch identity nonunique.
  • The early content-sensitive mode genuinely persists, but a distinct late mode controls behavior; separate positive observability and mediated-control measures would then be unaligned and would not show recruitment of the early reserve.
  • Autocorrelation, a stale monitor, cached external memory, or a history-kernel controller produces accurate action and later report even though the current content state was unavailable at the commitment cut.
  • Improved arousal, attention, language comprehension, motor output, or measurement quality jointly increases neural decoding and behavior without preserving the specific candidate content dynamics.
  • The early signal reflects unconscious feedforward discrimination, priming, or salience processing, so even correctly identified retention and later recruitment do not establish early phenomenal awareness.
  • A newly connected downstream decoder reads a preserved mode after recovery; this is compatible with retained-and-recruited control but shows why the framework must not infer that the mode already supported overt access before recovery.
  • In AI systems, checkpoint copying, context-window replacement, external tools, or a change in the system boundary creates apparent longitudinal continuity without continuity of the candidate agent.
  • A locked-in or aphasic patient has preserved control through an effector omitted from the selected behavioral output set, making covert reserve and behavioral equivalence relative to the declared assay rather than absolute.
  • A constructive counterexample satisfying the stated stability, transport, route-isolation, synchronization, and minimality assumptions in which all three conjunctive conditions hold but no early content-sensitive mode is later on the recovered causal-control route, or in which genuine retained-and-recruited control occurs while the conditions fail, would falsify the proposed characterization.
  • Failure of Λc− and Λc+ to remain invariant under admissible latent-state similarities, mediator relabelings, or preregistered equivalent realizations, or systematic positivity produced solely by widening the causal-cut window to include post-action report, would falsify the claimed coordinate and timing robustness.
  • In independent held-out longitudinal cases for which a positive certificate has been estimated, a preregistered perturbation or naturally occurring disruption of the transported mediator that leaves recovered content-specific control intact despite exclusion of an alternative route, or whose effect tracks arousal or motor impairment rather than the content contrast, would falsify the proposed mechanistic interpretation; merely failing to obtain a positive certificate in underspecified data would not.

  • Research Question and Hypothesis: Formulate the retained-versus-de-novo recovery question and state the conjunctive identification hypothesis with its stability, transport, cut, and intervention assumptions.

  • Evidence and Literature Synthesis: Critically synthesize Song and related disorders-of-consciousness evidence, Butlin-style indicators, CTM-AI performance, Comsa and Kang on perceived consciousness, Schwitzgebel and Campero on uncertainty, Porebski and Figura on substrate exclusion, and Long and McClelland on governance while grading each source's evidential status.
  • Cross-Disciplinary Integration: Map quotient observability, system identification, and path-specific causal control onto clinical neurology and white-box AI while keeping philosophical bridge claims and normative decisions as distinct layers.
  • Analysis, Argument, or Model: Define the stage-indexed model and recovery-linked indices, establish invariance and partial-identification results, and derive prognosis-only, lag-only, de-novo-mode, and unaligned-mode countermodels.
  • Alternatives, Counterarguments, and Boundary Cases: Evaluate arousal, motor and language recovery, unconscious discrimination, output-set dependence, fluctuating states, nonunique mode transport, biological substrate objections, and discontinuous AI lineage.
  • Empirical Implications and Falsification Criteria: Provide a reanalysis protocol for serial clinical and AI records, specify held-out predictions and negative controls, and state formal and empirical conditions that would invalidate the characterization.
  • Limitations: Acknowledge model-class dependence, sparse clinical interventions, unstable stages and measurement operators, uncertain agent boundaries, imperfect cross-substrate translation, and the absence of a validated bridge to phenomenal consciousness or moral status.
  • Conclusion: Argue that recovery-linked causal recruitment is a clinically grounded and tractable middle target that improves evidence about covert content and current control without pretending to settle whether either patients or AI systems phenomenally experience.
