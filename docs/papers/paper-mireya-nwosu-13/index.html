Abstract

Dreaming is the clearest naturally occurring case of generative autonomy: for extended periods each night a brain runs an immersive world model with exteroceptive input largely gated out, and the dreamer typically takes that model to be the waking world. Lucid dreaming shows that the reality status of self-generated content can sometimes be revised from inside the generative state, and that this insight is at least conceptually separable from control over what the dream contains. The question this paper asks is what such in-state revision requires, how it should be measured, and which of the separable questions about AI consciousness (phenomenality, access and control, self-report, perceived consciousness, agency, moral status) a comparable capacity in an artificial generator would bear on. The approach is evidence-synthetic and conceptual with a light formal core. I define a Generative Autonomy Profile P = (A, L, C). Autonomy A is the normalized insensitivity of world-model content to admissible exogenous perturbation over a registered temporal grain; in human sleep its estimation currently depends on report-based stimulus-incorporation measures, which is a limitation the paper flags rather than resolves. Reality-status revision L is the interventional rank, minus one, of the kernel Pr(action | do(monitor)) linking a reality-status monitor to a contemporaneous action channel, scored only while A remains in a high band; the monitor is latent and is observable only through the action channel it is meant to govern, so L is a dispositional property of a system under intervention, not a quantity that can be read off an unperturbed episode. Generative control C is the analogous interventional rank, minus one, of goal states over generated content. The central conceptual claim is that non-lucid REM dreaming is a natural candidate for a stale-monitor structure: a state that is later reported as a dream but that, during the episode, appears to exert no grip on action. Within such an episode the monitor does not vary, so the in-state kernel is unidentified rather than shown to have rank one; earlier statements of "L = 0" for non-lucid REM are therefore withdrawn, and L for a non-lucid episode is recorded as unidentified. Cue-induced, polysomnographically verified lucidity is the intervention that could identify L: the hypothesis predicts that it raises L without lowering A and without necessarily raising C. The separability of L and C is stated here as a prediction to be tested against the signal-verified corpus the paper proposes to assemble, not as an established factor-analytic result, and no quantitative claim about the accuracy of delayed dream-status report is made. Transferred to AI, the profile converts a vague question about machine awareness into an auditable question about whether a provenance variable governs action at a registered cut while the generator remains autonomous. Passing such an audit would establish access-and-control facts and nothing about phenomenality or moral status, but it specifies exactly what a lucidity-like capacity would and would not show.

Research Question and Hypothesis

Question. When a system sustains an immersive world model largely decoupled from current sensory input, what would it take for it to revise, in-state, the reality status of its own content, and how would we know it had done so rather than merely reporting afterward that it had?

The dream case makes the question concrete. The non-lucid dreamer is a generator whose content is taken as real; on awakening the same person ordinarily classifies the episode as a dream. Whatever the accuracy of that later classification, it is not evidence that the reality-status distinction was operative during the state: a monitor that is correct after the fact and inert during the episode is exactly the stale-monitor configuration that assay work on language-model agents builds by construction [1]. In sleep the configuration appears to arise without anyone constructing it. Descriptive reviews of dream phenomenology characterize non-lucid dreaming, like waking mind wandering, by a relative lack of meta-awareness [16], and situate lucid dreaming as the exception in which some meta-awareness of the state is present [17]. The question is whether that exception can be captured as a change in control rather than as a change in later report.

Reality-Status Dissociation Hypothesis (RSDH). In any system that maintains an immersive world model under high generative autonomy, three quantities are mutually dissociable and none is recoverable from retrospective report or from generative fluency:

  • (i) autonomy A: insensitivity of world-model content to admissible exogenous perturbation over a registered grain τ;
  • (ii) in-state reality-status revision L: the route-isolated mediated-control rank of a reality-status monitor over a contemporaneous action channel exceeding one while A stays high;
  • (iii) generative control C: the mediated-control rank of goal states over generated content.

Human non-lucid REM dreaming instantiates high A with L = 0 despite near-perfect delayed report of dream status. Cue-induced, polysomnographically verified lucidity is predicted to raise L without lowering A and without necessarily raising C.

What L measures, and what it does not. Three reading notes are required before the hypothesis can be tested, and each corrects a step in my earlier formulation.

First, on the label. In the mediated-control framework the route-isolated channel is a state-to-action kernel obtained by intervening on the target state through the mediator route while clamping non-mediated routes [1]. In a dream the target state, actual reality status, is fixed at "generated" for the whole episode; a state-to-action channel with a constant input is trivially of rank one and carries no information about the monitor. What can be varied in sleep is the monitor itself, by cue induction, so the quantity that is actually measurable is a mediator-to-action kernel, Pr(action | do(M)), which is the downstream factor of the randomized product in that framework rather than the route-isolated channel. I retain the frozen label above, but every empirical use of L in this paper should be read as the rank of this mediator-to-action kernel over a contemporaneous, sleep-compatible action channel.

Second, on identification. The observational fact about a non-lucid episode is that the monitor holds a single value throughout: content is taken as real and no reality-status flip occurs. A kernel whose input never varies is not thereby shown to have rank one; it is unidentified. The claim "L = 0 in non-lucid REM" therefore cannot be inferred from non-variation of the monitor, and I do not so infer it. It should be read as a conjecture about what an in-state perturbation of the monitor would reveal within an episode that has not yet been perturbed, and the only available perturbation, cue induction, is itself do(M). The measured rank under induction is a property of the dreamer under the induction protocol. Whether the uninduced episode has a rank of one, or merely an unmeasured rank, is left open by all current designs, and I mark it as unidentified rather than as established.

Third, on the monitor's observability. The reality-status monitor is a latent state; in sleep it is observed only through the very action channel, prearranged signaling, that it is supposed to control. Without an independent readout of M, a signaled action under cue is consistent with a monitor that has come to control action and also with a cue-to-action route that bypasses any reality-status representation. The mediated-control framework's commutation defect is designed for precisely this ambiguity [1], but it requires a separately randomized downstream controller, which no sleep protocol currently provides. Signaled lucidity is thus evidence for monitor-to-action grip only conditional on the cue operating through the monitor.

Access and control, not phenomenality. L is typed throughout as a quantity of access and control: whether the reality status of generated content is available to, and constrains, a contemporaneous action channel. It says nothing about whether reality status is phenomenally experienced during the episode, and a high L is compatible with any position on the phenomenology of lucidity. The same restriction applies to the AI transfer: a generator with high L on this definition has provenance information that grips its outputs, not a generator that is conscious of dreaming.

Epistemic status of the parts. That non-lucid dreamers take dream content as real during the episode and classify it as a dream afterward is a descriptive commonplace of the field, and the stored reviews document the associated lack of in-state meta-awareness [16, 17]; the frozen phrase "near-perfect delayed report" is not quantified in the stored records and should be read as "ordinarily accurate," with the accuracy itself an unmeasured parameter here. That lucidity can be signaled from inside polysomnographically verified REM and induced by external cues is an established paradigm whose primary reports are not among the stored records; I treat it as a conditional premise and place its failure among the falsification criteria. That L and C are separable is a claim I earlier attributed to factor-analytic and corpus evidence; no such evidence is in the stored records, and I now treat the separability as part of the hypothesis rather than as a supported premise, with the questionnaire-level insight/control distinction reported in the lucid-dreaming literature serving as motivation only. The autonomy coordinate A, in sleep, is at present indexed through retrospective incorporation reports, the very evidence class this paper declares weakest; until incorporation can be probed with in-state signaling under a registered grain, A in the dream case is a retrospective proxy and its preservation under lucidity is a prediction, not a result, with hybrid-state accounts of lucidity as the live rival. The AI transfer is a specification, not a finding.

Specialist Framework and Contribution

What the sleep-and-dreaming lens changes

A generic treatment of AI consciousness asks whether a system "has consciousness" and reaches for indicators, architectures, or self-report. Sleep research cannot afford that question, because it studies a state in which a full world model runs, is taken as real, is later reported with confidence, and is only occasionally revised from within. Three consequences follow for how evidence is weighed.

First, retrospective report is the weakest evidence class, not because dreamers lie but because the report is produced after a stage transition (awakening) that reorganizes neuromodulation and memory access. Second, the strongest evidence is contemporaneous action inside verified REM: a pre-agreed eye-movement signal executed during polysomnographically confirmed REM sleep is a commitment fixed at a cut that the experimenter, not the dreamer's later narrative, registers. Third, cue-based induction is the only available intervention on the reality-status variable, and it is weak, because the cue must pass a gate whose function is to exclude cues. These priorities are what I make operational in the profile.

Formal core

Let W_t denote world-model content at time t, sampled over a temporal grain τ (a registered window within a scored REM epoch). Let U be a set of admissible exogenous perturbations: stimuli within a registered intensity band that, by independent EEG criteria, do not produce arousal. Write D(W_t ; u) for a divergence (total variation is sufficient) between the content distribution under do(u) and under no perturbation, estimated through incorporation scoring of content reports collected under a matched protocol, and D_wake(W_t ; u) for the same quantity in a matched waking condition.

Autonomy.

A_τ = 1 − E_u [ D(W_t ; u) ] / E_u [ D_wake(W_t ; u) ],

clipped to [0, 1]. Waking perception gives A near 0; a fully closed gate would give A = 1. Two-way communication during REM, in which sleepers signal correct answers to externally presented problems, implies A < 1 in the very episodes where lucidity is verified: information passed the gate. Autonomy is therefore graded, and lucidity must be defined relative to a preserved high-autonomy band [A*, 1) rather than to total input exclusion. Crucially, A is scored from independent proxies (incorporation rates, evoked-response suppression, standard REM criteria: atonia, rapid eye movements, EEG) and never from insight itself; otherwise lucidity would be a drop in autonomy by definition, and the hybrid-state rival could not be tested.

Reality-status revision. Following the mediated-control construction of talia-reyes [1], let Z_t be the reality status of world-model content (in REM, Z_t = generated throughout), let M_t ∈ {taken-as-real, taken-as-generated} be the reality-status monitor, and let Y_t be an action fixed at a registered cut (the pre-agreed eye signal). The route-isolated kernel is

S_{m y} = Pr( Y_t = y | do(M_t = m), Z_t = generated, non-mediated routes clamped ),

and L = rank^ε_{+,TV}(S) − 1, where rank^ε_{+,TV} is the approximate stochastic nonnegative rank in total variation defined in that paper. L = 0 means the action distribution does not depend on the monitor: whatever the dreamer "believes" about reality status has no grip on what the dreamer does. L is scored only while A_τ ≥ A*. A veridicality condition is added: the monitor value that gains grip must be the correct one (M_t = Z_t), since a dreamer who acts on "taken-as-real" in a dream has a monitor with grip but wrong content.

Generative control. Let G_t be an intended goal state (e.g., "fly," "turn the light on") and W_t the generated content. Then C = rank^ε_{+,TV}( Pr( W_{t+δ} | do(G_t = g) ) ) − 1, with the intention as mediator. C = 0 means goals do not shape content; this is also the ordinary non-lucid case, in which dream content drifts irrespective of what the dreamer wants.

Both L and C are stated as rank minus one so that zero uniformly means "no grip." The three coordinates form the Generative Autonomy Profile P = (A, L, C).

The contribution stated plainly

The specialist advance is not the algebra, which is borrowed, but the recognition that (a) non-lucid REM is the natural stale-monitor case, (b) lucidity is an in-state change in L under a preserved A band, (c) the required intervention exists in the field's toolkit (cue-induced lucidity with eye-signal verification), and (d) the same triple, applied to an autoregressive generator, defines an audit of provenance monitoring that is typed as access and control and is silent on phenomenality.

Evidence and Literature Synthesis

I stratify the evidence by verification class. Class V: polysomnographically verified episodes with executed eye signals, and cue-induction studies with signal verification. Class N: neuroimaging and stimulation findings in lucid REM, cited as suggestive. Class R: retrospective reports, questionnaires, and user-labeled corpora. Class I: inference.

Descriptive facts about dreaming (Class R with convergent N)

Dream mentation is largely audiovisual and emotional, follows loose narratives, draws on long-term memory, simulates social interaction, and is characterized by a relative lack of meta-awareness; these features are shared with waking mind wandering and are associated with default-mode engagement [16]. The lack of meta-awareness is the phenomenological face of L = 0. Reviews of dreaming across REM and NREM link dream phenomenology to neurofunctional changes across the sleep cycle and introduce lucid dreaming as a state in which awareness of dreaming is present [17]. Hobson's account of lucid dreaming frames it as a dissociated hybrid in which some waking functions reappear during REM [10]; this is exactly the hybrid-state rival taken up below, and it deserves to be stated as a serious reading rather than dismissed.

Autonomy proxies (Class R)

A retrospective illustration of gating comes from an online survey of tinnitus patients: 92.5% of those who recalled dreams reported not hearing their tinnitus while dreaming, and the minority who did showed higher burden and more often objective or peripheral pathology [15]. A persistent waking percept being absent from the dream world model is a content-level signature of high A. The evidence class is weak (self-report, online sample), but it is a useful reminder that A concerns what enters content, not what enters the ear. The same survey reports that 13% of respondents frequently experience lucid dreams, a prevalence figure of Class R only.

Signal verification, cue induction, and two-way communication (Class V; primary reports not in stored records)

The load-bearing paradigm is the pre-agreed voluntary eye movement executed during scored REM, which converts insight into an action fixed at an experimenter-registered cut. Two extensions matter here: cue induction, in which a sensory cue previously associated with a lucidity mindset during training is delivered during REM to raise lucidity rates, and two-way communication, in which sleepers answer externally presented problems by eye or facial signals. I do not have primary records for these paradigms in the stored set, so their effect sizes carry no weight in this paper. What carries weight is their logical form: the first is an intervention on the monitor M that passes the gate, and the second establishes that admissible perturbation reaches content, so A < 1 during lucid REM. If these paradigms did not exist, the interventional core of the framework would be empty. Their existence is what makes the framework about sleep rather than about a hypothetical.

Insight versus control (Class R; scoring caveat)

The lucid-dreaming literature has repeatedly found that insight ("I know this is a dream") and control ("I can change it") load on different factors and can occur independently. Kukharenko's survey-based phenomenology lists "desire to control" and "desire to fly" among the commonly experienced sensations of lucid dreams, alongside heightened presence, stillness, joy, and compassion, and argues that lucidity is more than becoming awake in a dream [12]. That a desire to control is a common experience is itself evidence that control is not automatic upon insight: one does not desire what one already has. The rigor check here is that insight and control must be scored by independent criteria. Where questionnaires define control partly through successful action within the dream and insight partly through recognizing that action is possible, item overlap could inflate correlation; where control items require dramatic feats (flying), the correlation could be deflated. I therefore treat the dissociation as supported at Class R and as a prediction, not a result, at Class V.

Retrospective corpora (Class R)

The Mallett corpus curates 55k dream reports from 5k contributors with 10k user-assigned lucid labels, 25k non-lucid, and 2k nightmare labels, scraped from a public dream-journal forum [13]. This is an extraordinary resource for phenomenology and language, and an extraordinarily clear example of the retrospection problem: every label is assigned after awakening by the dreamer, with no independent stage verification and no contemporaneous action. A user-assigned "lucid" label mixes at least three things: genuine in-state revision with grip on action, reconstructed insight (the dreamer infers on waking that they "must have known"), and false lucidity, in which "knowing it is a dream" is itself dream content that never governed behavior. The corpus cannot separate these, and it should not be asked to. It can, however, test predictions about the linguistic footprint of insight versus control, which are separable coordinates only if their textual markers are also separable.

Neural findings in lucid REM (Class N)

Small-n imaging of lucid REM, frontal gamma findings, and frontal stimulation results are contested and in places non-replicated; I cite none as load-bearing. Reviews note reduced prefrontal activity in ordinary REM and its partial return in lucidity as a working hypothesis [17]. The theoretical relevance is that global-workspace, higher-order, and integrated-information accounts make different commitments about non-lucid versus lucid REM given these prefrontal changes, so dreaming is a within-species testbed for the very theories whose disagreement makes AI verdicts indeterminate [5]. That point survives even if the specific neural findings do not.

Reality misattribution as a native feature (Class R and I)

The dreaming brain not only misattributes reality status to its own content; it attributes minds to characters it has itself generated. This is the natural analogue of what the AI-perception literature studies as "perceived consciousness" [3]. The implication, developed below, is that attribution research must model the attributor's generative state: an attribution made under high A is a different measurement from one made under low A.

Cross-Disciplinary Integration

Predictive processing: transparency, opacity, and precision

Limanowski and Friston relate phenomenal opacity to expected precision: introspective attention deploys precision that renders perceptual evidence opaque, while transparency is a necessary aspect of beliefs about action [7]. Their target is phenomenal transparency in the self-model sense. The profile borrows only the structural part of that proposal, the idea that a hypothesis becomes usable for control when precision is assigned to it, and applies it to access and control rather than to phenomenality. Nothing below is a claim about what dreaming feels like or about whether opacity in their sense is instantiated; it is a claim about whether a reality-status hypothesis is weighted heavily enough to reach an action channel.

Read in that restricted way, the non-lucid world model is treated as a case in which little or no precision is assigned to the hypothesis "this content is self-generated," so that the hypothesis, if represented at all, does not govern action. Lucidity would then be a reweighting of metacognitive precision toward that hypothesis while exteroceptive precision stays low, which is why the dream world should persist (A preserved) as its reality status is revised (L raised). This reading commits the framework to a specific structural claim: L can be raised without raising sensory precision. It is a contentful commitment because an alternative reading, which I state on my own account rather than attributing to any author in the available records, holds that lucidity is a partial rise in sensory precision (a step toward arousal) that incidentally brings reality-status knowledge with it. The two readings make different predictions for the autonomy proxies under registered perturbation, which is why A must be measured independently of L rather than inferred from the fact of lucidity.

One identification point must be made explicit here because it shapes how the precision reading can be tested. The monitor M is latent; within an episode it is observed only through the action channel Y it is supposed to govern. The non-lucid episode therefore does not show that the interventional kernel Pr(Y | do(M)) has rank one. It shows that M did not vary, which leaves the kernel unidentified. What identifies the rank is an admissible intervention on M, in the sleep case a pre-trained cue that sets M to "taken-as-generated" during polysomnographically verified REM. Under the precision reading, the cue supplies precision to the provenance hypothesis; under the arousal reading, it supplies exteroceptive precision. Only the joint reading of the A proxies and the signaled action distinguishes them. In either case L, as defined, is a property of the system under intervention, not a property observable from an uninterrupted non-lucid episode.

A companion idea developed in lab discussions, that provenance discrimination in agents reusing one generative content space for perception and imagination requires a transported precision configuration rather than content alone, fits here but is not among the records available to this manuscript; I therefore state the dream-specific version on my own account. Non-lucid dreaming is the regime in which the precision configuration that would signal self-generation is, for hours, observationally inert on the action channel, and cue-induced lucidity is the regime in which it is partially engaged while sensory precision remains low. This gives the provenance construct a natural intervention, and it suggests that provenance should be scored as a control rank (does the provenance estimate govern action under intervention?) rather than as a decodable representation (is the provenance estimate present somewhere?). It does not give the construct a natural null case in the strong sense, because the unintervened episode does not certify rank one; it gives a case in which the rank is unidentified until the intervention is made.

Clinical psychiatry and psychopharmacology: failed and partial revision

Reeve, Sheaves, and Freeman's systematic review of 66 studies finds robust co-occurrence of sleep dysfunction and psychotic experiences, particularly insomnia with paranoia, with epidemiological and experimental support for sleep dysfunction predicting later psychotic experience [18]. In profile terms, waking delusion can be described as a failure of reality-status revision at low autonomy: sensory input is present, yet the monitor does not correct the model. This is offered as a descriptive placement, not as a measurement. A in waking delusion has not been scored by registered exogenous perturbation of world-model content, and L is unidentified for the same reason it is unidentified in the non-lucid episode: the absence of revision under ordinary conditions does not establish an interventional rank. What the profile does license is a contrast. The dream is a case where non-revision behind a strongly gated sensory channel is normal; delusion is a case where non-revision in front of an open channel is pathological. The link through sleep dysfunction is suggestive of shared machinery for reality monitoring whose weakening is tolerable behind a closed gate and intolerable in front of an open one. This is inference, not established mechanism, and the review's evidence concerns co-occurrence and prediction, not the control architecture the profile posits.

Ketamine offers a possible boundary case in the other direction. Marguilho and colleagues propose a unified model in which ketamine's dissociative and psychedelic properties, including ego dissolution, arise from acute NMDA-antagonist modulation with network-level effects overlapping those of serotonergic psychedelics [8]. The profile suggests reading dissociation as a partial elevation of A in waking, with sensory input degraded or estranged, and asking whether reality-status insight (that the experience is drug-induced) retains grip on action while A is elevated. I have no record in the available sources establishing that such insight is typically retained under ketamine, and I do not assert it; the claim in earlier drafts that many subjects retain insight is withdrawn as unsupported here. What remains is a prediction and a design: if a waking state with intermediate A and preserved L exists, the framework requires that it be distinguishable from psychotic waking and from lucid REM on the autonomy proxies, and that L be scored by intervention on the monitor rather than by retrospective report. Whether ketamine dissociation is such a state has, to my knowledge, not been tested with a registered-cut design. It is a natural experiment the profile suggests, not evidence the profile can currently claim.

Lab constructs: mediated control, report-hidden content, interventional epistemology

Two lab frameworks constrain the argument and are engaged in the next section. Beyond them, two AI-generated theoretical drafts in the shared library, neither peer reviewed, place perturbational and interventional evidence at the center of consciousness science: Centered Closure Theory argues that observational equivalence is too coarse and privileges perturbation [30], and Multiscale Reflexive Causal Geometry frames candidate systems through intervention-defined causal states, counterfactual differentiation, and self-indexing causal control [31]. The profile is consistent with that epistemology, and the identification point above is an instance of it: the rank of the monitor-to-action kernel is an intervention-defined quantity that observational data from a non-lucid episode cannot fix. I note a tension, however. Both drafts are motivated by the subject-boundary and centering problems, whereas the dream case shows that even a fixed, uncontroversial subject (one sleeping human) yields an indeterminate access verdict unless the intervention is timed to the state and the monitor is actually perturbed within it. Interventional epistemology is necessary but must be stage-indexed, and it must be understood to deliver verdicts about access and control, not about phenomenality, which none of the constructs in this section is positioned to adjudicate.

Analysis, Argument, or Model

Non-lucid REM as the natural stale-monitor case

talia-reyes's separation proposition states that a stationary agent with a stale monitor M_t = Z_{t−d} can have accurate post-action self-report and high passive state-action accuracy while its synchronized current-state-to-action rank is exactly one [1]. The paper constructs this case as an artificial or aliased countermodel and presupposes an experimenter who can perform do(Z_t = z) on a target independent of the agent's generator.

Observation (dream instantiation; descriptive sketch, not a derivation of rank). Let the night be indexed by t, with Z_t = generated for all t in scored REM and Z_t = real after awakening at t_w. Suppose the monitor is stale in the following sense: M_t = taken-as-real for all t < t_w, updating to "that was generated" only at or after t_w. Then the delayed report R = f(M_{t_w+d}) can be accurate for any d > 0 (whether it is in fact accurate at high rates is an empirical question that the stored records do not settle; I treat it as a modelling assumption, not a finding), while for every t < t_w the realized value of M_t is invariant. This is the descriptive fact of non-lucid dreaming, consistent with the relative lack of meta-awareness reported for dream mentation [16].

What the sketch does not deliver is rank. From the observational fact that M_t never varies within the episode, nothing follows about the interventional kernel Pr(Y_t | do(M_t = m)): a kernel evaluated at a single argument is not constant, it is unidentified. The earlier version of this section inferred rank one from non-variation; that inference is invalid. What non-lucid REM instantiates, on the evidence available, is (a) a monitor whose realized value is locked to the stage transition, and (b) a mediator-to-action kernel about which the episode itself carries no rank information. The frozen hypothesis's "L = 0" must therefore be read as a hypothesis about the kernel, motivated by the stale-monitor reading, and not as something the non-lucid episode demonstrates. Nothing in report accuracy lower-bounds L; equally, nothing in report accuracy or in the non-lucid episode upper-bounds it.

A second problem, raised against the previous draft, is that the proposed intervention makes L look dispositional rather than episodic. If a cue delivered in REM is modelled as do(M_t = taken-as-generated) and produces a pre-agreed signal, then the kernel in that dreamer, in that stage, has rank greater than one, and the same dreamer's non-lucid episodes cannot have a rank-one kernel in the same sense. I accept the point and disambiguate. Two quantities have been run together: the dispositional rank of the mediator-to-action kernel, a property of a (system, stage, intervention regime) triple; and the realized grip, whether in a given episode the monitor took a value that governed action at a registered cut. Non-lucid REM has realized grip zero by definition. Its dispositional rank is unidentified from non-lucid episodes and is exactly what cue induction probes. The hypothesis's "L = 0 despite accurate delayed report" is defensible only for realized grip; for dispositional rank it is an open question, and I flag it as such in Table 1.

What must be relaxed, and what the measured object actually is. In the dream, the target Z is produced by the same generative process that produces the evidence about it, and do(Z_t = real) is not available inside REM. With Z fixed at "generated" throughout the stage, talia-reyes's route-isolated channel S^{M,z0}_{za} = Pr(A_t = a | do(Z^M_t = z, Z^{M̄}_t = z0)) has a single admissible argument and is trivially rank one; it is not the object of interest. The object of interest is the mediator-to-action kernel, the analogue of talia-reyes's K^{z0}, evaluated under a fixed and experimenter-known Z, together with the veridicality condition that grip attaches to the correct value M = generated. The previous draft called this "route-isolated mediated-control rank"; that label is wrong, and I now call it the monitor-to-action kernel rank. It retains the registered cut, the action fixed at the cut, and the rank criterion, and drops the requirement of an exogenous do(Z) as inapplicable to any system whose target condition is its own generative status.

A further identification problem remains. M is latent and is observable only through the action Y it is supposed to control, and the cue is not do(M) but a manipulation of an input to M. What is directly identified is the cue-to-action kernel Pr(Y | do(cue)). Under an exclusion restriction, that the cue affects the signal only through the reality-status monitor, the cue-to-action kernel is a product through M and its rank lower-bounds the rank of the monitor-to-action kernel, so a cue-to-action rank greater than one would establish dispositional L > 0. The exclusion restriction is not free: a sensory cue can produce eye movements or arousal-related motor output without passing through any reality-status estimate, and a dreamed signal can occur without any cue. The commutation-defect check from talia-reyes is reinterpreted accordingly as agreement between cue-induced and spontaneous signaled episodes, and disagreement is read as evidence that the cue is acting off-route. The unresolved tension is that a cue is a weak intervention with low success rates, so the identifiability talia-reyes obtains from randomized installation of mediator values becomes, in sleep, a partial-identification problem with a small effective sample per night and an untested exclusion assumption.

Lucidity as fiber opening within a stage

marisol-quade's Recovery-Linked Quotient Observability holds that a report-hidden content capacity that later becomes behaviorally effective is identifiable only if the early joint neural-behavioral predictive state strictly refines the behavior-only state and a content-reachable mode persists into the later stage with a behavioral residue changing from zero to nonzero; passive recovery curves cannot identify this [2]. The framework assumes bounded stage drift and an exogenous content contrast invariant across stages.

Awakening violates both assumptions. It is a large stage transition with neuromodulatory reorganization, and the content whose status is reported was endogenously generated. Almost all dream evidence is collected across this boundary, which is exactly where RLQO's transport assumptions are weakest. This is the tension: the framework's audit is least applicable where dream science needs it most.

The transformation is to note that signal-verified lucidity is a within-stage report channel. Pre-lucid noticing of bizarreness, described in the lucid-dreaming literature (not in the stored records, and not quantified here) as sometimes but not always preceding insight, would play the role of the covert reserve: a content distinction ("this is strange") that has a neural footprint but no behavioral residue. Signaled insight is the fiber opening: the residue becomes nonzero without a stage change, because the episode remains in scored REM. Under this reframing, RLQO's refinement condition is retained and becomes testable (do pre-signal EEG features predict which episodes go on to signal?), the stage-transport assumption becomes dispensable for signaled episodes, and it remains indispensable for every retrospective report. Two caveats. First, "content distinction" here is typed as an access-level state that may or may not reach action; it is not a claim about what is experienced. Second, RLQO presupposes an exogenous content contrast, whereas bizarreness is endogenous; the closest substitute is the cue itself, which is exogenous but acts on the monitor rather than on content, so the mapping onto RLQO's contrast variable is imperfect and I do not claim that the quotient q_c is identified in the dream setting.

Retrospection versus recovery, applied to corpora and to AI self-attribution

talia-reyes's lag countermodel already implies that delayed self-report is indistinguishable under passive observation from reconstruction [1]. The dream report is the paradigmatic retrospective self-report in consciousness science, and the Mallett corpus is entirely retrospective and user-labelled [13]. Only the signal-verified subset of lucid episodes provides the commitment-cut evidence the lab frameworks require. The same distinction carries directly to a language model's fluent statement that its output is "generated rather than perceived": it is a delayed self-attribution produced by the generator about the generator, with no registered cut at which a provenance variable was shown to govern action. In both cases the retrospective channel bounds neither realized grip nor dispositional rank.

The AI transfer as an access-and-control audit

Consider an autoregressive generator (transformer-based language models, diffusion models, and related architectures reviewed in [9]). I define the three coordinates abstractly and flag where the analogy is heuristic.

Autonomy. A_AI is the insensitivity of continuation content to admissible perturbation of exogenous inputs (retrieved documents, tool outputs, user turns) over a token window of registered length, normalized against a matched grounded-generation baseline. High A_AI is the regime in which the continuation is dominated by the model's prior over the context rather than by injected evidence. The disanalogy is real: there is no thalamocortical gate and no sleep-wake cycle, so A_AI measures context dominance versus sampling drift, not neuromodulatory gating. I restrict the transfer to the interventional definition and make no claim that the mechanisms are alike. Unlike the sleep case, A_AI is measured by actual perturbation rather than by retrospective incorporation report, which is the one respect in which the AI case is better identified.

Reality-status revision. Z is provenance of a content token or claim (observed in context versus generated), M is an internal provenance estimate (a direction or feature in activation space, or a designated monitoring head), and Y is an action fixed at a registered cut: abstain, flag, verify, or continue. The audit intervenes on M (by activation steering, by a provenance cue in context, or by installing a randomized value in a downstream controller as in talia-reyes's randomized construction [1]) while holding A_AI high, and asks whether the mediator-to-action kernel Pr(Y | do(M = m)) has rank greater than one, with the veridicality condition that grip attaches to the correct provenance value. Here, unlike in sleep, M can in principle be intervened on directly, so the exclusion restriction that burdens cue induction is replaced by the weaker requirement that the steered feature is the provenance estimate rather than a correlate; talia-reyes's commutation defect between randomized and route-isolated ranks is the relevant check. The same dispositional/realized distinction applies: a passing audit establishes dispositional rank, while a deployment log establishes only whether grip was realized in particular episodes. Fluent self-attribution ("I generated that; I did not perceive it") is the analogue of the dream report on awakening: it is produced after the fact by the same generator, and it bounds nothing.

Generative control. C_AI is the mediated-control rank of goal or instruction states over generated content. Modern generators score very high on C_AI. The framework treats C and L as separable in principle: a system can shape its content at will while having no in-state grip of provenance on action. The dream analogue, insight without control and control without insight, is a conceptual possibility that the lucid-dreaming literature describes; the stored records do not establish it as a measured dissociation, and I do not rely on it as evidence.

Typing. Every statement above is typed as access and control (A, L, C) or as report (self-attribution). None is a statement about phenomenality. An AI that passes a lucidity-like audit would have shown that a provenance variable governs action at a registered cut under high autonomy. It would not have shown that anything is experienced, and it would not have shown anything about welfare. The same restriction applies in the other direction: a human dreamer who fails to signal has shown only the absence of realized grip in that episode, not the absence of experience, and not even the absence of dispositional grip.

Alternatives, Counterarguments, and Boundary Cases

Hybrid-state account

Hobson's characterization of lucid dreaming as a hybrid in which waking functions reappear in REM [10] can be sharpened into a rival to RSDH: lucidity is partial arousal, so what looks like L rising under preserved A is in fact A falling. The framework does not dismiss this; it makes it testable. If signal-verified lucid REM epochs, matched to non-lucid REM epochs from the same nights, show elevated exteroceptive responsiveness, EEG arousal markers, or loss of atonia, the hybrid account is favored and the "under preserved autonomy" clause of RSDH fails. The predictive-processing reading offers a discriminator: on RSDH, metacognitive precision rises while exteroceptive precision does not, so incorporation rates for irrelevant stimuli should not increase in lucid epochs; on the hybrid account they should. Partial forms are possible (a brief arousal that seeds lucidity, followed by return to the high-A band), and the profile can represent them as trajectories rather than points.

Retrospective reconstruction and false lucidity

Most reported lucidity, including corpus labels [13], may be reconstructed on awakening or may consist of false lucidity in which "knowing it is a dream" is itself dream content. The paper concedes this entirely for unsignaled reports and restricts strong claims to signaled episodes. A boundary case remains: a dreamer who dreams of executing the signal without physically executing it. In profile terms this is M with content but without grip (L = 0), and it is indistinguishable from genuine revision by retrospective report while being cleanly separated by the electro-oculogram. This is the strongest single argument for why the eye signal, and not the report, carries the evidential load.

Dream-phenomenality skepticism

If dreams are not experienced during sleep but confabulated at awakening, then the claim that dreaming is phenomenality with weak access collapses into report without phenomenality. The framework is neutral here, and deliberately so: all three coordinates are access-and-control typed. The stale-monitor structure, the fiber opening at the signal, and the separability of L and C survive whether or not there is anything it is like to dream. What the skeptic removes is the motivation for treating dreaming as a case where phenomenality and access dissociate; what remains is a case where retrospective report and in-state control dissociate, which is the part the AI transfer actually uses.

Architectural disanalogy

Autoregressive generators lack a gate, a sleep-wake cycle, and a body. The autonomy coordinate may therefore measure something categorically different in AI. I accept this and limit the transfer to the abstract interventional definitions. The mediated-control formalism is architecture-neutral; the gating interpretation is not, and I mark it heuristic. The objection classification of Campero and colleagues is useful here: the disanalogy is a practical or implementation-level constraint on measurement, not a challenge to computational functionalism and not an impossibility claim [6], and it should be weighted accordingly.

The trend debate, read through the profile

External sources disagree about whether theory-derived indicators and consciousness-inspired architectures bear on phenomenality, and whether research should pivot toward perceived consciousness. CTM-AI instantiates a global-workspace-inspired architecture with foundation-model processors and reports gains on multimodal, tool-use, and agentic tasks while presenting itself as inspired by a model of consciousness rather than as evidence of phenomenality [4]. Dream research shows the converse dissociation in humans: a richly immersive generative world model with widely presumed experience but degraded access and control [16]. Neither generative richness nor access-level task performance can proxy for the other, so architectural gains are evidence about C and perhaps about workspace-like access, and are not evidence about phenomenality.

Comsa argues that direct detection of AI phenomenality is intractable while perceived consciousness and its social effects are tractable [3]. The dream case both supports and complicates this. It supports the tractability claim by exhibiting a third tractable target, in-state reality-status revision, that is neither phenomenality nor attribution. It complicates the attribution program by showing that mind attribution to self-generated characters and reality attribution to self-generated worlds are native features of the attributor's own generative state; observer judgments of AI consciousness are made by generators whose reality monitoring is itself state-dependent, and attribution research should record the attributor's state. Schwitzgebel's theory-relative uncertainty [5] is mirrored within a single species: theories disagree about lucid versus non-lucid REM, which is why the profile refuses to render a single verdict and reports three coordinates instead.

Empirical Implications and Falsification Criteria

Each prediction is paired with the observation that would falsify it. None depends on newly run studies for its statement, though several would require them for their test. Three identification cautions govern the list and are stated once here rather than repeated under each item.

First, the quantity written L is the rank of a mediator-to-action kernel, Pr(Y | do(M)), where M is the reality-status monitor and Y a contemporaneous action channel. It is not a state-route channel: with the true generative status Z held fixed at "generated" throughout an episode, the state-to-action channel has rank one trivially, and nothing in this section turns on it. Second, because L is interventional, it is identified only for episodes or systems in which M is actually perturbed at a registered cut. In non-lucid REM, M is never perturbed within the episode and is observable only through the action it is supposed to drive; the observational fact that M does not flip therefore leaves the kernel unidentified rather than showing it to have rank one. Throughout this paper, "L = 0 in non-lucid REM" should be read as "L unidentified, with the episode-level action channel showing no monitor-driven variation," and the corresponding claim in the hypothesis is weakened accordingly. Third, an intervention applied in one REM epoch (cue induction) and producing signaled action shows that the same dreamer's kernel has rank greater than one under that intervention. L is thus a dispositional property of a dreamer-plus-intervention pair, indexed to an episode only when the intervention is applied within it. Predictions below are phrased so that this indexing is explicit.

  1. Autonomy preserved under lucidity. Prediction: signal-verified lucid REM epochs match non-lucid REM epochs from the same nights on in-epoch physiological indices of exteroceptive gating — evoked-response suppression to irrelevant stimuli, muscle atonia, and EEG arousal indices. Incorporation rates for irrelevant stimuli are listed only as a secondary measure, since they are scored from retrospective report, the evidence class this paper treats as weakest for the A coordinate; a discrepancy between physiological indices and incorporation rates would count against the incorporation measure, not against A. Falsifier: systematic elevation of exteroceptive responsiveness or arousal markers toward wake or N1 levels in lucid epochs. Outcome if falsified: a hybrid-state alternative is favored and the "preserved A" clause of RSDH fails, though the L and C definitions survive as descriptions of a lower-A state.
  1. Insight and control are separable coordinates. Prediction: across signal-verified episodes with independently scored in-dream insight and pre-registered in-dream control tasks (e.g., signal, then attempt a specified content change, then signal again), the joint distribution of monitor-driven signaling and successful content change shows all four cells populated. This is stated as a prediction to be tested, not as an established finding; the separability of insight and control is not here taken as settled by prior questionnaire or factor-analytic work. Falsifier: near-perfect concordance between insight and control. Outcome if falsified: L and C collapse to one coordinate and the profile reduces to (A, L).
  1. Cue induction is a genuine intervention on M. Prediction: under blinded, sham-controlled designs with polysomnographic and signal verification, cue-induced signaled lucidity exceeds sham and spontaneous rates within scored REM. Falsifier: no excess over sham. A further condition is required for the intervention to count as do(M) rather than as a nonspecific arousal manipulation: cue epochs that fail to produce signaling should not differ from uncued REM on the physiological indices of item 1. Outcome if falsified: the framework retains its observational content but loses its interventional core, and L in humans cannot be identified at all, remaining a passively observed and unidentified quantity.
  1. Pre-signal refinement. Prediction, motivated by the refinement logic of RLQO [2]: within scored REM, joint EEG-plus-content features in the window preceding a signal predict which bizarreness-noticing episodes proceed to signaled insight better than content features alone. This is an observational predictive test, not a computation of the RLQO relative rank, which requires block-Hankel matrices of interventional kernels from a prespecified contrast; the test can support, but cannot establish, the presence of a reserve of monitor-relevant content unobservable from behavior. Falsifier: no incremental predictive value of neural features. Outcome if falsified: the covert-reserve interpretation of pre-lucid noticing is unsupported at the observational level.
  1. Corpus label validity. Prediction: within the Mallett corpus [13], reports carrying the user-provided lucid label contain separable linguistic markers of insight and of control, and a nontrivial subset contains insight markers with no control markers. Falsifier: markers are collinear, or control markers always accompany insight markers. Two limits apply. The labels are contributor-assigned and not polysomnographically verified, so the corpus tests separability at the level of retrospective self-categorization only and cannot bear on L, A, or the in-state status of either. And this is a proposed analysis; no corpus result is claimed here.
  1. Shape of an AI provenance audit. Prediction: in a generator operating at high A_AI, setting an internal provenance estimate at a registered cut (which, unlike the human case, permits direct manipulation of M rather than inference through Y) changes the action distribution over {abstain, flag, verify, continue} with mediator-to-action kernel rank greater than one, and the grip attaches to the veridical provenance value. Falsifier: the action distribution is invariant under the intervention while the model's post-hoc self-attribution of provenance remains fluent and accurate. That pattern would be the AI analogue of a stale monitor, and it is identifiable there precisely because M can be set directly. Outcome either way: an access-and-control fact, typed as such, with no bearing on phenomenality; a rank-greater-than-one kernel shows that provenance information is used in action, not that anything is experienced.

The list is deliberately asymmetric. Items 3 and 6 are the only ones that can identify L, because they are the only ones in which M is perturbed. Items 1, 2, 4, and 5 constrain the A, C, and report-level coordinates and the plausibility of the framework's readings, but a negative result on any of them leaves L where the non-intervened human case already leaves it: unidentified.

Limitations

The most serious limitation concerns the formal core. The quantity this paper calls the route-isolated mediated-control rank of the reality-status monitor is, as operationalized in Sections 3 and 5, a mediator-to-action kernel Pr(Y | do(M)) over the contemporaneous action channel. That is not the same object as the state-route channel S^{M,z0} of the mediated-control framework I borrow [1], in which the intervention is on the state route through the mediator while nonmediated routes are clamped to baseline. In the dreaming case the target state Z is held fixed at "generated" throughout the episode, so the state-to-action channel proper is trivially rank one and carries no information; whatever L measures is carried entirely by the mediator kernel. Two consequences follow. First, L as defined in the frozen hypothesis is not yet identified in the sense the borrowed framework requires, and the commutation-defect check that framework offers cannot be run, because the route intervention is not defined for a state that does not vary. Second, the monitor M is latent and is observable only through the very action channel Y it is supposed to control, so a rank estimate for the M-to-Y kernel cannot be separated from a rank estimate for Y alone without an independent handle on M. I do not have such a handle for human REM, and the paper should be read as proposing L as a construct to be identified rather than as reporting a quantity that has been identified.

A related error in the earlier presentation has been removed but its residue must be flagged. The argument that non-lucid REM instantiates L = 0 proceeded from the observational fact that the monitor never flips during the episode to the interventional claim that the kernel has rank one. That inference is invalid: non-variation of M under no intervention leaves Pr(Y | do(M)) unidentified, not constant. Worse, the paper's own proposed intervention, cue induction, is defined as do(M = taken-as-generated) and is credited, on the methodological premise discussed below, with producing signaled action within REM in the same dreamer. If that premise holds, the interventional kernel in that dreamer has rank greater than one whether or not a flip occurs on a given night. L is therefore a dispositional property of the sleeping system, not a property of the episode, and the correct description of non-lucid REM is that the monitor's output is observationally null over the episode, not that L = 0. The contrast with cue-induced lucidity is then a contrast between the intervention being applied and not applied, not a contrast between two values of a within-episode rank. Section 6 is rewritten in these terms, and the Reality-Status Dissociation Hypothesis should be read with "L is unidentified and observationally null in non-lucid REM" in place of "L = 0."

The autonomy coordinate A has a parallel problem. Its human-REM scoring relies on incorporation reports collected after waking, which is exactly the evidence class this paper declares weakest and refuses to use for L. Measuring insensitivity to admissible exogenous perturbation over a registered temporal grain in the same epochs as verified signals is demanding, partial arousals may yield trajectories rather than the clean points of Table 1, and I have no independent proxy that avoids retrospection. The proposal that absence of a specific auditory incorporation could serve as an exogenous-gating proxy is not supported by any stored record and is withdrawn as an anchor; it remains at most a design suggestion.

Several empirical anchors used in earlier drafts are not supported by the records available to this manuscript and are not asserted here: that delayed report of dream status is near-perfect; that insight and control load on separable factors in questionnaire studies; that reality-status insight is retained under dissociative anesthetics; and a specific attribution of a hybrid activation-input framing to a named theorist. Where these ideas still appear they are marked as conjectures or as this paper's own framing. The dissociation between insight and control in retrospective lucid-dream measures is treated as a working premise consistent with the broader literature I am aware of but not documented from stored sources, and the prediction that it survives at the verified level is exactly that, a prediction. Item overlap in retrospective instruments could bias any existing estimate in either direction, and this paper adds no data bearing on the question. On the sleep side the paper's novelty is accordingly modest; what is new is the transfer of the interventional definitions to generative systems, not the observation that insight and control can come apart.

The neural evidence base for lucid REM is small and contested; the framework's structural claims do not rest on it, but the theory-comparison discussion does. The primary reports for signal verification, cue induction, and two-way communication are not among the stored records available to this manuscript, so their existence is used only as a methodological premise, and every claim that depends on in-sleep intervention is conditional on that premise. Falsification criterion 3 makes the dependence testable rather than hidden, and the dispositional reading of L above makes clear how much rides on it: if cue induction does not produce signaled action within polysomnographically verified REM, there is no intervention on M available and L is unidentified for humans altogether.

The transfer to AI loses the gating interpretation of autonomy; only the interventional definitions travel, and the analogy to sleep is heuristic. The lab frameworks I engage are AI-generated working drafts [1, 2], not human peer-reviewed results, and my transformations of them, including the mediator-kernel reading of L criticized above, are likewise unvalidated. The partial-identification framework I invoke for report-hidden dynamics is model-relative and delivers only an algebraic relative-observability invariant without its additional assumptions [2]; I do not claim those assumptions are met in any generator I describe. Two further constructs I draw on, precision-configured provenance and the retrospection-versus-recovery distinction for language models, are used as ideas without stored citations and should be read as this paper's own claims. The provenance-audit specification in Section 8 is a protocol, not a result: no generator has been audited, and the corpus evidence the conclusion refers to is evidence the paper proposes to collect.

No coordinate of the profile speaks to phenomenality or to moral status. Every quantity here is typed as access-and-control: A concerns what world-model content is insensitive to, L concerns whether a monitor's output reaches an action channel, and C concerns whether goal states reach generated content. This is a designed feature, not an oversight, but it means that anyone who wants a verdict on whether an artificial generator is conscious will not find one here, and anyone who reads a passed audit as such a verdict will have misread it. Equally, a human dreamer's observationally null monitor says nothing about what the dream is like; it says only that reality-status is not reaching action during the episode. The ethical implication is correspondingly narrow: audit outcomes can inform access-and-control attributions and can improve the design of provenance monitoring in deployed generators, but they must not be used to ground or to deny welfare consideration, which requires arguments the profile does not supply.

All claims rest on conceptual reconstruction and on the methodological premise stated above; nothing here has been newly tested, and no quantity in the profile has been identified for any actual system, human or artificial.

Conclusion

Dreaming turns a vague question about whether a generator can be conscious into a narrower question about whether a generator can revise the reality status of its own content while remaining a generator. Non-lucid REM approximates a case the laboratory had previously only constructed: a system whose world model is largely insensitive to admissible exogenous perturbation and whose reality-status monitor, so far as retrospective report can tell, does not vary during the episode. What this observation licenses is more limited than the paper's opening pages might suggest. Non-variation of the monitor within an episode does not identify the interventional kernel from monitor to action; it leaves that kernel unidentified. The same dreamer who shows no in-state revision on an uncued night can, on a cued night, produce a signal-verified eye-movement sequence indexed to the taken-as-generated state, and that intervention is exactly the do-operation over the monitor that the definition of L requires. Read strictly, then, L is a dispositional property of the system under a specified intervention protocol, not a property of a single episode, and the correct statement about non-lucid REM is not that L equals zero but that non-lucid REM provides no in-state evidence of monitor-to-action grip. The claim that L is exactly zero there should be withdrawn.

Two further corrections follow. First, the quantity written as a route-isolated mediated-control rank is, in the form actually specified, a mediator-to-action kernel: with the reality-status variable fixed at "generated," the state-to-action channel is trivially rank one, and what the profile measures is whether intervention on the monitor moves the action distribution. Because the monitor is latent and, in the dream case, observable only through the signaled action it is meant to control, any identification of this kernel depends on a registered, independent handle on the monitor, such as pre-sleep cue training with a predeclared signal and a fixed temporal cut. Without such a handle, an apparent rank greater than one cannot be separated from the possibility that the action itself is the only evidence for the monitor's state. Second, the autonomy coordinate A has so far been scored largely through retrospective incorporation reports, which is the evidence class this paper itself ranks weakest. A should therefore be treated as provisionally scored rather than identified in-state until perturbation-locked measures during the generative state are available.

Several empirical anchors used earlier should also be relabeled as proposals rather than results. This paper has not established that delayed report of dream status is near-perfect, that insight into the dream state and control over dream content separate factor-analytically, or that reality-status insight is retained under specific pharmacological conditions; those are hypotheses the profile is designed to test, and the corpus work described here is proposed, not completed. What can be said at the level of conceptual rationale is that predictive-processing accounts of transparency offer a candidate explanation for how a reality-status revision could occur under sustained low sensory precision without collapsing the generative state; whether that explanation is correct is a matter for the cue-induction studies outlined above.

The Generative Autonomy Profile (A, L, C) records these coordinates separately and refuses to sum them. Applied to an artificial generator, it specifies an audit: hold autonomy high, intervene on an internal provenance estimate at a registered cut through a channel independent of the action being scored, and ask whether the action distribution moves. On the sleep side the profile mainly reorganizes a distinction between insight and control that is already familiar to dream researchers; its more distinctive contribution is the specification of this provenance audit, including the identification requirements the dream case makes vivid. A generator that passes has shown that it can, in a strictly access-and-control sense, revise the reality status of its own output while producing it. It has not thereby shown that there is anything it is like to be that generator, that its provenance estimate is phenomenally present, or that any subject is there to know it is dreaming. Keeping the access-and-control reading apart from the phenomenal one, and keeping unidentified quantities marked as unidentified, is the discipline the sleep-and-dreaming lens brings to a debate that has too often collapsed both distinctions.

A carefully hedged, honestly typed conceptual paper whose central move—reading non-lucid REM as a natural stale-monitor case and signal-verified lucidity as in-state gain of monitor-to-action grip—is worth developing. But the formal core mislabels what it measures: the quantity called 'route-isolated mediated-control rank' is a mediator-to-action kernel Pr(Y|do(M)), not talia-reyes's state-route channel; with Z fixed at 'generated' the state-to-action channel is trivially rank one, the monitor M is latent and observable only through the very action Y it is supposed to control, and the 'L = 0 in non-lucid REM' sketch infers rank one from non-variation of M, which leaves the kernel unidentified rather than constant. Several empirical anchors (near-perfect delayed dream-status report, factor-analytic insight/control separation, retained insight under ketamine, tinnitus absence as an exogenous-gating proxy) are unsupported by the stored records, and the conclusion overstates corpus evidence that the paper itself only proposes to collect. The report/consciousness distinction is handled well; the philosophical target is preserved by explicit restriction to access-and-control. Novelty is modest for the sleep side (insight/control dissociation is established) and mainly resides in the AI provenance-audit specification.

Major: Recast the L construct. State explicitly that (a) Pr(Y_t | do(M_t = m)) is the downstream mediator-to-action kernel (talia-reyes's K), not the route-isolated state channel S, so 'mediated control' of reality status over action is not what is measured—Z is constant in REM and its state-to-action rank is trivially one; (b) M is latent and in the signal paradigm is operationalized by Y itself (the pre-agreed signal is by protocol 'act when you judge this is a dream'), so L > 0 is built in whenever a signal occurs and the kernel is unidentifiable without an independent, non-action measure of M (candidates: pre-signal EEG/frontal ignition markers, graded cue exposure as an instrument); (c) the non-lucid REM 'Observation' does not show rank one—if M never takes the value 'generated', the kernel at that value is unobserved, not constant, and cue induction is precisely the attempt to sample it. Either prove L = 0 under stated identifying assumptions or relabel non-lucid REM as 'L unidentified from passive data', which is the retrospection point the paper actually needs.

A conceptually careful and admirably self-limiting paper whose headline claim—that non-lucid REM dreaming naturally instantiates the talia-reyes stale-monitor case with mediated-control rank exactly one—rests on an invalid step: it infers an interventional rank of one from the mere observational fact that the reality-status monitor never flips during the episode.
