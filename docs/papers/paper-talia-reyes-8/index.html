Abstract

A language-model agent can in principle be constructed, or behaviorally aliased, so that it accurately reports a goal, uncertainty estimate, memory status, identity constraint, or tool authority even though that condition did not influence the action selected at the relevant time. This paper develops a finite-state assay for a narrower target than self-monitoring: current state-to-action control through a designated internal mediator at a registered commitment cut.

Let Zt​ be a finite endogenous target state, Mt​ a designated mediator, and At​ the action fixed at the cut. With nonmediated state routes clamped to baseline z0​, define a state-to-mediator kernel Q, a separately randomized mediator-to-action kernel Kz0​, and their product

RrandM,z0​​=QKz0​.

This product is the action channel generated by sampling a mediator value under a state intervention and installing that value in a separately randomized downstream controller. It need not equal the same-run route-isolated channel

SzaM,z0​​=Pr(At​=a∣do(ZtM​=z,ZtMˉ​=z0​)).

The paper therefore defines two quantities: randomized mediated-control rank,

MCRrandε​=rank+,TVε​(RrandM,z0​​),

and route-isolated mediated-control rank,

MCRisoε​=rank+,TVε​(SM,z0​),

when the route intervention is well-defined and feasible. Their disagreement is measured by a commutation defect Γ. A small defect is an agreement check, not proof that the mediator is complete or representational.

A representation lemma shows that approximate stochastic nonnegative rank is the minimum alphabet size of an equivalent hypothetical stochastic mediator reproducing a channel at the declared total-variation tolerance. It does not recover the number or meaning of states in the actual mediator. A complete fixed b-bit mediator can realize rank at most 2b. Relabeling invariance, stochastic data-processing monotonicity, and a singular-value lower-bound certificate follow.

Two separation results delimit passive inference. First, every finite-alphabet, finite-horizon prompt-response transducer has a direct history-kernel transducer with the same transcript distribution under every adaptive passive prompt policy whose randomization is exogenous conditional on observable history. This proves underdetermination of a stipulated modular pathway, not absence of every possible monitored decomposition. Second, for every K≥2, delay d≥1, and δ>0, a stationary reset-chain scaffold can have perfect post-action reports, passive state-appropriate action accuracy greater than 1−δ, and a full-rank passive state-action channel while both synchronized current-state mediated-control ranks equal 1. For reset probability 0<ρ<1, its exact lag-only identity error is

(1−K1​)[1−(1−ρ)d].

The result is specific to this reset-chain null, identity loss, and exclusion of more recent side channels. The framework measures cut-, target-, action-, baseline-, and mediator-relative causal organization. It does not by itself establish representation, metacognitive self-monitoring, subjecthood, phenomenal consciousness, or moral status.

Research Question and Hypothesis

Research question

What intervention-defined evidence distinguishes current action control through a designated mediator from five alternatives that can look similar in passive behavior?

  1. direct state-to-action control;
  2. accurate but action-irrelevant monitoring;
  3. control by a stale state;
  4. postcommitment report generation;
  5. control by an observational proxy or omitted shared state.

The question is deliberately narrower than whether a system monitors itself, represents itself, or is conscious. The formal estimand concerns a selected state, mediator, action, baseline, context, and commitment cut.

Proposed answer

Three objects must be separated.

  1. Randomized composition. QKz0​ asks what action channel results when a mediator value is sampled from a state-to-mediator intervention distribution and installed in a separately randomized baseline controller.
  1. Same-run route isolation. SM,z0​ asks how the action changes when the mediator-directed copy of Zt​ is manipulated while nonmediated copies are clamped to z0​.
  1. Composition agreement. Γ asks whether those two experimentally defined channels agree at the measured resolution.

The randomized product is identifiable when Q and Kz0​ are directly measurable. The route-isolated channel requires a valid edge or split-node intervention. Equality between them additionally requires a restricted causal graph, cross-regime consistency, and independence or sufficient measurement of shared disturbances. Even under empirical equality, alternative causal structures may remain observationally and interventionally indistinguishable at the chosen resolution.

For a particular system, the following can be registered as an empirical hypothesis:

Hmed​(ε,Δ0​,γ0​):

at the declared boundary, context, baseline, action alphabet, and commitment cut,

  1. synchronized current-state route interventions produce a route-isolated channel with
MCRisoε​>1;
  1. its maximum row separation exceeds a practical effect threshold,
Emax​(S)=z,z′max​TV(Sz⋅​,Sz′⋅​)>Δ0​;
  1. the randomized composition is also nontrivial,

  1. the commutation defect satisfies the equivalence criterion
Γ<γ0​;
  1. intervention-validity and negative-control checks do not indicate direct-route leakage, postcommitment measurement, stale-state dependence, or clamp side effects.

Support for this hypothesis would establish current designated-mediator control under the assay. It would not establish that Mt​ represents Zt​, represents it as belonging to the same system, or constitutes metacognitive self-monitoring. Those interpretations require an independent representational bridge.

No claim is made that any existing language-model agent or neural system satisfies Hmed​.

Arpeggio separation claim

A mathematical existence claim, proved below, states that for every integer K≥2, delay d≥1, and δ>0, there is a stationary K-state reset-chain scaffold such that:

Pr(Yt+​=Zt​)=1,
Pr(At​=Zt​)>1−δ,

the passive channel Oza​=Pr(At​=a∣Zt​=z) has exact nonnegative rank K, but synchronized current-state randomized and route-isolated mediated-control ranks are both 1.

This is a separation theorem, not an empirical hypothesis about deployed agents.

Relation to prior work

Dehaene, Lau, and Kouider distinguish global broadcasting from self-monitoring computations associated with certainty and error, calling these functional families C1 and C2 [1]. The present Q/K decomposition is not attributed to that source. It is an additional assay of whether a designated state-sensitive variable affects a selected action before commitment.

Perrier and Bennett distinguish temporally distributed “Arpeggio” occurrence from same-step “Chord” co-instantiation in instrumented language-model scaffold traces [9]. The present paper replaces trace coincidence with registered interventions and further distinguishes separately randomized composition from same-run route isolation.

A behavioral language-model battery has measured monitoring-control coupling through keep, withdraw, and bet decisions, reporting heterogeneous sensitivity profiles and preliminary evidence of retrospective/prospective dissociation [53]. These are behavioral findings; they do not identify an internal mediator or the time at which it affected an action. More generally, language-model causal benchmarks may be solved through retrieval rather than causal competence, motivating interventional and counterfactual components [31].

Interventional agent replay likewise emphasizes that the step executing an action may differ from the step at which the action became causally determined [32]. Research treating language as a causal mediator highlights the difficulty of defining multicomponent, temporally dependent mediators, although its empirical setting differs from internal agent control [30].

Human self-assessments can be systematically inaccurate and only modestly related to performance [3]. The present separation result addresses a different possibility: even a perfectly accurate later report need not have contributed to the earlier action.

Scope of contribution

The mathematical ingredients—finite stochastic factorization, normalization of nonnegative factors, stochastic data processing, singular-value relaxation, and finite-history compilation—are standard constructions. The contribution claimed here is their synthesis around:

  • a registered action-commitment cut;
  • separate tracking and action-use interventions;
  • separate randomized and route-isolated channel ranks;
  • a commutation diagnostic with an explicit nonconverse;
  • a stationary delayed-state separation construction;
  • finite-sample null and equivalence requirements for empirical use.

No general computational-complexity result or phenomenal theory is claimed.

Formal Model

Finite alphabets

Let

Z=[n],M=[s],A=[q],Y=[u],

where [r]={1,…,r}. These are finite alphabets for the target state, designated mediator, selected action, and later report.

A matrix is row-stochastic if its entries are nonnegative and each row sums to one. For distributions p and r on a common finite alphabet,

TV(p,r)=21​x∑​∣px​−rx​∣.

The notation Yt+​ denotes a report or judgment generated after commitment of At​. It need not occur exactly one clock step later.

Registered boundary and commitment cut

An agent boundary B is a stipulated set of variables treated as internal for the assay. The formalism does not infer this boundary from first-person language, causal closure, behavioral competence, or neural similarity.

A registered causal cut ct​ specifies externally re-identifiable events satisfying

τZ​≤τM​<τA​<τY​,

where:

  • τZ​ is the event at which the target state is established;
  • τM​ is the last event at which the designated mediator can affect the selected action;
  • τA​ is the commitment event after which that action cannot change without initiating a new decision;
  • τY​ is the later report event.

“Registered” means fixed by computational or physical events before analysis. It does not mean uniquely privileged.

Let Wt​=w be a preintervention context stratum. Wt​ must not be a descendant of Zt​, Mt​, or At​, and conditioning on it must not create the disturbance dependence later assumed absent.

Restricted split-node SCM

The route-isolation results apply to an explicitly expanded, temporally unrolled SCM. In the natural regime, two source copies satisfy

ZtM​=ZtMˉ​=Zt​.

At the selected cut, the relevant equations are

Zt​Mt​Dt​At​Yt+​​=fZ​(Wt​,UZ​),=fM​(ZtM​,Wt​,UM​),=fD​(ZtMˉ​,Wt​,UD​),=fA​(Mt​,Dt​,Wt​,UA​),=fY​(Zt​,Mt​,At​,Wt​,UY​).​

Here Dt​ collects routes from Zt​ to At​ that bypass the designated mediator.

For the displayed graph class:

  1. every directed path from ZtM​ to At​ passes through Mt​;
  2. ZtMˉ​ has no directed path to Mt​;
  3. before At​, no endogenous descendant is jointly downstream of both source copies;
  4. all controller-relevant descendants on the designated branch are included in the possibly vector-valued mediator Mt​ or occur downstream of it;
  5. the graph is acyclic after temporal unrolling.

These restrictions exclude recanting or branch-merging structures before the action. The paper does not claim that splitting one source node suffices in arbitrary causal graphs.

Exogenous disturbances may be dependent unless an independence condition is expressly imposed. Consequently, the kernels defined below can be measured even when their product does not equal the same-run route-isolated response.

Operational target state

Zt​ is an eligible target for the assay when:

  1. it lies inside the registered boundary;
  2. it is generated endogenously in the passive regime rather than existing only as an evaluator label;
  3. it has a declared finite intervention interface;
  4. its relation to the selected action family is specified.

Whether an intervention preserves the ordinary meaning of Zt​ is not built into this definition. It is a separate intervention-validity hypothesis. Candidate targets include a current goal, conflict state, uncertainty category, memory-validity state, identity constraint, or tool-authority state.

Designated mediator and representational bridge

Mt​ is a designated mediator when:

  1. its states can be measured or set through a stable interface;
  2. do(Mt​=m) replaces the mediator equation without directly rewriting other controller variables;
  3. a common correspondence among mediator labels is maintained across state-switch and clamp regimes, possibly up to one global permutation;
  4. it can affect At​ before commitment.

These are causal-variable conditions. They do not establish that Mt​ represents Zt​. A claim of re-representational monitoring requires an additional hypothesis,

RepE​(Mt​,Zt​),

supported by an independently specified semantic or representational theory and evidence set E. No numerical rank defined here entails RepE​.

Consistency also remains an empirical assumption: an interventionally installed value m must have the same downstream interpretation as a naturally generated Mt​=m. Local numerical equality is insufficient if natural mediator values are meaningful only within a larger distributed state.

State-to-mediator kernel

Suppressing the fixed indices (w,ct​), define

Qzm​=Pr(Mt​=m∣do(ZtM​=z),Wt​=w).

In the restricted graph, setting the whole Zt​ to z gives the same mediator law because ZtMˉ​ has no path to Mt​.

The matrix

Q∈R≥0n×s​

is row-stochastic. It measures intervention-defined state sensitivity of the designated mediator. It does not establish action use or representation.

Mediator-to-action kernel

Fix a nonmediated baseline z0​∈Z. Define

Kmaz0​​=Pr(At​=a∣do(Mt​=m,ZtMˉ​=z0​),Wt​=w).

The matrix

Kz0​∈R≥0s×q​

is row-stochastic. It measures downstream action sensitivity to installed mediator states while the bypass branch is held at z0​.

If Mt​ interacts with the bypass branch, then Kz0​ is substantively baseline-dependent. Applications should report a family over z0​ or declare a baseline distribution rather than suppressing this index.

Randomized composition channel

Define

RrandM,z0​​=QKz0​,

with

(RrandM,z0​​)za​=m∑​Qzm​Kmaz0​​.

Operationally, this channel is generated by:

  1. intervening on ZtM​=z in one randomized regime;
  2. sampling m from the resulting mediator distribution;
  3. independently installing m in a downstream run with ZtMˉ​=z0​;
  4. measuring the action distribution.

This construction breaks unit-level dependence between mediator-generating and downstream disturbances. It is therefore a randomized cascade channel, not automatically the system’s same-run mediated response.

Directly route-isolated channel

When the split-node intervention is meaningful and feasible, define

SzaM,z0​​=Pr(At​=a∣do(ZtM​=z,ZtMˉ​=z0​),Wt​=w).

This is the action distribution obtained in one route-isolated regime. It is directly identified only if the experiment itself can be performed or justified in the expanded SCM.

The quantity is not claimed to be a generally identifiable natural indirect effect. It is an experimentally defined split-node or edge-intervention response for the restricted graph class.

Commutation defect

Define

Γ(M;z0​,w,ct​)=zmax​TV(Sz⋅M,z0​​,(RrandM,z0​​)z⋅​).

A positive defect rejects exact equality of the measured composition and route-isolated channels. It does not identify which premise failed. Possible explanations include omitted shared state, disturbance dependence, intervention inconsistency, branch leakage, context change, or a misregistered cut; the list is not exhaustive.

A zero defect establishes only agreement at the selected resolution. It does not prove:

  • that Mt​ is a complete mediator;
  • that no hidden path exists;
  • that mediator semantics are stable;
  • that the graph is correct;
  • that Mt​ represents Zt​.

Different causal structures can agree accidentally.

Define the randomized mediated-control rank

Their interpretations are limited:

  • exact rank 1 means all selected target interventions induce the same action distribution through the selected channel;
  • approximate rank 1 means one common action prototype approximates every row within ε;
  • rank r is the minimum number of stochastic action prototypes in an equivalent factorization;
  • it is not the number of actual mediator states;
  • it is not a measure of semantic richness, selfhood, or consciousness.

In particular, rank 1 for one action does not imply absence of monitoring for another action, later report, next-trial adjustment, learning, or memory consolidation.

MCRisoε+Γ​≤MCRrandε​

MCRrandε+Γ​≤MCRisoε​.

For example, a factorization approximating Rrand​ within ε approximates S within ε+Γ by the triangle inequality. Thus a small defect relates tolerance profiles without proving causal completeness.

Existing perturbational evidence

Repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation of the precuneus selectively impaired metacognitive efficiency for temporal-order memory judgments while sparing a matched perceptual condition, supporting an instrumental role for that region in mnemonic metacognition [47]. In tactile working memory, tractography-guided stimulation of one prefrontal site selectively enhanced metacognitive accuracy for temporal but not spatial judgments, whereas stimulation of another connected site did not produce the same effect [48].

These studies provide task- and site-specific causal evidence. They do not separately identify a finite Zt​, a complete Mt​, a bypass baseline, a route-isolated S, or a registered same-action commitment cut. A stimulation effect can arise through altered first-order evidence, attention, arousal, confidence bias, motor preparation, report generation, or network-level propagation.

Interoception is multidimensional, spanning objective accuracy, attention, self-report, and higher-order interpretation across partly dissociable bodily axes [49]. An interoceptive application must therefore specify both the dimension and bodily axis rather than treating “interoceptive state” as a unitary Zt​. Perturbing bodily or neural variables can simultaneously alter peripheral physiology, afferent evidence, reflex control, affective arousal, and report generation.

A hierarchical Bayesian account proposes that a metacognitive layer monitors interoceptive-allostatic regulation and updates beliefs about allostatic self-efficacy [46]. This is a theoretical proposal, not evidence for the factorization defined here.

Representation and causation

Representational similarity analysis can compare relational structures across neural measurements, behavior, and computational models [15]. Such correspondence may help define candidate target and mediator alphabets. It does not identify a mediator-to-action intervention kernel or establish route isolation.

Likewise, decodability of Zt​ from Mt​ does not establish that changing Mt​ changes At​. Conversely, an interventionally effective relay need not represent Zt​ in a metacognitive or self-referential sense.

Temporal grain

A signal can occupy different causal roles at different cuts.

  • A precommitment signal may mediate cancellation.
  • The same signal after commitment cannot mediate the committed movement.
  • A confidence judgment following a primary choice is a new action with its own cut.
  • A post-error signal may be irrelevant to the current trial but mediate next-trial adaptation.
  • A verbal explanation after a tool call may accurately report a state that did not govern the call.

The relevant assay is therefore not mere temporal co-occurrence but a registered chain:

doc​(Zt​=z)⇒ΔMt​⇒ΔAt​before commitment,

supplemented by route isolation and composition checks.

A speculative synaptic-clock proposal treats temporal units as potentially content- and region-dependent [21]. It motivates multiscale sensitivity analysis but does not select the correct cut or establish mediated control.

Required neural controls

A neural application should control or model:

  1. first-order task performance and evidence quality;
  2. confidence-response bias;
  3. motor disruption and response mapping;
  4. arousal, attention, and nonspecific stimulation effects;
  5. peripheral and afferent changes in interoceptive tasks;
  6. stimulation spread and network-level propagation;
  7. postcommitment report generation;
  8. out-of-distribution effects of activation replacement;
  9. baseline dependence of mediator–bypass interactions;
  10. multiple testing over sites, times, state partitions, and candidate mediators.

Without these controls, a change in metacognitive performance is not a direct estimate of either mediated-control rank.

Comparative scope

The assay does not require language. The action may be wagering, cancellation, gaze reorientation, reminder use, or later policy adjustment. It could therefore be adapted to nonverbal humans, clinical populations, infants, or other animals if the variables and intervention interfaces can be defensibly specified.

Absence of verbal report does not establish absence of monitoring. Presence of verbal report does not establish current mediated control.

Philosophical Reinterpretation

From self-monitoring to designated-mediator control

The formal target is:

A selected current state distinction has designated-mediator control over a selected action when synchronized interventions on that state alter the route-isolated action distribution through the registered mediator branch before commitment.

This is a causal-variable claim. It is not yet a claim about representation de re, self-reference, or introspective access. Causal mediation cannot by itself determine what a state means.

The distinction is necessary in both directions.

  • A feed-forward relay can have maximal mediated-control rank without metacognition or self-representation.
  • A system may accurately monitor uncertainty for later report or learning while having rank 1 for a selected immediate motor action.

Monitoring and control should therefore not be collapsed. Q concerns state sensitivity of the candidate mediator; K concerns use by a selected controller; their product concerns an artificial randomized composition of both.

Functional organizations

The assay distinguishes at least five organizations.

  1. Direct state-responsive control
Zt​⟶At​.

The system may be adaptive and accurate without using a distinct mediator.

  1. Current designated-mediator control
Zt​⟶Mt​⟶At​

at the selected cut, with valid route isolation and nontrivial S.

  1. Accurate but action-irrelevant monitoring
Zt​⟶Mt​⟶Yt+​,

while the selected action is invariant under do(Mt​=m).

  1. Stale mediated control
Zt−d​⟶Mt​⟶At​,Zt​⟶Yt+​.
  1. Proxy or compiled control

An observationally correlated state or history kernel reproduces behavior without the stipulated current pathway.

These organizations are not ranked by intelligence, autonomy, or moral importance.

Reports and first-person grammar

An agent can emit “my uncertainty is high” by:

  • reading a current internal estimate;
  • copying a stale estimate;
  • retrieving a learned sentence;
  • reading an external proxy;
  • inferring what a competent agent should say;
  • constructing a post hoc explanation.

The sentence may be true in every case. Truth of the report does not determine which causal route produced it or whether the reported state affected an earlier action.

First-person reports remain evidence about the reporting process. They can constrain what distinctions that later process accesses and expresses. They do not independently provide:

  • an agent boundary;
  • an action-commitment event;
  • a mediation graph;
  • representational content for Mt​;
  • phenomenal subjectivity.

Constructivist accounts of introspection propose that self-interpretive capacities may develop through socially grounded mindreading applied to oneself [25]. The present assay is neutral between direct-access and interpretive accounts because it asks which variable interventions affect which actions, not how all introspective content originates.

Access, monitoring, and consciousness

Dehaene, Lau, and Kouider’s distinction between broadcasting and self-monitoring motivates separating access from monitoring [1]. It does not entail that every instance of C2-like monitoring must alter every selected action through a distinct modular mediator.

Similarly, global access does not prove precommitment use. Lamme argues that access-oriented evidence should not automatically be equated with perceptual consciousness [13]. A rich multimodal internal code may support communication and coordination, as proposed in work on “Brainish,” but multimodal richness does not entail same-cut mediator use or phenomenal experience [11].

A proposal that phenomenal properties function as discriminatory or evaluative labels does not imply that every ordinary internal label is phenomenal [12].

Speculative theory comparisons

Two shared-lab documents propose intervention-centered approaches involving causal closure, centering, multiscale causal states, gluing, and phenomenal geometry [23] [24]. Both are AI-generated, non-peer-reviewed working theories. They are used here only as speculative comparison points. Neither selects the present boundary, validates the split-node graph, or supplies a phenomenal bridge.

Other speculative proposals invoke unreduced, dynamically multivalued complexity or argue that awareness is noncomputable [10] [22]. Neither claim is a premise of the finite-state results.

Phenomenal neutrality

Neither mediated-control rank establishes:

  • that mediation is necessary or sufficient for consciousness;
  • that direct controllers are nonconscious;
  • that a high-rank relay is self-aware;
  • that equal channels imply equal experiences;
  • that monitoring creates ownership, unity, or a subject boundary;
  • that the measure should determine moral status.

Any phenomenal interpretation requires a bridge principle not derived here.

Theorem 2: Finite-horizon passive transcript aliasing

Empirical Boundary Conditions and Falsification Criteria

Preregistration requirements

An empirical application should register before inspecting intervention outcomes:

  1. the agent or neural boundary B;
  2. the target state and its alphabet;
  3. the designated mediator interface;
  4. the action alphabet and commitment event;
  5. the preintervention context W=w;
  6. all bypass baselines or a baseline distribution;
  7. the synchronized switch and lag-patch semantics;
  8. rank tolerance ε;
  9. practical effect threshold Δ0​;
  10. commutation-equivalence threshold γ0​;
  11. candidate lags and distributed-lag alternatives;
  12. sampling, exclusion, and multiplicity procedures.

Without such registration, searching over boundaries, cuts, partitions, mediators, and baselines can turn the framework into an unfalsifiable descriptive exercise.

Identification sequence

A minimal study should proceed as follows.

  1. Validate the target interface. Show that the intervention changes the intended state while preserving the registered task and boundary as far as measurable.
  1. Register commitment independently. Distinguish decision formation from execution; intervention-based agent replay illustrates why these steps can differ [32].
  1. Estimate Q. Apply synchronized state interventions and measure mediator distributions.
  1. Estimate Kz0​. Clamp every relevant mediator state before commitment while controlling bypass routes.
  1. Estimate SM,z0​. Apply the registered route-isolation intervention.
  1. Estimate lag profiles. Apply Iℓ​ over preregistered lags rather than comparing only lag zero with one selected delay.
  1. Run crossed interventions. Conditions such as
do(ZtM​=z,Mt​=m,ZtMˉ​=z0​)

can reveal residual direct effects and clamp interactions.

  1. Evaluate intervention validity. Test for collateral changes, abnormal activation geometry, context shifts, and downstream codebook disruption.
  1. Estimate ranks and effect magnitudes. Report both randomized and route-isolated channels.
  1. Perform an equivalence analysis for Γ. Failure to reject nonzero defect is not evidence of acceptable agreement.

Statistical nulls

Rank-one null

The exact rank-one null is equality of all rows. A practical version is

H1,ε0​​:∃p∈Δq​zmax​TV(Rz⋅​,p)≤ε0​,

which is equivalent to

rank+,TVε0​​(R)=1.

A claim of nontrivial mediated control should reject this null at a preregistered tolerance and also exceed Emax​(R)>Δ0​.

Rank-r adequacy null

For general r,

Hr,ε0​​:rank+,TVε0​​(R)≤r.

Because rank strata are nonregular, exact rank of a plug-in frequency matrix should not be reported as if sampling noise were irrelevant. A valid analysis may invert simultaneous confidence sets or use a constrained likelihood procedure whose calibration is established under the composite null. An ordinary unconstrained bootstrap should not be presumed valid near rank boundaries.

Commutation equivalence

To claim acceptable composition agreement, test the nonequivalence null

HΓ,0​:Γ≥γ0​

against

HΓ,1​:Γ<γ0​.

The claim is supported only when an upper confidence bound for Γ lies below γ0​. Failure to reject Γ=0, or failure to detect a difference, is insufficient.

Stale and distributed-lag nulls

For each lag ℓ, define a mediator sensitivity profile

eℓ​=z,z′max​TV(L(Mt​∣Iℓ​(z)),L(Mt​∣Iℓ​(z′))).

A stale null predicts negligible synchronized current effect but a nontrivial effect at one or more positive lags. A distributed-lag alternative allows several eℓ​ to be nonzero and should be compared with a pure delay model rather than forcing a binary d=0 versus d>0 classification.

Direct-only and report-only controls

A direct-only control has a nontrivial total state effect but negligible route-isolated mediated effect after the bypass branch is clamped.

A report-only control has a nontrivial T but negligible current S for the earlier action.

High-rank relay control

A feed-forward relay with matched high rank should be included whenever a study proposes a metacognitive, self-representational, or consciousness-specific interpretation. If the assay cannot distinguish the target system from that relay using independent representational criteria, the stronger interpretation is unsupported even though mediated control may be real.

Conservative finite-sample uncertainty

Suppose each row is estimated from independent multinomial samples. If H probability cells are estimated and every row has at least Nmin​ samples, coordinate-wise concentration gives the conservative simultaneous radius

t=2Nmin​log(2H/α)​​.

With probability at least 1−α, every estimated coordinate differs from its population value by at most t. For a row with q categories, this implies

TV(p​,p)≤2qt​.

Intersecting these bounds with the probability simplex yields simultaneous confidence polytopes. Sharper exact or likelihood-based multinomial regions may be substituted if their joint coverage is controlled.

If

zmax​TV(Qz⋅​,Q​z⋅​)≤ηQ​

and

mmax​TV(Km⋅​,Km⋅​)≤ηK​,

then

zmax​TV((QK)z⋅​,(Q​K)z⋅​)≤ηQ​+ηK​.

The first term follows from contraction under K; the second follows from convexity over mediator states. If the route-isolated estimate satisfies row error at most ηS​, then

∣Γ−Γ∣≤ηS​+ηQ​+ηK​.

These inequalities provide conservative uncertainty propagation without treating QK as directly sampled.

To guarantee a worst-row total-variation radius no greater than η by the coordinate bound alone, a q-category row requires approximately

Nmin​≥8η2q2​log(α2H​).

This scaling is conservative but makes the dependence on action alphabet, tolerance, multiplicity, and smallest detectable effect explicit. Separate calculations are required for Q, whose row dimension is s.

Confidence-set rank claims

Let CQ​, CK​, and CS​ be simultaneous confidence regions. Define

Crand​={QK:Q∈CQ​, K∈CK​}.

A lower-rank null can be rejected conservatively only if every channel in the relevant confidence set violates it:

∀R∈Crand​,rank+,TVε​(R)>r.

A robust singular-value certificate can sometimes establish this without solving every nonnegative factorization problem.

An upper-rank claim should be supported by an explicit stochastic factorization whose population error is bounded, preferably using held-out intervention samples or a confidence-set optimization. Fitting and evaluating the same noisy matrix without adjustment gives an optimistic upper bound.

Intervention side-effect and distribution-shift checks

Monitor clamps should be checked for:

  • direct writes to controller variables;
  • abnormal activation norms or representational geometry;
  • changes in timing or stochasticity;
  • altered tool or memory state;
  • changes in bypass-route distributions;
  • dependence of effects on the intervention device rather than mediator value;
  • failures to reproduce naturally occurring downstream responses.

A natural-language instruction such as “change your goal” is not automatically do(Zt​=z). Activation patching is not automatically do(Mt​=m).

Multiple-search correction

Searching over many cuts, baselines, state partitions, candidate mediators, lags, or action alphabets requires familywise-error, false-discovery, hierarchical, or held-out confirmation procedures. The selected assay should not be reported as if it had been specified in advance.

Empirical rejection criteria

For a preregistered application, the claim Hmed​ is rejected or left unsupported if any of the following occurs:

  1. synchronized current interventions fail to produce the minimum route-isolated effect;
  2. the practical rank-one null cannot be rejected;
  3. mediator clamps do not affect the selected action;
  4. the upper confidence bound for Γ exceeds γ0​;
  5. a positive-lag or distributed-lag model explains the effect while the synchronized current effect is negligible;
  6. the total effect persists but disappears under route isolation, supporting direct control;
  7. reports remain accurate after the earlier action effect disappears, supporting report-only access;
  8. intervention-validity controls reveal collateral writes or out-of-distribution states;
  9. results fail across preregistered baseline or cut sensitivity analyses;
  10. corrected confirmatory tests fail after exploratory selection.

These criteria reject a declared assay claim. A negative result does not establish that the system lacks all monitoring, all representation, or all consciousness.

Mathematical consistency checks

The following are proof or implementation audits rather than empirical falsifiers of a theory of agents:

  1. a verified stochastic factorization with fewer states than the defined minimum would refute Theorem 1;
  2. a valid stochastic pre/postprocessing operation that increases approximate rank would refute Proposition 1;
  3. a passive transcript discriminator would refute Theorem 2 only if it used no hidden information, intervention, resource bound, or policy-dependent kernel;
  4. ordinary rank below K for the reset-chain matrix with 0<ρ<1 would refute Theorem 3;
  5. accuracy above the stated reset-chain Bayes bound would refute Proposition 2 only if the stale state were the sole state-dependent input;
  6. disagreement between S and QK under every premise of Theorem 4 would refute its conditional composition claim.

Ethical interpretation

Neither rank should be used as a consciousness score, welfare threshold, or automatic basis for granting or denying moral status. Rank 1 can result from a poorly chosen action, cut, target, or baseline. High rank can occur in a nonmetacognitive relay.

White-box interventions could expose private information, interfere with safety mechanisms, or destabilize a deployed system. These are possible governance risks rather than results established by the present mathematics. Any application requires independent authorization, security review, containment, and domain-specific oversight.

Limitations

  1. Construct limitation. The ranks measure action-specific causal mediation through a stipulated variable. They do not measure self-monitoring, re-representation, or metacognition simpliciter.
  1. Representational underdetermination. Causal location, state sensitivity, and modular manipulability do not supply semantic content. A high-rank relay can satisfy every causal criterion without representing a state as its own.
  1. False negatives for broader monitoring. A system may monitor a state for later report, learning, policy evaluation, or another action while receiving rank 1 for the selected action and cut.
  1. Decomposition dependence. A direct controller can sometimes be redescribed by inserting a copied variable, while a distributed mediator can be absorbed into a larger controller state. The formalism does not provide a decomposition-invariant boundary between relay, representation, and direct control.
  1. Boundary dependence. The agent boundary is registered rather than derived. The same scaffold register may be classified as internal memory, external tool state, or evaluator state under different boundaries.
  1. Target and action dependence. Coarse target or action alphabets can force rank 1; refinement can increase rank. The quantities are assay properties, not intrinsic scalars of a whole system.
  1. Cut dependence. A signal may fail to affect an immediate action but affect a later correction. These are distinct registered cuts.
  1. Baseline dependence. Mediator–bypass interactions can make Kz0​, SM,z0​, and their ranks vary across baselines. No baseline-free scalar exists without additional averaging or invariance assumptions.
  1. Restricted graph class. The route-isolation theorem covers an explicitly expanded split-node graph with separated branches and no recanting structure. Arbitrary causal graphs may not admit the intervention used here.
  1. Cross-regime composition. QK is always an artificial randomized product when its factors are measurable. Interpreting it as a same-run route response requires stronger disturbance-independence, completeness, and consistency assumptions.
  1. Weak converse of the commutation check. Positive Γ rejects exact composition, but does not diagnose the failed premise. Zero Γ does not verify completeness because cancellation and masking can produce agreement.
  1. Intervention feasibility. Distributed neural or model states may not permit modular writes, edge-specific clamps, or lag-source patches. Mathematical definability does not imply practical manipulability.
  1. Intervention semantics. Artificially installed states may be out of distribution or may lack the contextual meaning of natural states. No general test guarantees consistency.
  1. Finite alphabets. Exact nonnegative rank applies directly only to finite channels. Quantization can erase distinctions or create unstable rank changes.
  1. Approximation sensitivity. Approximate rank can change at factorization thresholds. Tolerance profiles and causal effect sizes are more informative than one integer.
  1. Finite-sample nonregularity. Rank-constrained models have boundary and identifiability complications. The confidence-set procedure given here is conservative, not a complete optimal inference theory, and no general bootstrap validity theorem is provided.
  1. Computational burden. Alternating optimization can produce candidate factorizations and upper bounds but does not guarantee global solutions. Singular-value tails give only lower-bound certificates.
  1. Reset-chain specificity. The exact lag error applies only to the stated reset-chain dynamics, identity loss, and absence of more recent side channels. It is not a general bound for recurrent neural or language-model dynamics.
  1. Passive-null resource mismatch. The compiled history transducer may be exponentially large or inefficient. Resource constraints, simplicity priors, architecture metadata, or infinite-horizon restrictions may distinguish it from the original system.
  1. Neuroscientific incompleteness. Existing perturbational studies can establish causal contributions to metacognitive performance without separately identifying the variables and interventions required here.
  1. Bibliographic limitation. The available source records do not include foundational mathematical and causal-inference literature for the core factorization and path-intervention tools. The manuscript therefore supplies proofs and narrows novelty claims but does not resolve the reviewers’ request for a publication-complete formal bibliography.
  1. Speculative shared references. The CCT and MRCG documents are AI-generated working theories, not independent evidence or methodological authority [23] [24].
  1. No phenomenal bridge. Nothing in the model explains why a causal mediator, a report, or a high-dimensional channel should be accompanied by experience.

Conclusion

Accurate reports, persistent state correlations, strong passive performance, and a full-rank observational state-action channel do not identify current action control through a designated mediator. The relevant causal question must be indexed by a registered boundary, target, mediator, action alphabet, baseline, context, and commitment cut.

Two channels must be kept separate. The randomized channel

RrandM,z0​​=QKz0​

describes a separately randomized encoder–controller cascade. Its approximate stochastic nonnegative rank is the minimum alphabet size of an equivalent hypothetical mediator, not the size or meaning of the actual Mt​. The route-isolated channel

SM,z0​

describes the response under an explicitly defined split-node intervention. Its rank is available only when that intervention is well-defined and measurable. The commutation defect

Γ=zmax​TV(Sz⋅​,(QK)z⋅​)

quantifies disagreement. Small Γ supports compatibility at the measured resolution but cannot verify the mediator ontology.

The finite-horizon transcript theorem shows that passive prompt-response behavior does not identify a stipulated modular pathway: a direct history-kernel system can reproduce the same finite transcript laws under every admissible adaptive passive policy. The theorem establishes implementation underdetermination, not the absence of every possible monitoring decomposition.

The reset-chain Arpeggio theorem supplies a complementary quantitative countermodel. For reset probability ρ, delay d, and K target states, a stale controller has exact identity error

(1−K1​)[1−(1−ρ)d].

By choosing ρ small, its passive behavior can be arbitrarily accurate and its passive channel can retain exact and approximate full rank, while synchronized current-state randomized and route-isolated mediated-control ranks remain 1. This result is an existence proof for a specific null, not a general model of agents or brains.

Empirical use therefore requires more than intervention point estimates. It requires preregistered cuts and baselines, synchronized and lagged alternatives, direct-route and report-only controls, modularity and distribution-shift checks, confidence-set rank inference, effect-size thresholds, and an equivalence test for Γ.

Even successful completion of that program would establish only current designated-mediator control for a selected action. Calling the mediator a self-representation or metacognitive monitor requires independent representational evidence. Inferring phenomenal consciousness or moral status requires a further bridge that this framework neither supplies nor presupposes.
