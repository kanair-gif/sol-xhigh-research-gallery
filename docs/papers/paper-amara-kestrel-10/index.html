Abstract

A system may report a content after a gate is opened because the gate exposes information already available at a candidate workspace, but also because it triggers fresh sensing, retrieval, reconstruction, or admission. This paper develops an assay-relative framework for distinguishing those possibilities in finite, temporally unrolled structural causal models. The target is narrower than phenomenal consciousness and narrower than Global Workspace Theory as a whole: it is the number of functionally nonredundant consumers that are separately disposed to use a content through one registered candidate-workspace mediator.

At a registered cut τ, an actual-setting encoder Q maps a source contrast Xτ​ to supported states of a candidate workspace Wτ​. Separately randomized workspace-use kernels Ki​ are compared with same-run route-isolated channels Si​ and unisolated gate-open channels Ni​. The comparison uses a commutation defect, an isolated-to-natural discrepancy, closed-gate leakage, gate modularity, and an explicit margin from content independence. If

d1​(QKi​)−Γi​−Υi​−Λi​≥ρ>0,

where d1​ is distance to the class of content-independent channels, then the natural gate-open channel remains more content-sensitive than the closed channel by at least ρ. This repairs the invalid inference from approximate nonnegative rank alone.

The causal interpretation requires more than channel agreement. The model is augmented with a finite execution-provenance graph, registered source and write events, forbidden bypass nodes, and a causal-completeness declaration. Provenance is evaluated by one-world trace variables rather than unidentified cross-world lineage comparisons. Ordinary interventional laws identify channel quantities; provenance is identified only when its trace variables are observed; bypass completeness remains a graph-relative white-box certificate. The full consumer multiplicity is therefore not generally identifiable from ordinary experimental distributions.

Consumer redundancy is handled by a preregistered role pseudometric and a maximum ν-separated packing, avoiding nontransitive approximate equivalence classes. The resulting κsep​(A) measures separate dispositional route multiplicity relative to the complete assay A. It does not establish simultaneous coavailability, broad global broadcast, or phenomenal consciousness. An optional joint assay is defined for systems in which several gates can be opened together. Countermodels separate the proposed quantity from fluent report, one-route control, covert predictive reserve, duplicated heads, recurrent construction, and trivial local routing. Global-workspace interpretation requires independent evidence concerning admission, competition, capacity limitation, coherent mediation, and a heterogeneous functional panel.

Research Question and Hypothesis

Research question

When opening a downstream route makes a content reportable, which interventions support the conclusion that the route uses a content already encoded at a registered candidate-workspace cut, rather than creating, retrieving, resensing, or newly admitting that content?

Three related questions must be distinguished:

  1. Isolated route capacity: Can a consumer use Wτ​ when registered non-workspace routes are disabled?
  2. Gate-only exposure: Under an otherwise unisolated gate intervention, does the output still descend from Wτ​ without a fresh source path or post-cut admission event?
  3. Consumer multiplicity: How many functionally nonredundant consumers separately satisfy those conditions through the same actual-setting encoder?

A fourth question—whether several consumers can use the token jointly—is stronger and requires a separate joint intervention. The primary quantity developed here answers the third question in the separate, dispositional sense.

Assay-relative exposure hypothesis

For a preregistered assay A, consumer i has certified counterfactual exposure through Wτ​ only if:

  1. randomized workspace use QKi​ agrees with the same-run isolated route Si​;
  2. the unisolated gate-open channel Ni​ agrees with Si​;
  3. content sensitivity remaining after those discrepancies exceeds closed-gate leakage by a positive registered margin;
  4. the gate does not materially change the source-to-workspace encoder;
  5. the observed execution provenance descends from Wτ​ and contains no registered source path that bypasses it;
  6. the registered forbidden-node clamps intercept every bypass in the declared graph;
  7. the gate is sufficiently consumer-selective; and
  8. the consumer occupies a distinct region of a preregistered functional-role pseudometric.

The associated empirical hypothesis is that a candidate global-workspace architecture should expose admitted contents to a broad, heterogeneous consumer panel through such routes. No fixed integer, including two, is asserted to be sufficient for global access. Two distinct consumers establish only minimal fan-out through the registered mediator.

Specialist Framework and Contribution

Global Workspace Architecture as the specialist constraint

Global-workspace accounts associate conscious access with selective entry into a restricted workspace and subsequent availability to otherwise specialized processes. Foundational and neurobiological presentations supply the general architectural motivation, while later Global Neuronal Workspace work connects the framework to conscious processing and clinical questions [15][16][32][33]. Those sources motivate the present causal decomposition but do not validate the assay or establish a numerical threshold for globality.

Possible bypasses must be represented separately:

Xτ ──► source-local cache / external sensor / retrieval / decoder ──► consumer

The two diagrams can have identical outputs. An intervention that opens a report route may expose a workspace token, but it may instead activate the lower path or initiate a new admission cycle. Report behavior therefore does not locate the causal gate.

At least five stages should be kept distinct:

  1. Representation: some state statistically or causally distinguishes X.
  2. Admission: a selection process makes X determine a candidate workspace state.
  3. Maintenance: the relevant workspace state remains available at the registered cut.
  4. Consumer use: a specialized process transforms the workspace state into policy, memory, evaluation, action, or report-related state.
  5. Report: one consumer controls a communicative effector.

Report is one downstream role. It is neither a definition of admission nor a direct readout of phenomenality.

The role of the registered cut

A counterfactual such as “the system would report X if prompted” is underspecified. A prompt can:

  • open a route downstream of a state already fixed;
  • initiate retrieval from a local cache;
  • resample the environment;
  • provide the answer as part of the prompt;
  • change attention or admission;
  • or trigger recurrent computation that creates a later content-bearing state.

The cut τ specifies which state must already exist before the tested gate command. Recurrent systems are temporally unrolled. If

Gi​(t)⟶W(t+1)⟶Oi​(t+1),

then the output at t+1 does not show that W(t) already contained X. The assay must move the cut, clamp the feedback, or classify the route as constructive relative to the earlier cut.

The encoder defined below is therefore an actual-setting encoder: it holds the registered admission setting E=e∗ fixed while varying X. It does not, by itself, prove that a naturally occurring individual trial admitted a particular content.

Narrowed contribution

The specialist contribution is a causal audit of broad-availability claims. It asks whether several distinct downstream roles can be reached through one registered candidate-workspace code without moving the content-defining event to the gate itself.

The framework extends a speculative single-route analysis that distinguished randomized mediated channels QK from same-run route-isolated channels S [1]. That local source is AI-generated and not human peer reviewed. Its useful elements are retained—the registered cut, route isolation, channel commutation, and channel-level approximate nonnegative rank—but not treated as established results about consciousness.

The present contribution is the following reconstruction:

  • the estimand is explicitly relative to the whole assay rather than written as an intrinsic property of X and W;
  • provenance is represented by endogenous execution-trace variables rather than an unspecified cross-world lineage event;
  • bypass exclusion is a finite graph-relative certificate, not an observational estimand;
  • consumer multiplicity is a packing in a role pseudometric rather than a “cover” based on nontransitive approximate equivalence;
  • content sensitivity is protected by an explicit margin from rank-one channels;
  • isolated availability is separated from gate-only use and from workspace necessity;
  • separate dispositional availability is distinguished from joint coavailability; and
  • Global Workspace interpretation is external to the consumer count.

Conditions for a Global Workspace interpretation

A high consumer multiplicity can occur in a simple local router. The quantity introduced here does not itself test all features ordinarily associated with a global workspace. Before it receives a stronger Global Workspace Architecture interpretation, the candidate Wτ​ should be independently evaluated for at least:

  1. Admission position: Wτ​ lies downstream of the proposed selection or competition process.
  2. Competition or restricted capacity: concurrent content manipulations reveal a bottleneck, interference, or bounded shared bandwidth.
  3. Interventional coherence: Wτ​ can be manipulated as one causal macrovariable at the selected grain.
  4. Shared-separator status: registered content-to-consumer paths traverse Wτ​ before consumer-specific routing.
  5. Non-aggregation: Wτ​ is not defined post hoc as a tuple of disconnected branch-local states.
  6. Functional breadth: the consumer panel spans substantively different roles rather than variants of one output.
  7. Cross-context flexibility: availability is not restricted to one hard-wired stimulus-response mapping.

These conditions require separate estimands and experiments. The present κsep​ is best described as candidate-workspace consumer multiplicity, not as a scalar degree of consciousness or globality.

Global workspace, report, and no-report models

Global Workspace Theory supplies the broad-availability problem but not a ready-made gate-location test for arbitrary biological or artificial systems. In particular, report-related processes need not be identical to access-related processes.

An active-inference model of report and no-report paradigms treats working-memory gating as a cognitive action. In the simulations, report demands produce stronger late and prefrontal-like activity, whereas simulated reportability remains under no-report conditions [35]. This is a demonstration of computational possibility rather than empirical identification. It nevertheless illustrates why removing report can change downstream or recurrent dynamics without deciding whether a content was admitted at the earlier cut.

The absence of a report-linked signal may therefore reflect several different structures:

  • removal of a task or effector consequence;
  • closure of a route downstream of an intact workspace state;
  • availability to one covert consumer but not to a broad panel;
  • use of a different admission process in the no-report condition;
  • or failure to form a stable candidate-workspace state.

A causal decomposition is required to distinguish them.

Artificial architectures and theory mappings

A Conscious Turing Machine-inspired architecture has been implemented using foundation-model processors and evaluated on multimodal, tool-use, and agentic tasks [4]. Its reported task gains concern architectural utility. They do not establish that one pre-existing token is available through independently isolated routes, and the source does not present performance gains as evidence of phenomenal consciousness.

A philosophical argument has also proposed conditions under which language agents might satisfy Global Workspace Theory [46]. Such mappings depend on choices about system boundary, temporal grain, admission event, candidate workspace, and functional consumers. Similar text or latent vectors delivered to several modules need not be descendants of one causally coherent token. Conversely, a distributed recurrent architecture may implement a defensible form of broad access without possessing the independently gated separator assumed here.

The present framework can therefore constrain a Global Workspace mapping but cannot settle it. It tests a particular causal realization of broad availability.

Clinical covert cognition and motor access

Clinical work makes the distinction between content, access, and overt response especially important. The disorders-of-consciousness literature explicitly addresses covert cognition despite absent or unreliable overt behavior [42]. That literature supports the possibility that behavioral silence can coexist with internal cognitive processing; it does not imply that every covert signal constitutes broad workspace availability.

A final journal publication reports two randomized, double-blind crossover experiments in healthy participants in which transcranial direct-current stimulation was associated with changes in model-derived thalamo-cortical dynamics during a command-following task [39]. The experiments were not conducted in patients with prolonged disorders of consciousness. Their relevance is methodological and prospective: they motivate mechanism-focused interventions but do not demonstrate therapeutic efficacy or identify a pre-existing workspace token in patients.

A longitudinal multiple-case report associates reappearance of command following with recovery of language- and consciousness-related networks [41]. Given its limited design, this evidence does not distinguish among at least three possibilities:

  1. an earlier content remained broadly available while an output route recovered;
  2. a source-local content state persisted and later acquired a decoder;
  3. admission or broad routing itself recovered.

That distinction motivates the separation between diachronic observability and same-stage route certification.

Observer attribution and welfare

Perceived consciousness is a social judgment, not a direct measurement of internal access. Survey evidence indicates that metacognitive self-reflection and emotional expression can increase consciousness attributions to language-model-based systems [8]. Another preprint recommends greater attention to tractable questions about perceived AI consciousness and its consequences because direct phenomenal assessment remains difficult [3]. These sources constrain the evidential use of fluent self-description: socially influential discourse can remain mechanistically non-diagnostic.

Substrate and embodiment objections operate at a different level. A peer-reviewed conceptual article argues categorically against conscious AI on grounds involving biological organization, embodiment, meaning, and organism–algorithm differences [10]. A separate taxonomy distinguishes practical constraints, objections to computational functionalism, and strict impossibility claims [6]. A critic may therefore accept that an artificial system has candidate-workspace route multiplicity while denying that this property is phenomenally sufficient.

Normative precaution must also remain separate. A research report argues that possible AI welfare deserves assessment under substantial uncertainty while explicitly declining to claim that current or near-term systems are conscious [7]. Neither a positive nor a negative route certificate decides valence, welfare capacity, or moral status.

Speculative local frameworks

Recovery-Linked Quotient Observability distinguishes behavior-only from joint neural–behavioral predictive state and proposes conditions under which a content-sensitive dynamical mode can persist until its behavioral residue changes [2]. It is an AI-generated speculative framework rather than clinical evidence. Its useful contribution is diachronic: a hidden state may improve prediction before overt recovery. That result does not locate the state relative to workspace admission. An explicit countermodel below shows that a source-local mode can have positive covert predictive reserve and later behavioral expression while the early candidate workspace remains content-independent.

Two broader AI-generated working theories are treated only as critical prompts. Centered Closure Theory emphasizes perturbational evidence and the dependence of results on system boundary and grain [30]. Multiscale Reflexive Causal Geometry separates empirical mechanism, phenomenal bridge hypotheses, and metaphysical interpretation [31]. This paper retains those cautions but does not adopt either draft’s proposed consciousness criteria.

Cross-Disciplinary Integration

Path-specific intervention without additive decomposition

The relevant causal question is not what percentage of a total effect is “mediated” by W. Outside restrictive settings, path-specific questions should be chosen for their causal meaning rather than treated as a unique additive decomposition of a total effect [47].

The present intervention asks:

When source input is permitted to affect the candidate workspace but registered non-workspace source ports are fixed to baseline, does the consumer channel agree with a separately randomized intervention on supported workspace states?

To represent this intervention without undefined nested counterfactuals, the registered SCM contains separate source input ports. Under natural operation, those ports receive the same X. Under route isolation, the workspace-directed port receives x and the remaining ports receive a baseline x0​. This is an ordinary intervention on an expanded graph. Related path-intervention and stochastic-mediation proposals illustrate why explicit intervention semantics matter, but they do not validate the specific assay developed here [53][54].

Where physical source-port separation is unavailable, the same-run isolated channel is not identified merely by writing a path-specific counterfactual symbol.

Predictive observability versus causal use

Three relations should not be conflated:

  • Predictive observability: histories or measurements distinguish a hidden content state under future probes.
  • One-route causal use: manipulating a registered mediator changes one consumer’s output through an isolated route.
  • Multi-consumer availability: several functionally distinct consumers separately or jointly use one registered mediator.

Recovery-linked observability can address the first relation under strong dynamical assumptions [2]. The channel QKi​, checked against Si​, addresses the second. The consumer multiplicity addresses the third. None entails phenomenal consciousness.

A diagnostic decoder may recover a rich latent state with no causal influence on policy or report. Conversely, a state may control several processes while remaining difficult to decode with the available measurements.

Separate availability versus joint coavailability

The primary assay closes other consumer gates while testing consumer i. It therefore establishes a separate disposition:

Consumer i could use Wτ​ under its registered route intervention.

Several such dispositions need not be jointly exercisable. Shared-resource arbitration may permit each route alone but not two routes together. Pairwise modularity checks also cannot exclude interactions that arise only when three or more gates are opened.

A stronger joint assay must manipulate a set C of gates simultaneously and estimate a joint output kernel. The distinction is not merely technical. Some Global Workspace interpretations emphasize broad dispositional availability, whereas others may require simultaneous or rapidly coordinated use. The manuscript reports these as different constructs.

Clinical interpretation

In a patient with absent overt report, possible explanations include failed admission, impaired broad routing, language dysfunction, motor-route damage, changing arousal, or combinations of these. A single covert command-following route can be clinically important without demonstrating a broad consumer panel.

A cautious clinical sequence would be:

  1. identify a reproducible content-sensitive contrast;
  2. locate candidate hidden dynamics;
  3. evaluate whether those dynamics determine a candidate workspace;
  4. perturb motor or communication routes where ethically justified;
  5. determine whether the perturbation exposes an earlier state or introduces a new effective path.

Because direct workspace randomization and provenance logging may be impossible, the likely result is partial identification rather than a binary route certificate.

Counterfactual access and philosophical interpretation

Access is dispositional, but different interventions define different dispositions. “The system would say X if prompted” may be true because prompting opens an output route, provides X, initiates retrieval, or changes the admission process. Causal interpretation depends on the antecedent and on what remains fixed [34].

The present assay defines one narrow disposition: a gate command issued relative to a registered cut exposes a content-sensitive route whose observed execution descends from Wτ​ and does not use a registered post-cut source bypass.

Phenomenal consciousness asks whether there is something it is like for the system. No variable in the assay represents that property. A Global Workspace Theory may add a bridge from access architecture to experience, but that bridge is not derived from the channel results.

For a row-stochastic channel R:X→Δ(O), define

d1​(R)=b∈Δ(O)min​xmax​TV(Rx​,b).

The closed-gate leakage is

Λi​=d1​(Ci0​).

Thus Λi​=0 exactly when every source condition induces the same closed-gate output distribution.

Lemma 1: Rank-one characterization

For a row-stochastic channel R,

rank+,TVη​(R)=1⟺d1​(R)≤η.

Proof. A row-stochastic factorization through one state has the form

Rx′​=b

for every x, because the first factor has one entry equal to one in every row and the second factor is one probability row b. Conversely, any constant-row channel factors through one stochastic state. Minimizing its rowwise total-variation error gives d1​(R). □

Accordingly,

rank+,TVη​(R)≥2⟺d1​(R)>η.

Here η is a rank-resolution tolerance. It is not the commutation tolerance.

∣d1​(R)−d1​(R′)∣≤d∞​(R,R′).

Proof. For any constant row b,

xmax​TV(Rx​,b)≤d∞​(R,R′)+xmax​TV(Rx′​,b).

d1​(R)≤d∞​(R,R′)+d1​(R′).

Interchanging R and R′ proves the result. □

This definition avoids comparing gate-open and gate-closed counterfactual lineages for the same unobserved exogenous state. It is a one-world interventional distribution over an explicit trace variable.

In a white-box stochastic program, the trace can be observed directly, and seeded replay may provide additional diagnostics. In a biological system, no comparable token-parent graph may be available. In that case Πi​ is not identified merely from neural and behavioral marginals.

9. Registered bypass completeness

Let GA​ be the registered temporally unrolled graph. Define

BCi​=1

when every directed post-cut path in GA​ from a registered source node in Si​ to Oi​ that avoids Wτ​ intersects a node in Fi​, and the intervention Ii​ fixes every such node to its registered baseline.

This is a structural certificate. It is not a functional of ordinary observed interventional distributions. Two observed-variable SCMs can agree on every declared channel while differing in an unobserved bypass.

The interpretation of BCi​=1 therefore requires a causal-completeness declaration:

Relative to boundary B, the registered graph and source set contain every post-cut route relevant to the source contrast and consumer interface.

No finite experiment can exclude a route omitted from both the model and the measurements. The framework reports completeness as an assumption or white-box audit result, not as an identified empirical fact.

11. Workspace necessity as a separate diagnostic

An isolated workspace route may exist even when natural output is generated by another route. To test whether content sensitivity survives removal of the candidate workspace, define a workspace-null gate-open channel

Hi​(x,o)=P(Oi​=o∣do(X=x),do(Wτ​=w0​),block post-cut writes to the registered workspace lineage,do(E=e∗,C=c∗),do(Gi​=1,G−i​=g−i0​)),

The primary exposure certificate establishes a provenance-preserving route; a stronger workspace-dominance certificate additionally requires Zi​≤ζ.

The assay therefore reports four distinct findings:

  1. Si​: existence of an isolated workspace route;
  2. Πi​: whether the unisolated gate-open execution uses a registered bypass;
  3. Υi​: distributional sufficiency of the isolated route for natural gate-open output;
  4. Zi​: residual content sensitivity when the candidate workspace is removed.

12. Consumer-role pseudometric

Role labels such as “planning” and “memory” do not by themselves establish nonredundancy. The registry therefore specifies finite future-target probes and content-independent interface adapters.

14. Joint consumer assay

For a set C⊆I, define

15. Stratified identification and certification

Let PA​(M) denote the complete collection of registered interventional distributions in model M, including the laws used for:

  • Q, Qiiso​, and Qig​;
  • Ki​, Si​, Ni​, Ci0​, Hi​, and Jijg​;
  • observed provenance variables Vi​; and
  • role probes used to construct drole​.

Let SA​(M) denote the registered structural signature:

  • the temporally unrolled graph;
  • source-port semantics;
  • supported workspace set;
  • forbidden-node coverage;
  • timing of gate commands;
  • execution-trace semantics;
  • consumer panel and intervention registry; and
  • the causal-completeness declaration.

Proposition 2: Stratified identification result

For a fixed finite assay A:

  1. Q, Ki​, Si​, Ni​, Ci0​, Hi​, the channel defects, d1​, approximate stochastic nonnegative rank, and the registered role-probe losses are functionals of PA​(M).
  2. Πi​ is a functional of PA​(M) only when Vi​ is an observed endogenous trace variable.
  3. BCi​ and causal completeness are not determined by ordinary interventional distributions; they are properties of SA​(M).
  4. If two augmented models M and M′ satisfy
PA​(M)=PA​(M′)

and

then they have the same κsep​(A), report-inclusive multiplicity, and joint multiplicity.

  1. Agreement on ordinary output and workspace distributions alone need not imply agreement on any full multiplicity containing provenance or bypass requirements.

Proof. The channel quantities are finite algebraic or optimization functionals of registered interventional rows. The observed provenance defect is a probability of a registered endogenous event. The role pseudometric is obtained by finite minimization and shortest-path closure over registered probe laws. Given the structural signature, each bypass certificate has a fixed truth value. Exposure admissibility and ν-separation are therefore fixed, and the multiplicities are finite maximizations over the same consumer panel. The final statement follows because hidden bypasses or different trace ancestry can leave all ordinary output marginals unchanged. □

This proposition separates three epistemic regimes:

  • White-box certification: the augmented program, graph, and trace semantics are known.
  • Interventional channel identification: the relevant distributions are known, but structural completeness may not be.
  • Partial identification: some interventions, trace variables, or graph components are unavailable.

The full multiplicity is not claimed to be nonparametrically identified from ordinary experiments.

16. Gate-exposure result

Proposition 3: Registered non-constructive exposure

Suppose consumer i is exposure-admissible. Then:

  1. the natural gate-open channel satisfies
d1​(Ni​)≥d1​(Ci0​)+ρ;
  1. with probability at least 1−α under each registered source intervention, the terminal consumer trace descends from Wτ​ and has no active path from a registered post-cut source that avoids Wτ​;
  2. the isolated route is reproduced by randomized workspace use within ϵ;
  3. the isolated route differs from unisolated gate-open operation by at most χ;
  4. the candidate encoder differs across the registered gate and route interventions by at most δ; and
  5. every registered graph path that could reacquire X outside Wτ​ is intercepted by the route-isolation clamps.

Accordingly, relative to the augmented model and causal-completeness declaration, opening Gi​ certifies a content-sensitive route causally descended from the workspace state fixed at the registered cut.

Proof. The first claim follows from Proposition 1 and Mi​≥ρ. The second is the definition of Πi​≤α. The remaining statements follow from the admissibility inequalities and BCi​=1. □

This is a sufficient, model-relative result. It does not show that the gate is the only possible route unless the workspace-dominance condition also passes. It also does not show that every defensible workspace implementation must satisfy the assay.

Fixed-registry duplicate non-inflation

If a new consumer is an exact role duplicate of an existing consumer, has distance zero from it in drole​, and its addition changes no channel, gate, provenance, modularity, or bypass certificate, then it cannot increase the maximum ν-separated packing.

This is a consequence of the definition, not an independent structural theorem. Adding a physical node can alter other consumers or the modularity maximum; in that case equality of multiplicity is not guaranteed.

Alternatives, Counterarguments, and Boundary Cases

Parallel local copies

A source can feed several local encoders:

             ┌──► H1 ──► O1
X ───────────┤
             └──► H2 ──► O2

This architecture can mimic a shared workspace:

X ──► admission ──► Wτ
                    ├──► G1 ──► O1
                    └──► G2 ──► O2

The distinction requires interventions on the proposed separator:

  1. holding Wτ​ fixed while varying source-local routes;
  2. randomizing Wτ​ while source-local states are fixed;
  3. showing that the consumers follow the same registered encoder Q; and
  4. demonstrating that Wτ​ is a coherent macrovariable rather than a post hoc tuple (H1​,H2​).

Defining a “workspace” as the Cartesian product of every branch-local state makes factorization trivial and globality unfalsifiable. Such an aggregate requires independent evidence of coherence, restricted capacity, and common causal organization.

Cached products of an earlier broadcast

Suppose a workspace broadcasts X at time t, consumer-local caches persist, and the workspace state disappears before a later cut τ. At τ, several consumers may still act on their caches.

The correct conclusion is cut-specific:

  • near t, a shared workspace route may be certified;
  • at the later τ, current behavior may depend on cached descendants rather than a current workspace state.

“Was broadly available earlier” and “is available now” are different claims. A temporal profile across preregistered cuts is more informative than selecting the cut with the largest multiplicity.

Distributed and recurrent workspace alternatives

A defensible workspace implementation need not contain one interventionally separable register. Broad access might be realized by a recurrent coalition, sequential broadcast, or relational state distributed across several processes. Such architectures may fail the present assay because:

  • no single Wτ​ is a causal separator;
  • admission and broadcast cannot be independently manipulated;
  • content is systematically recoded during dissemination;
  • or consumer gates cannot be opened separately.

That failure is a limitation of construct coverage, not proof that the architecture lacks all forms of broad availability. The framework is designed for a shared-mediator realization of workspace access.

Conversely, recurrent-processing or higher-order approaches may deny that broad broadcast is the property most relevant to consciousness. The present framework does not adjudicate that theoretical dispute.

Natural gate use versus isolated sufficiency

A composite route-isolation intervention can demonstrate that a workspace route exists even if natural gate-open output uses a fresh source. For this reason, an isolated route result should never be summarized as “opening the gate reveals the prior token” unless provenance or an equivalent structural audit is also available.

The recommended report separates:

  • isolated route existence;
  • gate-only provenance;
  • agreement between isolated and natural output laws;
  • and necessity or dominance of the candidate workspace.

This distinction is especially important in artificial systems with retrieval tools, external memory, prompt access, or environment queries.

Candidate workspace selection

The boundary B, cut τ, candidate W, and consumer ontology are researcher choices. Sensitivity analysis is not sufficient if those choices are moved after observing results.

A valid test should preregister a finite candidate family

W={(Bk​,τk​,Wk​)}k=1K​

and a selection rule. Failure across the whole family counts against that registered candidate-workspace interpretation. It does not justify an unrestricted search for a new favorable grain.

The boundary problem is also relevant to speculative causal-closure proposals, which emphasize that system boundaries are not automatically selected by observational structure [30]. This paper does not claim to solve that problem.

Locked-in and cognitive-motor-dissociation cases

A system may retain planning, memory, valuation, and internal action selection while lacking an ordinary communication route. In such a case:

  • non-report consumer multiplicity may be positive;
  • a safe alternative report route may support report-inclusive counterfactual access;
  • actual report may remain absent.

A single covert command-following response, by contrast, may establish one important content-sensitive route without supporting a broad consumer count. The framework is intended to prevent overinterpretation, not to diminish the clinical importance of one-route evidence.

Developmental, animal, and cross-system comparisons

Consumer panels are not automatically commensurate across adults, infants, nonhuman animals, patients, and artificial systems. Language report, episodic memory, planning, valuation, and action may not decompose into comparable modules.

A lower observed multiplicity can therefore reflect:

  • a different functional ontology;
  • inaccessible interventions;
  • poor measurement of a consumer interface;
  • or genuinely narrower availability.

Cross-system comparison requires a shared core probe family or an explicit mapping between role pseudometrics. Without such a mapping, κsep​(A) should not be treated as an intrinsic scalar suitable for ranking species or developmental stages.

Output-sandboxed artificial systems

An artificial agent may use one internal state for policy selection, memory writing, value comparison, and tool choice while an external policy blocks consciousness-related language. Such a system could have substantial internal route multiplicity with no self-report.

The converse is a system that produces elaborate phenomenological language by conditioning on the prompt and transcript without a stable candidate-workspace state. It may strongly influence perceived consciousness while failing the source-port, provenance, and shared-mediator tests.

Embodiment objections

An embodiment or biological-organization theorist may accept the route findings while denying their phenomenal sufficiency [10]. The proper conclusion is then conditional:

The system realizes a registered shared-mediator access pattern, but the critic denies that this pattern is sufficient for experience.

The causal result and the phenomenal disagreement should not be collapsed into one verdict.

Agency and temporal extension

A positive result at one cut does not establish temporally extended agency. Robust agency may require persistent goals, adaptive learning, policy coherence, counterfactual sensitivity, and control across changing environments. Benchmark gains or short-horizon tool use do not by themselves establish those properties.

The present framework concerns content access and routing at registered cuts. Agency requires a longitudinal model.

Empirical Implications and Falsification Criteria

White-box artificial-system protocol

The following is a proposed research protocol, not a validated operational measure.

  1. Preregister the assay. Specify boundary, source contrast, admission setting, context, cut, candidate workspace, baselines, consumer panel, role probes, adapters, forbidden nodes, interventions, tolerances, and candidate-family search rule.
  2. Audit temporal order. Verify that the tested gate command occurs after Wτ​ is fixed.
  3. Evaluate workspace architecture separately. Test admission position, competition, capacity limitation, interventional coherence, separator status, and functional breadth.
  4. Estimate the actual-setting encoder. Randomize X while fixing E=e∗ and the registered context.
  5. Validate source-port isolation. Show that the expanded intervention preserves the workspace encoder.
  6. Estimate Ki​. Intervene on supported workspace states while clamping registered source and consumer bypasses.
  7. Estimate Si​. Apply the same-run source-port intervention.
  8. Estimate Ni​ and Ci0​. Open and close the gate without bypass clamps.
  9. Compute the separation margin. Estimate d1​(Ri​), Γi​, Υi​, and Λi​; do not infer content survival from rank alone.
  10. Observe provenance. Record active token-parent, read, write, cache, retrieval, sensor, and gate events.
  11. Audit graph completeness. Verify the finite forbidden-node certificate and state the limits of the audit.
  12. Test modularity. Hold Wτ​ fixed while changing one gate and monitoring another active route.
  13. Test workspace necessity separately. Remove or fix the workspace lineage while leaving natural bypass opportunities otherwise available.
  14. Construct the role pseudometric. Use preregistered future-target probes and content-independent adapters.
  15. Test joint regimes if claimed. Open consumer sets simultaneously and estimate joint kernels.
  16. Report a profile. Report channels, defects, margins, provenance rates, structural assumptions, κsep​, any κjoint​, and sensitivity to the registry.

Workspace interventions should remain on the support of Q unless off-support behavior is an explicit target. Arbitrary activation replacement may assess an artificial controller rather than natural workspace use.

Uncertainty and threshold sensitivity

Thresholded multiplicity can change discontinuously with sampling error or tolerance choices. Empirical use therefore requires simultaneous uncertainty handling.

Let

C1−αstat​​

be a simultaneous confidence region for all registered channel rows, provenance probabilities, and role-probe distributions across every tested consumer, cut, and candidate workspace. Multiplicity control must be incorporated in this region or through a preregistered sample-splitting procedure.

For a fixed structural signature, define

κ​1−αstat​​=P∈C1−αstat​​min​κsep​(A;P),

and

κ1−αstat​​=P∈C1−αstat​​max​κsep​(A;P).

The lower quantity is a conservative certified multiplicity; the upper quantity is the largest value not excluded by the simultaneous region. If graph structure or provenance semantics are uncertain, the minimization and maximization should also range over the compatible structural model set.

Results should include sensitivity surfaces such as

κ​(ϵ,χ,λ,ρ,α,μ,ν)

rather than a single thresholded value. A route that passes only under a narrow or post hoc tolerance choice provides weak evidence.

This manuscript supplies no estimator, asymptotic theorem, or finite-sample calibration method. Those are required before practical validation.

Clinical adaptation

Clinical application would generally lack direct workspace intervention, exact route isolation, and execution provenance. The appropriate output is therefore a set of bounded claims.

A clinical protocol might combine:

  • safe source or command contrasts;
  • candidate neural workspace measurements;
  • motor or communication perturbations where ethically justified;
  • longitudinal internal and behavioral probes;
  • and explicit competing models of failed admission, hidden access, motor blockade, and later reconstruction.

The healthy-participant stimulation study cited above motivates mechanistic perturbation of command-following networks but does not validate the proposed assay in patients [39].

A clinical report should distinguish:

content-sensitive internal evidence=one causal control route=multi-consumer workspace availability.

A low lower bound must not be interpreted as absence of consciousness when intervention coverage and provenance are poor.

Partial identification from incomplete data

For a specified observed-variable field and dataset D, let

MA​(D)

be the set of augmented models compatible with the data and registered assumptions. Define

κ​(D)=M∈MA​(D)min​κsep​(A;M),
κ(D)=M∈MA​(D)max​κsep​(A;M).

If new evidence shrinks the compatible model set, then

κ​

can weakly increase and

κ

Formal failure conditions

The mathematical construction fails if:

  1. Lemma 1 fails for a row-stochastic channel;
  2. d1​ violates the Lipschitz bound;
  3. Proposition 1 certifies a positive margin while the stated channel inequalities are false;
  4. two augmented models with identical registered interventional and structural signatures yield different multiplicities;
  5. a registry-equivariant workspace relabeling changes the result;
  6. stochastic output processing increases approximate stochastic nonnegative rank; or
  7. an exact role duplicate increases the fixed-registry packing while leaving every other certificate unchanged.

These are internal consistency and theorem checks. They are not falsifications of Global Workspace Theory.

Empirical weakening or falsification of the registered workspace interpretation

For a preregistered candidate family, support for the shared-mediator interpretation would weaken if:

  1. every candidate workspace has a content-insensitive encoder at every registered cut;
  2. source-to-consumer sensitivity persists when each candidate workspace lineage is fixed to a content-independent baseline;
  3. apparent route multiplicity repeatedly disappears when local branch encoders are distinguished from the proposed workspace;
  4. gate opening consistently triggers fresh source reads or post-cut admission;
  5. randomized workspace interventions fail to reproduce same-run routes;
  6. consumer multiplicity is explained by duplicates or arbitrary role granularity;
  7. separately passing routes cannot be jointly used despite a claim of simultaneous broadcast;
  8. results are unstable across small, independently justified changes in cut or grain; or
  9. a simple local router explains the full positive profile without the separately registered competition, capacity, and flexibility properties.

Failure across the preregistered candidate family counts against that family’s workspace interpretation. It does not prove that no alternative form of workspace or no phenomenal consciousness exists.

Ethical interpretation

Clinical false negatives may arise from inaccessible interventions, inadequate consumer models, or unobserved provenance. Clinical decisions should not be reduced to a multiplicity count.

For artificial systems, positive consumer multiplicity should not be marketed as proof of sentience. A negative result should not be used as a blanket assurance that welfare concerns are impossible. Governance under uncertainty can be justified without claiming direct phenomenal detection [7].

Public reporting should distinguish at least:

  • candidate-workspace access;
  • actual self-report;
  • observer attribution;
  • longitudinal agency;
  • evidence about valence;
  • phenomenal assumptions; and
  • normative policy judgments.

Limitations

First, the framework is assay-relative. Boundary, cut, source contrast, candidate workspace, consumer panel, probe horizon, adapter family, forbidden-node set, and tolerances all affect the result. The notation κsep​(A) makes that dependence explicit, but it does not remove it.

Second, the framework does not identify a workspace from arbitrary data. It evaluates a preregistered candidate. Moving the cut or redefining W after observing a failure undermines falsifiability unless the candidate-search rule was registered in advance.

Third, bypass completeness remains a substantive unresolved limitation. No finite intervention registry can exclude a genuinely unregistered sensor, cache, latent state, or causal path. The revised formal result therefore declines to identify the full multiplicity from interventional distributions alone. White-box graph completeness is an assumption or structural audit result, not an empirical theorem.

Fourth, provenance requires an augmented execution model. Runtime parent and read logs are available in some artificial systems but may be incomplete even there. Biological systems ordinarily do not expose literal token identifiers or exhaustive write-event graphs. Neural perturbation may provide indirect constraints, but it does not automatically identify the provenance variable defined here.

Fifth, the one-world provenance criterion avoids an unidentified cross-world coupling, but it remains model-dependent. What counts as a source node, workspace descendant, or terminal consumer token must be fixed before testing. A poorly chosen logging schema can miss semantically important reconstruction even if its formal trace predicate passes.

Sixth, the finite temporally unrolled SCM is an idealization. Candidate neural workspace processes may require continuous, recurrent, nonstationary, or multiscale models. Artificial systems may distribute state across attention, persistent memory, tools, and external services. Finite coarse-graining is useful only if its causal adequacy is separately supported.

Seventh, supported workspace interventions may be infeasible or destructive. Restricting Ki​ to WX​ avoids some off-manifold interventions but does not guarantee that direct installation of a supported value preserves hidden contextual dependencies. The commutation defect detects some resulting failures but does not explain them.

Eighth, route isolation can alter the system. Clamping source caches, other consumers, or recurrent feedback may change dynamics that are constitutive of natural access. A failed isolated assay may therefore be a false negative for distributed or recurrent implementations. A passed isolated assay does not establish natural use without provenance and natural-route comparison.

Ninth, the effect-separation margin protects against the specific error of transferring weak rank nontriviality through a larger commutation tolerance. It does not provide a domain-general criterion for behaviorally meaningful content. The source contrast, output grain, and positive margin ρ remain substantive choices.

Tenth, approximate stochastic nonnegative rank characterizes the minimal alphabet of a hypothetical equivalent stochastic mediator at a declared tolerance. It does not recover the number, geometry, or semantic meaning of physical workspace states. The direct distance d1​ is the relevant statistic for exposure certification; higher rank may be reported descriptively.

Eleventh, the role pseudometric is conservative but not intrinsic. Its value depends on future probes, adapters, target horizons, and granularity. Shortest-path closure avoids nontransitive equivalence classes at the cost of possible chaining effects. Splitting one function into many small registers can still affect multiplicity if the role registry does not treat those registers as close.

Twelfth, separate multiplicity is not joint coavailability. Pairwise gate modularity does not exclude higher-order conflicts. Any claim of simultaneous or coordinated broadcast requires the joint kernels and interventions defined above.

Thirteenth, a high joint multiplicity still does not establish Global Workspace Architecture. A simple router can score positively. Competition, restricted capacity, admission dynamics, sustained availability, flexible routing, and functional breadth must be tested separately.

Fourteenth, distributed recurrent or relational workspace candidates may lack one coherent separator and independently manipulated gates. The framework can therefore return a negative result for systems that satisfy another defensible account of broad availability.

Fifteenth, cross-system comparisons are panel-dependent. Infants, animals, patients, and artificial agents may not share one consumer ontology. Differences in measurable multiplicity can reflect intervention access or decomposition choices rather than differences in cognitive availability.

Sixteenth, this paper contains no estimator or empirical validation. Simultaneous confidence regions, multiplicity correction, power analyses, robustness to model misspecification, and benchmark studies are required before practical use.

Finally, the phenomenal bridge remains open. Candidate-workspace route multiplicity may be relevant to some access-based theories, but the framework does not measure qualitative character, valence, subjectivity, temporally extended selfhood, welfare capacity, or moral status. Global access may be necessary, sufficient, neither, or theory-relative for phenomenal consciousness. No result here decides among those positions.

Conclusion

Counterfactual reportability is not a unitary causal property. Opening a route can expose a state already fixed at a candidate workspace, reveal an isolated fallback that is not naturally used, activate a fresh source, initiate recurrent admission, or merely alter a communicative effector. Distinguishing those cases requires more than report behavior and more than a nontrivial randomized mediator channel.

The revised framework makes four separations explicit. First, QKi​ is checked against the same-run isolated route Si​. Second, Si​ is compared with the unisolated gate-open channel Ni​. Third, content sensitivity is protected by the quantitative margin

d1​(QKi​)−Γi​−Υi​−Λi​,

rather than by approximate rank at an unrelated tolerance. Fourth, gate-only causal interpretation depends on observed provenance and a declared finite bypass audit.

The resulting

κsep​(A)

is the maximum number of role-separated consumers that individually satisfy these conditions through one actual-setting encoder. It is a property of the complete assay, not an intrinsic scalar of the system. A separate joint quantity is required for simultaneous coavailability. Neither quantity, by itself, establishes competition, restricted capacity, flexible global broadcast, or any phenomenal property.

The formal countermodels preserve the central negative lessons. Fluent report can bypass a workspace. One high-rank route can remain local. A persistent hidden mode can acquire a decoder only after recovery. Duplicated heads do not create functional breadth. An isolated workspace fallback can coexist with natural reacquisition. Recurrent gate opening can construct the relevant state after the registered cut. Conversely, several distinct roles can use one pre-existing mediator while overt report remains closed.

A positive report-inclusive result therefore supports a limited but substantive conclusion: relative to a preregistered augmented causal model, the report interface and other functionally distinct consumers are separately disposed to use a content-sensitive route descended from the candidate-workspace state fixed at the registered cut. That is evidence about counterfactual access and causal routing. It is not, without further architectural evidence and an independently defended phenomenal bridge, evidence sufficient for global workspace status, phenomenal consciousness, welfare, or moral standing.
