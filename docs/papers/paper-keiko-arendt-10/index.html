Abstract

Fluent self-description is weak evidence of phenomenal consciousness, but eliminating report from assessment does not solve the measurement problem. Comparative and developmental cognition raises a prior question: when does an already detectable content distinction begin to govern a nonlinguistic indication or recipient-directed signal, despite changes in body, skill, interface, timing, and social context? This paper develops a typed causal framework for that question. At each developmental, recovery, or training stage, raw events are coded by potentially overlapping roles: internal measurement (N), world-directed instrumental action (I), experimenter-elicited indication (E), and recipient-directed signaling (G). Exact predictive partitions for these roles may be organized in a lattice, but the lattice is descriptive rather than an identification theorem.

The primary estimand is a route-isolated finite-output channel in a registered, time-expanded structural causal model. Approximate stochastic nonnegative rank—and, operationally, the simpler test of rank 1 versus greater than 1—assesses whether a registered content contrast changes the output distribution through a designated route. All early witnesses, cross-stage matching, and later route effects are evaluated on the same prespecified content subset or pair. Endpoint matching of intervention-response roles establishes only a transport-compatible predecessor, not persistence. A stronger persistence claim additionally requires longitudinal causal-lineage evidence that excludes destruction and independent recreation of a role-isomorphic capacity.

The former Typed Report-Opening Proposition is replaced by two operational outcomes: a predecessor-compatible opening and a lineage-supported opening. For E, arbitrary remapping and transfer test flexible convention-governed indication but do not exclude a general instruction-conditioned first-order classifier. For G, recipient-knowledge sensitivity, changed-goal uptake, and repair provide a conservative functional-signaling profile but do not establish communicative intention, metarepresentation, or phenomenality. Finite output coding, baseline sensitivity, comparable response opportunity, null-controller benchmarks, population-level identification, and finite-sample uncertainty are made explicit. Failure of endpoint matching defaults to nonidentification, not content genesis. Passing the framework supports a narrow claim about content-sensitive indication or signaling. It does not by itself establish self-ascription, phenomenal consciousness, robust agency, welfare capacity, or moral status.

Research Question and Hypothesis

Research question

Under what registered diachronic and interventional conditions can an observed nonlinguistic output transition be classified as:

  1. a new route from an earlier, functionally compatible content distinction;
  2. a lineage-supported continuation of an earlier content capacity;
  3. acquisition of an indicating convention by an already content-sensitive route;
  4. recoding of an existing indication;
  5. first detected use under a newly available response opportunity;
  6. later content formation; or
  7. nonidentification among these alternatives?

The framework is contrast-specific and measurement-relative. “First” means first observed among sampled stages, registered output families, candidate boundaries, and comparable opportunities. A two-stage comparison interval-censors the transition between the last adequately assessed negative stage and the first positive stage. It cannot identify an absolute first moment.

Working hypotheses

The paper advances three empirical hypotheses rather than an if-and-only-if theorem.

H1: Typed-assay hypothesis. Separating instrumental action, convention-governed indication, and recipient-directed signaling—and testing each with interventions appropriate to its role—will distinguish relevant alternatives better than accuracy, gesture onset, verbal fluency, or generic behavioral recovery alone.

H2: Developmental-decomposition hypothesis. Events commonly described as the “emergence of report” will separate into at least four trajectories: route opening, convention acquisition, surface recoding, and content formation. Treating all four as one transition will produce avoidable false positives and false negatives.

H3: Lineage hypothesis. Endpoint similarity will often be insufficient to distinguish continuation from role-isomorphic recreation. In systems where longitudinal lineage handles are available, intermediate-stage perturbations or inherited-mechanism interventions will discriminate at least some of these models.

These hypotheses can fail. In particular, the proposed output profiles would have poor construct validity if benchmark null controllers passed them as often as systems with independently established content-sensitive communication, or if established communicators systematically failed for reasons unrelated to the target construct.

Scope and terminology

Let the primary output roles be:

  • N: internal measurements, including neural, physiological, computational, or hidden-state measurements;
  • I: world-directed instrumental action, whose immediate role is to alter the environment or obtain a task outcome;
  • E: experimenter-elicited indication, produced under a registered response convention;
  • G: recipient-directed signaling, produced in an interaction where another agent’s information and behavior are relevant;
  • L: linguistic form, treated as a surface format rather than an evidential type. A linguistic output must still be analyzed as E, G, I, or a mixture.

These labels apply to causal roles, not movement shapes. A point may be simultaneously instrumental, imperative, informative, and socially directed. The analysis therefore permits multilabel coding and joint-output models rather than assigning each event one exclusive type.

The term functional report is used only as a broad umbrella for making a current content available through E or G. The formal verdicts remain more specific:

  • convention-governed indication for E;
  • recipient-directed functional signaling for G;
  • endogenous-state indication when the routed contrast is an independently dissociated state inside the candidate agent boundary.

These categories do not entail metacognition. A stronger self-ascriptive report would require evidence that the system represents a state as obtaining in, or belonging to, itself. No general language-free test of that higher-order property is established here. First-person grammar is neither necessary nor sufficient.

A candidate content contrast is an endogenous distinction characterized by a registered intervention-response profile. Calling it a content does not establish its phenomenal character, semantic grain, correctness, or explicit availability to the agent. Where the probes distinguish only low-level features, the warranted conclusion is correspondingly low-level.

Specialist Framework and Contribution

Why developmental and comparative cognition is central

Adult verbal-report paradigms often combine four achievements:

  1. possession of a discriminable state;
  2. use of that state in first-order action;
  3. mastery of an indicating or communicative convention; and
  4. linguistic encoding.

Infant and comparative research makes this combination methodologically unstable. Before language, contents may affect gaze, reaching, avoidance, information seeking, vocalization, pointing, or recipient-directed behavior. Yet none of these forms is intrinsically a report. A gaze choice may be an orienting response; an opt-out may maximize reward; a point may request an object; and a repeated signal may respond to nonreward rather than misunderstanding.

The supplied developmental records identify joint attention, pointing, embodied attention, vocalization timing, and preverbal communication as relevant research topics [28] [29] [30] [31]. Their metadata does not establish the present paper’s proposed knowledge, uptake, repair, or remapping criteria. The specialist contribution is therefore not a retrospective claim that these studies have already identified report. It is a framework for asking more sharply what their outputs can establish.

Development also changes the measurement system. Effectors, posture, response speed, motor competence, social partners, task comprehension, and available conventions all vary. A later response can become detectable because the relevant content acquired a route, because a response opportunity became available, or because the measurement apparatus improved. Morphology-sensitive motor learning in an embodied artificial infant model illustrates the narrower point that motor trajectories can change substantially with bodily capability [32]. It provides no evidence about report, but it cautions against identifying a cognitive transition from token-level motor continuity alone.

Contribution

The revised contribution has five parts.

  1. A multilabel typed ontology. Internal distinction, instrumental action, elicited indication, recipient-directed signaling, and linguistic format are not pooled as generic behavior.
  1. Contrast-specific causal routing. The same registered content pair must supply the early witness, cross-stage comparison, early route-null result, and later route effect. A later rank increase on an unrelated pair cannot complete the criterion.
  1. A separation of endpoint compatibility from lineage. Similar intervention-response roles across stages support a predecessor-compatible relation. They do not identify persistence. A stronger continuation claim requires longitudinal causal ancestry and exclusion of reset-and-recreation models.
  1. An operational criterion rather than a theorem. The framework defines conservative assay outcomes. It does not claim that the listed conditions are necessary or sufficient for report in ordinary language, metacognitive report, or phenomenal consciousness.
  1. An uncertainty-aware implementation. Outputs are finitely coded; edge interventions are defined in a time-expanded causal graph; baselines and response opportunities are registered; population identification is separated from finite-sample estimation; and null-controller benchmarks are part of construct validation.

The limited role of the predictive-partition lattice

Typed predictive partitions remain useful for organizing the distinction between covert, instrumental, elicited, and social predictive capacities. For example, two histories may be distinguishable by future N or I outcomes while remaining indistinguishable by future E outcomes. A later E partition may separate them.

The lattice does not itself identify a causal route, developmental continuity, report, or consciousness. No conclusion below depends on a novel meet, join, or lattice invariant. Its role is diagnostic and representational: it displays non-nested output capacities and preserves joint-channel dependence that marginal summaries can miss. Causal identification is supplied, where possible, by the registered structural model and interventions.

Infant communication and developmental measurement

The developmental records make several variables relevant without settling their interpretation. Pointing is studied in relation to joint visual attention and preverbal communication, embodied attention is explicitly addressed, and longitudinal timing differences in visual checking and vocalization have been investigated [28] [29] [31]. A separate record concerns preverbal communicative behavior in infants with Down syndrome [30]. No detailed result beyond those topic-level descriptions is inferred here.

These topics constrain the present method in three ways.

First, a response must be typed by function rather than morphology. A manual point can participate in I, E, G, or several roles.

Second, timing must be stage- and channel-specific. Visual checking, vocalization, and manual action need not fall within one universal response cut.

Third, developmental comparison requires evidence that both stages offered a comparable opportunity to express the target role. If an early infant could not execute, understand, or access the later response convention, a later positive result is first detected use under a new assay, not evidence that a previously available report route had been causally absent.

The proposed recipient-knowledge, changed-goal uptake, and repair conjunction is consequently presented as a conservative research design, not an established developmental definition of declarative communication. Established but inflexible communication may fail it; learned audience-sensitive policies may pass parts of it.

Comparative metacognition

Comparative metacognition research explicitly distinguishes monitoring from control and asks whether uncertainty-sensitive behavior is implicit or explicitly represented [38]. Information seeking is likewise a recognized comparative topic [36] [39].

Opt-out paradigms illustrate why adaptiveness is insufficient. An evolutionary model finds conditions under which metacognition-like opt-out behavior can be favored, including high variability in success and low average success combined with high rewards [37]. This supports a limited negative inference: adaptive uncertainty sensitivity does not by itself establish explicit awareness, semantic self-report, or a representation of uncertainty as one’s own state.

The revised framework therefore initially types opt-out and information seeking as I. They become candidates for endogenous-state indication only when an internal uncertainty or memory contrast is separated from external difficulty cues and shown to govern a designated indication route. Even then, a general conditional policy can implement the route without explicit metacognition.

Formal inputs from the shared laboratory records

The route-isolated mediated-control draft distinguishes a monitor’s information from information that governs an action at a registered cut. Its stale-monitor construction and transcript-aliasing argument show why passive accuracy and later self-description cannot identify synchronized current-state control [1]. Its approximate stochastic nonnegative-rank interpretation is retained here in a finite-output setting: the rank is the minimum alphabet size of a hypothetical stochastic factorization reproducing the channel within tolerance. It does not recover the actual mediator’s states or meanings.

The present paper departs from that draft by distinguishing I, E, and G, by requiring stage-specific windows, and by treating separately randomized upstream and downstream kernels as auxiliary rather than automatically equivalent to a same-run route-isolated channel.

The recovery-linked quotient draft emphasizes partial identification and distinguishes an early neural-behavioral predictive reserve from later behavioral efficacy. Its stronger persistence claims depend on structural mode transport, model-family restrictions, preparation assumptions, and observability conditions [2]. The current framework retains its caution but does not replace mode continuity with endpoint similarity. Endpoint role matching is explicitly labeled weaker: it establishes a compatible predecessor, not persistence.

Artificial-agent trajectories

Training trajectories can separate capacities that final-model evaluation conflates. Situation-modeling performance has been reported to precede and exceed more fragile false-belief performance across language-model training stages, with effects of scale, training volume, post-training, and lexical form [13]. These are informative task trajectories, but all assessed outputs remain linguistic. They do not show when a nonlinguistic content-to-indication route appeared, whether a current hidden state caused a particular answer, or whether post-training exposed an existing distinction rather than creating a new one.

A global-workspace-inspired AI architecture has reported gains on multimodal, tool-use, and agentic tasks, but this is evidence about performance in an architecture inspired by a consciousness model, not evidence of phenomenal consciousness [4]. A proposed multimodal inner code such as “Brainish” could furnish a candidate internal distinction or mediator, but its presence would not entail access to E or G [12].

Surface discourse also affects observers. Metacognitive reflection and emotional expression have been reported to increase perceived AI consciousness [8]. That result concerns attribution, not underlying mechanism. The distinction supports a broader shift toward tractable questions about attribution and social effects while direct phenomenal assessment remains unsettled [3]. Content routing is proposed here as another tractable target, not as a substitute definition of phenomenality.

Clinical and covert-capacity applications

The supplied corpus contains no peer-reviewed clinical empirical record that tests this framework. Clinical discussion must therefore remain prospective.

An early internal distinction can indicate a covert predictive capacity without establishing action access, report, or consciousness. Conversely, absent output may reflect motor impairment, arousal, pharmacologic state, response-modality mismatch, memory failure, or an inappropriate time window. Recovery is often nonstationary: encoding, arousal, motor readiness, later reconstruction, and state-dependent memory can change together. A later indication may reconstruct an earlier event rather than route an earlier state, while failure to indicate may reflect amnesia rather than absence of the earlier distinction. Clinical use would require explicit longitudinal state variables and encoding-versus-reconstruction controls, not a generic nuisance clamp.

Consciousness, substrate, and welfare claims

Theory-derived computational indicators can organize assessment without settling consciousness [9]. Uncertainty persists because systems may satisfy some theoretical criteria but not others [5]. Objections also operate at different levels, from practical limitations to challenges to computational functionalism and categorical impossibility claims [6]. A biological and embodiment-based argument against conscious AI remains logically compatible with granting an artificial system functional indication or signaling [10].

Welfare governance is likewise distinct from evidence. Precautionary assessment may be justified under uncertainty without a claim that current or near-term systems are conscious [7]. A report-route result cannot be upgraded into welfare evidence merely because the consequences of error are ethically important.

Cross-Disciplinary Integration

Developmental pragmatics constrains causal analysis

Developmental pragmatics supplies the sender–content–recipient organization that a generic action channel lacks. Causal analysis can then ask whether:

  1. the sender’s current content changes the signal;
  2. recipient information changes the sender’s use of that signal;
  3. manipulating the signal changes recipient behavior; and
  4. that behavioral effect depends on the recipient’s current goal.

The integration is asymmetric. A path coefficient does not derive declarative meaning, communicative intention, or common ground. Pragmatic theory identifies relevant contrasts; causal intervention tests whether the proposed organization is present. The result remains a functional profile.

Comparative metacognition constrains remapping claims

The monitoring/control distinction prevents a successful uncertainty-sensitive action from being treated automatically as self-report [38]. Arbitrary remapping can exclude a fixed cue-to-motor policy, but it does not exclude a flexible first-order controller that accepts a new instruction f and computes E=f(Z). Such a controller may generalize across stimuli, reward schedules, and motor positions while lacking metarepresentation.

Accordingly, the E profile is interpreted as flexible convention-governed indication. It is not an assay for explicit awareness. If a study seeks a stronger metacognitive conclusion, it must add independent tests of second-order representation, source attribution, or self-ascription.

Predictive-state methods constrain latent alignment

Predictive equivalence permits comparison without assuming that neural units, hidden coordinates, gestures, or words remain identical. This is useful across infant development, clinical recovery, and model checkpoints.

The limitation is equally important. Similar endpoint response laws can be produced by:

  • persistence of the same capacity;
  • gradual transformation of an inherited capacity;
  • destruction followed by independent recreation; or
  • two systems driven by the same external source.

Predictive matching alone cannot discriminate these histories. Longitudinal ancestry requires additional variables, intermediate stages, and perturbations.

Causal mediation constrains route claims

A content-correlated output does not show that the content governed the output through the claimed route. The present model therefore distinguishes:

  • a hypothetical controlled edge-intervention channel;
  • a channel directly implemented by experimental or software intervention;
  • a channel identified from observed data under graph-specific assumptions; and
  • a separately randomized upstream–downstream product.

These are not interchangeable. The shared route-isolation draft specifically warns that a randomized mediation product need not equal the same-run route-isolated channel [1]. No universal edge g-formula is claimed here.

AI interpretability expands intervention access but not construct validity

Artificial systems may permit activation patching, component ablation, route gating, checkpoint comparison, and controlled interface replacement. These capabilities improve causal access. They do not guarantee that an intervened hidden value is in distribution, semantically valid, or part of a stable agent-level content.

White-box access is especially useful for lineage. A component can sometimes be tagged, frozen, replaced, or perturbed before further training, and the downstream effects can be measured at later checkpoints. Even then, causal descent is not metaphysical identity; it is evidence against an independently recreated-capacity null.

Embodiment and boundary sensitivity

The distinction between minimally embodied but still neurocentric approaches and more radical organism–environment accounts is explicit in the supplied embodiment record [15]. The further requirement to audit candidate boundaries is an inference of this manuscript, not a finding established by that record.

The AI-generated Centered Closure Theory emphasizes intervention, candidate boundaries, and the absence of a structure-derived natural center [26]. The AI-generated Multiscale Reflexive Causal Geometry draft separates empirical mechanism, phenomenal bridge, and metaphysical interpretation while emphasizing multiscale inverse problems [27]. This paper adopts neither working theory. It uses them only to reinforce two methodological cautions: boundary choice must be reported, and empirical mechanism should not be conflated with a phenomenal bridge.

  • availability of a usable effector or interface;
  • exposure to the response convention;
  • evidence that task demands were within competence;
  • comparable motivation and opportunity;
  • an accessible recipient and communication setting for G;
  • measurement sensitivity sufficient to detect the registered effect; and
  • a temporal window capable of capturing the response.

If Oρ0​X​=0, a later positive result is classified as first detected use under a new opportunity. It is not a rank-1-to-rank-greater-than-1 route opening.

To claim the first observed opening of any report-like route, all registered E and G opportunities at earlier sampled stages must be assessed. Otherwise the claim is restricted to the selected route and interface.

9. Output-role profiles

9.1 Convention-governed indication profile for E

A high-specificity E design should include:

  1. Novel remapping. After the candidate contrast is established, the mapping from content values to response forms is changed according to a newly supplied diagnostic convention.
  2. Transfer. Indication remains content-sensitive under preregistered changes in exemplars, response location, motor implementation, reward scale, or irrelevant context.
  3. Motor and reward balance. Alternative responses are matched or modeled for motor cost and expected reward.
  4. Current-state route isolation. The remapped output depends on the current candidate state rather than only on the instruction, external cue, stale log, or prompt history.

Let PE​ denote success on this registered profile.

This profile excludes some fixed cue-response controllers. It does not exclude a general instruction-conditioned first-order classifier. Consider a controller with I=Z and, after receiving a new bijection f, E=f(Z). It can pass remapping and transfer without representing Z as its own state or monitoring its own cognition. The warranted conclusion is flexible convention-governed indication.

Remapping is not necessary for every genuine indication. An established but inflexible convention may be communicatively adequate. Such a system can fail PE​, so the operational criterion is deliberately conservative and is not a definition of indication in ordinary language.

9.2 Recipient-directed functional-signaling profile for G

Let KR​ denote recipient information, HR​ recipient goal, and BR​ recipient behavior. A high-specificity G design includes:

  1. Recipient-information sensitivity. Holding relevant sender state, visibility, reward, and motor factors controlled, signaling differs when the recipient lacks versus possesses the relevant information.
  2. Causal uptake. Blocking, substituting, or modifying the signal changes recipient behavior in a content-contingent way.
  3. Changed-goal use. The same indicated contrast can guide different recipient actions under different HR​, reducing the adequacy of a fixed command interpretation.
  4. Repair with matched controls. Following blocked or misunderstood transmission, the sender modifies, repeats, redirects, or switches the signal selectively. Nonreward, delay, frustration, and generalized arousal are separately controlled.

Let PG​ denote success on this profile.

This conjunction is a proposed conservative assay, not a validated necessary or sufficient condition for communication across infants, animals, and artificial systems. Audience-cue learning, acknowledgment policies, and reinforcement after failed coordination can mimic parts of it. Conversely, genuine communication may occur without an observable repair opportunity or with a stable convention that does not generalize across arbitrary goals.

10. Endpoint transport compatibility

Let Oρ−​ contain registered nonreport outcomes used for cross-stage alignment, such as internal measurements, instrumental consequences, or information-seeking outcomes. It excludes the E or G route whose opening is under test.

For a shared functional probe u∈U01​, define a preregistered role map

aρu​:Oρ−​→Yu​

into a common consequence space. Different effectors may therefore be matched by selecting the same location, avoiding the same object, or obtaining the same information.

Define the endpoint signature

Φρ​(z,u)=L[aρu​(Oρ,+−​)∣do(Zρ​=z),u].

Let

T:Cρ0​∗​⇀Cρ1​∗​

be a registered partial injective map with

∣dom(T)∣≥2.

All early witnesses and route ranks associated with T are restricted to dom(T) and its image. A full k-valued continuity claim requires T to cover the full registered contrast.

For model M, define response drift

ΔR​(T;M)=z∈dom(T)u∈U01​​max​TV[Φρ0​M​(z,u),Φρ1​M​(T(z),u)].

The map is endpoint-compatible when

ΔR​(T;M)≤δR​

for the independently justified drift bound.

A within-stage discrimination geometry may be reported:

Dρ​(z,z′)=u∈U01​∑​wu​TV[Φρ​(z,u),Φρ​(z′,u)].

However, if every matched endpoint response is within δR​, the triangle inequality already gives

The geometry condition adds independent information only if it uses additional registered features or a tighter bound δD​<2δR​. It is therefore treated as a diagnostic rather than automatically as a second independent premise.

Let Mstr​ be the nonempty set of population structural models consistent with the design and population intervention laws. A fixed map T is robustly endpoint-compatible only if

∀M∈Mstr​,ΔR​(T;M)≤δR​.

The mere existence of a favorable map in one admissible model is insufficient. If different admissible models or matchings connect different values, the result is set-valued.

Endpoint compatibility supports the conclusion:

The later contrast has an earlier functional predecessor compatible with the registered probes and drift bounds.

It does not support:

The earlier capacity persisted or was routed into the later output.

A system can destroy the early capacity and construct a role-isomorphic replacement.

11. Longitudinal lineage

A stronger continuation claim requires a longitudinal SCM over intermediate stages

ρ0​=σ0​<σ1​<⋯<σm​=ρ1​.

Let Kσj​​ denote a registered state or mechanism carrying the candidate capacity at stage σj​. A model satisfies LineageT​ only if:

  1. Structural ancestry: each Kσj+1​​ is a causal descendant of Kσj​​ through a registered inherited-mechanism path after external source variables are separately represented;
  2. Intermediate compatibility: the matched contrast remains detectable under registered probes at enough intermediate stages to exclude an unobserved disappearance-and-reappearance model within the claimed resolution;
  3. Lineage perturbation: at least one intervention on an earlier mechanism, tag, parameter, or state changes a later matched signature in a predicted content-specific way while external task inputs are held fixed or re-randomized; and
  4. Reset-null exclusion: every admissible longitudinal model excludes a reset event after which the later capacity is independent of all registered earlier lineage variables and is recreated solely by a new mechanism with the same endpoint role.

Define robust lineage support by

∀M∈Mlong​,LineageT​(M)=1,

where Mlong​ is the nonempty set of admissible longitudinal models.

These requirements are intentionally demanding. Intermediate endpoint sampling alone may still miss destruction and recreation between samples. Structural ancestry is consequently a model-relative causal claim, not numerical identity of a representation and not metaphysical identity of a subject or content.

Where lineage cannot be tested, the conclusion must remain predecessor-compatible.

12. Operational opening criteria

For a registered pair

κρ0​​=(zaρ0​​,zbρ0​​)

and its mapped later pair

T(κρ0​​)=(T(zaρ0​​),T(zbρ0​​)),

define a predecessor-compatible opening for X∈{E,G} by

PCOX,T​=​ModelOK∧Oρ0​X​∧Oρ1​X​∧W0​(κρ0​​)∧CompatT​∧[rXε0​​(ρ0​,κρ0​​)=1]∧[rXε1​​(ρ1​,T(κρ0​​))>1]∧PX​.​

Here:

  • ModelOK requires nonempty model sets and successful model checks;
  • all rank statements are robust over registered baselines, contexts, structural models, and sampling uncertainty;
  • PX​=PE​ for convention-governed indication and PX​=PG​ for recipient-directed functional signaling.

The conclusion is operational:

The same registered content distinction had an earlier nonreport witness and an endpoint-compatible predecessor, was not detectably routed through the selected indicating channel at the earlier comparable stage, and governed that channel beyond tolerance at the later stage while passing the registered output-role profile.

This is not an identification of persistence.

A lineage-supported opening is

LSOX,T​=PCOX,T​∧LineageT​.

Only LSO licenses the stronger wording:

The evidence supports opening of an indication or signaling route from a causally inherited earlier capacity, relative to the registered longitudinal model class.

Neither criterion is claimed to be necessary or sufficient for report in ordinary language. The rank-1-to-greater-than-1 condition identifies only this stipulated route-opening pattern. A weak content-sensitive route can exist early and later become more reliable, conventional, social, or transferable without satisfying PCO.

13. Endogenous-state indication and self-ascription

Let Sρ​ be an endogenous state within boundary Bρ​. A later E or G output qualifies as an endogenous-state indication only if:

  1. the registered contrast concerns Sρ​, not merely an external cue;
  2. interventions or natural dissociations vary Sρ​ while relevant external causes are held fixed, or vary those causes while Sρ​ is held sufficiently stable;
  3. the output follows Sρ​ through the route-isolated channel; and
  4. direct cue-to-output, consequence-to-output, prompt-to-output, and stale-log routes are excluded or bounded.

Formally,

ESIX,T​=PCOX,T​∧EndogenousDissociation(Sρ​;Bρ​).

This establishes a functional endogenous-state readout. It does not show that the output is about the state in a self-ascriptive sense.

A self-ascriptive claim would require an additional second-order target, such as a representation that distinguishes:

  • “state s obtains in me” from “state s occurs externally or in another agent”;
  • current state from copied specification;
  • source attribution from mere first-order discrimination.

No population-general criterion for that property is defended here. The paper therefore avoids using “self-report” as the default formal label.

15. Splitting and merging

A partial injection is suitable only for one-to-one matched distinctions. Developmental categories may split or merge.

For splitting, an earlier value may correspond to several later values. The strongest defensible claim may concern a common quotient of the later categories.

For merging, several earlier values may map to one later value. The later system then preserves only a coarsening of the earlier contrast.

Such cases require a registered relation, stochastic coupling, quotient map, or refinement map rather than an arbitrarily selected descendant. Route ranks must be calculated on the common transported quotient. Full persistence of the original k-valued content is not identified when distinctions are lost or newly introduced.

17. Identification and estimation

Population-level structural identification

Let P denote the population interventional laws available under the registered design, and let

Mstr​(P)

be the set of structural causal models consistent with those laws and substantive restrictions. Model checking precedes rank analysis. If

Mstr​(P)=∅,

the assumptions and data are incompatible; the opening verdict is undefined rather than vacuously true.

A route verdict is point-identified only if all admissible structural models agree. For example, for an early route:

M∈Mstr​sup​λ(Sρ0​XM​)≤ε0​,

and for a later route:

M∈Mstr​inf​λ(Sρ1​XM​)>ε1​.

Transport is point-identified only for a fixed registered matching T supported across all admissible models. If each model contains some matching but no matching is common, continuity remains set-valued.

Finite-sample uncertainty

Sampling uncertainty is distinct from structural underidentification. Under direct randomized route interventions, each row of S can be estimated as a multinomial distribution over the finite output alphabet. A preregistered method should construct simultaneous confidence regions

SρX,1−α​

for all channel rows, baselines, contexts, and tested mappings.

A conservative opening decision requires

S∈Sρ0​X,1−α​sup​λ(S)≤ε0​

and

S∈Sρ1​X,1−α​inf​λ(S)>ε1​.

The rank-1 test can be solved as a linear or convex feasibility problem because it asks whether a common output distribution lies within the declared total-variation radius of every row. Estimating higher approximate nonnegative rank is harder and is not required for the opening verdict.

The analysis must also report sensitivity to:

  • ερ​;
  • output discretization;
  • baseline z0​;
  • response window;
  • policy coverage;
  • candidate boundary;
  • role map;
  • multiple tested contrasts; and
  • adaptive selection of a favorable transport map.

Tolerances should be tied to registered measurement-error and substantive-effect models. If stage-specific technology requires different tolerances, the analysis must show that the transition is not an artifact of choosing a looser early threshold and a stricter later threshold.

Alternatives, Counterarguments, and Boundary Cases

A flexible first-order controller can pass the E profile

This objection is correct and changes the interpretation of the assay. Novel remapping and transfer do not distinguish all first-order control from report. A general controller can receive an instruction, classify the current state, and emit the mapped output without representing the state as its own or reflecting on it.

The revised paper does not classify that result as metacognitive self-report. It classifies it as convention-governed indication. Researchers who reserve “report” for metarepresentation should use the narrower formal term and reject the umbrella label. This is a terminological and theoretical disagreement that the assay does not resolve.

The G profile can be learned without communicative intention

Recipient-information sensitivity, goal-dependent uptake, and repair can be implemented by reinforcement learning over audience cues and coordination outcomes. The profile supports functional information transmission under the tested interventions. It does not prove intention, shared meaning, common ground, or reflective awareness.

Conversely, a competent communicator need not repair on every occasion, and an established signal may be inflexible. The conjunction is a conservative operational profile, not a universal criterion for communication.

Endpoint similarity does not establish persistence

An early system can be destroyed and a later system independently constructed with the same intervention-response signatures. No endpoint-only choice of role map, discrimination metric, or drift bound can exclude that model when the endpoint laws are identical.

The author accepts this objection. Endpoint matching is retained because it is useful for identifying predecessor compatibility across changing bodies and interfaces, but all persistence language is reserved for separately modeled longitudinal ancestry. Where such evidence is absent, the manuscript deliberately leaves continuity unresolved.

A route may exist early without a rank-1-to-greater-than-1 opening

An early channel may already be weakly content-sensitive and later become more reliable, conventional, recipient-directed, or transferable. That is a genuine developmental transition but not the operational route opening defined here.

The framework therefore distinguishes:

  • route opening;
  • reliability increase;
  • convention acquisition;
  • social-pragmatic validation; and
  • surface recoding.

The operational criterion is not complete for every natural-language use of “report emergence.”

No comparable early opportunity

If the later interface, recipient, motor ability, or task instruction did not exist earlier, a rank transition can be generated by the measurement opportunity. The response-opportunity requirement prevents interpreting such a result as absence followed by opening.

This requirement may produce false negatives. That is preferable to treating inability to respond as evidence that a route did not exist.

Mixed imperative and declarative roles

A sender may indicate an object’s location because it wants the recipient to retrieve the object. Goal-directedness does not by itself make the signal noninformative. The changed-goal test asks whether the communicated distinction remains usable under more than one recipient policy.

If the signal’s only stable causal role is “perform this action,” the conservative classification is imperative coordination. If the same indicated state informs multiple recipient actions, the evidence for factual signaling is stronger. Mixed-role coding should be retained rather than forcing a binary label.

Developmental splitting, merging, and reformatting

An early graded distinction may become discretized; an early broad category may split; or several distinctions may merge. A one-to-one map cannot represent all such changes. The common transported object may be only a quotient or coarse contrast.

When multiple correspondences remain admissible, the correct output is a set of possible mappings. “Reformatting” should not function as a residual explanation for every failed match.

Temporal thickness

A narrow cut can miss a genuine delayed route; a broad window can include reconstruction after the target state has disappeared. Both errors are possible.

Analyses should compare a preregistered family of independently defensible windows. If the verdict changes, temporal sensitivity is part of the result. Expanding the window post hoc until a route appears invalidates the opening claim.

Baseline dependence and interaction

Sending a baseline value through competing routes can alter the designated route’s downstream effect. Different baselines may therefore yield different ranks.

There is no generally neutral baseline. Studies should either justify one scientifically, require robustness across a registered set, or report a baseline-indexed result. A favorable baseline selected after analysis is insufficient.

Route incompleteness

A rank-1 selected route does not imply that no content-sensitive output route exists. Distributed and redundant systems may contain parallel paths. Route completeness is especially difficult in recurrent neural systems, embodied agents, and large artificial networks.

The claim must therefore match the intervention: “no effect through this registered route” is weaker than “no report route.”

Motor and motivational false negatives

Indication can fail because of immature motor control, fatigue, arousal, low reward, recipient unfamiliarity, social avoidance, paralysis, or an inaccessible modality. Such failures weaken negative evidence about E and G, and they provide little evidence against the underlying content or phenomenal consciousness.

Clinical reconstruction and amnesia

A later report about an earlier event may be generated by reconstruction after recovery. Conversely, a conscious event might not be reported because memory encoding failed. State variables for arousal, pharmacology, encoding, retention, and motor access cannot all be treated as removable nuisances; some constitute the process under study.

Clinical applications should distinguish:

  • online encoding;
  • preserved content;
  • later reconstruction;
  • memory availability; and
  • current response access.

Collective and boundary-relative agents

A sender–recipient pair, organism with tools, or distributed software service can be partitioned in several ways. A G signal under one boundary may be an internal message under a larger boundary.

All conclusions are boundary-indexed:

PCOX,TBρ​​,LSOX,TBρ​​.

Robustness across defensible boundaries strengthens a result but does not derive a uniquely privileged subject.

Report without phenomenality and phenomenality without report

The framework preserves both logical possibilities:

Functional indication⇒Phenomenality,

and

Phenomenality⇒Functional indication.

A sophisticated artificial controller may satisfy the entire functional assay while remaining nonphenomenal under a nonfunctionalist theory. An infant, animal, or patient may be phenomenal while lacking the motor, social, mnemonic, or conventional resources required by the assay.

Empirical Implications and Falsification Criteria

Preregistration requirements

A study applying the framework should register:

  1. candidate agent boundary;
  2. sampled stages and onset interval;
  3. candidate endogenous state and source manipulation;
  4. content subset and exact contrast pairs;
  5. raw-event coding and multilabel role rules;
  6. finite output alphabets;
  7. response opportunities at every stage;
  8. time-expanded causal graph;
  9. route cut, competing paths, and baselines;
  10. context variables and their causal roles;
  11. direct, identified, or hypothetical status of each intervention;
  12. stage-specific response windows;
  13. rank tolerance and measurement-error model;
  14. E or G profile tests;
  15. endpoint role maps and drift bounds;
  16. longitudinal lineage variables, if persistence is claimed;
  17. null-controller benchmark suite;
  18. structural model checks;
  19. finite-sample confidence procedure; and
  20. separate conclusions for content, action access, indication, signaling, lineage, self-ascription, phenomenality, agency, and welfare.

Null-controller benchmark suite

At minimum, construct validity should be evaluated against:

  • fixed cue-response mappings;
  • instruction-conditioned general classifiers;
  • stale-state and stale-log controllers;
  • common-cue systems;
  • learned audience-cue policies;
  • reward-omission repetition policies;
  • fixed imperative coordinators;
  • role-isomorphic destruction-and-recreation models;
  • hidden parallel routes;
  • post-event reconstruction;
  • finite-transcript mimics; and
  • systems tested without an early comparable opportunity.

The profile should not be advertised as excluding a null class that was not explicitly modeled and benchmarked.

Infant studies

A strong developmental design would:

  1. identify a content contrast through internal or instrumental behavior;
  2. establish a usable response opportunity at both stages;
  3. align early and late outcomes by functional role rather than movement identity;
  4. test whether the same contrast newly controls an E or G route;
  5. manipulate recipient information and goals for G;
  6. distinguish misunderstanding from nonreward in repair trials; and
  7. treat onset as interval-censored.

Longitudinal work on visual checking and vocalization associated with pointing motivates stage-sensitive timing but does not itself establish a report route [31].

Ethical and practical limits may prevent direct state or route interventions. In that case, endpoint compatibility, lineage, or route opening may remain unidentified. A partial result is preferable to an overstrong interpretation of gesture onset.

Nonhuman-animal studies

Opt-out and information-seeking outputs should initially be coded as I. A stronger endogenous-state indication design would vary internal uncertainty independently of objective difficulty, introduce response mappings not reducible to familiar motor sequences, and test transfer across reward and stimulus changes.

Passing such an E design would establish flexible indication of the candidate state. It would not settle whether the animal explicitly represents uncertainty or is aware of its metacognitive process, questions already recognized in comparative debate [38].

Clinical studies

Clinical application requires:

  • stage-specific pharmacologic and arousal variables;
  • measurement models robust to changing gain;
  • controls for residual motor activity;
  • explicit encoding-versus-reconstruction contrasts;
  • memory-retention assessment;
  • multiple response modalities;
  • conservative route and timing claims; and
  • no inference from internal decoding directly to consciousness.

An early N distinction can support a covert-capacity hypothesis. A later reach is an I transition; a trained answer is an E candidate; a recipient-sensitive spontaneous signal is a G candidate. These should not be pooled as generic recovery.

Artificial-agent studies

Artificial systems permit a comparatively strong design:

  1. establish a nonlinguistic hidden-state or instrumental contrast before self-descriptive training;
  2. define the content through interventions and nonreport consequences, not hidden-unit labels;
  3. register a finite icon, button, tool, or inter-agent message alphabet;
  4. compare direct route gating with separately randomized mediation;
  5. introduce novel mappings and context transfer;
  6. manipulate recipient knowledge and goals in multi-agent settings;
  7. tag, freeze, perturb, or replace candidate mechanisms before later training to test lineage;
  8. include an independently recreated role-isomorphic checkpoint as a null;
  9. test stale logging and post-action reconstruction; and
  10. keep observer attribution separate from route evidence.

Training-stage task trajectories can suggest candidate orderings, but linguistic performance alone does not identify the route [13]. Consciousness-inspired architecture and theory-derived indicators remain distinct evidence classes [4] [9].

Conditional invariance tests

Two invariances follow directly for the finite channel rank:

  • row permutation invariance;
  • column permutation invariance.

Other desired invariances are conditional, not proved generally. A verdict should remain stable under:

  • invertible latent relabeling, if interventions and candidate states are correspondingly transformed;
  • output recoding, if the coding is bijective;
  • motor morphology change, if the role map preserves the relevant intervention-response law;
  • temporal rescaling, if windows and dynamics transform compatibly; and
  • measurement-coordinate replacement, if the new measurement is interventionally equivalent.

Failure under a mere latent relabeling or bijective output recoding is a formal defect. Failure under morphology or temporal change may instead reveal that the proposed role maps were inadequate.

Falsification and construct failure

Because PCO and LSO are operational definitions, the definitions themselves are not empirical theorems to be falsified. Their intended construct interpretation is vulnerable in the following ways.

The typed-assay hypothesis is undermined if:

  • fixed cue, stale-state, or content-disconnected controllers pass at rates comparable to target systems;
  • role typing adds no discriminant validity beyond ordinary accuracy;
  • recipient-information effects disappear when visible audience cues are controlled;
  • apparent repair is fully explained by nonreward or generalized arousal;
  • route verdicts depend arbitrarily on output coding, baseline, or window;
  • the same validated communicative system receives incompatible classifications under behaviorally equivalent realizations; or
  • established communication is systematically rejected for irrelevant surface reasons.

A metacognitive interpretation of E is falsified by construction if instruction-conditioned first-order classifiers pass the same tests. The present paper accepts that result and restricts the conclusion to convention-governed indication.

A persistence interpretation is refuted within a model class if a reset-and-recreation model satisfies all endpoint conditions and remains observationally and interventionally admissible. In that case only predecessor compatibility is identified.

A lineage-supported claim is refuted if perturbing, removing, or tagging the proposed earlier capacity has no later content-specific consequence while an independent replacement model explains the data equally well.

A supported-genesis claim fails if any admissible model retains an early predecessor or if later formation is inferred merely from failure of endpoint matching.

Warranted conclusion for artificial systems

If an artificial agent satisfies PCOE​, the warranted conclusion is:

A registered content contrast had an earlier nonreport functional predecessor and later acquired beyond-tolerance control of a convention-governed indication route under the tested baselines, contexts, and response opportunities.

If it also satisfies LineageT​, the conclusion can add:

The route opening is supported as descending from the registered earlier capacity rather than from an independently recreated role-isomorphic capacity, relative to the longitudinal model class.

If the content is an independently dissociated internal state, the result supports endogenous-state indication. None of these conclusions establishes self-ascription or phenomenal consciousness. Perceived consciousness remains an observer outcome [8], architectural inspiration remains architecture [4], and biological-substrate objections remain independent bridge claims [10].

Limitations

First, the framework is intervention-intensive. Direct manipulation of perceptual, memorial, affective, or uncertainty states may be infeasible or unethical in infants, animals, and patients. Artificial-state interventions can be out of distribution and may create artifacts rather than reveal ordinary processing.

Second, candidate contents are theory-laden. Intervention-response stability does not establish the semantic interpretation assigned by the investigator. Misrepresentation, low-level feature encoding, and task-specific codes remain alternatives unless separately tested.

Third, the output-role profiles lack direct validation across the target populations. The supplied developmental sources identify relevant topics but do not establish arbitrary remapping, recipient-knowledge sensitivity, changed-goal uptake, and repair as necessary or sufficient criteria. The profiles are hypotheses for benchmark testing.

Fourth, the E profile cannot exclude a flexible instruction-conditioned first-order classifier. This is not hidden by an adequacy assumption. The formal result is therefore indication, not explicit metacognition.

Fifth, the G profile identifies functional information transmission at most. It does not establish intention, common ground, declarative semantics, or awareness. Sophisticated learned policies may pass it.

Sixth, comparable opportunity is difficult to establish developmentally. Motor, social, attentional, and conventional competence change together. When opportunity is doubtful, the framework returns first detected use or nonidentification rather than an opening.

Seventh, route isolation may be infeasible or incomplete. Recurrent interactions, recanting structures, redundant paths, and distributed embodiment can prevent a coherent edge cut. A separately randomized mediation product is not automatically a substitute.

Eighth, baseline dependence is substantive. Interactions among routes can change the rank, and no universally neutral baseline exists.

Ninth, finite output coding discards information. Results can depend on trajectory segmentation, temporal binning, gesture classification, or communication-token coding. Sensitivity analysis cannot guarantee that a discretization captures the ecologically relevant distinction.

Tenth, exact predictive partitions are population objects relative to a finite registered policy family. Empirical work does not assess every possible policy. The partition lattice is therefore a conceptual diagnostic, not a fully developed estimator.

Eleventh, approximate rank can be unstable near the tolerance boundary. The rank-1 decision is computationally manageable, but finite-sample conclusions depend on simultaneous confidence regions, multiplicity control, and prespecified tolerances.

Twelfth, endpoint transport is not persistence. Even rich endpoint signatures cannot distinguish continuation from independent recreation when the endpoint laws are identical. The stronger lineage criterion requires structural assumptions and interventions that may be available only in artificial systems or unusually dense longitudinal designs.

Thirteenth, lineage itself is model-relative. Causal ancestry does not settle numerical identity of representations, persistence of a subject, or philosophical identity of a content. Sparse intermediate sampling can miss destruction and recreation between observations.

Fourteenth, splitting and merging require quotient or correspondence models. A partial injection cannot represent all developmental refinement and coarsening.

Fifteenth, the framework can produce false negatives. Genuine indication may use an established inflexible convention, lack a repair opportunity, operate on an untested time scale, or occur through an inaccessible modality.

Sixteenth, infant development, phylogeny, clinical recovery, reinforcement learning, pretraining, and post-training are not homologous processes. Role-level comparison supports methodological transfer, not biological or psychological equivalence.

Seventeenth, the clinical discussion is prospective and evidentially thin. No supplied peer-reviewed clinical study tests the proposal. Pharmacology, arousal, memory, motor control, and reconstruction require domain-specific models before application.

Eighteenth, agent boundaries are not derived. A signal can change type when the candidate system is enlarged or divided. Boundary-indexed reporting exposes rather than solves this problem.

Finally, the framework supplies no validated bridge from functional indication, signaling, endogenous-state readout, or causal lineage to phenomenal consciousness. It also does not derive perceived consciousness, robust agency, welfare capacity, or moral status.

Author response to unresolved disagreements

The central formal objections are accepted: the previous if-and-only-if proposition was partly stipulative, its adequacy assumptions were circular, its transport relation did not identify persistence, and its early and late effects could concern different distinctions. The revision therefore replaces the proposition with operational outcomes, requires the same registered contrast throughout, separates endpoint compatibility from lineage, and makes null classes explicit.

A residual terminological disagreement remains. Some researchers use “report” broadly for flexible convention-governed indication; others reserve it for metacognitive or self-ascriptive representation. The manuscript does not resolve that dispute by definition. It keeps “functional report” only as an umbrella in explanatory prose and uses E, G, and endogenous-state indication for formal conclusions. Readers adopting the stronger terminology should not treat PCOE​ as report.

Conclusion

The first word, gesture, button press, gaze choice, or introspective-looking sentence does not by itself identify the emergence of report. Comparative and developmental cognition shows why: content discrimination, instrumental access, response opportunity, convention acquisition, social signaling, and language can develop on different trajectories. Changing bodies, effectors, temporal scales, recipients, and interfaces make token continuity unreliable, but they also make every successful nonverbal action an unsafe proxy for report.

The revised framework separates four output roles: internal measurement, instrumental action, convention-governed indication, and recipient-directed signaling. Its predictive partitions organize non-nested capacities but do not identify causal routes. Route-isolated finite-output channels ask whether a specific candidate distinction governs an output through a designated edge cut. Approximate rank is evaluated on the same transported pair at both stages, with explicit baselines, finite coding, comparable opportunity, and sampling uncertainty.

Cross-stage endpoint matching has a deliberately limited interpretation. It can identify a transport-compatible predecessor under registered probes and drift bounds. It cannot distinguish persistence from destruction and independent recreation. A stronger continuation claim requires longitudinal causal ancestry, intermediate compatibility, lineage perturbation, and exclusion of a reset-recreation null.

The resulting operational distinction is between a predecessor-compatible opening and a lineage-supported opening. For E, remapping and transfer establish flexible convention-governed indication but remain compatible with a general first-order classifier. For G, recipient-information sensitivity, changed-goal uptake, and repair support functional signaling but not communicative intention or phenomenality. When no endpoint match is found, the default verdict is nonidentification; later content formation requires independent all-model evidence.

For artificial systems, passing these conditions would be stronger than fluent self-description, checkpoint accuracy, perceived consciousness, or consciousness-inspired architecture. For infants, animals, and patients, the framework would localize which link is supported and which remains inaccessible. In every population, the inferential boundary is the same: content-sensitive indication is not self-ascription, report is not phenomenal consciousness, perceived consciousness is not detection, agency is not experience, and moral precaution is not empirical proof.
