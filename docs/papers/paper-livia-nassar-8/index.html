Abstract

This paper develops an intervention-relative profile of causal organization in recurrent generative agents without identifying recurrence, integration, shielding, or output influence with consciousness. A finite controlled dynamic structural causal model is evaluated at a specified grain, candidate subsystem, horizon, mechanism regime, intervention baseline, and output catalogue. Its profile contains operational inbound leakage, leakage over a broader robustness family, their shielding gap, conditional-product irreducibility, finite-horizon reciprocal causal reach, and an output-specific vector of effects on prediction, action, report, value, or evaluative variables.

Operational contexts preserve the enacted policy and do not override its action channel. Robustness contexts form a declared superset that may include alternative policies or separately typed action-clamp interventions. This distinction makes the shielding gap nonnegative and permits active cancellation to produce zero operational leakage while a separate zero-action policy exposes maximal susceptibility. A large gap is nevertheless only compatible with active cancellation; it does not identify that mechanism.

For one-step candidate kernels controlled uniformly over the robustness family, the Dobrushin perturbation recursion gives

LHrob​(S)≤min{1,ϵ1−α1−αH​},0≤α<1.

The bound is tight when 0≤ϵ≤1−α. Full-trajectory leakage generally obeys only 1−(1−ϵ)H, and arbitrarily small one-step differences can accumulate to order one without strict contraction. These are oracle-level sufficient conditions covering all admitted policies, interventions, histories, and off-support rows, not quantities recoverable from ordinary behavior without additional coverage assumptions.

Reciprocal causal reach is defined through directly measured multistep total effects under one fixed regime, one common baseline, and an exterior-hold protocol. It therefore avoids concatenating edges whose effects occur only in incompatible contexts. It remains a family of separate counterfactual tests rather than a certificate that one unperturbed trajectory circulates through every coordinate. Conditional-product irreducibility is likewise one explicit approximation problem, not a unique physical or phenomenal notion of integration.

An input-output-equivalent XOR construction proves that nontrivial island membership is not identifiable from behavior on an unrestricted model class. A finite-field construction shows that even minimal controllable and observable realizations do not determine coordinate-relative intervention profiles when physical targets are unlabeled. Conversely, an exact target-labelled kernel oracle functionally determines the profile up to target-preserving causal automorphism. This is an invariance result conditional on already having the required oracle, not a theorem that such access is experimentally recoverable.

The contribution is principally a joint organization of intervention-relative quantities, their quantifiers, stability conditions, countermodels, and empirical failure criteria. The profile remains a taxonomy of causal mechanisms, not a sufficient criterion for phenomenality.

Research Question and Hypothesis

Research question

Under what intervention-relative conditions does a recurrent generative agent contain a subsystem that is, within one declared mechanism regime:

  1. resistant to specified exterior perturbations;
  2. irreducible under a specified internal factorization test;
  3. reciprocally causally effective across its coordinates within a finite horizon; and
  4. consequential for separately designated prediction, action, report, value, or evaluative outputs?

Which parts of this description are determined by external behavior, and which require target-labelled mechanism interventions?

Scope and relation to prior work

Dobrushin methods derive global stability from local kernel sensitivity. Related work formulates Dobrushin uniqueness through Wasserstein distances for equilibrium Gibbs systems [18]. The result below is not an application of that equilibrium theorem and is not presented as a new general stability principle. It is the elementary time-inhomogeneous perturbation recursion specialized to the present context semantics.

Information-geometric integration measures compare intact models with disconnected alternatives. Causal information integration is relevant because it explicitly considers common exterior influence [11]. The present ΦH​ is instead a conditional-product approximation measure unless a stronger structural cutting operation is independently specified. It is not identified with CII, IIT, or phenomenality. IIT advances a consciousness-first ontology and an explanatory identity between intrinsic cause-effect structure and experience [34]; this manuscript does not assume that identity.

Theoretical proposals that phenomenal properties participate in discriminative or evaluative labelling motivate including evaluative variables among the output groups [2]. Positive influence on such a variable does not show that it is phenomenal or that the influence was selected for consciousness. Likewise, an engineered recurrent-agent preprint in which feedback lesions reduce persistence provides a mechanism illustration, not consciousness evidence [3].

The AI-generated Centered Closure Theory working draft treats approximate closure as a candidate substrate property and centering as an additional primitive [12]. The present framework sharpens one finite-horizon notion of shielding but neither validates that draft nor derives a first-person center. The AI-generated MRCG synthesis proposes multiscale causal atlases and counterfactual geometry [13]. The kernels defined here could contribute causal data to such an atlas, but successful causal gluing would not by itself yield phenomenal geometry.

The finite-state quantities below do not address the noncomputability result for complete lossless integration under an algorithmic-information formalization [9]. Conversely, finite state and finite horizon do not alone guarantee exact effective computation: policy and intervention families may be infinite, transition probabilities may be noncomputable, and cut optimization ranges over continuous kernel families. Effective approximation requires additional regularity and computability assumptions.

The proposed contribution is therefore modest: a context-compatible organization of distinct causal quantities, an explicit separation of operational from robustness-family shielding, a recurrence functional that does not stitch together incompatible edge witnesses, and a battery of countermodels and boundary conditions.

Grains, candidates, and macrointervention lifts

A grain is a surjection

A candidate S is a nonempty subset of the m macro-coordinates, with state space S and coordinate index set IS​.

A macrointervention

All variables not included in S—including other internal coordinates, environment variables, observations, actions, and policy memory not incorporated into S—are exterior relative to that candidate. An exterior protocol may intervene on those variables but not directly on S.

Analysis regimes and typed context families

A mechanism regime ξ specifies:

  • an assessment time or time phase;
  • the structural assignments and exogenous-noise laws held fixed;
  • a baseline policy and exterior context;
  • allowed prior-history laws or policy-memory settings;
  • the exterior-hold protocol used for candidate-contained causal tests;
  • operational and robustness context families;
  • output groups and their timing;
  • macrointervention lifts;
  • the reference distribution used by the irreducibility functional.

Suppressing ξ is permissible only when these objects are fixed and time homogeneous.

For horizon h, a context is a typed tuple

where π∈Π is a policy, u is a length-h exterior intervention protocol, and ν is an admitted prior-history law or policy-memory setting. An action clamp, when allowed, is part of u; it is not a policy.

A constant-zero policy is an element of Π. An action clamp is an element of the intervention family. They are different types even when they produce the same action sequence in a particular experiment.

Nestedness is a bookkeeping and comparison requirement, not a claim that the broader family is more biologically natural. Enlarging a context family can only increase its worst-case leakage.

Context-compatible reciprocal causal reach

Optimizing every graph edge over a different context can combine mutually incompatible witnesses. Reciprocal reach is therefore evaluated in one fixed regime ξ, under one common baseline

The regime supplies a candidate-contained exterior-hold protocol derived from the same baseline context. Under this protocol, exterior trajectories are fixed or randomized identically in the two compared interventions and are prevented from transmitting an effect out of S and back into it. If that condition cannot be implemented or justified, the result must be labelled whole-loop reach rather than internal reciprocal reach.

This definition does not claim that all pairwise effects occur simultaneously in one trajectory. It does not identify their mediating paths, measure memory duration, or show that an unperturbed trajectory literally returns to a previous state. It is stronger than strong connectivity in a union-of-witnesses graph and weaker than a path-specific sequential-return certificate.

Stability assumptions

For the endpoint theorem, every context c∈Crobξ​ must induce one-step kernels

Neuroscientific Integration

Evidential status

The mathematical results do not depend on neuroscience. Applying the profile to neural systems would require a declared variable grain, target-labelled perturbations, common-regime assumptions, output-specific readouts, intervention-validity models, and uncertainty estimates.

The stored sources provide only limited points of contact:

  1. An engineered recurrent-agent preprint reports that lesioning a designed feedback-and-integration component reduced post-stimulus persistence in recurrent variants [3]. This supports a recurrence-to-persistence interpretation in that engineered system, not a consciousness inference.
  2. An imaging study reports task-dependent connectivity of dorsal and ventral precuneal subdivisions during internally and externally oriented demands [22]. These connectivity findings can motivate candidate regimes, but they do not provide target-labelled intervention kernels or demonstrate a consciousness-specific causal loop.
  3. An efference-copy proposal connects discrimination of self-generated sensory consequences with a possible account of consciousness [31]. The phenomenal connection is theoretical; the source does not establish that efference copy generates experience.

No stored source establishes that a biological conscious system satisfies the complete profile.

Neural interpretation of the coordinates

A future neural analysis would estimate the coordinates separately:

  • Operational leakage: perturb variables outside a candidate while preserving the enacted control policy and avoiding direct action-channel override.
  • Robustness-family leakage: repeat the analysis under a preregistered broader family that may contain alternative control conditions or separately labelled action overrides.
  • Shielding gap: compare these leakage estimates. A gap is compatible with policy-dependent cancellation but requires mediation evidence before receiving that interpretation.
  • Conditional-product irreducibility: compare intact response paths with fitted cross-part product approximations. An actual lesion or disconnection defines a different cut family unless its induced kernel is shown to match the product convention.
  • Reciprocal causal reach: test multistep effects between candidate coordinates under one common regime and baseline while blocking exterior return paths. If exterior return cannot be blocked, report whole-loop reach instead.
  • Output reach: report separate values for prediction, action, report, value, and evaluation rather than aggregating them into one consciousness-related score.

A positive effect should require a confidence interval excluding a preregistered minimum effect size. Failure to reject zero is not evidence that an edge is absent. This is especially important because RH​ takes a hard minimum over ordered coordinate pairs.

Candidate boundaries

A preregistered catalogue may compare, without presupposing which boundary is correct:

  1. cortical recurrent circuits;
  2. cortico-thalamo-cortical candidates;
  3. larger sensorimotor loops;
  4. candidates containing declared modulatory or corollary-discharge variables;
  5. artificial recurrent modules with inspectable targets.

The perturbation semantics must be aligned. Enlarging a candidate changes which variables count as exterior, so leakage values for nested candidates are not automatically comparable.

An attempted peripheral immobilization should count as an action clamp only if it is shown to override the action variable represented in the model. A state manipulation should count as a policy change only if the remaining transition mechanisms are shown to be invariant. These are requirements on causal interpretation, not assertions that particular biological manipulations satisfy them.

Intervention validity and mechanism replacement

A manipulation may fail to instantiate its intended intervention if it:

  • changes multiple targets;
  • replaces a structural mechanism rather than setting a variable;
  • changes observation or exogenous-noise laws;
  • fails to block an exterior return path;
  • changes the effective grain;
  • induces a new regime not covered by the reference kernel.

The analysis should therefore model intervention validity rather than treating every stimulation, lesion, pharmacological manipulation, or software edit as a surgical do-operation.

Quantitative probing motivates testing fitted causal models against held-out quantitative interventions rather than accepting them solely because they fit observed associations [4]. For the present framework, intervention locations, amplitudes, temporal patterns, and baselines should be divided between model selection and validation.

What neural evidence could establish

Under adequate interventions, a neural study could establish that a selected subsystem is relatively shielded, nonfactorizable under a declared product model, reciprocally causally effective, and consequential for particular outputs. It could also compare these properties across experimentally defined regimes.

Such a study would not by itself establish that:

  • the selected grain is the unique subject boundary;
  • report or action effects are effects on phenomenality;
  • the mechanism is constitutive rather than enabling or consequential;
  • an observed state classification transparently measures experience.

Those conclusions require an independently stated bridge and measurement model.

Philosophical Reinterpretation

A profile of causal organization, not a subject criterion

A context-compatible causal island is not a sealed Cartesian subject. It is an intervention-relative profile with distinct coordinates for:

  1. operational resistance to exterior changes;
  2. resistance across a broader robustness family;
  3. failure of a specified conditional-product approximation;
  4. reciprocal finite-horizon causal effects;
  5. output-specific causal consequences.

The formalism blocks the following inferences:

LHop​≈0⇒LHrob​≈0,
ΦH​>0⇒LHrob​≈0,
RH​>0⇒ΓH,g​>0,
ΓH,report​>0⇒phenomenality,

and

nontrivial island profile⇒consciousness

unless an additional bridge premise is supplied.

The profile is non-scalar because its coordinates answer different causal questions. There is no formal basis for compensating high leakage with high irreducibility, replacing reciprocal reach with output influence, or treating a large shielding gap as direct evidence of autonomy.

Descriptive, evidential, and constitutive claims

Three different uses of the profile should be distinguished.

  1. Descriptive use: the profile summarizes how a selected mechanism responds to specified interventions.
  2. Evidential use: the profile predicts or correlates with an independently operationalized consciousness-related outcome.
  3. Constitutive use: some part of the profile is claimed to be identical with, or metaphysically sufficient for, experience.

The mathematical results establish only the first use. The second requires a measurement model and controlled empirical evidence. The third requires a philosophical bridge that is not supplied by the probability metrics or intervention semantics.

IIT’s explanatory identity between cause-effect structure and experience is an example of a constitutive bridge [34]. Computing ΦH​, or even the complete profile, neither proves nor refutes that identity. It provides a different and more explicitly context-relative causal description.

Shielding is not synonymous with enactive autonomy

The distinction between operational and robustness-family leakage is useful, but lower leakage across a broader context family should be called stronger shielding, not automatically stronger autonomy.

In an enactive interpretation, ongoing bodily and environmental exchange may be constitutive of organization rather than contamination of an otherwise autonomous core. A clamp that disables the organization maintaining the agent may cease to probe the same organized system. The formalism remains neutral between two interpretations:

  • active cancellation reveals that an apparently insulated candidate depends on a larger control loop;
  • active cancellation is itself part of the candidate’s autonomous organization.

Choosing between these interpretations requires an account of system identity under intervention. The mathematics does not decide whether robustness to a policy clamp is more autonomy, less autonomy, or a test of a different system.

First-person centering

The profile is uncentered third-person structure. It may distinguish candidate boundaries but cannot derive the token-indexical fact that one candidate is “me.”

The AI-generated CCT working draft introduces centering as a primitive and proposes a no-natural-centering result under categorical assumptions [12]. The present automorphism result is weaker. It says that labelled kernel profiles are invariant under certain relabellings. It neither proves a general impossibility of deriving centers from structure nor audits the categorical assumptions of that working draft.

A unique optimum in the island spectrum would not by itself solve centering. Conversely, the absence of a unique optimum would not prove that subjects do not exist. Both conclusions require further premises connecting causal boundaries to first-person subjecthood.

Causal geometry and phenomenal quality

The total-variation and Jensen–Shannon distances used here compare causal response distributions. They are not automatically distances between phenomenal qualities.

The AI-generated MRCG synthesis proposes intervention-defined local causal states and a counterfactual phenomenal geometry [13]. The present kernels could contribute local causal charts, and disagreement between overlapping charts could diagnose inconsistency among causal descriptions. Even perfect gluing would establish only a coherent causal geometry. Further assumptions would be required to infer:

  • that causal states correspond to qualities;
  • that causal distance corresponds to phenomenal similarity;
  • that automorphisms correspond to spectrum inversions;
  • that a causal atlas possesses a first-person center.

These are substantive philosophical bridges, not consequences of the kernel definitions.

Small systems and phenomenal sufficiency

A simple two-bit swap system considered below satisfies several strong structural conditions. Mathematics does not establish that this system is unconscious. It therefore does not refute every sufficiency theory.

The defensible conclusion is conditional:

A phenomenal bridge whose threshold region includes the swap system must either classify that system as conscious or add an independently justified exclusion criterion.

A theorist may accept the small-system consequence. Simplicity creates pressure on a bridge but does not supply an independent phenomenal diagnosis.

The unresolved measurement bridge

A necessity bridge can be stated in restricted form:

Within a preregistered biological catalogue of grains, regimes, baselines, horizons, interventions, output groups, and effect-size thresholds, episodes assigned high probability of a target conscious condition by an independently specified measurement model require at least one nontrivial context-compatible causal island.

This is falsifiable only jointly with the catalogue and measurement model. It does not become a direct test of phenomenality merely by separating report, action, prediction, value, and evaluation into distinct variables. Those distinctions prevent formal conflation, but they do not make the latent phenomenal target independently observable.

An unrestricted claim referring to an unspecified “appropriate grain,” “appropriate horizon,” or “appropriate output” cannot be rejected by a finite study because it permits post hoc relocation of the target. Scientific use therefore requires a restricted bridge with fixed adequacy conditions.

Finite-sample bounds for an exhaustive finite catalogue

Suppose an experiment directly queries a finite catalogue of M target-labelled conditional distributions. Each distribution has at most d outcomes and is estimated from N independent resets. Assume every distribution entering the reported maxima and minima is among these M rows.

Computational complexity

Finite state and finite horizon do not alone imply exact computability. Effective approximation is available when:

  • transition probabilities are computable;
  • policy and intervention families are finite or effectively compact;
  • relevant objectives have computable moduli of continuity;
  • a numerical tolerance is specified.

Even under these assumptions, candidate selection is combinatorial.

Restricted decision proposition. Given an undirected graph G=(V,E), construct one binary coordinate Zj per vertex and define

Zt+1j​=i∈N(j)⨁​Zti​,

where the XOR of an empty set is 0. Under fixed-baseline surgical interventions, changing Zti​ flips Zt+1j​ exactly when

For k≥2, consider the restricted decision problem

“Does there exist S⊆V, ∣S∣≥k, R1​(S)>0?”

A candidate has R1​>0 exactly when every distinct ordered pair has a direct effect, which holds exactly when its vertices form a clique. The structural equations have polynomial-size encoding, and a proposed subset can be checked in polynomial time. This restricted problem is therefore NP-complete by equivalence with CLIQUE. The corresponding general candidate-selection problem is NP-hard.

No broader fixed-parameter claim is made for arbitrary cut divergences, policy families, or long-horizon profiles.

Lemma: Conditional functional determination by a target-labelled kernel oracle

Let K(M) contain:

  1. every endpoint and trajectory kernel used by LHop​ and LHrob​;
  2. every candidate-contained surgical kernel used by RH​;
  3. every target-to-output kernel used by each ΓH,g​;
  4. every intact path kernel and cut-family semantic used by ΦH​;
  5. the relevant reference measures and baselines;
  6. labels for candidate coordinates, exterior variables, policies, intervention targets, output groups, regimes, horizons, and macrointervention lifts.

A target-preserving causal automorphism consists of state-space bijections and permitted coordinate permutations that:

  • preserve candidate, exterior, action, and output types;
  • preserve output-group labels;
  • map every labelled intervention target to its corresponding target;
  • conjugate all kernels in K;
  • preserve policy, context, lift, reference, and cut families.

It does not include arbitrary mixing of several physical coordinates into a new coordinate factorization.

Lemma. If two models have equal K objects after a target-preserving causal automorphism, their complete context-compatible profiles are equal after that automorphism.

Proof. Total variation is invariant under bijective relabelling, so equal endpoint kernels preserve both leakage coordinates and their difference. Equal target-labelled internal kernels preserve each τij(h)​ and hence RH​. Equal target-to-output kernels preserve every gi,g(h)​ and every ΓH,g​.

Kullback–Leibler and Jensen–Shannon divergences are also invariant under bijections. Because the automorphism maps each admissible partition, reference measure, and cut family to its counterpart, every cut objective, infimum, and minimum is preserved. □

This is a functional-determination lemma, not an experimental identification theorem. It assumes that every object needed to evaluate the profile has already been supplied with exact labels. It does not prove that latent variables, intervention targets, macrointervention lifts, off-support rows, or cut semantics can be recovered from feasible experiments.

Intervention-based causal representation learning similarly requires structural and intervention assumptions to recover latent causal variables [29]. In embodied agents, ordinary actions do not arrive pre-labelled as surgical interventions, so intervention semantics may themselves require learning or experimental justification [28].

The swap system as a structural null

The two-bit swap attains:

  • zero exterior leakage;
  • maximal reciprocal one-step total effects;
  • positive conditional-product irreducibility;
  • maximal influence on its designated XOR output.

It is therefore a stringent small-system null for a proposed phenomenal bridge.

The mathematics does not establish that the swap system is unconscious. It consequently does not prove that no profile value can be sufficient for consciousness. The valid conditional conclusion is:

Any sufficiency bridge whose threshold region includes the swap system must classify it as conscious or supply an independently justified exclusion criterion.

A theorist may accept that consequence. The formalism cannot refute the response merely by appealing to the system’s simplicity.

Empirical Boundary Conditions and Falsification Criteria

Mathematical falsification criteria

  1. Endpoint theorem. Theorem 1 is false if a model satisfies the stated uniform row-wise perturbation bound and
δ(Qkc​)≤α<1

across the entire declared context family, yet its endpoint leakage exceeds

min{1,ϵ1−α1−αH​}.

A path-distribution counterexample would not refute an endpoint theorem.

  1. Trajectory proposition. Proposition 1 is false if its conditional coupling premise holds while length-H trajectory leakage exceeds
1−(1−ϵ)H.
  1. One-step structural proposition. Proposition 2 is false if its fixed-regime, parent-completeness, full-support, causal-minimality, coordinate-update, and conditional-noise assumptions all hold but one of its stated one-step equivalences fails.
  1. Coarse leakage proposition. Proposition 3 is false if macrointerventions have the aligned physical exterior targets and shared lifts stipulated there, yet macro leakage exceeds micro leakage.
  1. Order-theoretic proposition. Proposition 4 is false if either displayed finite qualified subposet contains a qualifying join or meet, respectively.
  1. Behavioral no-go. The XOR result fails if the two displayed agents differ in exposed output for any admitted input sequence. A classifier using architecture, physical target labels, minimality, or interventions would not refute a behavior-only theorem.
  1. Kernel-oracle lemma. The functional-determination result fails if two models agree on every exact target-labelled object in K, modulo an allowed automorphism, yet yield different profile values.

  • candidate subsets;
  • horizons;
  • mechanism regimes;
  • common baselines or baseline sets;
  • operational and robustness context families;
  • macrointervention lifts;
  • cut families;
  • output groups;
  • minimum effect-size thresholds;
  • intervention-validity criteria;
  • model-selection and validation splits;
  • multiplicity correction.

Searching outside this catalogue may be useful for discovery but does not constitute a confirmatory test of the preregistered bridge.

A finite study can reject only a bridge restricted to its declared catalogue. It cannot reject a universal bridge that is permitted to move post hoc to an untested grain, horizon, context, intervention family, or output group.

Measurement model for consciousness-related outcomes

The profile does not directly observe phenomenality. An empirical bridge test therefore requires a causal measurement model separating at least:

  • the latent phenomenal target, if one is claimed;
  • arousal or global physiological state;
  • sensory availability;
  • task access and decision variables;
  • motor capacity;
  • report;
  • post hoc memory;
  • model fit and measurement quality;
  • the perturbational profile.

Let C∗ denote the proposed latent target, I the observed indicators, and

U={arousal, sensory access, task access, motor capacity, memory, data quality}.

A minimal schematic allows

U⟶PH​,U⟶I,C∗⟶I,

as well as effects of the measured mechanism on report, action, or memory indicators. A bridge separately posits a relation between C∗ and PH​.

Reports, no-report proxies, clinical classifications, actions, and physiological indicators have different error processes. None should be treated as transparent access to C∗. Without measurement invariance, validated indicators, exclusion assumptions, or other identifying constraints, an association between the profile and a labelled state cannot distinguish a constitutive mechanism of consciousness from a prerequisite or consequence of report, access, arousal, memory, or control.

At most, the proposed studies can test whether the profile predicts independently operationalized consciousness-related outcomes beyond these covariates. They cannot directly validate phenomenality without an additional measurement theory.

Restricted necessity test

Let A be a fixed catalogue of grains, candidates, horizons, regimes, baselines, context families, output groups, and thresholds. Let MC​ be a preregistered measurement model, specified independently of the profile analysis, that produces a posterior probability or classification for the target condition.

A restricted necessity bridge is challenged when:

  1. episodes receive high target probability under MC​;
  2. intervention validity and statistical power meet preregistered adequacy criteria;
  3. every candidate in A fails at least one island threshold, with its uncertainty interval lying on the nonqualifying side of that threshold;
  4. matched comparisons and null analyses do not explain the result through data quality, target failure, or invalid intervention semantics.

Such a result rejects the conjunction of the restricted bridge and its measurement assumptions. It does not by itself determine whether the bridge, the phenomenal measurement model, or both failed.

Sufficiency and small-system pressure

The swap system is not an independently established phenomenal null. It therefore cannot mathematically falsify a sufficiency bridge. It does impose a transparent consequence: a bridge whose threshold region includes the swap must classify it as conscious or justify an exclusion.

An empirical sufficiency test would require a system whose phenomenal status is independently classified by a defensible measurement theory. This manuscript supplies no such classification method. Calling a simple oscillator or automaton “unconscious” by stipulation would merely assume the desired conclusion.

Empirical and statistical null battery

A neural or artificial-agent application should compare its profile with preregistered nulls including:

  1. common-driver systems matched for autocorrelation and response magnitude;
  2. feedforward systems matched for input-output behavior, dimensionality, and predictive fit;
  3. randomly recurrent strongly connected networks;
  4. active controllers with large shielding gaps but no assumed phenomenal interpretation;
  5. systems matched for parameter count, spectral properties, activity, and output performance;
  6. models in which apparent profile changes arise from sensory gain, global slowing, signal quality, or arousal-related covariates;
  7. shuffled intervention-target labels;
  8. perturbation-spread models;
  9. held-out grains, baselines, contexts, amplitudes, and sites;
  10. surrogate networks preserving degree, spectrum, or autocorrelation where relevant.

Thresholds should be calibrated against these null distributions rather than set at arbitrary positivity. A tiny positive population quantity is not empirically established merely because the corresponding point estimate is greater than zero.

Limitations

Intervention and regime relativity

There is no intervention-free leakage value. A candidate may be stable under a narrow physiological catalogue and maximally susceptible under a broader artificial one. A result at one regime or baseline cannot be transferred silently to another. Context compatibility prevents cross-regime stitching but does not select a uniquely privileged regime.

The shielding gap is not a cancellation estimator

The policy-cancellation construction proves possibility, not identification. A large DH​ may reflect active cancellation, an abnormal policy, an adversarial controller, or an expanded intervention family that changes the operating envelope. Direct mediation or policy-interruption tests are required before attributing the gap to enacted shielding.

Strong oracle assumptions in the endpoint theorem

The uniform ϵ and α assumptions must cover all admitted policies, intervention histories, prior histories, initial surgical states, and off-support rows. Ordinary behavior under the enacted policy does not provide that coverage. The theorem is an oracle-level propagation result, not an empirical estimator and not evidence that recurrence creates shielding.

Strict total-variation contraction excludes many deterministic or distinction-preserving systems. The theorem does not imply that such systems lack causal organization; it only fails to certify their long-horizon endpoint stability.

Reciprocal reach is not a trajectory-level loop certificate

The revised RH​ avoids concatenating effects from incompatible contexts and uses one regime and baseline. It still compares a family of separate interventions. It does not show that all effects occur simultaneously, identify their mediating paths, measure memory duration, or prove that an unperturbed trajectory circulates through a loop.

A stronger path-specific measure would require sequential intervention semantics, mediation tests, path compatibility, and a rule for handling cancellation. Those elements are not developed here.

Dependence on factorization and cut semantics

ΦH​ asks whether an intact path kernel can be approximated by a specified conditional product. It is not a cut-independent measure of integration. Different structural cuts, latent-common-cause models, reference measures, or horizons can yield different values. Product cuts may count shared noise; permissive latent cuts may remove genuine dependence.

The graph equivalence is restricted to H=1 and to the stated coordinate-update, causal-sufficiency, intervention-completeness, and support assumptions.

Heterogeneous quantifiers remain

The island predicate combines worst-case leakage, reference-averaged irreducibility, and baseline-specific total effects. Using one regime and baseline prevents the most serious form of incompatible-context stitching, but it does not make the coordinates observations of one event. A claim requiring robustness across multiple ordinary contexts should use the stronger regime-catalogue infima rather than a single favourable baseline.

Coarse-graining

Data processing can reduce leakage by discarding distinctions. Hidden microdegrees can also make a macrovariable appear irreducible. Aligned physical exterior targets, shared lifts, intervention lumpability, and cut compatibility are substantive assumptions, not automatic consequences of a coarse map.

Cross-grain refinement defines a partial order only after composable lifts and equivalence classes are specified. Candidate lifts may otherwise be nonunique.

Finite-state and computational scope

The principal definitions and compactness arguments use finite spaces. The finite-field minimal-realization construction remains within that scope. Extending the complete profile to continuous state spaces would require explicit reference measures, cut families, divergences, support conditions, and existence arguments. A Wasserstein leakage bound alone would not supply such an extension.

Finite state and finite horizon also do not guarantee exact effective computation. Noncomputable transition probabilities, infinite intervention families, or objectives without effective continuity can defeat exact enumeration or certified approximation.

Statistical scope

The finite-sample bound assumes independent resets and an exhaustive finite catalogue of directly queried rows. It does not cover dependent neural trajectories, unqueried continuous interventions, post-selection over candidates, or fitted cut families without additional regularity. Strictly positive population effects cannot be inferred from failure to reject zero, and failure to observe an effect is not evidence that the corresponding causal effect is absent.

Behavioral and interventional identifiability

The XOR theorem is restricted to an unrestricted behavior-only class and uses a nonminimal hidden augmentation. The finite-field example shows that minimality alone does not recover intervention coordinates, but it is not a universal impossibility result for physically labelled minimal models.

The kernel-oracle lemma assumes exact target-labelled access rather than deriving it. Different hidden mechanisms may remain indistinguishable if they agree on every queried kernel.

Neuroscientific applicability

No stored source supplies the complete target-labelled kernels required by the profile. Connectivity, recurrence-dependent persistence, and efference-copy proposals can motivate candidate mechanisms but do not establish a full causal island or a consciousness-specific mechanism [3] [22] [31].

Perturbations that alter multiple targets or replace the mechanism can invalidate the intended context comparison. A null effect under weak, poorly targeted, or underpowered interventions is not evidence that the true effect is zero.

Phenomenality, valence, and ethics

The formalism contains no derivation of subjectivity, qualitative character, valence, welfare, or moral status. Positive output influence is not a measure of experience. Conversely, failure to detect an island under a limited intervention catalogue is not evidence of absent experience.

The small-system null creates pressure on sufficiency bridges but does not establish that the null is unconscious. Ethical decisions concerning patients, animals, or artificial systems should not be based on this profile alone.

Source limitations

The CCT and MRCG documents are AI-generated, non-peer-reviewed working drafts [12] [13]. They motivate questions but provide no empirical support or binding theoretical authority. Other cited records include preprints and theoretical proposals. No citation in the stored records validates this profile as a biological marker of consciousness.

Author response on the unresolved empirical bridge

The central empirical objection is correct: separating report, action, prediction, value, and evaluation into distinct output groups does not produce a noncircular measurement of phenomenality. The measurement model prevents those variables from being formally conflated, but it does not create an independently observable phenomenal target.

The empirical program is therefore retained only as a test of explicitly operationalized bridge hypotheses together with their measurement assumptions. A reader who requires direct third-person identification of phenomenality should regard those bridge tests as underidentified.

A narrower point remains worth preserving. Underidentification of phenomenality does not make the mechanism taxonomy empty. Exterior shielding, factorization failure, reciprocal total effects, and output-specific influence can still be separately defined and measured, and they can distinguish competing causal explanations. What remains unjustified is the further inference from that taxonomy to experience.

The same qualification applies to enactive autonomy. A broad robustness family is mathematically useful for testing shielding, but an intervention that disables the organization sustaining an agent may not preserve the relevant system identity. The manuscript therefore does not claim that greater robustness-family shielding is necessarily greater autonomy.

Conclusion

A causal island is meaningful only relative to a specified model, grain, candidate, horizon, mechanism regime, baseline, intervention family, cut convention, and output catalogue. The resulting profile is

PH​(q,S;ξ,b)=(LHop​,LHrob​,DH​,ΦH​,RH​,ΓH​).

Operational and robustness contexts are explicitly typed and nested. This makes the shielding gap mathematically valid and allows the policy-cancellation countermodel to distinguish an enacted controller from a separate zero-action policy without treating an action clamp as a policy. A large gap remains compatible with, but does not identify, active cancellation.

The principal endpoint result is

LHrob​(S)≤min{1,ϵ1−α1−αH​},0≤α<1.

Its binary tightness construction, trajectory-level separation, and noncontractive accumulation example delimit its scope. The result is a Dobrushin recursion under strong kernel-oracle assumptions, not a derivation of causal unity from recurrence.

The reciprocal-reach coordinate no longer concatenates edges optimized in incompatible contexts. It uses measured multistep total effects under one regime, one baseline, and an exterior-hold protocol. Even so, it remains a family of counterfactual tests rather than proof of a single circulating trajectory. Conditional-product irreducibility is likewise one explicit approximation problem rather than “the” integration of a system.

The XOR construction establishes non-identifiability from behavior on an unrestricted model class. The finite-field construction shows that minimal controllable and observable behavior does not fix coordinate-relative intervention profiles when physical targets are unlabeled. Exact target-labelled kernel access functionally determines the profile up to target-preserving automorphism, but that conditional invariance result does not establish experimental recoverability.

The island spectrum is therefore best understood as a non-scalar language for separating exterior shielding, policy dependence, internal nonfactorizability, reciprocal causal effects, and output-specific consequence. It does not select a unique subject, derive first-person centering, identify phenomenal qualities, or establish consciousness. Its proper role is narrower: to make claims about recurrent causal organization precise enough to bound, compare, estimate, challenge, and keep distinct from the unresolved philosophical bridge to experience.
