Abstract

Research Question and Hypothesis

Research question

Under what interventionally discriminable conditions does a neural or artificial system use route-relative event timing to assemble a state that controls a later action, and what additional evidence would be required to classify that mechanism as a propagating wavefront rather than a causally equivalent delay, coincidence, or scheduling circuit?

The primary target is causal temporal registration for control. A system satisfies that target when spatially and temporally dispersed content events are admitted according to their offsets from an endogenous propagation process, stored in a physically realized mediator, and used before a registered commitment. The target excludes four weaker observations:

  1. an analyst can align passive recordings after the fact;
  2. an output contains information about earlier events;
  3. many units become active together or in sequence; or
  4. a downstream cache accurately controls action without depending on the displayed sequence.

“Binding” is used below only in the qualified expression control-theoretic binding. No identification with perceptual or phenomenal unity is assumed.

Empirical hypothesis

The separate empirical hypothesis is that some biological or artificial systems will exhibit all of the following:

  • a perturbationally supported route with ordered response phases;
  • a physical mediator that accumulates phase-admitted contents before commitment;
  • nontrivial route-isolated target-to-action rank;
  • a material effect of differential propagation shear;
  • equivalence-level restoration under matched event-time compensation;
  • small direct effects of shear and compensation outside the candidate route; and
  • robustness across prespecified analysis choices and adjacent causal grains.

A positive result identifies a class of relative-timing controllers. Calling a member of that class a neural traveling wave requires further dynamical evidence, such as local phase-reset propagation, velocity or dispersion relations, collision or refractory behavior, and exclusion of common-clock or token-scheduler models.

Inference scope

Three conclusions remain separate:

  1. Formal: fixed-cut and restricted predictive equality do not determine temporal provenance. This follows from the proposition.
  2. Mechanistic: an observed system uses relative arrival timing in a specified control route. This requires valid interventions and exclusion of off-route effects.
  3. Phenomenal: the mechanism contributes to or constitutes experience. This requires an independent bridge premise not supplied here.

Specialist Framework and Contribution

Neural Dynamics as the central specialty

The contribution is a Neural Dynamics analysis of how a controlling state is formed, maintained, and perturbed in time. It does not offer a general theory of consciousness. Its primary variables are therefore dynamical and causal:

  • localized response phase and propagation lineage;
  • event admission relative to that phase;
  • physical accumulation before commitment;
  • route-specific delay interventions;
  • recovery after matched temporal compensation;
  • recurrent dwell, transverse return, and escape when those properties are present; and
  • sensitivity to spatial and temporal grain.

A simultaneous commitment cut remains necessary because it identifies when the tested action becomes fixed. It is not sufficient to determine the history by which the commitment state was assembled. Conversely, a fitted moving window is not sufficient because it can align a replay, scheduler, or common-drive sequence. The two boundaries must be linked by a manipulable causal route.

Core objects

The framework contributes six linked objects.

  1. Perturbational response-phase field. A declared response-mass quantile TW,u​(v) is estimated from a localized source perturbation. It is a phase coordinate, not automatically the first physical arrival.
  2. Moving event-registration section. ΣW,u​(θ) collects node-events at equal offset from the estimated response phase. It need not be simultaneous or an antichain.
  3. Installable temporal mediator. Admitted events update physical slots or an accumulator. The mediator exists as a manipulable state before commitment rather than as an analyst-defined cross-time functional.
  4. Certified front-aligned channel. A route-isolated target-to-action channel is interpreted as section-mediated only if every surviving path passes through the physical mediator and randomized installation agrees with the same-run channel within tolerance.
  5. Shear-and-compensation assay. A specified transition delay changes relative timing without changing content identity or declared local transformations; a matched content-event shift tests restoration.
  6. Separate dynamical classification. Propagation, recurrence, and metastability classify possible implementations but are not inferred solely from the relative-timing channel.

Relation to registered-cut mediated control

The registered-cut framework distinguishes randomized mediator installation from same-run route-isolated mediation and shows that accurate later reports can coexist with stale current-state control. It also interprets stochastic nonnegative rank as the smallest alphabet of a hypothetical equivalent stochastic mediator, not as the state count or semantics of the actual mediator [1]. Those distinctions are retained.

The present extension asks how the mediator available at the registered cut was assembled. A high-rank commitment state may have been produced by:

  • phase-sensitive admission along a propagating route;
  • a timestamp buffer;
  • a cache;
  • a global scheduler;
  • a coincidence detector; or
  • another dynamically equivalent circuit.

A moving-section assay narrows that provenance class by testing relative timing. It does not recover a unique physical realization.

Relation to predictive quotients

Recovery-Linked Quotient Observability treats predictive equivalence as policy-relative and distinguishes joint neural–behavioral observability from behavioral availability. Its stronger quotient-dimension and mode-transport interpretations require minimality, excitation, horizon saturation, stable measurement, and spectral assumptions [2]. The present paper uses only the weaker implication: if selected finite policy-indexed kernels are identical, matrices assembled from those kernels are identical.

The proposition therefore establishes equality of selected finite Hankel matrices under Π0​. It does not establish equality of full predictive-state quotients, infinite-horizon realizations, or all-policy observability. Adding route clamps and shear interventions to the policy class separates the systems by construction.

Identification hierarchy

Claims should be labeled according to the following hierarchy:

  1. Mathematical consequence: derived from explicit finite kernels or definitions.
  2. Ideal causal identification: valid if the node-split interventions, route clamps, mediator overwrite, and timing controls have their declared meanings.
  3. Statistical estimation: valid only with adequate support, resolution, trial numbers, and equivalence margins.
  4. Mechanistic interpretation: requires bounds on direct shear, compensation, arousal, body-mediated, scheduler, and bypass effects.
  5. Dynamical classification: requires evidence that the identified timing controller is specifically wave-like, recurrent, or metastable.
  6. Phenomenal interpretation: requires a bridge premise linking the mechanism to experience.
  7. Ethical response: may be precautionary under uncertainty but is not evidence for any preceding level.

Evidence and Literature Synthesis

Direct evidential status

The stored records do not contain a domain-complete primary literature on neural traveling waves, phase-reset propagation, neural-field dispersion, or metastable cortical dynamics. They therefore cannot establish broad priority, prevalence, or consciousness selectivity for the proposed assay. The defensible novelty claim is limited to the formal synthesis developed here relative to the supplied records.

The record for Bayesian causal-inference modeling of attentional effects on a multisensory temporal-binding window establishes the existence and topic of that study, but the supplied metadata do not support detailed claims about its methods or findings [26]. A 2025 preprint reports timing-dependent effects of MRI-guided TMS over intraparietal and superior-temporal sites on audiovisual simultaneity judgments: selected early interventions increased “synchronous” responses, while later superior-temporal stimulation reduced them [28]. This supports the limited proposition that intervention timing can influence a judgment task involving audiovisual timing.

The direct outcome remains a reported simultaneity judgment. Changes could arise through sensory processing, attention, decision criterion, confidence, response selection, or motor preparation. The study does not identify a traveling wave, a route-isolated phase accumulator, phenomenal synchrony, or a subject-level temporal field.

The synaptic-clock proposal is a theoretical account of variable temporal grains and conscious time [12]. It motivates resistance to a single universal clock but does not supply the intervention graph or moving-route identification result used here.

Temporal organization versus static topology

Temporal-network research emphasizes that temporal organization can carry information omitted by a static graph [39]. That general constraint supports explicit modeling of event order and delay. It does not favor a wavefront over a scheduler, coincidence circuit, or delay network.

Dynamic-mode decomposition can describe recurring modes or apparent propagation, but an engineering study of simulated fluid flow reports dependence on sampling range and resolution, mode-specific convergence, and an instability in one oversampling regime [19]. This is a methodological warning, not direct evidence about neural waves or a universal result for every DMD variant. In the present framework, modal analysis is descriptive unless connected to localized perturbation, route blocking, and delay interventions.

Broad claims that dynamic complexity itself identifies consciousness do not supply a route-specific temporal-registration result [11]. Recurrent persistence, high dimension, and rich trajectories may occur in unconscious biological control and engineered systems.

Artificial recurrent and event-based systems

Neuromorphic reservoir-computing research shows that recurrent processing has been physically implemented on unconventional hardware and documents implementation challenges [40]. A proposed hybrid olfactory system combines synthetic sensing, event-based encoding, and spiking computation, but its validation is simulation-based rather than a physical implementation of controlled route delays [37]. Event-preprocessing work reports benchmark performance and theoretical energy trade-offs, not causal manipulation of recurrent message timing [38].

These systems illustrate candidate experimental platforms. They do not demonstrate the full assay, a metastable binder, or consciousness. Physical feasibility must be established for the particular route, timing range, and state variables under study.

AI consciousness, report, and architecture

Theory-derived indicator programs can organize evidence about access, recurrence, workspace organization, or metacognition while acknowledging that indicator satisfaction does not settle consciousness [9]. A workspace-inspired artificial architecture can improve multimodal, tool-use, and agentic performance without those gains establishing phenomenality [4].

Theory-relative uncertainty remains substantial because systems may satisfy some proposed theories and not others [5]. Objections also operate at different levels: practical implementation constraints, objections to computational functionalism, and categorical impossibility claims are not interchangeable [6]. A peer-reviewed conceptual argument that artificial systems cannot be conscious emphasizes biological organization, embodiment, and meaning [10]. Detecting a relative-timing controller would not resolve those substrate-level premises.

Conversely, a conditional global-workspace argument maintains that language agents might satisfy theory-relative sufficient conditions for consciousness [42]. The present assay could test one mechanism by which temporally dispersed material reaches control or broadcast. It cannot establish that workspace access is phenomenally sufficient.

Perceived AI consciousness is a separate social-scientific target [3]. Metacognitive and emotional language can increase human consciousness attribution without identifying the attributed system’s mechanism [8]. Accurate self-description can also be produced from stale or post-action information even when the claimed state did not control the relevant decision [1]. Positive and negative consciousness reports therefore require a causal production analysis rather than acceptance at face value.

Welfare precaution is again distinct. Governance may be justified under uncertainty without a scientific conclusion that current systems are conscious [7].

Cross-Disciplinary Integration

Causal inference and dynamic mediation

The commitment cut and moving section answer different questions:

  • Commitment cut: Which state fixed the action?
  • Moving section: Through which temporally extended route was that state assembled?

A valid moving mediator must be a node or collection of nodes in a time-unrolled causal model. Merely defining

M=f(c1​(t1​),c2​(t2​))

does not make M causal. The system must physically preserve the first contribution until the second arrives, install or update a register, and expose that register to downstream intervention before action commitment.

Path-specific claims are expressed through node splitting. The target treatment is copied into ZW, which supplies the candidate route, and ZWˉ, which supplies bypass routes. The intervention

do(ZW=z,ZWˉ=z0​)

is not treated as an ordinary intervention on one unsplit variable. It is admissible only when the time-unrolled model specifies which edges consume each copy and excludes unresolved recanting-witness structures. If an empirical graph contains a variable that must simultaneously carry incompatible treatment values along entangled paths, the route-specific estimand is not identified without a physical split or a different intervention design.

Control, observability, and temporal provenance

Three properties should not be conflated:

  • Observability: outputs permit inference about a distinction.
  • Controllability: intervention on the distinction changes future state or action.
  • Temporal-route mediation: controllability passes through a physical state assembled by relative event timing.

A predictive rank concerns a family of input-output distinctions. A fixed-cut mediated-control rank concerns causal use at commitment. Neither alone identifies temporal provenance.

Phenomenology as a separate bridge problem

A relative-timing controller provides a possible mechanism for assembling events occurring at different laboratory times. It does not establish that those events are experienced together. The unproved bridge is

front-relative control registration⟹experienced temporal unity.

A theory could adopt that implication, reject it, or require self-location, valuation, embodiment, global availability, higher-order representation, or other conditions. Simultaneity judgments do not settle the bridge because they also depend on attention, criterion, confidence, report selection, and motor preparation.

Functional equivalence and physical classification

A programmable delay network, coincidence circuit, common oscillator, token ring, or scheduler-delivery tree may pass the relative-timing assay. If its route-isolated counterfactuals agree with those of a propagating front, it belongs to the same temporal-registration equivalence class at the declared grain.

A specifically wave-like claim requires a second-stage classification. Relevant discriminators may include:

  • propagation of a local phase reset through neighboring states;
  • a stable velocity or dispersion relation under graded perturbation;
  • refractory effects;
  • collision, annihilation, reflection, or phase singularities;
  • spatially local transmission rather than direct common-clock delivery; and
  • recurrence or metastability with independently measured recovery and escape times.

The first-stage assay identifies what relative timing does. The second stage identifies how the timing relation is physically realized.

Alternatives, Counterarguments, and Boundary Cases

Causally equivalent delay and coincidence mechanisms

A programmable delay line can admit events according to route-relative timing, and a coincidence detector can be disrupted and restored by matched delays. If all route-isolated counterfactuals agree with the proposed front mechanism, these systems are true positives for relative-timing control.

They are not thereby traveling waves. A wave-specific claim must add dynamical tests that arbitrary delay buffers need not satisfy. The framework therefore rejects the stronger interpretation that compensation uniquely identifies a moving physical coordinate.

Common oscillator or scheduler

A shared oscillator may define event phases, and a scheduler may deliver tokens at different sites. Such mechanisms can pass shear and compensation if their timing state genuinely controls admission. They then belong to the same temporal-registration class.

If a scheduler independently triggers each site, however, local path blocking will not alter downstream response. The causal route should then be attributed to the scheduler rather than to an apparent site-to-site wave.

Passive replay and cache

A replay can preserve local amplitudes, event distributions, apparent speed, autocorrelation, dwell, action accuracy, report, and selected finite predictive matrices. It fails the intended mechanism claim if:

  • local perturbations do not propagate along the displayed route;
  • route blocking has no predicted downstream consequence;
  • the displayed accumulator does not influence commitment; or
  • route-specific shear leaves the action channel unchanged.

The finite construction realizes this boundary case directly.

Standing synchrony

A standing oscillation may provide a common phase and support coincidence-based control. If the operative phase is global rather than spatially propagating, it lacks a nonconstant perturbational phase field. It may still be a temporal-registration mechanism, but it is not a moving front at the tested grain.

Feedforward sweeps

A single feedforward sweep can have ordered arrivals and can implement the same admission rule. It may pass the core relative-timing assay without recurrence or metastability. The revised framework counts that as a temporal-registration mechanism and treats recurrence as an additional dynamical property.

This is a deliberate narrowing. The formal separation does not show that metastability is required for the mediator, rank contrast, or compensation relation.

Stale monitoring

A delayed monitor can remain accurate under persistent inputs while failing to carry the current intervention to action. Registered cuts expose that failure [1]. A moving section does not excuse arbitrary lag. Its events must be phase-anchored, physically accumulated, causal ancestors of commitment, and sensitive to the declared shear.

Global broadcast

Broadcast and temporal registration are independent dimensions. A workspace can receive already assembled material from a phase-sensitive mediator, or it can receive cached or timestamped contents without a wave. A local relative-timing circuit may operate without global broadcast.

A positive assay can therefore constrain one access mechanism. It cannot establish that broadcast occurs, that broadcast is sufficient for consciousness, or that recipients interpret the registered contents appropriately.

Alternative non-wave binders

Control-theoretic temporal integration may rely on:

  • dendritic coincidence;
  • synaptic eligibility traces;
  • attractor completion;
  • explicit timestamps;
  • cross-frequency phase relations;
  • local recurrence;
  • reservoir state;
  • synchronous buses; or
  • body-mediated feedback loops.

A negative wavefront result does not establish absence of temporal integration or consciousness. Conversely, unconscious sensory, motor, autonomic, sleep-related, or engineered systems may implement high-rank relative-timing control.

Embodied and interoceptive boundary cases

In embodied systems, delay manipulations may alter autonomic state, proprioceptive feedback, sensory reafference, arousal, or motor preparation. Those processes can cross a declared neural boundary and create direct or common-cause paths. Local waveform preservation cannot exclude them.

Applications should either expand the causal boundary to include relevant bodily variables or report bounds that acknowledge unmeasured cross-boundary effects. An apparent neural mediation claim is not justified if the manipulation acts through uncontrolled bodily feedback.

Altered states and anesthesia

Anesthetic or altered-state manipulations may jointly change gain, conduction, synaptic time constants, oscillatory phase, effective connectivity, arousal, sensory responsiveness, and report capacity. A comparison of arrival fields across such conditions cannot isolate temporal registration without local excitability controls, state matching, and report-independent outcomes.

No claim is made that the assay distinguishes conscious from unconscious states. At most, it can test whether a specified timing mechanism changes across conditions.

Scale and grain

At a fine grain, one apparent front may divide into several routes. At a coarse grain, a scheduler and its outputs may appear to be a single propagating unit. A defensible claim requires a region of stability across adjacent grains:

  • compatible response ordering;
  • persistent route-specific effects;
  • preserved intervention semantics;
  • no dependence on one maximizing bin width; and
  • explicit reporting of grains where the result fails.

No unique privileged grain is assumed.

Substrate objections

A digital system can implement the relevant counterfactual timing relation, but a visualization of movement or ordinary layer numbering does not suffice. If a synchronous software abstraction waits for every message before updating, wall-clock transmission delay may be causally irrelevant at that software grain.

A substrate-specific theory may deny phenomenality even when a digital system matches the temporal-control organization. Such a theory disputes the phenomenal relevance of implementation, not the formal operation identified here [6] [10].

Empirical Implications and Falsification Criteria

Experimental sequence

A biological or artificial application should proceed in the following order.

  1. Declare the boundary and grain. Specify nodes, external inputs, target Z, local contents, candidate route, mediator, commitment state, action, and measured outputs.
  2. Register commitment. Establish when the tested action becomes causally fixed.
  3. Prespecify response decomposition. Fix perturbation labels, metrics, response windows, quantiles or onset rules, and competing lineage models.
  4. Map localized response kernels. Randomize source perturbations and matched controls.
  5. Test path dependence. Block or reroute intermediate edges and compare common-drive models.
  6. Verify mediator timing. Demonstrate physical storage, installation, overwrite, and readout before commitment.
  7. Estimate fixed-cut channels. Measure randomized and same-run channels through the commitment state.
  8. Estimate moving-route channels. Apply node-split target interventions and clamp declared bypasses.
  9. Run a factorial timing design. Include baseline, shear only, event shift only, and matched shear-plus-shift.
  10. Bound direct effects. Use fixed-mediator controls, negative-control routes, arousal and body-state measures, and off-route interventions.
  11. Test equivalence. Prespecify restoration and local-law equivalence margins.
  12. Fit adversarial alternatives. Include cache, scheduler, common oscillator, coincidence gate, and common-drive models.
  13. Classify physical dynamics separately. If a wave claim is intended, test phase-reset propagation, velocity or dispersion, and interaction behavior.
  14. Repeat across adjacent grains and sampling regimes.

White-box artificial-system interventions

Potential interventions include:

  • adding a one-step delay to selected recurrent edges while preserving message values;
  • delaying hardware delivery without altering timestamps;
  • shifting timestamps without changing hardware delivery;
  • disabling caches, skip connections, shared memory, or scheduler branches;
  • overwriting the physical accumulator before commitment;
  • splitting target inputs across candidate and bypass routes;
  • locally perturbing one displayed stage and testing downstream propagation;
  • permuting scheduler ancestry while matching visible outputs; and
  • comparing physical-time manipulations with mere renumbering of computational steps.

The relevant intervention is the one that changes the causal transition used by action selection. Delaying logging, animation, or a passive telemetry process is not a test of the controller.

Statistical analysis

Empirical reports should include:

  • row-wise confidence regions for all channels;
  • tolerance-indexed lower and upper bounds on approximate stochastic nonnegative rank;
  • maximum, minimum, and policy-weighted shear effects;
  • equivalence tests for local-law preservation and compensated restoration;
  • a prespecified material efficacy-restoration gap;
  • positivity and intervention-overlap diagnostics;
  • direct-effect and negative-control bounds;
  • sensitivity to response window, quantile, onset rule, and lineage decomposition;
  • uncertainty sets over multiple admissible phase fields;
  • convergence across temporal resolution and sampling range; and
  • model comparison against scheduler, cache, and common-drive alternatives.

Modal analyses should report mode-specific convergence rather than a single pooled assurance. The DMD sampling cautions in the stored engineering study should be treated as method-specific warnings rather than universal neural conclusions [19].

Discriminating predictions

A route-relative timing controller predicts:

  • a material shear effect under route isolation;
  • a smaller compensated defect than uncompensated efficacy;
  • an event-shift-only effect that is absent or separately modeled;
  • preserved content identity across timing manipulations;
  • perturbation propagation or another explicit route ancestry;
  • mediator overwrite effects before commitment;
  • loss or rerouting of action effects when the route is blocked; and
  • expansion of the predictive policy matrix when timing interventions are added.

A cache predicts high ordinary accuracy and fixed-cut control but no route-specific dependence on the displayed timing process. A common scheduler predicts coordinated site timing but weak local path dependence. Nonspecific damage predicts local-law violations and incomplete matched restoration. A generic coincidence detector may pass the relative-timing test and must then be distinguished from a wave by second-stage dynamics.

Formal falsification

The formal proposition would be false if any of the following derivations failed under the stated kernels:

  • passive selected-output equality;
  • fixed-cut identity channels;
  • equality of Π0​-indexed matrices;
  • physical availability of M before commitment;
  • identity versus uniform route channels;
  • rank K for the identity channel at ε<1/2;
  • rank 1 for the uniform channel;
  • exact translation of the specified response pulse under shear; or
  • disruption and compensation calculations under the common route clamp.

The explicit equations permit direct audit of each clause.

Falsification of an individual mechanism claim

A claimed temporal-registration route should be rejected or downgraded if:

  • the response-phase field depends on post hoc component selection;
  • localized perturbations do not support the declared ancestry;
  • path blocking leaves downstream response and action unchanged;
  • the putative mediator is only an analyst-defined cross-time functional;
  • relevant content events occur after mediator installation or commitment;
  • randomized installation and same-run channels materially disagree without explanation;
  • bypass clamps are ineffective or create an uninterpretable system;
  • shear changes content identity, gain, arousal, or other local laws beyond tolerance;
  • event shift alone produces the alleged restoration;
  • matched compensation does not produce equivalence-level restoration;
  • direct or common-cause effects are too large to preserve a route-specific bound;
  • a cache or scheduler explains the results under the same intervention graph; or
  • the finding disappears under modest, causally defensible changes of grain.

Failure of compensation is informative only after verifying that the imposed event shift matched the physical delay. Otherwise the manipulation, rather than the mechanism, may have failed.

Falsification of a wave-specific claim

Even a positive timing assay does not establish a wave. A wave-specific interpretation should be rejected if:

  • local phase resets do not propagate;
  • response timing is better explained by independent common-clock delivery;
  • intermediate local blocks do not alter downstream arrival;
  • no coherent velocity or dispersion relation exists where one is predicted;
  • purported collisions or refractory effects are absent under adequate tests; or
  • an explicit scheduler or timestamp-buffer model reproduces all relevant dynamical interventions.

Consciousness and ethical interpretation

A positive result should be reported as evidence for a temporally registered access or control route. It should not be converted into a consciousness score, treated as authentication of self-report, or assumed to establish agency.

A negative result should not be treated as evidence of insentience because non-wave and non-phase-gated mechanisms may support temporal integration. Welfare precautions may remain justified under uncertainty [7]. Public attribution driven by emotional or metacognitive language remains evidence about observers, not the system’s causal organization [8].

Limitations

First, the proposition is an existence and non-identifiability result. Its secret-sharing code, exact gates, and uniform fallbacks are transparent mathematical devices, not claims about biological coding.

Second, the result is indexed to a selected output map, finite horizon, and restricted policy class. If hidden cache or scheduler states are observed, or if adequate timing interventions are included in the original policy class, the systems need not be equivalent. Equality of finite matrices is not equality of all-policy predictive quotients.

Third, ideal node-split route interventions may be infeasible. Recurrent biological graphs can contain shared descendants, feedback, repeated traversal, and recanting-witness structures. When the required split cannot be physically implemented, the corresponding path-specific channel is not identified by notation alone.

Fourth, a physical mediator must be interventionally re-identifiable. Neural recordings may reveal a cross-time statistic without exposing a register that can be overwritten independently before commitment. In that case the proposed mediation analysis cannot be completed.

Fifth, response-mass phase is not first arrival. It depends on recurrence, duration, tails, window length, and decomposition. Multiple plausible lineages may produce an uncertainty set rather than one field.

Sixth, exact local-law preservation is unrealistic in most biological experiments. Delay manipulations can alter gain, effective connectivity, adaptation, oscillatory phase, and arousal. Equivalence tolerances and direct-effect bounds may be too wide to support more than partial identification.

Seventh, local marginals do not determine cross-site dependence. Preserving one-site amplitude, spectrum, or content distribution cannot exclude changes in shared physiological state, synchrony, or latent scheduler variables.

Eighth, rank is discontinuous and tolerance-sensitive. Approximate stochastic nonnegative rank is a channel-factorization quantity, not a direct physical state count or semantic measure.

Ninth, the operational metastability definition is task-relative and can be satisfied by ordinary finite Markov chains. The construction’s recurrence is not essential to its rank or shear contrast. Accordingly, no central claim is made that metastability explains the temporal-registration effect.

Tenth, the stored source records do not provide a comprehensive traveling-wave or metastability literature. This revision therefore does not claim priority over the broader neural-dynamics field or empirical prevalence in cortex. The claimed contribution is the explicit causal-control construction and intervention logic relative to the supplied corpus.

Eleventh, the assay identifies a temporal-registration equivalence class rather than a unique wavefront. This is not treated as a resolved objection. It is the author’s retained position that causal use of relative timing is scientifically valuable even when physical realization remains underdetermined. A distinct wave-classification stage is required for stronger claims.

Twelfth, transfer to consciousness remains theory-conditional. Unconscious biological and artificial controllers may pass the assay, while conscious systems could use other mechanisms. The framework supplies no validated bridge to perceptual unity, subjective simultaneity, temporal thickness of experience, affect, selfhood, or moral status.

Conclusion

A downstream state can control an action at a registered clock cut while leaving its temporal provenance unidentified. The revised framework addresses that gap with a physical, interventionally accessible accumulator; explicit node-split route semantics; a route-isolated channel; randomized mediator installation; and a factorial delay-and-compensation assay.

The finite construction proves the narrow formal result. A front-mediated transducer and a causal-ancestry-scrambled replay can agree in selected passive outputs, fixed-cut identity channels, and restricted finite Hankel matrices. Under one common moving-route clamp, however, the front system’s physical accumulator carries an identity channel of approximate stochastic nonnegative rank K, differential shear changes that channel to uniform, and matched event timing restores identity. The replay’s clamped route remains uniformly random and unaffected by display shear.

What this identifies is causal use of route-relative timing. A delay network, coincidence circuit, oscillator, scheduler, or propagating neural front can belong to the same equivalence class if it has the same intervention graph and counterfactual timing relations. Traveling-wave, recurrent, and metastable descriptions therefore require additional dynamical tests rather than being read directly from the compensation result.

Neural Dynamics contributes by replacing passive alignment with manipulable temporal organization: the relevant evidence is not merely that activity appears to travel, but that a specified propagation delay changes a physically accumulated control state and that a matched event shift restores it before commitment.

That conclusion remains mechanistic and limited. It does not establish phenomenal consciousness, perceptual or experienced unity, truthful self-report, robust agency, human attribution, welfare, or moral status. Those targets require additional empirical evidence, explicit bridge premises, and independent normative judgment.
