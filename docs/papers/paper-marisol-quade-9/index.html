Abstract

Later command following, accurate labeled output, or improved task performance does not by itself identify an earlier content-to-decision route. The later capability may reflect reopening of a downstream gate, restoration of an earlier route, recruitment of a new route, changing state mixtures, replay from memory, or altered measurement. This problem arises in longitudinal assessment of patients and in artificial systems modified by fine-tuning, checkpoint replacement, tools, or architectural scaffolding.

We separate three objects. First, a genuine joint telemetry–behavior channel defines behavior-hidden content response dimensions and an endpoint response-opening index. These statistics concern matched intervention contrasts; they do not establish persistence of one latent neural mode or realization. Research Question and Hypothesis

Research question

Under what stage-coupled causal conditions does later restoration of content-sensitive action or output justify a lower bound on an earlier, registered content-to-commitment route?

The motivating clinical question is often framed in terms of covert awareness, but the formal target is narrower. The following claims must be kept separate:

  1. Representation: an internal or predictive response varies with controlled content.
  2. Behavior-hidden joint response: telemetry, or telemetry–behavior coupling, distinguishes content contrasts that selected behavior does not.
  3. Architectural access: a content distinction is available to some declared workspace, monitor, memory, or consumer system.
  4. Registered mediated control: content changes a policy variable at a declared temporal cut through a specified mediator route.
  5. Total control: content changes that policy variable through any route.
  6. Correct or flexible control: the resulting action is appropriate across tasks or counterfactual contexts.
  7. Overt behavior or report: a commitment is successfully rendered as movement, communication, or language.
  8. Perceived consciousness: an observer attributes consciousness to the system.
  9. Phenomenal consciousness: there is something it is like for the system.
  10. Moral status: a normative conclusion based on sentience, interests, agency, relationships, rights, risk, or other considerations.

The paper formally addresses items 2–5. It does not derive items 6–10 from channel rank.

Hypotheses and scope

The revised claims are:

  1. Typed no-go. In a fully registered K-target, K-commitment model class, fixed external recovery evidence can be compatible with every early exact route-isolated rank in {1,…,K}.
  1. Untyped non-identification. If the compatibility class does not fix the target and commitment alphabets or exclude externally silent but internally intervenable states, the same construction establishes attainability of ranks 1,…,K, not a universal upper endpoint K.
  1. Conditional backward transport. Approximate encoder and controller commutation transports a rank lower bound backward, with explicit penalties for randomized-reconstruction error and, when applicable, incomplete route isolation.
  1. Phenomenal non-entailment. A structural theory containing no phenomenal bridge entails neither phenomenal consciousness nor its absence.

The first, third, and fourth claims are proved below. The second is demonstrated by an alphabet-enlargement counterexample.

At each stage the assay may include

Their roles are:

The notation Aρ∗​ does not assert a unique central decision state or a localized neural commitment. It denotes a model-dependent assay cut. Registered content states

The external label Cρ​ is a handle for a registered subset of values of Zρ​. A valid registration requires either:

  1. an intervention equivalence showing that do(Cρ​=c) installs the corresponding Zρ​=zc​; or

Predictive equivalence and genuine joint response channels

Let Pρ​ be a finite, preregistered family of future probe policies. Give each policy π∈Pρ​ a fixed weight wπ​>0, independent of content. Let Nρ+​ and Bρ+​ denote the selected future telemetry and behavioral trajectories under the probe.

Define the actual joint channel

TρJ​(c,(π,n,b))=wπ​P(Nρ+​=n,Bρ+​=b∣do(Cρ​=c),π).

Define the behavioral marginal channel

TρB​(c,(π,b))=wπ​P(Bρ+​=b∣do(Cρ​=c),π).

Both are row-stochastic. There is a stochastic marginalization matrix MB​ such that

TρB​=TρJ​MB​.

This construction replaces concatenation of telemetry and behavioral marginals. Concatenated marginals are not a joint distribution and can miss content-sensitive coupling. For example, one content may produce only (N,B)=(0,0),(1,1), while another produces only (0,1),(1,0), each pair with probability 1/2. Both marginals are identical across contents, but the joint channels differ.

Two histories are behaviorally predictively equivalent relative to Pρ​ when every registered probe induces the same future behavioral law. They are jointly predictively equivalent when every probe induces the same future joint telemetry–behavior law. Joint equivalence refines behavioral equivalence because behavior is a marginal of the joint outcome.

Command following, communication, and output

A healthy-participant fMRI study used the distinction between command following and communication to motivate a dissociation at the task level; the supplied record does not establish that dissociation as a validated diagnostic mechanism in patients [25].

A later study of 38 healthy participants examined EEG, electromyography, and cardiac signals during imagined movement. Fifteen participants showed motor imagery under the conventional analysis. Prognosis does not identify the early route

A multidomain model combining resting-state functional networks and clinical characteristics reported approximately 90% accuracy for one-year outcome prediction across three datasets from two centers [17]. This is prognosis, not identification of present awareness or of an early mediator route.

A preprint involving 30 patients reported associations between diffusion-tensor measures and clinical scores, together with outcome-prediction accuracies for selected white-matter measures [38]. The small sample and outcome-specific analysis do not validate a route-specific mechanism. A conference multiple-case report also studied reappearance of command following in relation to language and consciousness-associated networks, but the supplied record contains only title-level information and does not warrant a general mechanistic conclusion [26].

These results can inform priors about recovery. They do not distinguish, without further intervention or registration, among:

  • reopening of a downstream gate;
  • restoration of arousal or task engagement;
  • persistence of behavior-hidden content processing;
  • recruitment of a compensatory mediator;
  • creation of a new content-to-policy route;
  • changing measurement maps;
  • changing mixtures of responsive and unresponsive states.

Intervention requirements

Estimating the formal kernels requires distinct interventions:

  • do(Cρ​=c) tests response to controlled content;
  • do(Zρ​=z) estimates Qρ​ only if target installation is valid;
  • do(Mρ​=m) estimates Kρ​ only under adequate support and downstream modularity;
  • a same-run edge or path intervention estimates Sρiso​;
  • ordinary do(Zρ​=z) estimates total control Θρ​;
  • perturbing Gρ​ can help separate commitment from execution.

Biological stimulation is often pleiotropic. It may alter arousal, sensory processing, context, or downstream dynamics rather than install one mediator state. In AI systems, hidden-state replacement may be off-manifold and can change downstream computation. Valid interpretation therefore requires consistency, specificity, positivity, no interference, comparable context, and an explicit account of off-manifold interventions.

Temporal grain

Content encoding, recurrent processing, policy selection, motor preparation, overt movement, and later output occur at different temporal grains. Timestamp coincidence is not enough to define a common cut. The target, mediator, and policy variables must be registered relative to the decision process being tested.

Theoretical proposals relating consciousness to content-specific synaptic clocks motivate multiscale temporal analysis but remain speculative and are not premises of the present results [13].

Clinical status of the framework

At present, the framework is primarily a non-identification warning, not a validated clinical assay. The internal interventions needed to identify Qρ​, Kρ​, Sρiso​, and Γρ​ may be unethical, unsafe, nonspecific, or unavailable in patients. Rich telemetry cannot substitute automatically for those interventions.

Failure to establish a transported control route is not evidence that such a route or experience is absent. Patient welfare, legal standing, and human worth must not depend on a favorable rank or response-opening statistic.

Philosophical Reinterpretation

Recovery evidence is genealogical and assumption-dependent

The invalid inference is

late content-sensitive output+early content-sensitive telemetry⇒early registered mediated control.

A defensible backward inference instead has the form

​late route-specific control+registered content identity+registered mediator transport+registered commitment identity+bounded reconstruction and route defects⇒a tolerance-dependent lower bound on early route rank.​

This is a claim about causal ancestry of a declared route. It is not a claim that rank measures awareness, action correctness, flexible access, or experience.

Partial operational embeddings of consciousness theories

The present variables do not preserve the complete structure of any major consciousness theory. At most they provide a partial operational embedding. Such an embedding requires:

  1. a registered content variable;
  2. a declared candidate mediator;
  3. a policy or commitment cut with stable semantics;
  4. interventions corresponding to the theory’s causal claims;
  5. longitudinal maps with explicit drift tolerances; and
  6. a statement of which theory-specific structures are omitted.

These are necessary bookkeeping conditions, not sufficient conditions for preserving an entire theory.

Global-workspace theories

A workspace state may be assigned to Mρ​, but one mediator-to-one-policy channel does not represent global availability. A fuller embedding would require heterogeneous consumer systems, competition or entry conditions, broadcast or ignition dynamics, and flexible use in memory, planning, valuation, report, and action. A high-rank Mρ​→Aρ∗​ route can exist without a global workspace, and a workspace can broadcast information irrelevant to the selected action.

Workspace-inspired architectural gains therefore concern organization and function [4]. Arguments that language agents may satisfy conditions derived from global workspace theory remain explicitly conditional on accepting the relevant version of that theory as sufficient for phenomenal consciousness [43].

Higher-order and metacognitive theories

A higher-order state may be assigned to Mρ​, but the finite channel tests only tracking and downstream use at a selected cut. It does not establish that the state is genuinely higher-order, self-directed, conceptually appropriate, or constitutive of experience. A stale monitor can support accurate later language while failing to govern the current policy variable.

Recurrent-processing theories

Recurrent dynamics cannot be represented adequately by a static endpoint matrix alone. Dynamic response channels may register recurrence and behavior-hidden content structure, but recurrence does not entail access to Aρ∗​. A recurrent-processing theory’s constitutive claim about phenomenality would remain an additional bridge.

Integrated, closure, or causal-geometry theories

Integration and approximate closure concern candidate boundaries and internal organization. They do not by themselves specify content semantics, intervention equivalence, a commitment cut, or a transported route. Centering is therefore conditioned on rather than derived here [23]. Multiscale causal-atlas language can motivate a candidate H, but successful finite commutation does not establish a phenomenal geometry [24].

Biological or substrate-specific theories

A biological theory may add the premise that only particular living organizations can realize experience. The channel theorems neither imply nor refute that premise. Strong conclusions against artificial consciousness depend on additional claims about embodiment, biology, organization, or meaning [10].

Output is not automatically report

An exact equality

Y1+​=C0​

establishes only that a later output token equals an earlier label. Calling it a self-report requires assumptions about:

  • provenance from the candidate system;
  • semantic interpretation;
  • memory of the relevant event;
  • reliability and calibration;
  • absence of cue leakage or replay;
  • absence of post hoc reconstruction from public information;
  • connection between the report content and the purported experience.

The formal no-go intentionally uses “delayed labeled output” rather than “self-report.”

Perceived consciousness

Perceived consciousness can be represented as an observer-dependent attribution process,

ΦO​=fO​(Y,B,framing,observer state).

Surface metacognitive reflection and emotional expression can alter this attribution without identifying Q, K, Siso, or phenomenal consciousness [8]. This does not make perceived consciousness unimportant; it makes it a different empirical target.

Moral status

Moral status is not a readout of route rank. A normative decision may depend on credence in sentience, possible valence, interests, agency, social relationships, rights, uncertainty, and asymmetric harms. Precautionary governance can therefore be justified while uncertainty about consciousness remains unresolved [7].

Interpretation

The member SK​ is a gate-recovery model: the full early content-to-commitment route exists, while overt behavior is blocked downstream. The member S1​ is a route-growth model: early joint responses distinguish every content, but the declared early mediator route carries no content distinction.

The theorem concerns registered mediated control, not awareness. It also does not establish that the delayed labeled output is introspective or that the early and late response encodings share a latent neural mode.

Causal scope of Theorem 2

Theorem 2 is a channel inequality. It does not identify Qρ​, Kρ​, H, Sρiso​, or their defects from D.

A low in-sample defect can be misleading when:

  • H is optimized after observing both stages;
  • PZ​ or PA​ is selected from many candidate alignments;
  • one channel is rank deficient, allowing many transports;
  • mediator interventions lack positivity;
  • installed mediator states are off-manifold;
  • stagewise interventions change different downstream contexts;
  • measurement maps drift;
  • only eventual responders are retained.

The backward causal interpretation requires a transport registration independent of the tested outcomes, or a held-out validation procedure with a matched-complexity null.

Equal alphabet size and similar response geometry do not define a causal bijection between interventions on U and C1​. Measurement invariance is therefore distinct from response alignment. The latter is not evidence of a common latent mode.

A related selection null occurs when only eventual responders contribute usable late-stage data. Conditioning on recovery can induce apparent continuity between early telemetry and late behavior even if the corresponding relation is absent in the full population.

Post hoc transport-map overfitting

If H, PZ​, and PA​ are jointly selected from large classes after viewing both stages, a small fitted value of

A matched-complexity permutation or relabeling null and held-out intervention cells are required before the fitted defect is interpreted.

Direct sensorimotor bypass null

A system may support command following through

while the declared monitor remains constant. Total control can then be genuine and high-rank, but the selected mediated route has rank one. Such a system is a countermodel to claims about a particular monitor, not to command-sensitive control in general.

AI intervention-nonmodularity null

In an artificial system, replacing Mρ​ with a stored activation may also change normalization statistics, attention patterns, cache contents, or later computation. Then the experimental Kρ​ does not represent the downstream use of a naturally generated mediator state. The discrepancy can make Γρ​ large even when the same tensor location is manipulated.

What the null systems establish

The null suite blocks the following unqualified implications:

  • content decoding ⇒ current decision control;
  • endpoint response opening ⇒ persistence of a latent mode;
  • accurate delayed output ⇒ earlier introspective access;
  • total late control ⇒ late control through the declared mediator;
  • late mediated control ⇒ early mediated control;
  • low in-sample transport error ⇒ causal ancestry;
  • mediated control ⇒ phenomenal consciousness.

It does not establish the negations of those conclusions.

Evidence that can narrow the compatibility class

Potentially informative evidence includes:

  1. controlled content interventions that distinguish content response from arousal or nuisance variation;
  2. repeated within-stage sampling that resolves engagement mixtures;
  3. an independently validated policy variable at the assay cut;
  4. selective mediator perturbations with support and specificity checks;
  5. same-run and separately randomized interventions permitting estimation of Γρ​;
  6. perturbations of downstream gates that separate policy selection from execution;
  7. anatomical or implementation evidence constraining H before late outcomes are analyzed;
  8. explicit bypass audits constraining β1​;
  9. report-provenance tests excluding public logs, cue leakage, and retrospective reconstruction;
  10. simultaneous confidence sets covering channel estimation, measurement drift, transport, and route defects.

Healthy-participant central and peripheral recordings may help develop motor-independent task assays, but they do not identify the complete route required here [28].

Prospective validation protocol

A prospective implementation should, at minimum:

  1. Preregister the assay. Fix the candidate boundary, K content interventions, mediator, commitment cut, external outcomes, and tolerated off-manifold behavior.
  1. Fix the mappings before outcome analysis. Specify PZ​, PA​, and the admissible family for H using anatomy, implementation documentation, or an independent calibration sample.
  1. Reserve held-out interventions. Estimate kernels and choose any transport parameters on one subset, then evaluate commutation on held-out target and mediator interventions. Held-out quantitative probing has been proposed as a general strategy for testing causal models against independent quantitative constraints [46].
  1. Estimate both mediation objects where feasible. Compare Qρ​Kρ​ with a same-run route intervention to bound Γρ​.
  1. Audit total versus route-specific control. Estimate or bound β1​ rather than assuming that all late control passes through the candidate mediator.
  1. Use matched-complexity nulls. Compare optimized transport defects with permutation, relabeling, measurement-drift, and independently generated stage-pair nulls.
  1. Construct simultaneous uncertainty sets. Account jointly for estimation error, model selection, channel drift, and all defect bounds.
  1. Apply a preregistered rank-margin rule. Claim an early lower bound above r only if the total tolerance is below μr​ for the late estimate.
  1. Declare rejection outcomes. A held-out defect above threshold, failure of positivity, unstable commitment semantics, or a bypass exceeding its registered bound must reject the transport interpretation rather than merely prompt a new post hoc mapping.

If these measurements are unavailable, the appropriate conclusion is non-identification, not a positive or negative diagnosis of covert awareness.

Mathematical verification criteria

The formal results would be mathematically invalid if:

  1. a model in Theorem 1 failed to generate DK​;
  2. the actual joint and behavioral channels in that theorem failed to have ranks K and 1;
  3. a constructed early route channel failed to have stochastic nonnegative rank j;
  4. row-stochastic matrices satisfying Theorem 2 violated its total-variation bound;
  5. the approximate-rank inequality failed under the stated permutation and tolerance conventions.

Such a counterexample would reveal a definition or proof error. It would not be an empirical falsification of a consciousness theory.

Empirical rejection criteria

An applied backward-transport claim should be rejected when any of the following occurs:

  • registered content interventions do not reproduce across stages;
  • the proposed early mediator is absent or not interventionally re-identifiable;
  • the commitment cut changes functional meaning;
  • mediator interventions are unsupported or strongly off-manifold;
  • held-out δQ​, δK​, or Γρ​ exceeds the preregistered threshold;
  • the fitted transport performs no better than a matched-complexity null;
  • measurement drift explains the apparent response alignment;
  • unresolved selection or stage mixtures account for recovery;
  • a late bypass makes β1​ too large;
  • delayed output depends on cue leakage, public logs, or later reconstruction;
  • the combined tolerance reaches the relevant rank margin.

These failures reject the proposed ancestry certificate. They do not establish absence of experience.

Scope condition for phenomenal claims

A phenomenal conclusion is outside the validated scope unless the claimant supplies an explicit bridge axiom and states whether it is intended as an empirical hypothesis, constitutive theory, metaphysical thesis, or normative presumption. The conservative-extension lemma is not itself an empirical test of phenomenality.

Limitations

First, the sharp identified set {1,…,K} is conditional on a fully typed class. It does not hold for a compatibility class that leaves early target or commitment alphabets unrestricted. Externally silent internal states can increase the rank without altering the declared K-content evidence.

Second, the principal causal estimand requires a coherent same-run edge or path intervention. Such interventions may be undefined, infeasible, or nonidentifiable in systems with shared context, recanting structures, hidden confounding, intervention-induced context changes, or inadequate support. The reconstruction defect Γρ​ records disagreement between two channels but does not solve these causal problems.

Third, Theorem 2 is oracle-level. The declared external data do not generally identify Qρ​, Kρ​, H, Sρiso​, Γρ​, or βρ​. A transport selected after observing both stages can fit without representing ancestry.

Fourth, qC​ and ωCvis​ are finite assay statistics. The former describes behavior-hidden joint response dimensions; the latter describes endpoint visibility of matched contrasts. Neither establishes latent-state, spectral, or neural-mode persistence. Dynamic realization claims require stronger preparation, excitation, minimality, measurement-invariance, and separation assumptions [2].

Fifth, Aρ∗​ is a model-dependent policy variable, not an established clinical observable. No supplied source validates a unique motor-independent commitment measure in patients with disorders of consciousness. Without such a measure, separating policy selection from preparation, arousal, working memory, and retrospective decoding remains difficult.

Sixth, exact stochastic nonnegative rank can be high for a channel arbitrarily close to rank one. Robust interpretation requires effect-size reporting, simultaneous confidence bounds, and a nontrivial rank margin. Exact factorization may also be computationally difficult in practice, but this manuscript makes no independent complexity-theoretic claim.

Seventh, two stages compress potentially continuous recovery, learning, plasticity, lesion evolution, medication effects, fluctuating engagement, and compensatory recruitment. More stages may improve description but do not eliminate the route-ancestry problem.

Eighth, delayed labeled output may be generated by memory, inference, cueing, replay, or public records. Conversely, failure to produce such output may result from language or motor impairment. The model does not validate either inference without additional evidence.

Ninth, artificial-system interventions can be especially nonmodular. Fine-tuning, checkpoint replacement, retrieval augmentation, tool restoration, and scaffolding may change the candidate boundary, mediator alphabet, policy semantics, or temporal cut. Functional gains in consciousness-inspired architectures remain evidence of function rather than direct evidence of phenomenality [4].

Tenth, the empirical source base is limited. Some relevant records are preprints, reports, healthy-participant studies, conference records, or AI-generated speculative drafts. The two immediate formal precursors are not human peer reviewed [1] [2]. They document conceptual provenance but do not establish authority, priority, or clinical validity.

Finally, the framework contains no theory-neutral detector of phenomenal consciousness or moral status. The conservative-extension lemma identifies a missing formal bridge; it does not resolve the metaphysics of consciousness.

Author response to unresolved review concerns

The revision accepts the formal objections that the former manuscript’s untyped compatibility class did not justify a universal upper endpoint K, that QK was incorrectly described as route-isolated, that concatenated marginals were not a joint channel, and that the former finite opening index did not identify mode persistence. The exact no-go is now restricted to a typed class; randomized and same-run channels are separated by Γρ​; a genuine joint channel replaces concatenation; and the opening statistic is explicitly limited to endpoint response visibility.

The clinical identification problem is not claimed to be resolved. In many patient settings the required internal interventions, policy variable, and transport validation may remain unavailable. The retained conclusion is therefore deliberately asymmetric: the framework can expose invalid backdating and specify sufficient conditional bounds, but it does not currently provide a generally executable diagnostic assay.

The revision also preserves substantive disagreement about phenomenal interpretation. Some consciousness theories may treat particular architectural or biological properties as constitutive or sufficient. The formal results do not refute those bridges; they show only that such bridges are additional premises rather than consequences of the registered channel algebra.

Conclusion

Later recovery does not determine the earlier causal route. Exact delayed labeled output, content-sensitive telemetry, behavior-hidden joint response, and maximal late control can arise from an early route hidden behind a closed output gate, a newly constructed route, a stale monitor, replay from memory, changing engagement mixtures, measurement drift, or an unmeasured bypass.

The no-go result is sharp only after the model class is fully typed. When the target and commitment alphabets are exactly the K registered labels, every value is intervention-supported, and the same-run route agrees with the randomized reconstruction, one external recovery law is compatible with every early route rank in

{1,…,K}.

Its interval hull is [1,K]. Without those restrictions, K is not a justified upper endpoint.

A positive backward inference requires more than recovery. It requires registered content and commitment identity, a constrained mediator transport, approximately commuting encoder and controller maps, and explicit bounds on the disagreement between randomized and same-run mediation. Under those premises,

rank+,TVε​(S0iso​)≥rank+,TVε+Γ0​+δQ​+δK​+Γ1​​(S1iso​).

If total late control is used instead, the route-completeness defect β1​ must also be included. If the kernels, transport, and defects are not independently identified or validated, the theorem remains an oracle-level conditional statement.

The result backdates, at most, a tolerance-dependent lower bound on differentiation through a declared content-to-commitment route. It does not establish correct action, flexible global access, introspection, awareness, phenomenal consciousness, or moral status. Those targets require additional empirical registrations, theory-relative bridges, and, for moral policy, separate normative reasoning.
