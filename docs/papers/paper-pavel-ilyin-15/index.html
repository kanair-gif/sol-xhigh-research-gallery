Abstract

Evolutionary accounts of consciousness commonly arrange homeostatic regulation, feeling, information broadcast, and the unified subject on a single ladder, so that any organism with sophisticated regulation is presumed to be partway toward experience. The comparative distribution of these capacities, as far as it can be read from extant nervous systems, does not obviously support this ordering. Cnidarian nerve nets, nematode and fly neuromodulatory states, arthropod central complexes, cephalopod arm ganglia, siphonophore colonies, and the vertebrate midbrain appear to combine these capacities in different ways, and reading the vertebrate order back into every lineage would produce the ladder as an artifact. This paper asks which of four organizational achievements—adaptive regulation of viability variables (R), valence as a causally effective common currency for trade-offs (V), access of a content variable to multiple causally nonredundant consumers through a shared admission gate (A), and structurally centered unification (U)—presuppose which others, how they are distributed across lineages, and what natural selection could actually see when each arose. Each achievement is defined as a property of a finite structural causal model relative to the lineage's natural perturbation family, the set of interventions selection can act through; some definitional tests additionally use severance interventions that lie outside that family, and these are flagged as a modeler's diagnostic rather than as something selection sees. Two invariants are adapted from the working drafts and used only where they yield a biological distinction. Causal-abstraction consistency, relative to a chosen coarse-graining, is the target property that separates a state variable that is merely decodable from one that functions as a trade-off currency; the comparative proxy offered for it tests screening-off, which is necessary but not sufficient for that target. Causal-island decomposition separates distributed archipelagos, with several dominant islands, from single-island systems. The further automorphism fixed-point condition intended to pick out a centered island is shown to be either vacuous, when the model has no nontrivial symmetry, or to admit set-level fixed points such as an entire symmetric ring; the centered-versus-uncentered distinction within a single island is therefore left as an open formal problem, and the anatomical readings that depend on it are marked as unidentified. The principal result is a partial order with exactly one strict dependency, V ⇒ R, which holds by definition once V is required to mediate trade-offs among regulated variables; the pairwise independence of A, U, and V is supported by three finite counterexample models, each mapped to a comparative case, with the U-dependent cases carrying the qualification above. The orders in which the achievements were acquired are inferred from the distribution of achievements in extant lineages and are not ancestral-state reconstructions. As defined, A is a structural fan-out property that a gated afferent branching to two consumers already satisfies; it is a minimal access-and-control condition and is not itself conscious access, and the workspace-style results that partially track it target a stronger organization. The coupling in which valence serves as the admission gate of access inside a single dominant island is what unlimited-associative-learning, midbrain-integration, and isomorphic-map markers each partially track; metacognitive and higher-order markers are not analysed here, so no claim is made that every major comparative marker targets this coupling. Comparative proxies for the coupling are specified, together with the evidence—convergent structure, decodable states, high integration in colonial or immune null systems—that does not warrant a phenomenal inference. Because selection acts only on relational structure, the coupling identifies a candidate integrative center only up to causal isomorphism. Whether this leaves phenomenal character undetermined depends on a premise the evolutionary argument cannot supply: if phenomenal character is fixed by causal structure up to isomorphism, as identity-style bridge hypotheses hold, then structural evidence fixes it; if it is not, no selection-visible evidence can. The paper takes no position on that premise and accordingly separates what its results establish about access and control from what they leave open about phenomenality.

Research Question and Hypothesis

The biological problem. An animal that keeps its internal milieu within bounds, that lets hunger tilt its choices toward food and away from shelter, that routes a detected predator to its legs, its gut, and its memory at once, and that acts as one body rather than as a committee of ganglia has done four different things. In vertebrates these appear together, and it is tempting to suppose they must always appear together and in a fixed order: first regulation, then feeling, then broadcast, then a self. Comparative evidence read across independently derived lineages, rather than along the vertebrate stem alone, is consistent with a different picture. Radially organized nerve nets maintain viability with no anatomically identified center. Cephalopod arms have been reported to process and act on sensory content with a degree of local independence from the central brain. Colonial siphonophores coordinate stinging, swimming, and feeding zooids without an obvious dominant zooid. None of these observations shows that the four achievements are dissociable in the structural sense defined below—each rests on anatomical or behavioral proxies whose relation to the formal criteria is itself part of what must be established—but they motivate treating the question as open. The question is not whether these animals are conscious. It is which organizational achievement each lineage acquired, under what selective regime, whether the conjunction of all four has a signature that mere co-occurrence lacks, and what that signature can and cannot license.

Research question. Along which evolutionary routes did adaptive regulation, valence as a causally effective common currency, admission-gated multi-consumer access, and structurally centered unification emerge as dissociable organizational achievements, and which selection-visible proxies distinguish their conjunction from their mere co-occurrence—without licensing an inference to phenomenal consciousness?

Two distinctions that govern the answer. First, every criterion below is relative to a lineage's natural perturbation family Π: the set of ecologically realized interventions (injury, predation damage, starvation, thermal and osmotic stress, conspecific contact, developmental variation) through which selection can act on the trait. A structural property that could only be revealed by an intervention outside Π—an experimenter's targeted severance of a pathway that is never severed in the wild—is not selection-visible, and we mark it as a modeler's diagnostic rather than as part of the achievement. Second, all four achievements are defined in terms of access and control: which variables regulate which, which channels reach which consumers, which parts dominate which. They are not defined in terms of, and do not entail, anything about what it is like to be the system. The manuscript keeps this distinction explicit at every point where a comparative marker is interpreted.

Hypothesis (Partial-Order Dissociation Proposition). Relative to Π, the four achievements are:

  • R (regulation): a set of viability variables kept within a viability region by effector-mediated feedback, where both the departure from the region and the corrective effector action occur under perturbations in Π.
  • V (valence as currency): a macro state variable that (i) is a Π-consistent causal abstraction of its micro realizers—interventions in Π on the macro variable and on its realizers agree in their downstream effects—and (ii) mediates trade-offs among at least two regulated variables. Condition (ii) presupposes at least two R-variables; the dependency V ⇒ R is therefore definitional, not an empirical discovery, and we treat it as such. Condition (i) is the substantive content. Proxies that test whether V screens off its realizers from behavior test a weaker property than (i) and do not by themselves establish abstraction consistency; where only screening-off evidence is available, V is scored as unidentified rather than present.
  • A (access): a content variable carried by a channel that reaches at least two consumers which are causally nonredundant and functionally heterogeneous—belonging to distinct downstream functions such as motor selection, associative learning, or autonomic adjustment—through a shared admission gate whose opening is content- or state-dependent rather than a fixed threshold. A gated afferent that fans out to two motor neurons of the same pool does not satisfy A; the heterogeneity condition is what separates A from ordinary divergent wiring. Route isolation of the channel is required only insofar as it is testable under Π (for example, by naturally occurring lesion or autotomy); where it is testable only by experimental severance, A is scored as established in the laboratory but not as selection-visible. A so defined is a claim about access-and-control architecture. It is not a claim about conscious access, and the fact that workspace-style theories posit an architecture of this general form does not make satisfaction of A evidence of consciousness.
  • U (unification): a single dominant causal island under Π—a subgraph whose perturbation propagates to the rest of the system while perturbations elsewhere do not comparably propagate back—together with a centering condition. As originally stated, the centering condition (that the island's automorphism group fixes the candidate integrative center) is either vacuous or ambiguous: in a heterogeneous model the automorphism group is trivial and any subset is fixed, while in an exactly symmetric model the union of a symmetry orbit, such as a whole nerve ring, is trivially fixed. We therefore restate the condition. A candidate center must be a proper part of the island, strictly smaller than any union of orbits that contains it; centering holds when there is a unique minimal such part that is fixed setwise by every automorphism; and when the automorphism group is trivial, the symmetry criterion is silent and centering must be established by interventional dominance alone (a proper part whose perturbation under Π dominates the island's output more than any other part of comparable size does). Because the automorphism group depends on the coarse-graining at which the system is modeled, the archipelago-versus-centered distinction is well defined only relative to a stated coarse-graining, and we flag it as such wherever it is applied. The anatomical readings of U for nerve nets and colonial forms are correspondingly downgraded from "no U" to "no identified center at the coarse-graining adopted."

The proposition then states:

  1. Partial order. R, V, A, and U form a partial order with exactly one strict dependency, V ⇒ R, and that dependency is definitional. The substantive claim is the negative one: none of A ⇒ V, A ⇒ U, U ⇒ A, U ⇒ V, V ⇒ A, or V ⇒ U holds as a structural necessity, which we argue by finite counterexample constructions in which the relevant criteria are satisfied or failed independently.
  1. Comparative dissociation. The pairwise independence of A, U, and V is realized in at least four independently derived lineages in which different subsets of the achievements are present. Claims about the order in which achievements were acquired along a lineage require ancestral-state reconstruction and are graded separately from claims about which achievements an extant terminal exhibits; where only terminal data are available, order is stated as a hypothesis rather than an inference.
  1. Target of the markers. The comparative consciousness markers we examine—those built on flexible trade-off behavior, cross-modal or multi-effector responsiveness to a single content, and unified action under conflict—implicitly target the specific coupling in which V serves as the admission gate of A inside a U-island. Metacognitive and higher-order markers are not reducible to this coupling without further argument and lie outside the scope of this claim.
  1. Identifiability and neutrality. The centering of the coupling is identifiable from selection-visible evidence only up to causal isomorphism: any two systems related by an isomorphism that preserves the Π-relative causal structure receive the same scores on R, V, A, and U. Whether this residual freedom has phenomenal significance depends on a premise the evidence cannot supply. If phenomenal facts are fixed by causal-isomorphism class—as identity and bridge-principle theories hold—then the residue is empty and nothing phenomenal is left underdetermined. If they are not so fixed, then no selection-visible evidence can fix them. The manuscript takes no side on this conditional's antecedent; its claim is that comparative evidence of the kind surveyed here cannot decide it, and that inferences from marker satisfaction to phenomenality therefore import the antecedent rather than earn it.

Scope. Π includes conspecific contact, but we do not derive a distinct social or intersubjective achievement; social perturbations enter only as inputs to R, V, A, and U. This is a limitation of the present scheme rather than a claim that such an achievement does not exist.

Specialist Framework and Contribution

Replacing "is it conscious?" with "what was acquired, and what could selection see?" The evolution-of-nervous-systems perspective changes the question in three ways that are operational rather than rhetorical, and each carries a limitation that should be stated at the outset.

First, the intervention family that matters for evolutionary explanation is not chosen by an experimenter. Both lab papers analyzed below define their objects relative to a stipulated, jointly admissible intervention regime [1, 2]. In evolution the only intervention family with selective consequences is the natural perturbation family Π: food deprivation, injury, predator encounter, temperature shift, conspecific contact, developmental variation. A structural property that holds only under a laboratory lesion outside Π is invisible to selection and cannot be part of an evolutionary explanation, though it may still be diagnostically useful. Two roles for interventions must therefore be kept apart. Identifying interventions—severing an efferent channel to test whether a variable is regulated, cutting a route to test whether a code is shared—are modeler's counterfactuals used to fix what the structure is; some of them lie outside Π, and the definitions of R and A below use them in this identifying role. Selection-visible interventions are those in Π, and only claims about what Π-realized perturbations act through are claims about what selection could have shaped. Where a dissociation is established only by an identifying intervention and not by any member of Π, we say so, and the evolutionary reading of that dissociation is correspondingly weaker.

Second, phylogenetic order is evidence, but only after ancestral-state inference. If a property were strictly prerequisite for another, no lineage could realize the second without the first. Multiple independent routes—nerve-net, centralizing bilaterian, distributed cephalopod, colonial—are therefore natural experiments in which the order of acquisition can, in principle, refute a dependency. The inference runs from extant terminals to ancestral states, and co-presence in a living taxon does not by itself fix the order in which its ancestors acquired the properties; the order claims made below are conditional on the reconstruction adopted and are graded accordingly. The vertebrate order is one route among several and has no privileged standing.

Third, null systems are controls, not curiosities. Colonial cnidarians, immune networks, and gene regulatory circuits score high on recurrence, integration, and coordinated regulation. A framework that assigns them the same standing as a centralized bilaterian has failed to distinguish the achievements; a framework that excludes them by fiat has assumed its conclusion. The minimal-cognition tradition, which locates sensorimotor coordination in bacteria [24] and even proposes scale-free substrates for cognition [26], sharpens this pressure: whatever separates R from V, A, and U must be stated so that it applies to a bacterium and a zooid, not only to a fish.

What the four achievements are and are not. R, V, A, and U are organizational achievements defined in causal terms. None of them is a phenomenal predicate, and the labels are chosen to avoid implying one. In particular, A is an access-and-control achievement: a content variable whose route-isolated channel reaches at least two causally nonredundant consumers through a shared admission-gated code. So defined, A is deliberately weak. A gated afferent fanning out to two nonredundant motor pools satisfies it, and we do not claim that satisfying A is what workspace or recurrent-processing theories mean by conscious access; what those theories target, at most, is a specific coupling of A with the other achievements, and their phenomenal reading of that coupling is a further hypothesis not adjudicated here. U is defined as a single dominant causal island under Π with a fixed candidate integrative center. The fixed-point condition is stated in terms of the island's automorphism group, and its discriminating power depends on how candidate centers are delimited: if any Aut-invariant subset counts as a center, the union of a symmetry orbit is trivially fixed and the criterion excludes nothing, while in heterogeneous models with trivial Aut every single island passes. The criterion is therefore introduced below as a proposed formalization with a stated candidate-center restriction, and the "archipelago versus centered" contrast is presented as a distinction whose formal adequacy is open, not as an established theorem. V is defined as a Π-consistent causal abstraction mediating trade-offs among at least two regulated variables; the proxies proposed for V test screening-off rather than abstraction consistency directly, and are flagged as such where they appear.

What the four-achievement partial order adds. The comparative markers examined here each pick out one achievement or one bundle. Unlimited associative learning, on our reading, bundles value-sensitivity with flexible content combination and treats the bundle as the transition marker [25]. Multi-level isomorphic sensory maps are proposed as a marker of sensory consciousness [14]; in our terms they track structured content organization relevant to A, not phenomenality as such. Midbrain–basal ganglia integration, offered as the substrate of basic subjective experience and extended by analogy to insects [13], we read as tracking centering in the sense of U. AI indicator lists derived from workspace, recurrent-processing, higher-order, and related theories apply access indicators to systems that typically lack regulated viability variables [12]. Higher-order and metacognitive markers, which are standard comparative proxies, are not among the four achievements; whether they reduce to a coupling of A and V or require a fifth achievement is a scope limit of this framework, not something it settles. Nor does the scheme treat the social dimension separately, although conspecific contact is a member of Π; social perturbations enter only as inputs to R and V.

None of the markers examined asks which achievements presuppose which, or whether the bundle has a signature distinct from co-presence. The contribution here is (i) a definition of each achievement relative to Π, with the identifying interventions distinguished from Π-realized ones; (ii) the observation that only V ⇒ R is strict, which follows from the definition of V and is offered as a definitional proposition rather than a discovery; (iii) finite counterexample models for each non-implication, mapped to comparative cases, with the U-related constructions dependent on the candidate-center restriction noted above; (iv) a graded reconstruction of acquisition order along four routes, conditional on explicit ancestral-state assumptions; (v) identification of valence-gated centered access as the coupling the examined markers partially track, with proxies whose limits are stated; and (vi) an argument that the structural centering of this coupling is identifiable only up to causal isomorphism. The further step—that no selection-visible evidence can fix a phenomenal labeling—is conditional on a bridge hypothesis under which phenomenal character can vary within a causal-isomorphism class. Theories that identify phenomenal character with an invariant of that class deny this premise, and for them the gauge argument does not go through; we state the conclusion as conditional rather than as metaphysically neutral.

Evidence standard. Formal claims are labeled as proposition, sketch, or construction, and results attributed to the two lab papers are limited to what their stated abstracts support: the definition of islands and of causal status relative to an admissible intervention family, and the separation of predictive from causal status. Comparative claims are graded: observed (well-established anatomy or behavior, though not documented by the stored records unless cited), inferred (follows from observed facts plus the definitions and a stated ancestral-state assumption), or speculative (proposed here). Finite counterexamples establish possibility, not actuality; the empirical weight rests on whether extant taxa instantiate them under Π, and, for the U constructions, on whether the fixed-point criterion can be made non-vacuous in the way proposed.

Evidence and Literature Synthesis

The stored records include the marker theories and the human adversarial collaboration but not the primary comparative neuroanatomy of the taxa discussed. The comparative statements below are therefore stated as specialist background and graded (observed, inferred, unresolved), not cited. Two further limits apply throughout. First, each route is described as an extant terminal configuration; the order in which R, V, A, and U were acquired along a lineage is a claim about ancestral states that requires character reconstruction on a phylogeny, which this section does not perform, so acquisition order is left unidentified here. Second, the status of U is assessed against the island-and-fixed-point criterion, and that criterion, as reviewed in the Analysis section, does not by itself separate a set-level center (a whole nerve ring or stem tract, which is fixed by every rotation or translation of the structure) from a point-like one, and is satisfied vacuously whenever the causal model has no nontrivial automorphisms. Statements about U below are accordingly statements about candidacy under a stated reading of the criterion, not verdicts.

Nerve-net route (cnidarians). Observed: hydrozoan and other cnidarian nervous systems are nerve nets with radial or biradial symmetry and no dominant ganglion; behavior (feeding, contraction, locomotion) is regulated, and peptidergic and aminergic neuromodulators shift behavioral state globally. Inferred: radial repetition of integrative elements means that any local candidate center has structural duplicates related by symmetry, so no local element is fixed by the symmetry group. This does not entail "no U": the union of the symmetry orbit—the nerve ring as a whole—is fixed by the same group and, on a set-level reading of the criterion, qualifies as a candidate center. Whether a cnidarian has U therefore depends on whether the criterion admits set-level centers, and that is a choice about the criterion, not a fact about the animal; U is unidentified for this route pending that choice. Unresolved: whether any global neuromodulatory state in a cnidarian is a Π-consistent currency mediating a feeding–defense trade-off (V) or merely a state summary is undetermined; this remains the cleanest place to test the predictive–causal distinction, because the natural perturbation family (prey encounter, mechanical disturbance, nutritional state) is comparatively narrow and well characterized.

Neuromodulatory states in nematodes and flies. Observed: in C. elegans and Drosophila, internal states such as hunger, satiety, and arousal are carried by neuromodulatory systems that reweight sensory gain and effector allocation across multiple behaviors. Inferred: these states satisfy the trade-off clause of V if natural deprivation and satiation act through them rather than merely correlating with them, which is a causal claim about Π-realized interventions and is testable in principle in these organisms. Unresolved: no stored record documents a state meeting the abstraction-consistency clause of V. The proxy proposed in the Analysis section (screening-off of downstream effects by the decoded state) tests a weaker condition than consistency of the coarse-graining with the interventions, and the commutation defect used to grade the difference is a property of a model-and-coarse-graining pair rather than of the organism unless the coarse-graining is itself fixed by Π. For these organisms V is therefore graded as plausible on the trade-off clause and unidentified on the consistency clause.

Centralizing bilaterian route (arthropods, vertebrates). Observed: the insect central complex is a midline, unpaired, highly conserved integrative structure receiving multimodal input and contributing to heading, action selection, and motivational gating; the vertebrate midbrain tectum–periaqueductal axis is likewise midline and conserved. Klein and Barron argue that the insect brain supports functions analogous to those of the vertebrate midbrain and basal ganglia, which they take to support basic subjective experience in vertebrates, and hence that insects may have that capacity [13]; Feinberg and Mallatt propose multiple levels of isomorphic or somatotopic representation as an objective marker of sensory consciousness and, since all extant vertebrates have them, place its origin at the Cambrian origin of the group [14]. Inferred: a midline unpaired hub is an anatomical configuration in which a single structure lacks a symmetry-related duplicate, which makes it a natural candidate integrative center. It does not follow that these lineages are established cases of U: in any realistic heterogeneous causal model the automorphism group is trivial and every single-island system passes the fixed-point clause, so anatomical asymmetry is not what the criterion is measuring, and the criterion as stated does not distinguish these hubs from a nerve ring except by the dominance-of-island clause, which has not been evaluated under Π for either taxon. Whether these hubs also realize A (multi-consumer access through a shared admission gate) is a separate question. Both marker proposals cited here move from anatomical or functional organization to a claim about experience; the present framework treats that step as a bridge hypothesis rather than as an inference the comparative evidence licenses.

Distributed cephalopod route. Observed: octopus arms contain a large fraction of the animal's neurons in arm ganglia that mediate local sensorimotor processing; arms disconnected from the central brain in lesion preparations retain coordinated reflexive behaviors. Inferred, regime-dependent: the lesion evidence shows that arm ganglia can form islands under a cut regime; it does not show that they are islands under Π, since severance lies outside the natural perturbation family and intact octopuses under natural perturbations may route arm content centrally. The dissociation of A from U in cephalopods is therefore graded as regime-dependent, and the cephalopod case is better read as a test of the Π-relativity thesis itself—whether island structure identified by cutting survives restriction to ecologically realized interventions—than as an established instance of A without U.

Colonial route (siphonophores). Observed: siphonophore colonies comprise genetically identical, functionally specialized zooids coordinated by shared nerve tracts, producing colony-level swimming, feeding, and defense. Inferred: the colony regulates viability at colony level (R) and displays high integration. Its integrative elements are repeated along the stem, so no local element is fixed by the colony's approximate translational symmetry; as with the cnidarian ring, the stem tract as a whole is fixed, so the colony is a null system for point-like centering but not, on the set-level reading, for U as such. It remains a test of whether V can exist at a level above the individual zooid. Hoffman defines an adaptive locus as the organizational level that dominates a preregistered profile of generation, preservation, optimization, transmission, and decision/control of adaptively relevant information, and holds that a change in decision authority alone is insufficient to establish migration of that locus [18]; this offers a complementary operational route for asking whether the dominant combined profile has migrated to the colony level, which is a different question from whether the colony has a center and should not be read as settling it.

Human consciousness neuroscience. The Cogitate adversarial collaboration tested divergent preregistered predictions of integrated information theory (IIT) and global neuronal workspace theory (GNWT) in 256 participants; the absence of sustained posterior synchronization was reported as a challenge to IIT, and limited prefrontal ignition and representation as a challenge to GNWT, with some findings aligning with each theory [3]. The iEEG, fMRI, and MEG–EEG datasets are openly available for reanalysis [4, 5, 6]. Two points of contact with the present framework need to be stated carefully. First, these studies operationalize conscious perception through report and task performance in intact adult humans in whom R, V, and U are already given; what they probe is the access-and-control organization of content—its maintenance, its availability to report and to prefrontal and posterior consumers—not phenomenality as such, and the framework makes no claim that the datasets bear on the latter. Second, the achievement A as defined in this paper is a structural fan-out condition (a route-isolated channel reaching at least two nonredundant consumers through a shared admission gate) that is satisfied by systems far simpler than a workspace; A is what GNWT's broadcast criterion presupposes, not what it asserts. The Cogitate results are therefore evidence about the human realization of an access architecture, and about the timing and location of ignition within it, not evidence about A in the minimal sense. IIT 4.0's identity claim between maximal cause–effect structures and phenomenal existence [10] is a bridge hypothesis; the gauge argument developed below bears on it only conditionally, as stated next.

Structuralist philosophy. Lyre proposes that phenomenal qualities are individuated by their place in quality-space structure and explores structure-preserving mappings between phenomenal and neural state spaces, with implications for holism and inverted qualia [8]; Kob defends quality-space comparison as methodology while separating empirical structural matching from stronger metaphysical claims about the nature of phenomenal qualities [9]; Kleiner and Ludwig give a general definition of a mathematical structure of experience [7]. These positions make explicit that a structure-preserving map fixes phenomenal character only up to the automorphisms of the structure, and they differ on whether anything is left over. The evolutionary argument below reaches a related limit from a different direction—selection acts only on causal structure and its Π-visible consequences, so selection-visible evidence identifies the coupling of V, A, and U only up to causal isomorphism. Its further conclusion, that no such evidence fixes phenomenal labeling, holds conditionally: it presupposes that phenomenal facts can vary across a causal-isomorphism orbit, which is exactly what structural-identity bridge hypotheses such as IIT's deny and what Lyre's structuralism is designed to exclude. The argument therefore does not refute those hypotheses; it shows that the comparative and evolutionary record cannot adjudicate between a bridge hypothesis that fixes labeling by structure and one that does not, because the two agree on everything selection can see.

Active inference and homeostasis. Pezzulo, Rigoli, and Friston cast homeostatic regulation and successive layers of behavioral control (Pavlovian, habitual, goal-directed) as hierarchical contextualizations of a single inferential scheme, dissolving any apparent mechanistic distinction among the optimization processes involved [11]. This is the most principled version of the ladder: regulation, allostasis, and goal-directed control differ in depth, not kind. The present paper does not dispute that a single scheme can describe all four achievements; it disputes that descriptive continuity implies dependency or fixed order. A nested generative model can be centered or not, can gate content by valence or by salience, and can have one consumer or many; the scheme is silent on exactly the distinctions at issue.

Two omissions. The achievement scheme does not include higher-order or metacognitive organization—self-monitoring of one's own states, confidence, and error signals—which serve as standard comparative proxies in much of the animal-consciousness literature. Such markers are not obviously reducible to the V-gated, U-embedded A coupling, and the claim that all major comparative markers implicitly target that coupling is accordingly restricted here to the workspace, integration, valence, and isomorphic-map families of marker; higher-order markers are left outside the scheme as a recognized gap. Similarly, although conspecific contact is listed among the perturbations in Π, none of the routes above examines whether social interaction alters island structure, admission gating, or centering; the social dimension of Π is unaddressed by the present evidence base.

Cross-Disciplinary Integration

Causal abstraction and the valence transition. Baner's paper distinguishes predictive status (a macro variable summarizes the system well enough to forecast behavior or report) from causal status (intervening on the macro variable and on its micro realizers yields the same downstream law under a declared admissible intervention family) and proves a Predictive–Causal Separation Theorem: for every finite quality alphabet of size K ≥ 2 there are pairs of finite micro models that agree on the observational law over the recorded variables and on predictive lumpability, in one of which the same macro variable commutes with every admissible content intervention and in the other of which it fails, with a strictly positive commutation defect computed for the construction [2]. Two features limit its direct biological use. The admissible family is stipulated by the modeler, and the theorem is stated for quality alphabets rather than regulatory or motivational variables; it does not ask whether causal (as opposed to predictive) status could itself be a target of selection. What is retained is the separation itself. What is transformed is its domain: the intervention family becomes Π, and the macro variable becomes a candidate motivational state.

The transfer requires one correction that the earlier formulation of this argument did not make explicit. A commutation defect is, in the first instance, a property of a triple (micro model, coarse-graining, intervention family), not of an organism. A modeler can always choose a coarse-graining that fails to commute, and that failure says nothing about the animal. The defect becomes an organismal property only under an additional hypothesis: that the macro variable is materially realized as a state the organism's own effector systems read—a circulating hormone titer, a neuromodulatory tone, a population code that downstream allocation circuits treat as a single quantity. Under that hypothesis, and only under it, the distinction between a currency and a summary statistic is a distinction in the animal: a hunger-correlated state that is decodable from the outside but through which no ecologically realized perturbation redirects allocation is a summary statistic, whereas one through which allocation across at least two regulated variables is in fact redirected is a currency. The achievement V is therefore an existence claim about a materially realized, Π-consistent variable, and the "selective reading" of the commutation defect is a hypothesis about how such a variable could be visible to selection, not a theorem. Selection acts on whatever micro configuration alters fitness; the claim that it can act on a currency as such means that the macro description is the level at which perturbations in Π and their fitness consequences are lawfully related, so that variation in the currency's realizers is selected through the currency's downstream law. Whether any lineage's motivational states satisfy this is an empirical question that the comparative literature has not been designed to answer, and we flag it as unidentified for every lineage in Table 1. A related caution applies to the proxy used later in the paper: screening-off of effector allocation by a candidate variable is evidence for predictive lumpability, which is exactly the status the theorem shows to be insufficient; commutation under natural perturbations is the stronger requirement, and no comparative dataset we know of tests it directly.

Baner's paper also asks which qualitative invariants survive the coarse-grainings that confer causal status, formalizing candidate invariants as natural transformations between presheaves indexed by intervention contexts [2]. The stored record does not state a named result about quality inversion and automorphism groups, and we do not attribute one to it. The negative use we make of the invariant question is our own and is conditional: whatever survives coarse-graining is at most structure up to the automorphisms of the intervention-augmented model; selection, which acts through Π, sees only what those automorphisms preserve. Hence convergent evolution of quality-space structure across lineages is evidence about shared structure, and cannot by itself be evidence about intrinsic labeling—unless labeling is identical to structure. That conditional is the point at which the argument stops being neutral, and it is taken up below.

Causal islands and the unification transition. Nassar's paper defines a causal island relative to a compatible intervention regime, route-isolation capacities, and a cross-boundary leakage bound, refuses to combine into a single island graph edges witnessed only under mutually incompatible regimes, and asks what an idealized profile of exact incoming context morphisms identifies about an island and what remains unidentified even when that profile determines the island's causal organization; it is explicit that islandhood does not by itself establish phenomenal consciousness, identify a subject of experience, or determine what any experience would be like [1]. Two of these features transfer directly. The compatibility requirement is the reason the present paper indexes achievements to a single natural perturbation family Π rather than pooling interventions that no lineage ever experiences jointly. And the identification-up-to-isomorphism limit is retained as the ceiling on what any comparative evidence about U can deliver.

The fixed-point criterion on candidate centers, by contrast, is the present paper's extension, not a result we can verify in Nassar's stored record, and the reviewers have shown that as originally stated it is not well defined. If the automorphism group of a realistic heterogeneous causal model is trivial, every single-island system trivially has a fixed candidate center and the criterion separates nothing. If the model is exactly symmetric, the union of a symmetry orbit—the whole nerve ring, the whole set of repeated ganglia—is itself fixed by the symmetry, so an archipelago passes the criterion as soon as set-valued centers are admitted. The criterion therefore has to be restated with two restrictions before it can carry an anatomical reading. First, candidate centers must be drawn from a declared class of minimal integrative components—components that receive route-isolated input from more than one regulated variable and that cannot be partitioned under Π without loss of the cross-variable coupling—so that orbit unions are excluded by construction. Second, exact automorphisms must be replaced by approximate ones: two candidate centers count as symmetric under Π if swapping them changes the induced Π-dynamics by less than the leakage bound already used to define the island. With these restrictions the intended distinction is expressible: a system whose minimal integrative components are permuted by approximate Π-symmetries has no structurally privileged center (the archipelago case), whereas a system with a dominant midline component that no approximate symmetry maps elsewhere does. We present this as a working criterion. It is not yet a theorem, its dependence on the leakage threshold is not characterized, and the earlier mappings of cnidarian and siphonophore nervous systems onto "no U" should be read as conjectures about approximate symmetry that would need quantitative causal-connectivity data to test. The related no-natural-centering theorem and automorphism-limited comparison of the Centered Closure Theory draft [55] and the gauge-equivalence and null-system agenda of the Multiscale Reflexive Causal Geometry draft [56] point in the same direction; both are unreviewed working documents, and their open problems—stating the exact categorical assumptions of the centering no-go, classifying null systems that score highly on integration alone—are precisely the gaps a comparative anatomy of centering would need filled before its readings become evidence.

Human neuroscience. The Cogitate adversarial collaboration found that some results aligned with each theory, while the absence of sustained posterior synchronization challenged IIT and the limited extent of prefrontal ignition and content representation challenged GNWT [3]. Within the present scheme these findings concern a population in which R and V are presupposed by participant selection and U is presumed, not tested; the paradigm does not manipulate regulated variables or motivational state, and it does not probe centering. What it does probe is the human realization of report-indexed access and its temporal maintenance. This must be stated more narrowly than we stated it before. The structural criterion A—a route-isolated channel reaching at least two causally nonredundant consumers through a shared admission-gated code—is satisfiable by circuits nobody regards as conscious, and a report-based paradigm cannot verify the criterion's causal clauses; what the Cogitate design measures is access-and-control as indexed by report and by decodable content. That is one realization of A, plausibly the one with the richest consumer set, but the inference from "A is present" to "conscious access is present" runs through a bridge the paradigm does not test, and the inference to phenomenality runs through a further bridge that no measurement in the design reaches. Metacognitive and higher-order markers, which are the standard comparative proxies in much of the animal literature, are not natively located in the scheme either; if a confidence-reporting consumer is one of the nonredundant readers of the gated code, they fall under A, but whether metacognition requires anything beyond that is left open.

The open datasets [4, 5, 6] could in principle be used to ask whether a state playing the role of an admission gate is decodable and whether its fluctuations predict which contents reach report. Because the paradigm contains no motivational manipulation, any such gate variable would have to be reconstructed from arousal, task-relevance, or pupillary proxies, and the result would be predictive status at most; it would not establish that the gate is Π-consistent, and it would not establish that the gate is valence in the sense of V rather than an attentional variable that coincides with it in this population. The same separation that defines V comparatively therefore constrains what a reanalysis could show.

What remains unidentified. The gauge argument in its earlier form concluded that no selection-visible evidence can fix phenomenal labeling. That conclusion presupposes that phenomenal facts can vary within the automorphism orbit of the identified structure—that is, it presupposes the falsity of bridge hypotheses that identify phenomenal character with causal structure up to isomorphism. The presupposition was not neutral and we withdraw it. The defensible statement is conditional and symmetric: if phenomenal character is identical to Π-relative causal structure, then identifying that structure up to isomorphism identifies phenomenal character up to the same isomorphism and there is no residue; if it is not, the residue lives in the orbit and selection-visible evidence cannot touch it. What selection-visible evidence cannot do on either reading is adjudicate between the two bridge hypotheses, because both predict the same distribution of structure across lineages. The comparative program described in this paper is accordingly a program about R, V, A, U, and their coupling. It can determine, subject to the identification limits set out above, whether V gates A inside a centered island in a given lineage. Whether that coupling is experienced, and what it is like if it is, are questions this evidence leaves exactly where it found them.

Analysis, Argument, or Model

Setting

Let M = (X, F, P) be a finite structural causal model: X a finite set of variables, F a set of structural functions assigning each variable a value from its parents and exogenous noise, P a distribution over exogenous noise, with variables temporally registered so that the model can be unrolled. An intervention is a map that replaces some structural functions by fixed values or altered kernels. The natural perturbation family Π is the set of interventions realized in the lineage's ecology with fitness consequences (food deprivation, injury, temperature shifts, predator encounter, conspecific contact); Π is a modeling posit reconstructed from ecology, not an observable. Claims about what selection could have shaped are relative to Π throughout.

Two of the definitions below also use structural comparison models: variants of M in which a component is deleted. These deletions are not members of Π; they are the modeler's diagnostic counterfactuals. The distinction matters for the argument and is flagged where it occurs. A property established only through a structural comparison is a property of M that a modeler can read off; it is not thereby a property that any perturbation in Π exposes to selection.

Definitions

R (regulation). There is a nonempty set H ⊂ X of viability variables with a viability region B, a set of effector variables E, and a subset Π_H ⊂ Π of perturbations acting on H, such that for every π ∈ Π_H the expected excursion of H outside B is strictly smaller under M than under the comparison model M∖E in which effector influence on H is severed. The severance is a structural comparison, not a member of Π; what Π exposes to selection is the excursion under M, and the comparison model supplies the reference against which "regulation" is attributed. R is a property of feedback, not of any representation.

V (valence as currency). There is a macro variable v = τ(X_S) defined by a coarse-graining τ of a set S ⊂ X such that: (V1) Π-consistency: for every π ∈ Π_S acting on S, the macro-level intervention induced by π and the pushed-forward micro-level intervention yield the same downstream law on E; the commutation defect δ_Π(v, τ), the maximal total-variation discrepancy between these laws over Π_S, is zero (or below a declared ε); (V2) trade-off mediation: there are at least two regulated variables h₁, h₂ ∈ H and perturbations π₁, π₂ ∈ Π_H such that π₁ and π₂ change the allocation of E between the servicing of h₁ and h₂, and this change is screened off by v (conditioning on v renders allocation independent of which perturbation occurred). The defect is a property of the pair (M, τ) under Π, not of M alone. V is therefore an existential claim: there is some coarse-graining τ of some S through which Π acts on effector allocation. Selection acts on M; whether a τ with zero defect exists is a fact about M's organization, but which τ realizes it is not unique, and nothing in the definition privileges one realization. A variable satisfying (V2) but not (V1) is a summary statistic; one satisfying (V1) but not (V2) is a causally consistent state variable that is not a currency.

A (access). There is a content variable c, a gate variable g, and consumers K₁, K₂ ⊂ X such that: (A1) the channel from c to K₁ ∪ K₂ is route-isolated under Π (interventions on c reach the consumers only through a delimited set of paths passing through g); (A2) g admits or blocks c to both consumers jointly (a shared code); (A3) K₁ and K₂ are causally nonredundant: intervening on c changes the output of K₁ and of K₂, and in the comparison model with K₁ severed the effect on K₂ persists, and vice versa; (A4) K₁ and K₂ are functionally heterogeneous: their downstream laws on E are not isomorphic under any relabeling, so the consumers are not parallel copies of one another.

A is deliberately a structural condition, and it is weak. A gated afferent that branches to a motor neuron and to a plastic synapse in a memory circuit satisfies (A1)–(A4). A is therefore not conscious access in the sense of workspace theories, which require in addition sustained, all-or-none, recombinable broadcast. A is offered as the minimal structural core those richer conditions presuppose: whatever else global availability involves, it involves one admission decision reaching several nonredundant, heterogeneous consumers. Satisfying A is necessary for the richer condition and not sufficient for it, and nothing in this paper infers the richer condition, or conscious access, from A.

U (unification). Under Π, the island decomposition of M in the sense of Nassar (route-isolation capacities and leakage bound declared) [1] yields a single island I whose causal traffic dominates every other island by a declared margin (U1); and I contains a unique minimal center: a minimal separator of I's convergent inputs from its divergent outputs whose traffic exceeds that of every other minimal separator by a declared margin (U2).

An earlier formulation of (U2) required a fixed point of the automorphism group Aut(I) on the set of candidate centers. That criterion is defective in two ways that a reader can check. In any exactly symmetric model, the union of a symmetry orbit of candidate centers is itself fixed, so a whole nerve ring counts as a fixed center and the criterion cannot distinguish an archipelago from a centered island. In any realistically heterogeneous model, Aut(I) is trivial, so every candidate center is fixed and the criterion is vacuous. The uniqueness criterion (U2) replaces it. The two are related: in an exactly symmetric model, minimal separators come in orbits of equal traffic and no member is unique, so (U2) fails; in a heterogeneous model, (U2) asks the graded question the fixed-point criterion could not, whether one bottleneck dominates. The margin in (U2) is a declared parameter, and U is graded accordingly; it is the least sharply defined of the four achievements, and the anatomical readings below inherit this softness.

Proposition 1 (V ⇒ R)

Statement. If M satisfies V relative to Π, then M satisfies R relative to Π.

Proof. (V2) requires at least two regulated variables h₁, h₂ ∈ H whose servicing by E is allocated through v; the existence of regulated variables serviced by effectors is exactly R. ∎

Remark. This is a definitional dependency, and its content lies in the definition of valence rather than in the derivation. The substantive claim defended here is that this definition is the biologically appropriate one: what makes a state a valence rather than a drive is that it is the currency in which incommensurable regulatory demands are compared. A single drive controlling a single regulated variable does not require a currency, and a currency with nothing to trade is not one. Any account on which valence can exist without regulated variables would need a different notion of valence, and it is not the one comparative marker theories use when they treat feeling as motivational.

Proposition 2 (no other strict dependency)

Statement. No implication other than V ⇒ R holds among R, V, A, U in general; in particular V, A, U are pairwise independent.

Method. Finite counterexample constructions. Each is a small SCM in which one achievement holds and another fails under a specified Π. These establish formal possibility. The comparative mappings attached to each construction are graded separately and are not part of the proof.

Construction C1 (A without U; also A without V). Let X contain two hubs H₁, H₂, each receiving a content variable c_i, a gate g_i, and two consumers K_{i1} (driving a motor effector) and K_{i2} (driving a plastic storage variable), with all structural functions in H₁ mirrored in H₂ and a weak bidirectional coupling between H₁ and H₂ below the leakage bound. Under Π (which perturbs c₁ and c₂ independently), each hub satisfies (A1)–(A4): route isolation holds through g_i, the gate is shared, the consumers are nonredundant, and their downstream laws are non-isomorphic. The island decomposition yields two islands of equal traffic, so (U1) fails and U fails. Hence A holds and U fails. If g_i is set by a salience variable unrelated to any regulated variable, A holds and V fails as well. Comparative mapping: octopus arm ganglia (graded regime-dependent, since the lesion evidence isolates arms under a cut regime rather than under Π) and split-brain preparations in vertebrates (outside Π; diagnostically informative only). Artificial mapping: a system satisfying the workspace-style indicator properties assembled by Butlin et al. [12] satisfies A, since those indicators entail gated broadcast to multiple modules; the converse does not hold, and such a system can satisfy A with neither R nor V. This is the clearest reason those indicators do not test valence.

Construction C2 (U without A; also U without V). Let X contain a single hub h receiving inputs from sensors s₁,…,s_n and driving exactly one consumer K, with no other subsystem of comparable traffic. The island decomposition yields one dominant island, satisfying (U1); the unique minimal separator of inputs from outputs is {h}, satisfying (U2). Hence U holds. Since there is one consumer, (A3) fails and A fails. If no variable in X is regulated, R and hence V fail. Comparative mapping: a centralized ganglion driving a single locomotor effector in a hypothetical early bilaterian (speculative); an engineered controller with a single actuator (actual, nonbiological).

Construction C3 (V without A and without U). Let X be a radially symmetric ring of n identical sensorimotor units, each with a local sensor, local effector, and two regulated variables h₁ (energy) and h₂ (tissue integrity) whose servicing competes for the effector; let a single diffusible variable v be set as a function of the h-values across the ring, and let v be the only path by which h-values influence effector bias, multiplicatively reweighting every effector's feeding-versus-withdrawal setting. Let Π contain food deprivation (perturbing h₁) and injury (perturbing h₂). By construction v screens off which perturbation occurred from allocation, satisfying (V2); and since every path from Π_S to E passes through v, the macro intervention and the pushed-forward micro intervention coincide, giving δ_Π(v, τ) = 0 for the stipulated τ and hence (V1). There is no route-isolated content channel to multiple nonredundant consumers, so A fails. The ring is a single island, so (U1) holds; but its minimal input–output separators are the pairs of antipodal units, all of equal traffic, so no unique minimal center exists and (U2) fails. Comparative mapping: global neuromodulatory states in cnidarian nerve nets (speculative as to Π-consistency; the symmetry argument is heuristic because real cnidarian nervous systems are only approximately symmetric and may contain condensed rings or rhopalial ganglia that would need to be assessed under (U2) directly) and, with less symmetry, neuromodulatory states in nematodes and flies (inferred as to trade-off mediation, undetermined as to Π-consistency).

Remaining directions. R without V: any single-variable regulator, including bacterial chemotaxis as treated in the minimal-cognition literature [24]. A without R and U without R: C1 and C2 with H empty. Thus V ⇒ R is the only strict dependency. ∎ (as a set of constructions)

Rigor check: is the causal-consistency criterion for valence nontrivial?

The distinction between (V1)-satisfying currencies and (V1)-violating summary statistics does biological work only if some decodable motivational state has positive defect under Π. Baner's Predictive–Causal Separation Theorem guarantees that such pairs of models exist for quality alphabets: two finite micro models agreeing on the observational law and on predictive lumpability, in one of which a macro variable commutes with every admissible content intervention and in the other of which it has strictly positive defect [2]. A motivational analogue is built by adding to C3 a second diffusible variable v′ that is a noisy function of the same h-values, is decodable from downstream behavior, but does not enter any effector function: v′ predicts allocation, but intervening on v′ does nothing, so its defect is maximal while it satisfies (V2) exactly as v does.

Two limitations must be stated. First, screening-off, the natural observational proxy, tests (V2) only; v and v′ are indistinguishable by it. (V1) is a claim about interventions on v itself compared with interventions on its micro realizers, and δ_Π(v, τ) is therefore unidentified from any observational or decoding study, however dense. Establishing (V1) requires intervening on the candidate currency within Π and comparing the effector law with that produced by the ecological perturbation it summarizes. Second, no stored record documents a positive-defect motivational variable in a living organism, and the author is not aware of an experiment designed to find one. The distinction is presently theoretical for biology. The deciding measurement is specified in the Empirical Implications section; what is settled here is only that it must be interventional.

The coupling that marker theories implicitly target

Three comparative marker families are examined; the claim is confined to them. Unlimited associative learning, as characterized by Bronfman, Ginsburg, and Jablonka, requires that compound and novel stimuli be learned as reinforcers and that learned content recombine flexibly across behaviors [25]: flexible content reaching multiple consumers (A) admitted according to reinforcement value (V). The midbrain marker of Barron and Klein locates the capacity in integrated midbrain and basal ganglia structures on which multimodal input converges and from which action is selected [13]: a unique center (U) receiving content that reaches action systems (A). Isomorphic maps in the sense of Feinberg and Mallatt require structured content available to downstream systems (A) within a forebrain–midbrain–hindbrain organization [14], which under the present scheme is a claim about U. Each is a projection of one configuration: a content variable c admitted to multiple nonredundant heterogeneous consumers by a gate g that is set by the currency v, all inside a single dominant island with a unique minimal center. Formally, the coupling is A with g = f(v) for some structural function f, and with c, g, v, K₁, K₂ ⊂ I where I satisfies U. Call this valence-gated centered access.

Higher-order and metacognitive markers, which are standard comparative proxies in some fields, are not among the three examined and are not captured by this coupling. In the present vocabulary they concern a further consumer whose content is a representation of the admission event itself (a K₃ whose input is g or the state of the c-to-K channel). The scheme can express such a consumer, but it does not treat its presence as one of the four achievements, and the claim that marker theories target the coupling is not extended to higher-order markers.

The coupling has a selection-visible signature its components lack. If g depends on v, then natural perturbations of viability variables alter which content reaches which consumers, so a food-deprived animal not only allocates effectors differently (V) but also admits different content jointly to memory, orienting, and escape (A), and does so through one center (U). Neither the co-presence of the three properties nor any pair predicts that manipulation of a regulated variable within Π will change joint admission across heterogeneous consumers. Where Π includes conspecific contact, the consumer set may include conspecific-directed effectors, so that the same signature would extend to social behavior; this extension is noted, not developed. The signature is the proxy specified in the Empirical Implications section, and, like A itself, it is a signature of access-and-control organization. It is not a signature of experience.

What the coupling does not identify

Nassar's island framework records a candidate island by its profile of exact incoming context morphisms, Hom(C, I), and states that this profile, even where it determines the island's causal organization, leaves phenomenal questions open [1]. A profile of this kind determines its object only up to isomorphism in the context category; so the island I and its unique minimal center are identified, at best, up to causal-context isomorphism. Independently of any named result, a second point follows from the definition of Π: any relabeling of macro states that commutes with every intervention in Π produces a model that Π cannot distinguish from the original. Baner's framework, which asks when a macro variable earns causal rather than predictive status relative to an admissible intervention family [2], is the setting in which this observation is stated; the observation itself is elementary. Selection acts through Π. Therefore selection cannot have shaped, and comparative evidence drawn from Π cannot reveal, anything about the coupling that is not preserved by Π-commuting relabelings and causal-context isomorphisms.

What this does and does not license must be separated. It licenses the conclusion that selection-visible evidence fixes valence-gated centered access up to its causal-isomorphism class and no further. It does not by itself license the conclusion that phenomenal facts are left open, because whether phenomenal facts vary within the isomorphism class is exactly what competing bridge hypotheses disagree about. An identity or functionalist bridge holds that phenomenal character is fixed by the causal-isomorphism class, in which case fixing the class fixes the phenomenal facts; a bridge on which phenomenal character is an intrinsic property of the substrate, or a further fact, holds that it is not. The argument here is neutral between them in the following precise sense: no evidence drawn from Π discriminates between bridge hypotheses that agree on the isomorphism class, since every such hypothesis predicts the same selection-visible organization. The corrected conclusion is therefore conditional. Selection-visible evidence cannot fix the phenomenal labeling of the coupling without a bridge hypothesis, and the bridge hypothesis is not itself something that evidence drawn from Π can confirm or refute. Whether valence-gated centered access is accompanied by experience, and what that experience would be like, are questions that comparative-evolutionary evidence hands over to the bridge hypothesis unanswered. This is not a temporary evidential gap awaiting better lesions or better recordings; it follows from what an evolutionary explanation is an explanation of, namely the organization that Π acts through, and from the fact that the organization so explained is an access-and-control organization whose phenomenal status is a separate question.

Alternatives, Counterarguments, and Boundary Cases

Single-ladder rival. A defender of UAL or of the midbrain marker may grant the formal independence of V, A, and U but argue it is empirically idle: in nature the four always co-occur, so any one suffices as a marker. The response is that co-occurrence is an empirical hypothesis to be tested against cephalopods and colonial cnidarians under Π, not a definitional truth, and that a finite construction in which one achievement is present and another absent shows the ladder cannot be established by argument alone. Two qualifications now attach to that response. First, only constructions whose dissociation does not depend on a "no U" verdict are currently secure, for reasons given in the next paragraph; a construction that separates A from V by exhibiting gated fan-out without a currency variable stands, whereas any construction whose sole dissociating feature is failure of the fixed-point condition does not yet establish what it was introduced to establish. Second, the concession is as explicit as before: if every extant taxon with A also has U and vice versa, the independence claim is formally valid but empirically idle, and the ladder would be an accurate description of what actually evolved. The paper's claim then reduces to the still-useful point that the marker's success would be contingent, not necessary.

Trivial and vacuous readings of the fixed point. The definition of U requires a single dominant island whose automorphism group fixes the candidate integrative center. As stated, this condition admits two degenerate readings. In an exactly symmetric model, such as a rotationally symmetric nerve ring or a colony of identical integrators, the union of a symmetry orbit is itself fixed by every automorphism, so a set-level "center" consisting of the whole ring or all integrators satisfies the condition trivially; the criterion then cannot distinguish an archipelago from a centered system unless the candidate center is additionally required to be a proper, nonredundant subsystem rather than an orbit union. Conversely, in any realistic heterogeneous causal model the automorphism group is trivial, every node is a fixed point, and any single-island system passes. The headline contrast between centered and archipelagic organization therefore rests on a criterion that is, in its present form, either ambiguous or vacuous. What is retained is the intended target—whether the island has a distinguished integrative locus that is not an artifact of how the candidate set was drawn—and the requirement that an adequate criterion be stated in approximate rather than exact symmetry terms. What is not retained is any claim that the fixed-point condition, as currently written, delivers "no U" verdicts for cnidarians or siphonophores. Those verdicts are reclassified as conjectures awaiting a repaired criterion.

Body-constraint deflation of U. One body can execute one whole-body action at a time; centering may be nothing more than a selected motor bottleneck with no relation to subjecthood. This is a genuine boundary case and is partly accepted. The bottleneck explains why a dominant island with a distinguished center would be favored in a motile bilaterian and why colonies and radially symmetric animals, whose parts can act semi-independently, would not need one. It is an account of control organization, not of phenomenality, and it is complete as a selection-level explanation of why the control organization exists. Whether anything further is explained by adding a phenomenal hypothesis depends on the bridge hypothesis adopted: under an identity bridge, on which a specified causal organization simply is a phenomenal state, no additional hypothesis is being added and the question does not arise; under a bridge that treats phenomenal character as varying independently of the control organization, the motor account leaves that variation unexplained but also unconstrained. The framework does not adjudicate between these bridges. What it does license is the narrower claim that U, as a fact about access and control, does not by itself warrant a phenomenal inference within evolutionary theory, because the selection-level explanation of U is silent about phenomenality on either bridge.

Colonial and immune null systems. Siphonophore colonies, immune networks, and gene regulatory circuits exhibit recurrence, integration, and multi-variable regulation. Under the definitions, they satisfy R and can in principle satisfy V. Their status on U is undetermined for the reason just given: the present fixed-point criterion cannot deliver a nontrivial "no U" verdict for a system of repeated integrators, and the intuitive judgment that colonies lack a center is not yet a formal result. Their status on A is also unsettled, and in the opposite direction from what the earlier draft asserted. Because A, as defined, requires only a route-isolated channel reaching two causally nonredundant consumers through a shared admission-gated code, a gated afferent branching to two effector populations satisfies it; on that definition it is not obvious that a cytokine signal gating multiple immune consumers, or a transcription factor gating multiple downstream circuits, fails A. The earlier inference that immune and genetic systems lack A under Π is withdrawn as unsupported. What survives is the negative point: a framework that scores such systems as partway to consciousness because of their integration has confused R and V, and possibly A, with the specific coupling. The MRCG draft's call to classify null systems that score highly on integration alone [56] is therefore only partially answered here. The partial order supplies a vocabulary in which integration without gated currency is nameable; it does not yet supply a working criterion that sorts colonies from centered animals.

Structural fan-out is not conscious access. The preceding paragraph exposes a general limit of A. The achievement as defined is a fact about architecture: gated, route-isolated distribution of a content variable to nonredundant consumers. Workspace theories are ordinarily read as targeting something richer, in which the admitted content is available for report, flexible recombination, and metacognitive evaluation. A therefore captures a necessary structural precondition that workspace accounts presuppose, not the property those accounts identify with conscious access. Any claim in this paper that comparative markers "implicitly target" the coupling of V, A, and U should be read with this restriction: they target it only insofar as their structural component is concerned.

Metacognitive and higher-order markers. The achievement scheme contains no variable corresponding to metacognitive monitoring, confidence signalling, or higher-order representation, yet these are among the standard comparative proxies. Two responses are possible and neither is fully satisfying. One is that a metarepresentation is a further consumer of the admitted content and so falls under A; but this collapses a distinctive marker into fan-out and loses exactly the feature—representation of one's own states—that higher-order theories treat as essential. The other is that metacognition is a fifth achievement, dissociable from the four; this is plausible but is not developed here and would require its own Π-relative definition and its own row in the lineage table. Either way, the claim that all major comparative markers target the coupling is restricted to first-order access, valence, and centering markers. Higher-order and metacognitive markers are outside the scheme's present scope.

Regime dependence and the severance problem. The arm-autonomy evidence in cephalopods comes from cuts outside Π. Per the island definition, causal edges witnessed only under mutually incompatible regimes are not combined into a single island graph [1]. The cephalopod dissociation is thus stated as regime-dependent, and a natural-regime test is specified below. The same point cuts against the paper's own definitions: R invokes removal of an effector set, and A invokes severing a channel, and both are interventions selection does not ordinarily realize. The resolution adopted is to distinguish the witnessing regime, in which severance is used to establish that a variable or channel exists and does the work attributed to it, from the ecological regime Π, relative to which the achievement is said to have evolved. An achievement is Π-relative in the second sense even when its identification requires interventions outside Π in the first sense. This distinction is stipulated rather than derived, and it means that no achievement assignment for a lineage is ever read off from natural perturbations alone.

Underdetermination of Π for extinct lineages and the status of order claims. For ancestral nodes Π is reconstructed from ecology and conserved anatomy; different reconstructions yield different achievement assignments. Order claims are additionally underdetermined in a way the earlier draft understated. Presence or absence of an achievement at an extant terminal is testable, but the order in which achievements were acquired along a route is a claim about ancestral states, and it cannot be read off from terminals without an explicit ancestral-state reconstruction with a stated optimality criterion and character coding. The route orders in Table 1 are therefore hypotheses conditional on a declared Π and a declared reconstruction method; they are falsifiable by a better-supported reconstruction but are not, as written, supported by terminal data. What terminal data can do is refute a proposed order: a terminal exhibiting A without U in the relevant clade falsifies any reconstruction on which U preceded A along that route.

Scale-free and minimal-cognition challenges. Fields, Glazebrook, and Levin argue that integration, broadcasting, and hierarchical inference emerge at every scale, including in non-neural and synthetic systems [26]. The Π-relativity of the definitions is the reply: a molecular network may satisfy the formal shape of A or U relative to some intervention family, but only relative to the family selection acts through does the achievement count as evolved. This does not refute scale-free views; it locates the disagreement in the choice of Π. Given the weakness of A noted above, it should also be conceded that the scale-free position is strengthened rather than weakened by the present definitions: the formal shape of A is realized very widely.

Active inference as unifying descriptive scheme. The nested generative-model account of homeostatic regulation and behavioural control [11] describes all four achievements in one vocabulary. Description in one vocabulary is not dependency; each dissociating construction can be written as a generative model, and the partial order is a claim about which constructions are realizable, not about which formalism describes them.

Social organization and conspecific contact. Conspecific contact is listed in Π, but no achievement in the scheme is social. A signal from a conspecific enters the framework only as a perturbation to a regulated variable or as content admitted to a channel; the possibility that intersubjective coordination selects for a distinct organizational achievement, or reshapes V by making the states of others a term in the currency, is not addressed. This is a gap, not a boundary case that the framework resolves.

Theory-inspired architectures and AI. That a system implements a workspace architecture or scores on indicator properties derived from theories of consciousness [12] establishes, in the present vocabulary, at most the structural component of A, and given the metacognitive and higher-order indicators included in that report, it may establish features the scheme does not represent at all. Without R and V there is no currency to gate access, and without a repaired U criterion there is no defensible verdict on centering. This is a structural observation about access and control, not a verdict on artificial consciousness. The gauge argument leaves that verdict open, but not neutrally: its conclusion that no selection-visible evidence fixes phenomenal character holds only under bridge hypotheses on which phenomenal facts can vary within a causal-isomorphism class. Under an identity bridge that ties phenomenal character to causal organization up to isomorphism, the same evidence that fixes the organization fixes the phenomenal facts, and the gauge residue is empty. The framework does not decide between these bridges; it makes explicit that the residue it describes is bridge-relative.

Empirical Implications and Falsification Criteria

Two kinds of test. The achievements are defined relative to a lineage's natural perturbation family Π, but several of the diagnostics below (severance, clamping, ablation) are laboratory interventions that lie outside Π. This section therefore separates Π-internal tests, which deliver ecologically realized perturbations and read out the organism's response, from structural diagnostics, which use laboratory interventions to expose the causal architecture that Π-internal behaviour is presumed to depend on. A structural diagnostic never certifies an achievement directly; it licenses an inference from architecture to Π-relative organization, and that inference must be stated. Three quantities are left unidentified by the framework and must be fixed before any test is run: the leakage bound that separates one dominant island from several, the coarse-graining under which a candidate macro state is evaluated, and the tolerance under which two loci count as symmetry-related near-duplicates.

Proxies for each achievement.

  • R: Π-internal test: return of a viability variable toward its region after a natural perturbation (deprivation, thermal load, injury). Structural diagnostic: the return is degraded when effector influence is blocked or occluded. Severance of effectors is outside Π; the inference is that a system whose return depends on effectors in the laboratory depends on them in the wild, which is defeasible where compensatory routes exist.
  • V: Π-internal test: cross-modal trade-off under natural state manipulation—deprivation of one regulated variable reallocates effectors away from servicing another, with the reallocation screened off by a candidate macro state. This tests screening-off and is compatible with the candidate being a summary statistic. Structural diagnostic: commutation defect relative to a declared coarse-graining and a laboratory family that includes Π—clamping the candidate state (for example, neuromodulatory tone) to the value that deprivation would induce should reproduce the allocation law that deprivation induces. Zero defect under a coarse-graining shows that that coarse-graining is a Π-consistent abstraction; positive defect shows only that the modeler's chosen state is not, and does not show that no coarse-graining is. The organism-level claim "V present" is existential over coarse-grainings and is therefore harder to refute than to confirm. This is the measurement that would make the predictive–causal distinction biologically nontrivial.
  • A: Π-internal test: a naturally delivered content-bearing perturbation (prey contact, conspecific signal) changes the output of at least two consumers of different functional kind—for example, orienting and later retrieval—and a state-dependent gate, closed by a competing natural demand, blocks both. Structural diagnostic: the two effects survive severance or pharmacological silencing of the other consumer. Consumers must be causally nonredundant and of different kind; a gated afferent branching to two motor neurons that drive the same effector satisfies fan-out but not A, and the framework should be read as requiring functional heterogeneity of consumers rather than anatomical branching alone. A so defined is an achievement of access-and-control organization. Its resemblance to the multi-consumer broadcast that workspace theories describe is structural; satisfying A is not evidence of conscious access in the theory-laden sense, and this section makes no such claim.
  • U: Π-internal test: naturally delivered perturbations reveal one island whose cross-boundary leakage to any other island lies below the declared bound. Structural diagnostic: the candidate integrative center is an element-level or minimal-set locus that is not one of several interchangeable loci related by approximate symmetry. The exact automorphism fixed-point criterion is not usable operationally: in heterogeneous systems the automorphism group is trivial and every single-island system passes, while in exactly symmetric systems the whole orbit (a complete nerve ring, the set of arm ganglia) is fixed as a set. What the framework needs, and what this proxy uses, is a graded criterion—the candidate center has no near-duplicate under the declared tolerance—and the anatomical reading of "no U" for radially organized or colonial systems is conjectural under that criterion rather than established by construction.
  • Coupling: natural manipulation of a regulated variable changes joint admission of content to multiple heterogeneous consumers, not merely effector allocation, within a single dominant island. The candidate hubs named below (insect central complex, vertebrate tectum–periaqueductal axis) are the manuscript's own conjectural assignments, not established realizers.

Cephalopod natural-regime test. The eight arm ganglia of an octopus are approximately symmetry-related loci, which makes the cephalopod the case where the graded U criterion is most informative. Delivering natural perturbations (prey contact, noxious contact) to one arm in an intact animal and measuring whether induced content reaches consumers in other arms and centrally, within the leakage bound, distinguishes three outcomes. Independent local admission with sub-bound leakage would indicate several islands and would support A without U; admission gated centrally, with the central brain as the unique locus not in the arm orbit, would place the lineage on the centered route; intermediate leakage leaves U undetermined until the bound is fixed. None of these outcomes bears on phenomenal character.

Markers the scheme does not type. Metacognitive and higher-order markers—confidence-based opt-out, uncertainty monitoring, post-decision wagering—are standard comparative proxies and are not typed by R, V, A, or U. The claim that comparative markers target the coupling must therefore be restricted to markers of regulation, valenced trade-off, multi-consumer access, and integration; whether metacognitive markers co-vary with the coupling across lineages is an open empirical question, and their dissociation from it would count against any stronger reading. Likewise, conspecific contact is listed in Π and supplies natural content for A tests, but the framework does not type social or intersubjective organization and should not be read as doing so.

Evidence that does not warrant a phenomenal inference. Convergent evolution of quality-space structure across lineages; decodability of an interoceptive or motivational state (predictive status only); high integration or recurrence in colonial, immune, or genetic systems (R or V without U); satisfaction of workspace indicators by artificial systems (A without R, V, U); self-report, anthropomorphic description, task performance, or welfare precaution (none is an intervention in Π); and satisfaction of the full coupling itself. The last requires care. The coupling identifies a candidate subject up to causal isomorphism. Whether that fixes phenomenal character depends on a bridge principle the framework does not supply: under a bridge that identifies phenomenal character with causal structure up to isomorphism, the coupling would fix it to exactly the extent the bridge does; under a bridge on which phenomenal facts can vary across isomorphic realizers, it would not. The framework's silence is a refusal to declare a bridge, not a demonstration that none can succeed. Phenomenal labeling is therefore unidentified by the evidence admitted here, which is weaker than the claim that no evidence could fix it.

Falsification and idleness criteria.

  1. If, across a broad comparative sample including cephalopods and colonial cnidarians tested under Π with a fixed leakage bound and tolerance, every organism exhibiting A also exhibits U and vice versa, the claim that A and U are realized along separate routes is empirically undermined. Because the finite constructions do not by themselves establish "no U" under the graded criterion, the comparative sample carries the evidential load for A–U independence.
  2. If interventions on a candidate hub under natural perturbations reveal multiple islands with cross-boundary leakage above the declared bound, or reveal that the hub is one of several near-duplicate loci under the declared tolerance, the claim that the hub realizes U is falsified for that lineage.
  3. If, in a well-characterized model organism, every predictively decodable interoceptive or motivational state has zero commutation defect under its natural coarse-graining, the predictive–causal distinction is biologically idle in that organism and V is not distinguishable there from decodable regulation. This is an idleness criterion, not a falsification of the partial order, and it must be evaluated per coarse-graining.
  4. If organisms satisfying the full coupling routinely execute simultaneous conflicting whole-body actions, or fail cross-modal trade-off tests that organisms lacking the coupling pass, the coupling does not track the unified trade-off organization attributed to it.
  5. If metacognitive markers dissociate from the coupling across lineages—present where the coupling is absent, or absent where it is present—the claim that comparative markers implicitly target the coupling is false for that class of markers.

Ethical interpretation. Because the framework declares no bridge principle, structural typing by R, V, A, U, or the coupling cannot by itself license denying moral consideration to organisms lacking U or the coupling, nor granting it on the basis of the coupling alone. A declared bridge could make structural typing relevant to welfare judgements, and different bridges would make it relevant in different ways. Welfare policy toward cephalopods, arthropods, or artificial systems must therefore rest on a separately declared bridge principle together with precautionary reasoning, with structural typing serving as an input to that reasoning rather than as its conclusion.

Limitations

The limitations fall into four groups: the formal definitions of the achievements, the evidential base for the lineage claims, the scope of the marker survey, and the phenomenal boundary.

Formal definitions. The unification criterion U is stated in terms of an exact automorphism fixed-point condition, and as stated it does not do the work the archipelago-versus-centered distinction needs. In a heterogeneous structural causal model the automorphism group is typically trivial, so the fixed-point condition is satisfied by any single dominant island regardless of whether it has a distinguished integrative center; in an exactly symmetric model, a set-level candidate center such as the union of a symmetry orbit (a whole nerve ring, for example) is fixed by the very rotations that move each of its points. Under the manuscript's own definition of "candidate center," constructions C1 and C3 therefore do not establish "no U," and the cnidarian and siphonophore rows of Table 1 should be read as mapping those lineages to decentered or multi-island organization under the informal gloss, not as proven negatives under the formal criterion. A criterion that separates a distributed ring from a centered hub needs either a graded measure of centering or an explicit exclusion of orbit-unions from candidacy; neither is supplied here, and the centering component of U is accordingly unidentified in the present formalization. Likewise, the access criterion A is met by any admission-gated afferent that fans out to two causally nonredundant motor consumers. That is a structural fact about access and control, and it is all the criterion asserts; it is not a criterion for conscious access, and nothing in the framework licenses reading multi-consumer fan-out as the organization that workspace theories or adversarial-collaboration results claim to detect. The comparison to those research programs is a comparison of targets, not an identification. Two further definitional inconsistencies remain. The definitions of R and A invoke severance interventions (removing the regulated effector set from the model, cutting the isolated channel) that are not members of any lineage's natural perturbation family Π, so the achievements are not purely Π-relative as the framing claims; a Π-internal reformulation would need to replace severance by the perturbations selection can actually realize, and it is not shown that the resulting criteria pick out the same systems. And the proposed field proxy for V tests whether the candidate macro variable screens off the regulated variables from downstream response (condition V2); it does not test Π-consistent causal abstraction (condition V1), which is the condition that would confer causal rather than predictive status. The proxy therefore identifies a predictive currency variable and leaves its causal status unidentified.

Evidential base. Π is reconstructed rather than observed for ancestral lineages, and the acquisition orders in Table 1 inherit this uncertainty. In addition, the orders are asserted for extant terminal taxa without explicit ancestral-state reconstruction; an order of appearance within a lineage requires character-state inference over a phylogeny, not a reading of the extant terminal, and the table should be understood as a set of hypotheses about order that such reconstruction could support or overturn. The arthropod order is left disjunctive for this reason. The finite counterexamples establish possibility, not actuality: the empirical burden lies on comparative tests under Π that have, for the most part, not been performed, and the stored records do not include the primary comparative anatomy on which the graded background claims rest, so those claims remain at the grades assigned to them and no stronger. Key dissociations—cephalopod arm autonomy, split-brain preparations—rest on interventions outside Π and are labeled regime-dependent accordingly; whether the same dissociations obtain under Π is unknown. The commutation defect used to distinguish predictive from causal motivational variables is a property of a specified coarse-graining relative to a specified intervention family; the "selective reading" that treats a positive defect as a fact about the organism goes through only if selection is shown to act through the same coarse-graining the modeler chose, which is asserted rather than demonstrated. Until that identification is made, the distinction is theoretical for biology.

Scope of the marker survey. The claim that all major comparative consciousness markers implicitly target the V-gated-A-within-U coupling is overstated as written. Metacognitive and higher-order markers—confidence judgments, opt-out behavior, monitoring of one's own uncertainty—are standard comparative proxies and are not represented in the four-achievement scheme; whether they reduce to a consumer of the gated channel, to a second-order valence variable, or to something outside R, V, A, and U is not settled here. The social and intersubjective dimension is likewise absent: conspecific contact is listed as a member of Π, but no achievement is defined that captures shared or reciprocal regulation, and the framework as stated cannot distinguish a lineage whose access channel is tuned to conspecific signals from one whose channel is not. The marker claim should therefore be read as covering the workspace, integration, and affective marker families discussed in the text, not as exhaustive.

Borrowed formal apparatus and the phenomenal boundary. The island and abstraction distinctions are borrowed from AI-generated working drafts that have not been human peer reviewed [1, 2], and only what their stored abstracts support is relied on: that a causal island is defined relative to an intervention regime, isolation capacities, and a leakage bound, and that predictive lumpability and causal commutation can be separated in finite models. Stronger results attributed to those drafts elsewhere in this manuscript—a fixed-point or no-natural-center theorem, and a named gauge-residue result—cannot be verified against the stored records and should be treated as this author's extrapolations rather than as citable findings. Proposition 1 is definitional and derives its force from the adequacy of the valence definition rather than from derivation. Finally, and by design, the framework says nothing about intrinsic phenomenal character. Everything R, V, A, and U identify is organization of access and control; anyone who wishes to infer experience from the coupling must supply and defend a bridge hypothesis of the kind IIT's identity claim exemplifies [10]. The gauge argument does not adjudicate among such hypotheses and is not neutral among them: it concludes that selection-visible evidence cannot fix phenomenal labeling only on the premise that phenomenal facts can vary within a causal-isomorphism orbit, a premise that identity theories deny and that functionalist theories reject by construction. The correct statement is conditional—if phenomenal character is not fixed by causal organization up to isomorphism, then no comparative or selective evidence can fix it—and the antecedent is a metaphysical commitment this paper does not establish.

Conclusion

Nervous-system evolution need not be described as a single ladder from homeostasis to phenomenality. The alternative defended here is a partial order over four organizational achievements—regulated viability variables, valence as a Π-consistent trade-off currency, gated multi-consumer access, and centered unification—each defined relative to a lineage's natural perturbation family. Of the relations among them, only one strict dependency is claimed, V ⇒ R, and that dependency is definitional rather than empirical: a currency that mediates trade-offs among regulated variables presupposes regulated variables. The substantive content of the proposition lies in the independence claims, and these are established to different degrees. The independence of A from R and V is shown by finite constructions and is not in dispute. The independence of U from A and V rests on the automorphism fixed-point criterion, and that criterion in its present form does not do the work asked of it: in heterogeneous systems with trivial automorphism group every single island passes it, and in exactly symmetric systems a set-level candidate such as an entire ring is fixed by the very rotations meant to exclude it. The constructions and the cnidarian and siphonophore mappings that were offered as "no U" cases therefore establish, as written, only that these systems lack a proper fixed subset, not that they lack a center in any organism-level sense. Until the criterion is restated so that the candidate center is constrained independently of the symmetry it is tested against, the archipelago-versus-centered distinction is a well-motivated hypothesis rather than a demonstrated dissociation.

Two further limits concern identification under Π. The definitions of R and A were stated using severance interventions—removing an effector set, cutting a channel—that lie outside the natural perturbation family of any lineage discussed. Since the whole point of Π-relativity is that selection can only act through ecologically realized interventions, the achievements should be identified by what Π-perturbations reveal, and it remains to be shown that R and A are so identifiable rather than merely definable. Similarly, the operational proxy proposed for valence tests whether the macro variable screens off its micro-realizers from downstream effects; it does not test whether the macro variable is a consistent causal abstraction under Π, which was the defining condition. The abstraction property is therefore unidentified by the proxy, and the commutation defect that was used to separate the two readings is, in the first instance, a property of a chosen coarse-graining relative to Π, not of the organism independently of the modeler's description. Whether a lineage "has" V in the abstraction sense is only as determinate as the choice of macro-description is constrained by Π.

The comparative claims should be read with matching caution. The lineage table records organizational profiles of extant terminals; the acquisition orders assigned to them were not derived from ancestral-state reconstruction, and the statement that the vertebrate route ran regulation, valence, centering, access is a hypothesis about a sequence, not an established phylogenetic fact. That cephalopod, nerve-net, and colonial organizations instantiate different combinations of the four achievements under their natural perturbations is plausible on the evidence surveyed, but the assignments involving U inherit the unresolved status of the U criterion.

What the framework does clarify is the target of several comparative markers. Unlimited associative learning, midbrain-integration accounts, and isomorphic-map criteria each partially track a specific coupling: a valence variable serving as the admission gate on a route-isolated, multi-consumer code inside a single island. This coupling has a signature its components lack—perturbing a regulated viability variable within Π changes joint admission of content across causally nonredundant consumers in one body—and that signature is, in principle, selection-visible. The claim is restricted to the three marker families examined; metacognitive and higher-order markers, which are standard comparative proxies, are not represented in the achievement scheme, and no claim is made that they reduce to it. It must also be stated plainly that A, as defined, is a structural condition on fan-out and gating: a gated afferent branching to two motor neurons satisfies it. The coupling is therefore an organization of access and control, in the functional sense in which those terms are used in the theories it is compared with, and not a criterion of conscious access as such.

Finally, the identification result should not be overstated. The coupling is identified up to causal isomorphism, because every intervention in Π acts on, and every fitness consequence flows through, structure preserved under that isomorphism. Whether phenomenal character is thereby fixed depends on a bridge hypothesis that the evidence cannot adjudicate. If phenomenal facts vary within the isomorphism class, no selection-visible evidence fixes them; if, as identity-style bridge hypotheses hold, phenomenal facts are constants of the class, the same evidence fixes them completely. The comparative and evolutionary evidence is silent between these options rather than favoring the first, and the earlier formulation—that the phenomenal question is "left open by the very evidence that establishes the structure"—should be read as neutrality about the bridge, not as an argument against identity. What remains is a set of concrete tasks: a fixed-point criterion for U that is neither vacuous nor satisfied by symmetry orbits; Π-internal identification conditions for R and A; a proxy that tests abstraction consistency rather than screening-off; a strengthening of A that distinguishes conscious-access organizations from mere gated fan-out; ancestral-state reconstruction for the claimed acquisition orders; and an extension of the perturbation family to the conspecific and social interventions that were listed in Π but not analyzed. Until those are done, the proposition is best regarded as a partially supported dissociation framework, well-posed in its access-and-control content and deliberately uncommitted in its phenomenal interpretation.
