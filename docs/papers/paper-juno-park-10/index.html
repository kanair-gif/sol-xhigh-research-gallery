Abstract

Dreaming, anesthesia, motor disconnection, amnesia, and computational suspension expose a limitation of static consciousness assessments: an apparently silent system may lack a content distinction, store it without using it, use it internally without reporting it, lose it before recovery, or reconstruct a report from another source. This paper proposes a Conditional Transition Audit for separating those possibilities without treating any of them as sufficient for phenomenal consciousness.

For a preregistered content contrast c, transition stage ρ, and finite grid of temporal grains τ, aligned predictive-feature Hankel operators distinguish internal telemetry I, registered commitments or control outputs C, overt actions A, contemporaneous reports R0, and recovery reports R+. Stage-local rank gaps quantify content-sensitive predictive distinctions hidden from selected outputs. An explicitly expanded structural causal model gives semantics to route-specific interventions on a proposed monitor M. Differences among the resulting intervention rows establish a route-specific response capacity; they establish endogenous control only when C is itself a registered commitment or when intervention on C changes a declared downstream target.

The framework no longer treats cross-stage transport as identified by stage-local similarity or a commuting diagram. A physical transition kernel descends to predictive quotients only under a compatibility condition, and its empirical identification requires paired longitudinal preparations, common probes, positivity, and independently constrained regenerative sources. Recovery linkage is therefore reported over all admissible longitudinal models. A silent controller and an independently reconstructed but predictively isomorphic recovery state remain observationally compatible unless a branch-selective intervention at the silent stage changes the recovery state while alternative logs or common sources are held fixed.

The formal contribution is correspondingly modest. Under valid route interventions, the audit can identify report-hidden local causal differentiation and, with additional control conditions, report-hidden control at a declared cut. Recovery ancestry is a separate, conditional conclusion. Predictive registration alone establishes type correspondence, not token lineage. The altered-state application remains a protocol proposal: the supplied evidence consists mainly of preprints, surveys, simulations, and conceptual work and does not validate clinical feasibility, sensitivity, or specificity. No audit result identifies phenomenal consciousness, agency, or moral status without additional empirical and normative premises.

Research Question and Hypothesis

Research question

When a biological or artificial system passes through an output-silent interval and later reports content, what evidence distinguishes:

  1. absence of the relevant content distinction;
  2. internal storage without contemporaneous causal use;
  3. content-sensitive response within a designated pathway;
  4. local endogenous control despite unavailable report;
  5. loss or amnesia before recovery;
  6. recovery-time reconstruction from an external, redundant, or newly generated source;
  7. causal ancestry from a silent-stage distinction to a recovered report distinction; and
  8. phenomenal consciousness, if anything can be inferred about it?

The altered-state perspective makes the transition path, rather than a static state label, the primary unit of analysis. Induction and emergence need not be inverses. Overt responsiveness, environmental connectedness, endogenous control, memory, report generation, and experience may change at different times or under different interventions. A method that collapses those variables cannot distinguish motor disconnection from loss of internal processing, or amnesia from absence of an earlier state.

Operational hypotheses

The original claim that a low-defect cross-stage transport identifies continuity was too strong. The revised framework separates three hypotheses.

H1: Local report-hidden causal differentiation

At a preregistered silent stage S, suppose that:

  1. the distribution of contemporaneous report R0 is invariant to the declared content intervention;
  2. adding the registered channel C to R0 reveals a nonzero content-sensitive predictive dimension;
  3. a valid route-specific intervention through a declared monitor M changes the distribution of C; and
  4. the intervention remains within the declared support and passes preregistered off-target diagnostics.

Then the content contrast has a report-hidden, route-specific causal effect on C at the registered cut.

This is a claim about a controlled channel. It becomes a claim about endogenous control only if C is itself an action or policy commitment fixed at that cut, or if a separately identified intervention on C changes a declared downstream task, policy, memory update, goal-relevant variable, or other controlled target.

H2: Conditional recovery ancestry

Suppose, in addition, that:

  1. an independently specified longitudinal transition kernel spans the silent-to-recovery interval;
  2. that kernel descends to the relevant predictive quotients;
  3. paired cross-stage interventions identify a confidence set of admissible descended kernels;
  4. every admissible kernel preserves the registered content contrast above a declared resolution;
  5. a branch-selective intervention at the silent stage changes the recovery predictive state or report while alternative regenerative sources are fixed or randomized; and
  6. all admissible content and monitor transports satisfy preregistered compatibility bounds.

Then the recovery distinction may be classified as a causal descendant of the silent-stage distinction within the declared model and boundary.

This conclusion does not establish numerical identity, subjective sameness, or token provenance. If branch-selective longitudinal intervention or control of regenerative sources is unavailable, recovery linkage remains underidentified even when the stage-local spaces are isomorphic and their fitted diagrams commute.

H3: Phenomenal non-entailment

Neither H1 nor H2 entails phenomenal consciousness. Such an inference requires an independently defended bridge premise,

Bphen​:Faudit​⟹P,

where Faudit​ is a specified audit profile and P is a phenomenal property. This manuscript supplies no such bridge.

Specialist Framework and Contribution

Altered states as transition problems

The framework separates six variables that are frequently conflated:

  • Environmental connectedness: whether external events enter the system’s ongoing content-sensitive dynamics.
  • Internal differentiation: whether a content contrast changes internal predictions or telemetry.
  • Endogenous control: whether the contrast changes a registered commitment or a process that in turn changes a controlled target.
  • Overt responsiveness: whether the contrast changes an externally observable action.
  • Reportability and memory: whether it can be reported now, encoded for later report, retrieved, and converted into a report.
  • Phenomenality: whether anything is experienced.

The specialist contribution is not a new scalar measure of consciousness. It is a registration discipline for suppression and recovery experiments. The same candidate system is compared across induction, silence, perturbation, and emergence, with report, memory, internal control, overt action, and possible reconstruction represented separately.

Let

ρ=(p,s),

where p∈{↓,↑} denotes induction or emergence and s is a registered stage descriptor. The descriptor may include elapsed time, intervention settings, physiological variables, or computational suppression parameters. It is not assumed to be a single drug concentration, a consciousness level, or chronological time. Nominally similar values of s on induction and emergence may represent different dynamical regimes.

Variables and registered temporal grains

At stage ρ, define:

  • Xρ,t​: physical or computational state inside a declared boundary;
  • Zρ,tc​: operational content variable for contrast c;
  • Mρ,t​: proposed monitor or mediator;
  • Iρ,t​: internal telemetry not yet classified as action or control;
  • Cρ,t​: registered commitment, control output, or candidate intermediate process;
  • Dρ,t​: downstream controlled target when C is not itself a commitment;
  • Aρ,t​: overt action;
  • Rρ,t0​: contemporaneous report;
  • Rρ,t+d+​: recovery or delayed report;
  • O: observer attribution of consciousness;
  • P: phenomenal consciousness, not assumed to be measured by the preceding variables.

The temporal grains form a finite preregistered grid,

T={τ1​,…,τK​}.

A continuum-wide plateau is not empirically claimed. If outcomes at different grains are to be interpreted as one coherent channel, the aggregation or refinement relation among them must also be registered. Otherwise, the audit reports separate results at each grain.

The objective commitment cut and stale-monitor countermodel are adapted from an AI-generated mediated-control proposal [1]. That source is not empirical authority. Its useful constraint is that a later accurate report or a passive state–action association does not establish that the current state governed the action fixed at the relevant cut. The present framework adds stage asymmetry, separates internal response from control, and makes longitudinal lineage explicitly conditional. A speculative synaptic-clock proposal motivates examining more than one temporal grain but does not identify a privileged neural or subjective timescale [13].

Operational content and natural support

A content contrast c consists of a finite set of intervention values z∈Zc​, a baseline z0​, and calibrated reference consequences. A semantic label such as “red,” “pain,” “dream object,” or “goal state” is insufficient.

Before outcome inspection, an application must specify:

  1. how Zc=z is prepared or perturbed;
  2. which awake, baseline, or otherwise calibrated measurements distinguish z from z0​;
  3. which variables must remain invariant for the intervention to count as content-selective;
  4. which naturally reachable or experimentally attainable set defines support;
  5. the minimum support probability or reachability criterion;
  6. how intervention-induced arousal or regime change will be detected; and
  7. which alternative sources could regenerate the same contrast at recovery.

For a finite system, a simple support condition is that every audited value is reached with probability at least pmin​>0 under at least one preregistered admissible policy at the same stage. In continuous systems, support must be defined by a registered attainable region and distance. Pretreatment variables and off-target sentinel channels should remain within declared tolerances. These diagnostics do not prove that an intervention is natural; they create conditions under which the claim can be rejected.

Many spontaneous dreams do not admit such an intervention. If a content contrast cannot be assigned or perturbed without creating, transforming, or terminating the state, the audit does not classify that dream. It applies only to experimentally anchored content contrasts.

Registered predictive-feature operators

For an output family Y, let ΦY​ be a preregistered set of future features. A feature may be a future word in a finite-output process, a bounded function of a future output block, or a conditional-mean coordinate in a linear system. Let h denote an admissible preparation and probe history, and let f denote a future probe policy and feature.

Define the centered feature Hankel operator

HYc​(ρ,τ)=[Eρ​[ϕf​(Y)∣do(h),do(Zc=z)]−Eρ​[ϕf​(Y)∣do(h),do(Zc=z0​)]](h,z),f​.

Its rank is

rYc​(ρ,τ)=rankHYc​(ρ,τ).

This is the dimension of the registered linear predictive contrast space, not necessarily the number of physical states. For complete finite-output probability Hankel operators it may equal the dimension of a minimal linear predictive realization. For finite feature sets it is explicitly feature-relative. In continuous stochastic systems, arbitrary event-probability operators may have infinite rank even when the latent dynamics are finite-dimensional.

The feature families must be aligned:

where marginal features are literally included in the larger families. This ensures rank monotonicity. Recovery report R+ is not included in a silent-stage report operator; otherwise later memory or reconstruction could make a silent stage appear contemporaneously report-sensitive.

and

Their meanings are limited:

  • uc​>0 means that the registered internal features add content-sensitive predictive dimensions beyond the selected C and R0 features.

A strict nested reserve requires both uc​>0 and ac​>0. A minimal report-hidden control profile requires ac​>0 but not uc​>0. Neither gap establishes a causal route from content to C.

For observational operators, passive covariance, state mixture, common causes, and measurement leakage can create apparent refinement. For randomized interventional operators, the relevant threats are invalid intervention semantics, direct off-target effects, conditioning on a changing stage, measurement leakage, and misspecified boundaries—not ordinary intervention confounding under a valid randomization.

Exact meaning of “fold” and “branch”

A content branch at stage ρ is the span or set of predictive contrasts generated by the registered interventions:

Bc​(ρ,τ)=span{μρ,z​−μρ,z0​​:z∈Zc​},

where μρ,z​ is the predictive state or feature vector induced by do(Zc=z).

A report fold relative to c occurs when the report projection annihilates the registered contrast while a nonreport projection does not. In the rank language, a minimal report fold has

rR0c​=0,rC⊕R0c​>0.

Here rR0c​=0 means only that the selected report distribution is invariant to c. It does not mean that all reporting has ceased.

“Branch” denotes an operational predictive contrast, not a phenomenal stream or a persisting subject. “Fold” denotes a projection relation, not a saddle-node bifurcation. Geometric terms are not used to imply phenomenal unity.

Revised contribution and claim level

The framework synthesizes three components:

  1. stage-local predictive refinement;
  2. route-specific causal testing at a registered cut; and
  3. set-valued longitudinal registration and ancestry testing.

The first two components are related to speculative AI-generated proposals on predictive reserve and mediated-control rank [1, 2]. The third is not presented as an established identifiable quantity. Low-defect alignment is evidential only when the underlying longitudinal maps are independently constrained. Otherwise it is descriptive registration.

White-box AI systems are included primarily as benchmark systems in which hidden logs, checkpoint copies, route ablations, and branch interventions can be constructed directly. They do not supply evidence about biological experience.

Evidence and Literature Synthesis

Evidence standard

The sources are interpreted by evidence class:

  1. Restricted formal results establish consequences only within stated models.
  2. Peer-reviewed empirical studies may constrain applications but do not automatically validate an audit.
  3. Preprints, surveys, and simulations provide provisional motivation.
  4. Conceptual articles address possibility, explanatory level, or normative interpretation.
  5. AI-generated shared drafts provide conjectures and formal motifs only.

The supplied corpus contains no peer-reviewed clinical dataset capable of estimating the sensitivity or specificity of this audit in dreaming, anesthesia, motor blockade, or emergence. It also does not contain the comprehensive clinical and sleep-methods literature required for claims about the prevalence of connected experience, covert command following, perioperative memory, or dream recall. Drug-specific diagnostic claims are therefore omitted.

Altered-state constraints in the supplied record

A macaque ECoG preprint describes time-resolved cortical connectivity during propofol induction. It reports an initial increase followed by a decrease in inferred connectivity and changes in directional Granger-causal relations before the study’s loss-of-consciousness endpoint [28]. The exact endpoint is not treated here as a measure of phenomenality. The record supports modeling induction as a trajectory rather than pooling all pre-endpoint samples. Granger-causal connectivity does not identify a route-specific intervention, a content contrast, or experience.

A cellular preprint reports concentration- and temperature-dependent effects of propofol on membrane nanodomains, including different behavior at lower and higher concentrations and dependence on intact-cell processes [27]. This motivates caution about assuming that one scalar stage coordinate captures all mechanisms. It does not show that macroscopic cognitive staging is impossible or supply a consciousness marker.

A preprint on model-invariant safety control for propofol explicitly uses model falsification to reduce uncertainty in a control setting [30]. Its target is physiological safety, not consciousness. Its methodological relevance is that anesthesia interventions may require model sets and active rejection of incompatible dynamics rather than selection of one best-fitting trajectory.

A retrospective online tinnitus survey reports that 92.5% of respondents who recalled dreams did not report tinnitus during them. Among respondents reporting frequent lucid dreams, tinnitus perception was associated with reported external-sound perception [29]. The record motivates separating external connectedness, dream content, awakening, and retrospective report. Its design does not establish exact timing, causal content transport, or completeness of recall.

A thalamocortical spiking-network study reports that simulated NREM–REM cycles changed synaptic structure and later waking task performance [33]. It demonstrates a model in which sleep-like dynamics affect later behavior. It is not evidence that the simulated system dreamed or experienced anything.

A composite integration, complexity, and metastability preprint reports differentiation among simulated states and an ordering across Wake, N2, and REM data [32]. A resonance-complexity proposal similarly treats organized oscillatory patterns as sufficient for awareness, supported by a neural-field simulation [31]. These studies may motivate dynamical measurements, but scalar or composite organization does not by itself distinguish storage, route-specific control, amnesia, or recovery reconstruction. The phenomenal interpretation requires a bridge not provided by simulation.

AI evidence and philosophical debate

Theory-derived indicator programs can organize evidence according to scientific theories of consciousness, but their proponents emphasize that satisfying indicators would not settle consciousness [9]. The present audit is narrower still: it assesses a report-hidden causal organization under a declared intervention.

A global-workspace-inspired AI architecture has been reported to improve multimodal, tool-use, and agentic performance [4]. Such gains demonstrate functional performance under the reported tasks. They do not establish that a particular content state remains active during output suppression, reaches a commitment through an isolated route, or causes a later report through the same lineage.

Theoretical uncertainty remains substantial because systems may satisfy some consciousness theories while failing others [5]. Objections to artificial consciousness also differ by explanatory level, ranging from practical implementation constraints to challenges to computational functionalism and categorical impossibility claims [6]. A peer-reviewed conceptual article defends a categorical biological and embodied objection to conscious AI [10]. An organizational audit cannot refute a premise that biological embodiment is necessary.

Perceived AI consciousness is an observer-side outcome. One preprint argues that perceived consciousness and its social effects are more tractable than phenomenal detection [3]. Another reports that metacognitive self-reflection and emotional expression increased human attributions of consciousness to language-model systems [8]. Neither result identifies hidden-state continuity or causal control in the target system.

Welfare governance is also distinct from detection. A research report argues for attention to possible AI welfare under uncertainty while declining to claim that current or near-term systems are conscious [7]. Precaution may be justified by possible severity and uncertainty, but it is not evidence that phenomenality exists.

Cross-Disciplinary Integration

Predictive-state identification

Controlled predictive-state methods can identify equivalence classes of histories by their future intervention-conditioned predictions. This is useful because latent coordinates are usually nonunique. It does not, by itself, align independently fitted stage-local spaces.

A local predictive quotient at stage ρ answers:

Which preparation histories produce the same registered future predictions at this stage?

A longitudinal quotient answers a stronger question:

Which histories also have the same distribution over future predictive classes after the stage transition?

If locally equivalent histories differ after transition, the local quotient is too coarse for longitudinal transport. It must be refined; a map cannot simply be imposed between the original quotients.

The Recovery-Linked Quotient Observability working paper motivates comparing joint and behavior-only predictive spaces and considering uncertainty over branch matchings [2]. The present author’s narrower objection is not that stage-linked mode analysis is always invalid, but that persistence of a fitted mode does not by itself establish causal ancestry or token continuity. Those require longitudinal intervention or independently trusted provenance.

Causal mediation and edge interventions

A route-specific operation that sends z through one outgoing route and z0​ through another is not an ordinary node intervention. It belongs to an expanded mechanism in which edge copies or modular input ports are explicit. If those ports cannot be independently manipulated, the route-specific quantity may remain hypothetical or require additional mediation assumptions.

The audit therefore distinguishes:

  • an ordinary intervention on Z;
  • a direct edge-copy intervention on ZM and ZMˉ;
  • a separately randomized mediator intervention on M; and
  • a passive conditional association.

These are not interchangeable. Agreement among them is a consistency check, not proof that the proposed monitor is causally complete.

Longitudinal alignment and partial identification

Time-varying system identification can fit separate realizations at induction and emergence. Latent alignment can then find rotations, permutations, or other registrations that make them similar. Such a registration establishes at most structural type correspondence unless it is derived from a longitudinal physical kernel.

The revised audit therefore uses partial identification. Let Aρσ​ contain every cross-stage transport, monitor map, drift model, and branch matching consistent with:

  • paired cross-stage intervention data;
  • registered positivity and attainable-set conditions;
  • feature and metric definitions;
  • independently justified drift bounds; and
  • the declared system boundary.

A favorable interpretation must hold for every member of Aρσ​, not merely for the member with the lowest commutation defect. If some admissible models transport the silent branch while others regenerate an isomorphic recovery branch, causal continuity is not identified.

Dynamical path dependence without phenomenal geometry

Induction and emergence may follow different paths. A path discrepancy can compare the suppression–recovery composition against an independently calibrated reference transition. This is useful for diagnosing path dependence, re-encoding, or model mismatch, but it does not uniquely identify their cause.

An AI-generated Multiscale Reflexive Causal Geometry draft proposes local causal states, gluing, obstruction spectra, and a counterfactual phenomenal geometry [26]. The present framework retains only the limited idea that independently obtained local maps should be mutually compatible. It does not infer phenomenal geometry from compatibility.

An AI-generated Centered Closure Theory draft emphasizes perturbational evidence and approximate causal closure [25]. The present author’s objection is that approximate closure does not by itself identify the scientifically appropriate agent boundary or the bearer of experience. Boundary choice remains an explicit modeling decision.

Delayed report as a multistage measurement

A recovery report depends on several mappings:

silent-stage state→encoding→retention→retrieval→report generation.

Failure at any mapping can eliminate a report even when earlier content-sensitive control existed. Conversely, an external log, redundant store, residual cue, or recovery-time inference can produce an accurate report without causal ancestry from the audited silent controller.

This decomposition prevents two invalid inferences:

no recovery report⇒no earlier processing,

and

accurate recovery report⇒continuous earlier state.

1. Repaired stage-local rank formulation

Linear conditional-mean model

At a fixed stage ρ and grain τ, consider

xk+1​=Aρ​xk​+Bρ,c​vk​+Bρ,q​qk​+wk​,

If a contemporaneous direct term EρY​vk​ exists, it must be:

  1. excluded by shifting the audited output window;
  2. represented as a separate static channel; or
  3. absorbed into an explicitly augmented state.

It cannot be included while retaining the ordinary reachability–observability rank formula.

The conditional-mean Markov parameters are

GℓY​=DρY​Aρℓ−1​Bρ,c​,ℓ≥1.

For sufficiently large block horizons p,q, form

HY,p,qmean​=​G1Y​G2Y​⋮GpY​​G2Y​G3Y​⋮Gp+1Y​​⋯⋯⋱⋯​GqY​Gq+1Y​⋮Gp+q−1Y​​​.

Let

Rc​=span{Bρ,c​,Aρ​Bρ,c​,Aρ2​Bρ,c​,…},

and let

NY​=Rc​∩j≥0⋂​ker(DρY​Aρj​).

Under horizon saturation,

rankHYmean​=dimRc​−dimNY​.

This claim concerns conditional means or Markov parameters. It does not apply to arbitrary probabilities of nonlinear future events in a continuous stochastic system. For example, threshold-event probabilities in a linear Gaussian model are nonlinear functions of the latent mean and need not have rank bounded by the ordinary state dimension.

Finite-state probability formulation

For a finite controlled hidden-state process with complete future-word probabilities, the probability Hankel operator factors through the finite-dimensional vector space of predictive state distributions:

HYc​=Pc​FY​.

Its rank is therefore finite and equals the dimension of the content-reachable minimal linear predictive realization when reachability and observability conditions hold. It need not equal the number of hidden Markov states or the minimal nonnegative realization size.

For a finite selected feature set, the rank remains a registered algebraic property. Horizon extension alone does not establish saturation; persistent excitation and a spanning condition are required.

Proposition 1: Restricted rank-gap result

Proposition 1. For the strictly proper conditional-mean linear model above, suppose that the chosen block horizons saturate content reachability and output observability. For aligned nested outputs,

R0⊆C⊕R0⊆I⊕C⊕R0,

the rank gaps satisfy

uc​=dimNC⊕R0​−dimNI⊕C⊕R0​,

and

ac​=dimNR0​−dimNC⊕R0​.

The corresponding finite-state statement holds for differences in minimal linear predictive dimensions when complete probability operators and the stated reachability conditions are used.

Proof. The strictly proper mean Hankel factors as

HYmean​=OY​Cc​,

where Cc​ spans the content-reachable subspace and OY​ is the block observability operator. Its rank is the dimension of the reachable subspace modulo the reachable directions annihilated by OY​. Stacking aligned outputs can only reduce the unobservable subspace. Subtracting the resulting dimensions gives the two equalities.

The finite-state statement follows from the complete probability-Hankel factorization through minimal linear predictive states.

The ranks are invariant under invertible latent similarities. They are also invariant under invertible feature-coordinate changes that preserve the registered feature span, and complete probability predictions are invariant under bimeasurable relabelings of outcomes. They are not claimed to be invariant under arbitrary linear mixtures of event probabilities, information-losing coarse-graining, or temporal aggregation.

2. Formal semantics for route-specific intervention

Expanded structural causal model

At a registered cut, introduce explicit edge copies:

ZM:=Z,ZMˉ:=Z

under the natural regime. Let

M=fM​(ZM,UM​),
W=fW​(ZMˉ,UW​),
C=fC​(M,W,UC​),
D=fD​(C,M,W,UD​),

where W collects nonmonitor pathways from Z, and D is a downstream target when required.

The route-isolating intervention

do(ZM=z,ZMˉ=z0​)

is well-defined in this expanded model because the two edge inputs are separate intervention targets. It is not generally identifiable from ordinary interventions on Z. An application must either manipulate the corresponding physical or computational ports directly or defend additional assumptions connecting ordinary interventions to the edge-copy intervention.

The model requires:

  • consistency: setting both copies to z reproduces do(Z=z);
  • modularity: manipulating one copy does not alter the structural mechanism receiving the other copy;
  • positivity or attainability: audited settings lie within the registered experimental domain;
  • causal ordering: the commitment cut precedes the audited outcome block;
  • no unregistered shared route: a post-treatment variable affected through both retained and clamped paths must be represented explicitly;
  • off-target diagnostics: route manipulation must not change preregistered sentinel variables beyond tolerance; and
  • regime stability: the intervention must not silently move the system into a different unmodeled stage.

When these conditions fail, the route-specific estimand may remain a hypothetical path intervention rather than an identified experimental channel.

Route-specific channel

Define

SzκM,z0​​=Pr(C∈κ∣do(ZM=z,ZMˉ=z0​),ρ,τ).

The direct binary effect diagnostic is

diso​=z,z′max​TV(SzM,z0​​,Sz′M,z0​​).

Thus diso​>0 means that at least two content interventions have different effects on C through the manipulated route.

For richer channel descriptions, retain the approximate stochastic nonnegative rank

mcϵ​=min{r:∃ row-stochastic Q,K, zmax​TV(Sz​,(QK)z​)≤ϵ}.

This rank is the smallest alphabet size of a hypothetical stochastic factorization at tolerance ϵ. It does not recover the actual number or meaning of monitor states.

Proposition 2: Rank-one characterization

Proposition 2. For an exact row-stochastic channel S,

mc0​=1⟺Sz​=Sz′​for all z,z′.

Proof. A one-column row-stochastic Q has every entry equal to one. Hence QK has identical rows. Conversely, a channel with identical rows has a one-state stochastic factorization.

For ϵ>0, mcϵ​=1 means that there exists one distribution q satisfying

zmax​TV(Sz​,q)≤ϵ.

Therefore,

TV(Sz​,Sz′​)>2ϵ

for any pair is sufficient to rule out approximate rank one by the triangle inequality. If each estimated row lies within total variation η of its population row, an observed pairwise separation greater than

2ϵ+2η

is a simple sufficient condition. This is not a complete finite-sample procedure; valid confidence bounds depend on sampling design, dependence, and intervention assignment.

Capacity, natural contribution, and control

The isolated channel establishes a response capacity under a manipulated baseline. It does not show that the route contributes under the ordinary intervention regime.

Define

Sznat​=Pr(C∣do(ZM=z,ZMˉ=z)),

and a monitor-route-blocked channel

Sz−M​=Pr(C∣do(ZM=z0​,ZMˉ=z)).

Then

χM​=zmax​TV(Sznat​,Sz−M​)

tests whether blocking the monitor route changes the ordinary content effect. A positive χM​ supports natural route contribution within the expanded model. It does not prove that M is a complete mediator, because parallel routes and interactions may remain.

If C is not itself an action or policy commitment, define a second-leg effect on a downstream target D:

βC​=c,c′max​TV(Pr(D∣do(C=c)),Pr(D∣do(C=c′))),

under a registered intervention that preserves the relevant upstream regime. The terminology is then:

  • diso​>0: route-specific response capacity;
  • χM​>0: contribution of the monitor route under the registered content intervention;
  • diso​>0 plus C as a commitment, or plus βC​>0: local control relation.

Calling arbitrary telemetry, autonomic variation, or activation a control output without this qualification is not warranted.

Separately randomized composition

Define

Qzm​=Pr(M=m∣do(ZM=z,ZMˉ=z0​)),

and

Kmκz0​​=Pr(C=κ∣do(M=m),do(ZMˉ=z0​)).

Their product is

RM,z0​=QKz0​.

The discrepancy

Γc​=zmax​TV(SzM,z0​​,RzM,z0​​)

compares the same-run edge intervention with a separately randomized composition. A large value rejects the simple composition. A small value does not validate mediator completeness, natural route use, absence of intervention-induced regime change, or phenomenality. This limitation is consistent with the speculative mediated-control source from which the diagnostic is adapted [1].

3. Longitudinal predictive transport

Physical transition kernel

Let Xρ​ be a physical or computational state at stage ρ, and let

Kσ←ρα​(dx′∣x)

be the controlled physical transition kernel to stage σ under cross-stage policy α.

Let

qρ​:Xρ​→Qρ​,qσ​:Xσ​→Qσ​

map physical preparations to registered predictive equivalence classes.

The physical kernel descends to a quotient kernel only if, for every admissible α,

qρ​(x)=qρ​(x′)

implies

Kσ←ρα​(qσ−1​(B)∣x)=Kσ←ρα​(qσ−1​(B)∣x′)

for every measurable B⊆Qσ​.

This is a controlled lumpability or quotient-compatibility condition.

Lemma 3: Quotient descent

Lemma 3. If the compatibility condition holds, then

Kˉσ←ρα​(B∣[x]ρ​)=Kσ←ρα​(qσ−1​(B)∣x)

defines a unique Markov kernel from Qρ​ to Qσ​.

Proof. Compatibility makes the right-hand side independent of the representative x chosen from [x]ρ​. Normalization and countable additivity are inherited from the physical kernel. Uniqueness follows because the quotient map fixes the probability assigned to every measurable quotient set.

The lemma proves existence of an abstract descended kernel given the physical kernel and compatibility. It does not show that either object is empirically identified.

Conditions for empirical identification

Identification of Kˉ requires, at minimum:

  1. paired or token-linked preparations across the transition;
  2. common future probes that identify the source and target predictive classes;
  3. reachability or positivity for the audited classes;
  4. stage stability within the measurement windows;
  5. a registered model for measurement drift;
  6. exclusion or manipulation of alternative regenerative inputs; and
  7. sufficient data to distinguish all admissible kernels.

Minimal predictive realizations remain unique only up to stage-specific coordinate transformations. The abstract descended kernel is gauge-covariant:

Kˉ′=Gσ​KˉGρ−1​

in linear predictive coordinates. This gauge freedom does not authorize arbitrary post hoc alignment. The transport must be estimated from the longitudinal experiment, not selected solely because it makes two stage-local spaces similar.

If the compatibility condition fails, the correct response is to refine the source quotient or report that no transport is defined at that grain. Branch merging is represented by a noninjective kernel. Branch splitting is represented by a distribution over target classes. Neither should be forced into an invertible deterministic map.

Content and monitor maps

A stage-local map

Jρ​:Qc,ρ​⇝QM,ρ​

from content predictive states to monitor predictive states is itself a Markov kernel unless determinism is justified. It descends from the physical model only if content-equivalent physical preparations induce the same distribution over monitor quotient classes. If not, the content quotient is too coarse to define Jρ​.

The monitor transition

KˉM,σ←ρ​

requires an analogous quotient-compatibility condition. Thus Jρ​, the content transport, and the monitor transport are independently testable objects. They are not free registration matrices.

4. Compatibility and path discrepancy

For each registered intervention value z, let μρ,z​ be its distribution over the content quotient. Let dpred​ be a preregistered distance between future prediction distributions, such as total variation for complete discrete predictions or a feature-space norm with a fixed Gram matrix.

Define the content–monitor compatibility defect

Δc​(ρ,σ)=z∈Zc​max​dpred​(Jσ​Kˉc,σ←ρ​μρ,z​,KˉM,σ←ρ​Jρ​μρ,z​).

This expression is coordinate-free when applied to prediction distributions. In a finite-dimensional feature realization, operator norms and singular values are meaningful only relative to explicitly specified inner products; those inner products must transform with the coordinates.

Branch preservation is evaluated directly:

gc​(ρ,σ)=z=z0​min​dpred​(Kˉc,σ←ρ​μρ,z​,Kˉc,σ←ρ​μρ,z0​​).

A small Δc​ with gc​≈0 is uninformative: two collapsed pathways can agree perfectly. A positive preservation margin must therefore be required separately.

Let A0​ be a reference stage before suppression, S a silent stage, and A1​ a recovered stage. Let GA1​←A0​​ be an independently calibrated reference transition, not an inverse chosen after fitting. Define

hc​=zmax​dpred​(KˉA1​←S↑​KˉS←A0​↓​μA0​,z​,GA1​←A0​​μA0​,z​).

This compares two maps with the same source and target and requires no inverse. A large hc​ may reflect path dependence, re-encoding, drift, changing model degree, or an inadequate reference. It is not a unique measure of hysteresis and has no phenomenal interpretation.

5. Admissible-map analysis

Let A be the set of all longitudinal models consistent with the registered intervention confidence sets, drift bounds, attainable sets, and structural assumptions. Define

Δc+​=θ∈Asup​Δc​(θ),
gc−​=θ∈Ainf​gc​(θ).

A robust compatibility claim requires

Δc+​≤δcomm​

and

gc−​≥gmin​,

for preregistered tolerances. If only

θ∈Ainf​Δc​(θ)

is small, the data establish that a favorable alignment exists, not that it is identified. If A is empty, the registered longitudinal model is rejected. If the bounds straddle the thresholds, the correct result is underidentification.

No universal formula for δcomm​, gmin​, or a general “identification radius” is claimed. Their interpretation depends on the registered prediction metric, sampling design, effect resolution, and application-specific loss.

6. Causal ancestry versus predictive registration

Let US​ be a physically re-identifiable carrier of the silent-stage branch and VA1​​ the recovery predictive state. Let L collect external logs, redundant memories, prompts, environmental cues, or other sources capable of regenerating the recovery distinction.

For registered values u,u′ and ℓ, define

λc​(u,u′;ℓ)=dpred​(Pr(VA1​​∣do(US​=u),do(L=ℓ)),Pr(VA1​​∣do(US​=u′),do(L=ℓ))).

A positive value establishes that the silent carrier causally affects the recovery state under the declared intervention and fixed alternative source. If VA1​​ is generated solely from L, then the model predicts λc​=0.

This intervention is stronger than stage-local registration. It may be feasible in a white-box system but infeasible in a brain. If US​ cannot be selectively manipulated, or if relevant regenerative sources cannot be fixed or randomized, causal ancestry remains unresolved.

Proposition 3: Local report-hidden route effect

Proposition 3. In the expanded structural causal model, assume that the route-specific intervention is randomized, well-defined, supported, and free of off-target changes under the registered diagnostics. If

rR0c​=0,ac​>0,diso​>0,

then the silent stage contains a content-sensitive predictive distinction that is hidden from the selected contemporaneous report and has a causal effect on C through the manipulated monitor route.

If C is a registered commitment, or if βC​>0, the result supports a local report-hidden control relation. Otherwise it establishes only a route-specific response.

Proof. The first equality states that the selected report predictions do not vary with the intervention. The positive rank gap states that adding C introduces a content-sensitive predictive dimension. The positive route separation states that a randomized edge intervention changes the distribution of C. The control qualification follows only from the declared status of C or the separately identified second leg.

The proposition makes no claim about R+, recovery ancestry, natural route contribution, or phenomenality. A natural-route claim additionally requires evidence such as χM​>0.

Proposition 4: Conditional exclusion of independent regeneration

Proposition 4. Suppose Proposition 3 holds at S. Assume further that the longitudinal physical kernel descends to the registered predictive quotients, all admissible descended kernels preserve the content contrast, and a supported branch intervention yields

λc​(u,u′;ℓ)>0

for preregistered u,u′,ℓ. Then a recovery model in which VA1​​ is generated independently of every descendant of US​, conditional on L=ℓ, is incompatible with the intervention distribution.

Proof. Under the independent-regeneration model, intervening on US​ while fixing L cannot change the distribution of VA1​​; it therefore implies λc​=0. A positive intervention distance contradicts that implication.

This proposition establishes causal ancestry under the stated SCM. It does not prove that the complete recovery report existed during silence, that every report dimension descended from US​, or that the recovery state is numerically identical to the silent state. A hybrid process may preserve one dimension and reconstruct another.

Proposition 5: Model-relative phenomenal non-identification

Proposition 5. Let E contain all interventional distributions used by the audit. If the empirical model contains no independently specified bridge from E to phenomenal variable P, then P is not identifiable from the audit.

Argument. Assignments P=1, P=0, or P∼π(E) can be appended without changing the likelihood of any variable in E. The audit therefore does not select among those assignments.

This is a scope observation about the model, not a proof that phenomenal duplicates are metaphysically possible. Identity theories, biological theories, and functionalist theories may add bridge constraints, but those constraints require independent defense.

7. Constructive countermodels

Stale monitoring

Let Zt​∈{1,…,K} follow

P=(1−ρ)I+ρJ/K,

and define

Mt​=Zt−d​,Ct​=Mt​,Rt+1+​=Zt​.

For small ρ, passive agreement between Zt​ and Ct​ is high. The delayed report is accurate. Under a synchronized intervention on Zt​, however, Mt​ remains determined by the past, so the same-cut channel has identical rows. Thus

diso​=0,mc0​=1.

This reproduces the central lag countermodel of the speculative mediated-control source [1].

Response without control

Let

M=Z,C=M,

but suppose C is telemetry that has no effect on any registered commitment or downstream target:

βC​=0.

Then route-specific response is present, but control has not been established. This countermodel prevents an internal activation from being redescribed as cognitive availability by stipulation.

Storage without contemporaneous use

Let a memory state U=Z persist during silence, while all registered commitments and downstream targets are independent of U. Then uc​ may be positive, but

diso​=0

for every audited control route. The state is stored rather than shown to govern a process.

Motor-output disconnection

Let Z affect a registered commitment C, while overt action is clamped:

C=Z,A=a0​,R0=r0​.

Then a valid audit may obtain

diso​(C)>0,diso​(A)=0.

This establishes a motor-output fold relative to the selected channels, not phenomenality.

State-dependent amnesia

Let Z control C during silence, but reset the relevant memory before recovery:

UA1​​∼μ

independently of the earlier contrast. Local report-hidden control can be positive while R+ is content-insensitive. Absent recall therefore does not identify absent earlier control.

Pure recovery reconstruction

During silence, let the internal state and all registered commitments be independent of z. At recovery, let an external log L=z drive

R+=g(L,prompt).

The recovery report can be perfectly accurate while

ac​(S)=0,diso​(S)=0,λc​=0.

If L lies inside the declared boundary, the profile becomes storage or extended-system reconstruction rather than external reinjection. The boundary changes the target but not the need to distinguish storage from control.

Silent controller plus independent reconstruction

Let

US​=z

control C during silence, while a separate log L=z generates recovery state VA1​​. Then local control is positive:

diso​(S)>0,

and the silent and recovery states may be predictively isomorphic. A fitted map can make their content–monitor diagram commute exactly. Nevertheless,

Pr(VA1​​∣do(US​=u),do(L=ℓ))

is invariant to u, so λc​=0.

This hybrid countermodel is decisive: silent control and recovery reconstruction are not mutually exclusive. Stage-local ranks plus a best-fitting registration do not identify recovery ancestry.

Type-identical regeneration

Let a new recovery state be generated from a common seed or template and have the same predictive organization as the silent state. Predictive quotients cannot distinguish the original token from a type-identical replacement. Token provenance requires trusted causal logging, randomized tags, or interventions on the particular stored token.

Passive transcript equivalence

A recurrent monitored agent and a history-kernel agent can generate the same finite-horizon prompt, action, and self-report distributions. The latter need not contain the proposed monitor route. Passive transcripts therefore do not identify mediated control, as formalized in the speculative language-agent source [1].

Persistent dynamical mode with changing loading

Let a dynamical mode persist across stages while the content loading rotates:

DρI​=[cosθρ​,sinθρ​].

The pole or decay rate can persist even though its relation to the registered content contrast changes. This does not refute mode-transport methods under their full assumptions; it shows why mode persistence alone cannot substitute for content-anchored longitudinal intervention.

Unconscious processing

An unconscious controller could satisfy the local and longitudinal causal conditions. This is not a false positive for the mechanistic target. It would be a false positive only if the audit were presented as a consciousness detector.

Processes such as priming, error correction, memory preparation, sensor classification, or autonomic regulation may be content-sensitive without satisfying a strong theory of access consciousness. The framework therefore uses “control” only for a declared commitment or an interventionally verified downstream control relation.

Recovery reconstruction

A recovery report can combine transported features, residual cues, expectations, external records, and newly generated narrative structure. Demonstrating ancestry for one registered contrast does not show that the complete scene or thought described at recovery existed during silence.

The content intervention determines the resolution of the conclusion. An audited object identity does not establish transport of spatial detail, affective tone, confidence, temporal order, or qualitative character.

Probe-induced reopening

A cue, command, awakening, or arousal probe may create the state it is intended to detect. An immediate report following a probe is therefore not automatically evidence of a pre-probe branch.

A valid design needs:

  • pre-probe internal measurements;
  • arousal and regime-change sentinels;
  • sham probes;
  • variation in probe timing and intensity; and
  • a model in which content creation by the probe is an explicit alternative.

If these controls are unavailable, the result is underidentified.

Wrong temporal grain

At a very short grain, a slowly integrated monitor may appear stale. At a long grain, a post-commitment update may be mistaken for a cause of the commitment. Reporting all preregistered grains reduces selective presentation but does not discover a uniquely correct grain.

A multigrain result is coherent only if causal ordering remains resolvable and the outcome definitions are related by declared aggregation maps. Otherwise, each grain addresses a different channel.

Wrong boundary

An external checkpoint, context window, retrieval system, caregiver, or environmental record may be placed inside or outside the candidate system. The mathematics cannot select the correct boundary.

Three interpretations of a stored checkpoint illustrate the issue:

  1. Outside the boundary: later use is external reinjection.
  2. Inside an extended storage boundary: information persists as storage.
  3. Inside an online agent boundary with active updating: the store may participate in control.

The speculative Centered Closure Theory draft motivates intervention-sensitive boundary analysis, but its closure proposal does not resolve this choice for the present audit [25].

State mixtures and nonstationarity

A nominal stage may mix brief arousals, measurement regimes, or latent substages. A content effect estimated from the mixture need not exist in any component stage. Applications should preregister mixture diagnostics, including:

  • stage-classifier uncertainty;
  • change-point tests;
  • sensitivity to excluding transition-adjacent samples;
  • repeated preparation at matched stage descriptors; and
  • model comparison between one-stage and mixture accounts.

A failed mixture diagnostic blocks the stage-local interpretation. It should not be repaired after inspection by choosing a more favorable interval.

Drift and branch matching

Sensor drift, changing internal logging formats, neural reconfiguration, or changes in model degree can imitate branch loss or creation. Bidirectional data do not eliminate this problem. Drift restrictions must be calibrated independently, and every admissible matching must be reported.

If no matching satisfies the registered drift and quotient-compatibility conditions, the correct result is “no admissible transport.” If both transported and regenerated models remain admissible, the result is “ancestry unresolved.”

Dreams without intervention or recall

Many dream reports concern spontaneous content that cannot be cleanly assigned by do(c). In those cases, predictive decoding or retrospective association may still be scientifically useful, but it is not the interventional audit defined here.

No recall after awakening is compatible with:

  • absent earlier content;
  • failed encoding;
  • failed retention;
  • state-dependent retrieval;
  • inability to report;
  • branch erasure before awakening; or
  • earlier content-sensitive control with no recovery ancestry.

The audit can distinguish some of these only when independent internal channels and longitudinal interventions exist.

Stateless language-model calls

An isolated model invocation with no persistent hidden state has no within-model transport across calls. A later invocation may describe an earlier interaction from an included transcript. Relative to a boundary excluding the transcript store, this is external reconstruction.

If the boundary includes orchestration, retrieval, and context storage, the extended system may have persistent information. The audit must then ask whether the information is merely stored, whether it controls anything during output silence, and whether the later report descends from that particular stored token.

Checkpoint copying and token provenance

A serialized state may preserve information while no online process operates. This supports storage but not contemporaneous control. Restart from the original serialization, restart from a causally derived copy, and restart from an unrelated type-identical template can be predictively indistinguishable.

Predictive transport alone cannot identify token provenance. A token claim requires trusted causal logs, cryptographic or randomized lineage tags, or interventions that distinguish the particular pre-suppression token from a matched replacement.

Workspace-style agents

A workspace architecture passes the audit only if investigators establish that:

  1. a registered content contrast survives output suppression;
  2. it changes a commitment or downstream controlled target;
  3. the effect uses the proposed workspace route;
  4. the route contributes under the relevant natural intervention;
  5. the recovery branch is causally descended rather than regenerated; and
  6. passive performance cannot be reproduced by a route-null model.

Task gains from a consciousness-inspired architecture do not establish these properties [4].

Biological-substrate objections

A biological-necessity theorist can accept the complete organizational profile and deny its phenomenal relevance to a digital system. An implementation-neutral functionalist can accept the bridge in principle but conclude that a particular system lacks the required organization. These positions disagree at different inferential levels.

The audit is neutral between them. It constrains organizational claims but does not establish substrate sufficiency.

Could the audit be too strong?

A phenomenally conscious state could fail because:

  • no valid content intervention exists;
  • every available intervention disrupts the state;
  • no internal commitment can be measured;
  • memory is erased;
  • the relevant system is nonlinear or effectively infinite-dimensional;
  • the selected monitor, boundary, grain, or output is wrong; or
  • branch-selective longitudinal intervention is impossible.

Failure is therefore not evidence of unconsciousness.

Could the audit be too weak?

Yes, if interpreted as a consciousness criterion. An unconscious but sophisticated controller may satisfy every mechanistic condition. As a diagnostic of report-hidden causal organization, however, that system is a legitimate positive case.

Empirical Implications and Falsification Criteria

Mandatory registration

Before examining outcomes, an application should specify:

  1. candidate system boundary;
  2. content intervention, baseline, and reference anchors;
  3. support and attainable-set criteria;
  4. induction and emergence stage coordinates;
  5. state-mixture and change-point diagnostics;
  6. I, C, D, A, R0, and R+;
  7. whether C is a commitment or requires a second control leg;
  8. monitor M and the expanded edge-copy SCM;
  9. route intervention, ordinary intervention, and mediator randomization;
  10. off-target and regime-change sentinels;
  11. finite temporal-grain grid and cross-grain aggregation maps;
  12. predictive features, horizons, metrics, and rank procedure;
  13. longitudinal preparation and pairing protocol;
  14. admissible transport and drift model class;
  15. alternative logs, redundant stores, and regenerative sources;
  16. commutation, preservation, and effect thresholds; and
  17. rules for declaring model rejection, underidentification, or a positive profile.

The boundary, monitor, and grain may be revised in a later confirmatory study, but not retrospectively within the same claim.

Altered-state implications

A suitable altered-state experiment could produce the following outcomes:

  • Content-insensitive contemporaneous report: rR0c​=0.
  • Internal reserve only: uc​>0 with no verified route effect.
  • Route-specific response: diso​>0 but no commitment or downstream control effect.
  • Report-hidden local control: ac​>0, diso​>0, and a valid control qualification.
  • Motor-output disconnection: internal control is positive while the overt-action channel is content-insensitive.
  • Amnesia-compatible profile: local control is positive, but no content effect reaches R+.
  • Reconstruction-compatible profile: R+ is sensitive while the silent-stage control and ancestry tests are negative.
  • Recovery ancestry: a silent branch intervention changes the recovery state after regenerative sources are controlled.
  • Unresolved ancestry: transported and reconstructed longitudinal models both remain admissible.
  • Model rejection: no longitudinal kernel satisfies the registered quotient-compatibility or drift constraints.

The propofol ECoG preprint supports dense transition sampling as a design motivation, but its connectivity measurements cannot estimate these route-specific or ancestry quantities [28]. The tinnitus survey supports separating dream content, external connectedness, and awakening report, but its retrospective design cannot identify the audit profile [29].

White-box AI benchmark program

White-box systems provide a practical initial domain because exact null and hybrid mechanisms can be constructed.

A benchmark should include:

  1. Uninterrupted transport: the same recurrent state evolves through suppression and recovery.
  2. Serialization without computation: state is stored but does not control any process during the pause.
  3. Restart from the original checkpoint: recovery uses the particular stored token.
  4. Restart from a causally derived copy: predictive type is preserved, but token interpretation depends on provenance.
  5. Unrelated type-identical restart: recovery uses a matched template.
  6. External transcript reconstruction: recovery report is generated from visible history.
  7. Stale monitor: passive accuracy is high while same-cut route effect is absent.
  8. Silent controller plus external reconstruction: local control is genuine, but the report has independent ancestry.
  9. Storage plus partial reconstruction: some dimensions descend and others are generated at recovery.
  10. State-mixture artifact: observed ranks arise from mixing two regimes.
  11. Measurement-drift alias: the same physical branch appears different under changing encoders.
  12. Post hoc registration trap: freely fitted maps produce low commutation defect between unrelated branches.

For each model, the study should report:

  • confidence sets for aligned predictive ranks;
  • row-separation intervals for route channels;
  • natural-route contribution and second-leg control effects;
  • every admissible cross-stage transport;
  • sensitivity to the registered grain grid;
  • bounds on Δc​, gc​, and hc​;
  • ancestry effects with alternative sources fixed;
  • false-positive and false-negative classifications; and
  • whether token provenance was measured or merely inferred.

No such benchmark result is reported in this manuscript.

Finite-sample analysis

The formal quantities are population objects. Finite-sample applications add:

  • intervention-assignment uncertainty;
  • dependent observations;
  • weak content effects;
  • rank-selection instability;
  • approximate nonnegative-rank difficulty;
  • model-degree uncertainty;
  • stage drift;
  • incomplete support;
  • latent mixture;
  • branch-matching ambiguity; and
  • invalid off-target assumptions.

Rank should be reported through application-appropriate confidence sets or sensitivity analyses rather than a single thresholded estimate. Near a rank boundary, neither ordinary rank nor nonnegative rank is stable. Singular values are meaningful only in specified feature-space inner products.

For route effects, direct confidence bounds on total-variation row separation may be more transparent than estimating a full nonnegative rank when the substantive question is merely rank one versus greater than one.

For longitudinal analysis, confidence procedures should propagate uncertainty jointly. Selecting the best stage-local realization and then treating its transport as fixed understates uncertainty. A valid favorable claim requires that every model in the simultaneous confidence set satisfy the preservation and ancestry conditions.

Operational rejection rules

An application should reject or withhold the corresponding claim when:

  • Natural support fails: the content intervention leaves the preregistered attainable region or alters off-target sentinels.
  • State stability fails: mixture or change-point diagnostics reject the stage model.
  • Route semantics fail: edge copies cannot be manipulated or linked to ordinary interventions under the declared SCM.
  • Route effect fails: the confidence set includes only effects below the minimum resolution.
  • Control qualification fails: C is not a commitment and no downstream effect is established.
  • Quotient compatibility fails: locally equivalent preparations have different cross-stage outcome distributions.
  • No admissible transport exists: every longitudinal model violates the registered data or drift bounds.
  • Transport is ambiguous: favorable and reconstructive models coexist in the admissible set.
  • Ancestry fails: intervention on the silent branch does not change recovery after alternative sources are fixed.
  • Provenance is absent: only type correspondence, not token lineage, can be reported.

These rules make the assumptions diagnostically consequential rather than universal escape clauses.

Formal falsification criteria

The restricted mathematical claims would be false if any of the following were shown:

  1. Within the strictly proper conditional-mean linear model and saturation assumptions, the stated Hankel rank failed to equal the reachable-observable dimension.
  2. A row-stochastic channel with exact stochastic nonnegative rank one had nonidentical rows, or a channel with identical rows required rank greater than one.
  3. The quotient-compatibility condition held but the descended Markov kernel was not well-defined or unique.
  4. In the expanded randomized SCM, diso​>0 occurred without a causal effect of the edge intervention on C.
  5. A model with recovery state independent of every descendant of US​, conditional on fixed L, generated λc​>0.

The following would not falsify the formal claims:

  • failure of an arbitrary event-probability Hankel to have linear state dimension;
  • rank change after information-losing coarse-graining;
  • failure to estimate a weak effect;
  • absence of a valid content intervention in spontaneous dreaming;
  • a conscious state failing the audit; or
  • a nonphenomenal controller passing the mechanistic audit.

Comparative empirical failure

The proposed diagnostic program would lose practical value if, in benchmark systems with known mechanisms:

  • stale-monitor, transcript-reconstruction, and independent-regeneration systems systematically passed the positive local or ancestry criteria;
  • uninterrupted causal transport systematically failed after valid intervention and adequate sampling;
  • results depended mainly on selecting a favorable grain or registration;
  • off-target diagnostics did not detect interventions known to create the audited state; or
  • the admissible-model analysis routinely collapsed to an unconstrained set incapable of distinguishing any alternatives.

For biological application, repeated inability to define supported content interventions or measurable control channels would establish a domain boundary: the audit would remain conceptually relevant but experimentally inapplicable to those states.

Ethical interpretation

No audit profile should directly determine moral status. A governance analysis may consider

Pr(P∣E),Pr(valenced state∣P,E),severity,duration,agency,relationships,irreversibility,

where E is the evidence. Precaution can be justified under broad uncertainty. The direction of inference is from possible harm and uncertainty to policy, not from precaution back to phenomenality. This is consistent with welfare proposals that explicitly avoid claiming current AI consciousness [7].

Limitations

First, the audit is conditional on a manipulable operational content contrast. Many dreams and altered states are spontaneous. A cue may create, alter, or terminate the state, making the central intervention invalid.

Second, the rank framework is restricted. The linear state-dimension equality applies to strictly proper conditional-mean or Markov-parameter operators, not arbitrary future-event probabilities. Continuous nonlinear or distributional predictive systems may have infinite rank. Finite feature ranks are relative to the registered feature family.

Third, rank gaps identify predictive dimensions, not physical states, semantic content, or causal use. Their interpretation depends on aligned output features, persistent excitation, horizon saturation, and stable stage conditioning.

Fourth, route isolation requires an expanded SCM with manipulable edge copies or a justified physical analogue. Such interventions may be technically impossible, ethically unacceptable, or state-disruptive in brains. Ordinary interventions and passive conditioning do not automatically identify the route-specific channel.

Fifth, a route-specific effect is not automatically endogenous control. If C is telemetry, activation, or autonomic response, a second causal leg to a downstream controlled target is required. Even then, the result is local and does not establish broad cognitive availability or agency.

Sixth, natural support and off-target validity cannot be guaranteed by a single statistic. The proposed support, sentinel, and regime-change tests can reject some interventions but cannot prove that all relevant perturbations are absent.

Seventh, cross-stage transport is not identified by stage-local similarity, injectivity assumed in a fitted map, or low diagram defect. It requires a physical transition model, quotient compatibility, paired longitudinal data, and constraints on alternative regenerative sources. In most biological settings, these conditions may be unattainable.

Eighth, the ancestry intervention requires a physically re-identifiable silent branch carrier. Manipulating that carrier may alter the state globally or create an artificial recovery pathway. A positive effect therefore remains relative to the intervention model and support conditions.

Ninth, causal ancestry is weaker than identity. It establishes that the recovery state depends on the silent branch, not that the two are numerically identical, semantically unchanged, phenomenally continuous, or owned by the same subject. Token lineage requires provenance-sensitive evidence beyond predictive equivalence.

Tenth, storage, control, amnesia, and reconstruction can coexist. A report may contain both transported and reconstructed dimensions. Binary classification should therefore be avoided.

Eleventh, temporal-grain analysis does not reveal a uniquely correct subjective timescale. A finite grid reduces post hoc cut selection but remains relative to the chosen commitments, aggregation maps, and measurements.

Twelfth, the system boundary is not identified by the audit. Including or excluding external memory, orchestration, sensors, caregivers, or environmental records can change whether a case is described as storage, extended control, or reinjection.

Thirteenth, loop discrepancy is multiply determined. Re-encoding, path dependence, sensor drift, model-degree change, and an inadequate reference can produce similar results. No unique hysteresis mechanism follows from hc​.

Fourteenth, finite-sample robustness is not established by a general perturbation theorem here. The proposed confidence-set logic is a research design requirement, not a supplied universal estimator or error bound.

Fifteenth, the specialist evidence base in the supplied records is weak. It consists mainly of preprints, a retrospective survey, simulations, and conceptual work. The manuscript does not provide a comprehensive peer-reviewed review of anesthesia, connected experience, covert command following, sleep interruption, perioperative memory, or dream-report methodology. The altered-state sections should therefore be read as protocol constraints, not empirical validation.

Finally, no phenomenal bridge is validated. Integration, recurrence, complexity, report, causal control, ancestry, and observer attribution can each be scientifically important without being equivalent to experience.

Conclusion

Silent intervals do not define a single state. A system may lack a content distinction, store it without using it, use it without overt action, fail to encode it for later report, lose it before recovery, or reconstruct a report from another source. Dreaming and anesthesia make these alternatives methodologically unavoidable, while white-box AI systems make some of them experimentally constructible.

The revised Conditional Transition Audit separates three questions.

First, aligned predictive operators ask whether internal telemetry or a registered commitment contains content-sensitive distinctions hidden from contemporaneous report. Their ranks are descriptive and model-relative.

Second, an explicit edge-intervention SCM asks whether the content contrast changes a registered process through a proposed monitor. This establishes a route-specific response capacity. It establishes local control only when the output is a commitment or has an independently verified downstream effect.

Third, longitudinal intervention asks whether the silent branch causally contributes to the recovery state after external logs, redundant stores, and other regenerative sources are controlled. Stage-local isomorphism and low commutation defect do not answer this question. They can align types while a recovery state is independently regenerated. Recovery ancestry therefore remains a separate, conditional conclusion over all admissible longitudinal models.

This revision withdraws the stronger claim that bidirectional predictive transport is identified merely from injective fitted maps and commuting diagrams. The defensible contribution is a conceptual and formal audit template: report-hidden local control can sometimes be distinguished from storage and passive association, while recovery ancestry requires stronger evidence that may be unavailable in biological altered states.

None of these results identifies phenomenal consciousness. A positive profile constrains causal organization. A negative profile is often inconclusive. Agency requires broader temporally extended control, token continuity requires provenance-sensitive evidence, and moral status requires normative judgment under uncertainty. The proper lesson of altered-state research is therefore not that silence conceals consciousness by default, but that responsiveness, memory, report, internal control, recovery ancestry, and experience must be represented as different inferential targets.
