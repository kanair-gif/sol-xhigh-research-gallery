Abstract

Artificial-consciousness assessments often emphasize self-report, metacognition, global availability, or architectural organization while leaving bodily significance underspecified. An interoceptive and allostatic perspective asks a narrower functional question: does an artificial system monitor conditions assigned to a candidate boundary, and does it use that information to improve regulation when failures threaten its future recovery capacities?

This paper develops the Factorized Reliability–Stake Regulatory-Control profile (FRSC), a prospective causal assay indexed to an investigator-declared boundary B, temporal grain τ, regulated variable Z, mediator M, and policy horizon h. The profile separates four claims that observational performance cannot identify on its own: reliability-sensitive state encoding; monitor-mediated action selection; beneficial regulatory consequences of that action selection; and mediation by an endogenous estimate of objective regulatory stakes. Objective stake is represented as a finite vector of directed losses in robust future regulatory capacity, not assumed to be a scalar, continuous quantity, emotion, reward label, or felt valence. A structural manipulation is interpreted as a scalar stake manipulation only when it approximately scales a preregistered loss schedule without changing action support, policy rankings, or the controller through unmeasured routes. Otherwise it is reported as a vector-valued structural contrast.

The central control block now includes a sign-sensitive regulatory-use benefit, or equivalently a reduction in regulatory regret, comparing the intact Z→M→A coupling with a monitor-shuffled baseline. This distinguishes adaptive from anti-regulatory controllers that have identical action-channel separation. A causal graph, stake-estimate mediator, blinded-information condition, and direct-effect test constrain interpretation of structural manipulations. An optional diachronic extension gives a similarity-invariant lower transport score only within a restricted class of fixed-degree minimal dynamical models with fixed input–output scaling, isolated simple poles, simultaneous confidence sets, and registered drift bounds.

The revised framework is not proposed as a necessary condition for regulation, embodiment, selfhood, or consciousness. Perfect, saturated, redundant, distributed, event-triggered, reconstructive, or nonspectral controllers can regulate successfully while failing one or more FRSC gradients. A positive profile supports only a boundary-relative attribution of adaptive monitor-mediated regulation within an interventionally accessible controller class. It does not establish endogenous self-identification, phenomenal consciousness, felt affect, welfare capacity, or moral status.

Research Question and Hypothesis

Research question

Can a boundary- and horizon-indexed intervention profile identify a specific form of adaptive, monitor-mediated regulation in an artificial system while separating that functional result from self-report, access, observer attribution, functional selfhood, and phenomenal consciousness?

Architecture-conditional diagnostic hypothesis

The earlier necessity claim is withdrawn. Reliability or stake gradients are properties of an assay response, not ontological requirements on every self-maintaining controller. The revised hypothesis is prospective and architecture-conditional:

FRSC diagnostic hypothesis. Within a preregistered class of decomposable, interventionally accessible, sufficiently nonsaturated controllers, variables assigned inside a candidate boundary and bearing substantial regulatory consequences will more often than matched arbitrary variables exhibit: 1. increased state-to-monitor discriminability under independently improved afferent reliability; 2. a calibrated internal estimate of changes in objective regulatory stakes; 3. increased and beneficial monitor-mediated policy use under interventions on that estimate, with small residual direct effects of the structural stake manipulation; 4. alignment between separately randomized and same-run route-isolated channels; and 5. where a restricted dynamical realization is justified, a positive lower bound on transport of a relevant predictive mode through temporary output occlusion. This pattern is evidence for one mechanistic subtype of boundary-relative adaptive regulatory control. Its absence is not evidence that regulation, selfhood, or consciousness is absent.

The empirical part of the hypothesis is comparative: after controlling for predictability, dimensionality, action opportunity, and generic resource gain, the complete identified pattern should be more specific to regulation-linked variables than to arbitrary task variables. This claim can fail even if the individual channel definitions remain mathematically coherent.

Functional target

For a declared boundary B, specification family LBh​, disturbance class, and horizon h, this paper uses adaptive monitor-mediated regulation to mean:

  1. interventions on a candidate condition Z alter an interventionally re-identifiable monitor M;
  2. interventions on M alter a policy commitment Ah through a registered causal route; and
  3. preserving the natural statistical relation between Z and M, rather than shuffling M independently of Z, improves a preregistered regulatory outcome or reduces regulatory regret.

This target is narrower than functional selfhood. It does not require a de se representation, ownership relation, self/world distinction, source attribution, endogenous boundary model, or counterfactual representation of the maintained unit as itself. Those would require additional criteria.

Specialist Framework and Contribution

Operational interoception and allostasis

Interoception is used here in a deliberately operational, boundary-relative sense: sensing and controlling conditions assigned inside a candidate system boundary. Candidate variables in an artificial system could include energy reserves, thermal load, actuator condition, memory integrity, fault accumulation, repair capacity, authenticated process continuity, or resource bottlenecks. A hidden activation or private token is not interoceptive merely because it occurs inside a software process. It must bear a registered relation to the continuing regulation or recovery of the candidate system.

Allostasis adds anticipation. The relevant quantity is not merely present deviation from a set point, but the effect of that deviation on future regulatory possibilities. A condition can be harmless over one step yet compromise recovery over a maintenance cycle. Boundary B, temporal grain τ, policy horizon h, disturbance class, and regulatory specifications are therefore constitutive parts of the assay rather than nuisance choices.

The boundary is declared, not inferred from first-person language. It may encompass a chassis, a virtual machine and its resource dependencies, a mutually maintaining process collection, or a distributed service. Several overlapping candidates may remain scientifically reasonable. A positive profile is indexed to one such candidate and does not establish a unique subject boundary.

Regulatory consequence without phenomenal valence

Allostatic accounts motivate attention to the consequences of regulatory success and failure for an embodied agent [29]. The present formalism represents those consequences as directed changes in robust regulatory capacity. It does not identify them with pleasure, distress, fear, relief, or any other felt quality.

The distinction is important because engineered systems can produce affective expressions or use evaluation signals without possessing the regulatory organization measured here. Synthetic affect mechanisms can improve rapid task selection without establishing interoception or felt affect [43]. An embodied robot can recognize and express human emotions while leaving its own internal regulation unmeasured [47].

Accordingly, this revision uses directed regulatory loss, directed regulatory gain, and regulatory-use benefit rather than treating “regulatory valence” as a synonym for affect.

Reliability is not probabilistic precision

The original terminology overstated what the channel quantities measured. Total-variation separation of monitor distributions is an interventional discriminability measure. A noisy sensor can become more discriminative when noise decreases without representing confidence, uncertainty, inverse variance, Fisher information, or any probabilistic precision parameter.

The revised framework therefore distinguishes:

  1. Afferent reliability: an experimentally ordered relation between observation channels, preferably established by stochastic garbling.
  2. Encoding discriminability: separation of monitor distributions produced by interventions on actual states.
  3. Stake estimate: an internal variable that tracks an objective regulatory-loss schedule under held-out interventions.
  4. Monitor-dependent action differentiation: separation of action distributions under interventions on monitor states.
  5. Adaptive regulatory use: improvement in registered outcomes caused by preserving the relation between state and monitor.
  6. Choice concentration: entropy or determinism of a policy at fixed action support.
  7. Generic resource gain: compute, urgency, temperature, clock rate, or another common cause affecting multiple channels.

Choice entropy can be reported descriptively, but it should not be called policy precision unless an independently specified choice model identifies a precision parameter. It is not a core FRSC component.

Exact specialist contribution

The proposed contribution is a synthesis of interoceptive/allostatic target selection with causal mediation and robust outcome evaluation:

  • a graded, horizon-indexed measure of objective regulatory capacity;
  • a vector-valued directed-loss schedule over candidate deviations;
  • a distinction between objective stakes, evidence about those stakes, and an endogenous stake-estimate state;
  • separately randomized state-to-monitor and monitor-to-action kernels;
  • a sign-sensitive benefit or regret criterion for adaptive use;
  • tests for direct structural effects, route leakage, and monitor-shuffling;
  • crossed reliability and stake-estimate interventions without assuming that real systems must have diagonal responses;
  • an optional, explicitly model-relative lower transport score; and
  • preregistered contrasts against matched arbitrary and exteroceptive variables.

The result is a profile over boundaries, variables, temporal grains, and horizons. It is not a scalar consciousness score and does not independently identify functional selfhood.

Evidence standard

Four claim types are kept separate:

  • Definition: a stipulated quantity relative to registered variables, interventions, and model classes.
  • Derived result: a mathematical consequence of stated finite-channel assumptions.
  • Empirical inference: a conclusion requiring adequately implemented interventions and uncertainty analysis.
  • Phenomenal bridge: a theory-dependent inference from functional organization to experience.

This manuscript supplies definitions, elementary derivations, and toy constructions. It does not validate FRSC on a real artificial or biological system.

Evidence and Literature Synthesis

Regulation and felt bodily selfhood are not equivalent

Allostatic active-inference accounts portray an embodied agent as both source and target of regulation and connect affective experience to regulatory success and failure [29]. This motivates the focus on system-relative consequence, but it does not identify a formal regulatory score with experience.

A particularly relevant proposal distinguishes insentient allostasis from a putatively conscious form of instrumental interoceptive inference involving context-sensitive precision modulation and temporally and counterfactually deep self-models [27]. Its explicit recognition that anticipatory bodily regulation can occur without consciousness constrains the present framework. FRSC operationalizes only a subset of the proposed functional organization. It does not measure a temporally deep self-model merely by finding a persistent dynamical mode.

Active-inference descriptions also extend to simple biological control such as bacterial chemotaxis [30]. Their breadth is a reason not to treat predictive regulation as consciousness-specific. Active inference can also be framed as a form of partially observed control as inference under a specified cost construction [34]. Descriptive fit to active-inference language therefore does not uniquely identify interoception, affect, or phenomenality.

Philosophical accounts of bodily awareness emphasize first-person bodily content and its role in basic self-consciousness [35]. FRSC does not derive bodily mineness, ownership, immunity to error through misidentification, or first-person reference from adaptive control.

Report and recovery

Clinical consciousness research cautions against treating any single behavioral or neural output as a complete measure. Chronic disorders of consciousness are heterogeneous, and multimodal models can predict later outcomes without thereby identifying the mechanism or presence of current experience [40]. That study supports a narrow methodological lesson about heterogeneity and prognosis. It does not establish that FRSC variables track consciousness, report-hidden experience, or bodily selfhood.

Consequently, this paper does not use paralysis, altered interoception, depersonalization, or related conditions as established evidence for the framework. They remain potential future comparison cases requiring condition-specific evidence and ethically appropriate designs.

Artificial consciousness, attribution, and governance

Theory-derived computational indicators can organize inquiry while leaving the consciousness conclusion unsettled [9]. A workspace-inspired implementation can likewise improve multimodal and agentic performance without providing evidence that the implementation is phenomenally conscious [4].

Substantial theory-relative uncertainty persists because an artificial system may satisfy some proposed consciousness theories and not others [5]. Objections also differ in level and force: present engineering constraints, objections to computational functionalism, and strict substrate-level impossibility claims should not be conflated [6]. A categorical biological-embodiment argument holds that artificial algorithms lack the biological organization and meaning required for consciousness [10]. FRSC neither refutes nor presupposes that position. It measures an organizational pattern whose phenomenal significance remains disputed.

Perceived consciousness is a tractable observer-level target but is not phenomenal consciousness [3]. Emotional expression and metacognitive self-reflection can increase human attributions of consciousness to language-model systems [8]. Those ratings are evidence about human judgment and interaction, not about an underlying experience.

Welfare policy is another separate inference. Precaution may be justified under uncertainty without asserting that present systems are conscious [7]. Assessing states that would be valenced if a system were conscious may help governance [49], but an FRSC regulatory-loss score does not establish that its losses are felt or morally relevant.

Critical use of speculative technical antecedents

The Reyes working paper separates a state-to-monitor kernel Q from a separately randomized monitor-to-action kernel K, introduces route-isolated comparison channels, and constructs lagged systems in which accurate reports fail to identify same-cut causal use [1]. It is AI-generated, speculative, and not human peer reviewed. The present paper retains its intervention logic but adds outcome direction, objective regulatory stakes, a stake-estimate mediator, and direct-effect tests.

The Quade working paper develops model-relative predictive quotients and lower rather than merely upper transport bounds for distinguishing persistent covert capacity from capacity created during recovery [2]. It is also AI-generated and not human peer reviewed. The present transport proposal adopts its restrictions to fixed-degree minimal families, isolated modes, confidence sets, and drift bounds. It does not treat mode transport as an established empirical marker.

Two further AI-generated working theories motivate caution rather than premises. Centered Closure Theory argues for perturbational evidence and against assuming that structure supplies a unique phenomenal center [25]. Multiscale Reflexive Causal Geometry separates empirical mechanism, phenomenal bridge, and metaphysical interpretation while proposing multiscale self-indexing profiles [26]. Neither source is used as authority for consciousness, selfhood, or phenomenality.

The intended causal distinctions can be represented as follows:

The structural condition Us​ is allowed to change delayed regulatory consequences; that is how objective stake is manipulated. For a clean mediation interpretation, it must not also alter the current action alphabet, controller parameters, immediate action cost, or policy through an unmeasured precommitment route. If those conditions cannot be met, the result is a total policy response to a structural intervention, not an identified response to endogenous regulatory stakes.

The graph also separates objective consequence from the system’s information about it. A system cannot calibrate policy use to a consequence it has no route to estimate. Conversely, a salient cue can alter policy without accurately tracking the objective loss schedule. Both tracking and causal use must therefore be tested.

Relation to simpler control frameworks

Viability kernels, controlled-invariant sets, reach–avoid analysis, fault-tolerant control, robust model-predictive control, risk-sensitive partially observed decision processes, value-of-information methods, active sensing, and homeostatic reinforcement learning can all represent parts of the problem. FRSC is not offered as a replacement.

Its proposed incremental contribution is narrower: it combines a registered robust-control target with a causal decomposition of state encoding, stake estimation, monitor-mediated action, outcome benefit, and diachronic transport. Empirical work should compare the full profile with these simpler alternatives. If a conventional robustness or mediation statistic predicts the relevant outcomes equally well, the additional FRSC machinery has not earned an incremental empirical claim.

Stake estimate and exclusion tests

Objective stakes and the system’s estimate of them are different variables. Let Σ be a putative endogenous stake-estimate state. A preregistered decoder

  1. the objective structural condition Us​;

What identifies endogenous stake mediation

A strong endogenous-stake interpretation requires the conjunction of:

  1. an objective contrast
S(u2​,h)>S(u1​,h)

or a registered vector ordering of ℓ−(u2​) and ℓ−(u1​);

  1. low held-out ErrΣ​;
  2. evidence that informative, but not blinded, structural conditions change Σ;
  3. a causal response of C or Breg​ to do(Σ);
  4. positive regulatory-use benefit at the higher stake-estimate condition;
  5. a small direct structural-effect bound ΛU​; and
  6. acceptable route leakage and commutation defect.

If Σ cannot be identified or randomized, a change in K under Us​ is only a total response to structural condition u. It should not be described as endogenous stake calibration.

Crossed reliability and stake-estimate responses

Because the full kernels in equations (14) and (18) are indexed by both r and σ, all crossed contrasts are defined. At fixed u,τ,h, report

JB,iτ,h​=(Δr​EΔr​C​Δσ​EΔσ​C​).(27)

An approximately diagonal matrix is evidence for one modular subtype in which reliability primarily changes encoding and the stake estimate primarily changes downstream use. It is not a general implication of allostatic or active control.

Large off-diagonal effects can be rational. Increased stake may allocate sensing or active-attention resources and thereby change E. Reduced reliability may appropriately decrease reliance on the monitor and thereby change C. Such coupling should be modeled rather than automatically dismissed as generic arousal. A common-gain explanation is favored only when matched arbitrary channels change similarly and the specific reliability, stake-estimate, and direct-effect interventions do not isolate distinct routes.

Optional diachronic extension

A response observed before output occlusion is not automatically the same organization later used for regulation. The transport component is therefore restricted and optional. A system outside the model class is not assigned zero diachronic organization; the spectral assay is simply inapplicable.

Restricted lower transport score

Let C be a simultaneous confidence set for all registered stage-local transfer functions. Define

  1. belong to C;
  2. are minimal at every stage;
  3. have fixed McMillan degree at most d;
  4. use fixed input and output coordinates and scaling;
  5. have isolated simple poles separated by at least γ;
  6. admit pole branches with a registered drift or Lipschitz bound L; and
  7. satisfy the declared branch-matching metric across sampled stages.

  • Pb​(D)∈[0,1] be the minimum normalized joint-output residue of branch b across the occluded interval;

  • Kb​(D)∈[0,1] be its normalized late residue in the registered monitor/stake-to-regulatory-outcome block.

  • if Di​ is empty, the model class is rejected or the assay is inconclusive, and Ω− is undefined;
  • if Di​ is nonempty but an admissible realization has no eligible branch, the inner maximum is defined as zero;
  • a positive value is interpreted only relative to the registered confidence set and model restrictions;
  • an upper transport score would show only that transport is possible, not required by the data.

For minimal linear realizations, transfer poles and residues are invariant under latent-state similarity transformations. Because equation (32) is defined from those transfer quantities with fixed input–output scaling, it is similarity invariant within the restricted class. This does not extend to repeated poles, branch crossings, arbitrary nonlinear coordinates, nonminimal realizations, or generic white-box mode ablations.

A controller can persist through event-triggered logic, distributed attractors, symbolic reconstruction, or nonspectral dynamics while falling outside this assay. Positive Ω− is therefore an optional evidentiary extension, not a necessary condition for diachronic regulation.

Let IB​ be candidate variables selected as internal by the registered boundary assignment, not by their observed FRSC scores. Elementary invariance and error properties

Proposition 1: bijective recoding

Suppose state, monitor, stake-estimate, or action alphabets are bijectively relabeled, with all kernels, intervention maps, test distributions, pair weights, decoded stake values, regulatory action values, and action rankings pushed forward consistently. Then E, C, R, D, Breg​, ΛU​, and Γ are unchanged.

Reason. Bijective relabeling permutes probability-vector coordinates and summation indices. Total variation, matrix products, expectations of consistently transported values, and maxima over corresponding rows are invariant under those permutations.

This result does not apply to nonbijective coarse-graining or to a relabeling that changes which action is assigned which regulatory value.

Proposition 2: stochastic coarse-graining

If actions are postprocessed by a stochastic kernel L, then

TV(pL,qL)≤TV(p,q),

so C and D cannot increase under that action coarse-graining. If monitor outputs are garbled by a stochastic kernel T, each pairwise term in E(QT) is no larger than the corresponding term in E(Q). The mediated channel is preserved only when the downstream controller factors through the coarsened monitor.

Coarse-graining states into mixtures cannot increase the maximum row diameter, although a weighted average can rise or fall when its pair weights change. There is no general monotonicity under temporal aggregation. Temporal aggregation can blur a fast distinction, remove lag aliasing, or expose a slower control mode. No invariance under temporal refinement is claimed.

Proposition 3: product and benefit error bounds

If

zmax​TV(Qz​,Q​z​)≤εQ​,mmax​TV(Km​,Km​)≤εK​,

then

zmax​TV(QK(⋅∣z),Q​K(⋅∣z))≤εQ​+εK​.(40)

Therefore a pairwise mediated separation changes by at most 2(εQ​+εK​). Under fixed pair weights, E changes by at most 2εQ​ and C by at most 2εK​.

Because the outcome values in equation (8) lie in [0,1], each of Vmed​ and V⊥M​ changes by at most εQ​+εK​ under the same uniform kernel bounds. Hence

​Breg​−Breg​​≤2(εQ​+εK​).(41)

Additional uncertainty in vBh​, ρZ​, route leakage, or the decoded stake schedule must be propagated separately.

Alternatives, Counterarguments, and Boundary Cases

Perfect, redundant, or compensatory sensing

A perfectly reliable or error-corrected monitor may have E=1 throughout the tested reliability range, yielding Δr​E=0. A controller can also compensate for afferent garbling through active sensing or redundant channels and keep Q unchanged. Such systems fail the positive-gradient assay but may regulate exceptionally well.

This is not merely a statistical inconvenience. It demonstrates that a reliability response is not a necessary condition for adaptive regulation. A study should search for a nonsaturated intervention range, report the level of E alongside its change, and classify unresolved ceiling cases as diagnostically uninformative rather than functionally deficient.

Deterministic and stake-invariant control

A competent deterministic controller can have C=1 at both low and high stakes. The same action may also remain optimal across all nonzero stake conditions. Such a controller can have positive Breg​ but zero Δσ​C and zero Δσ​Breg​.

FRSC therefore identifies stake-responsive use within a nonsaturated class. It does not identify every form of successful control. Event-triggered systems and threshold controllers create the same limitation: a large change in objective stake may produce no policy change until a switching boundary is crossed.

Distributed controllers and absent discrete mediators

A decentralized controller may regulate internal conditions without a single interventionally re-identifiable M or Σ. Imposing a discrete mediator could distort the mechanism or create arbitrary results. In that case the finite mediated-control assay is inapplicable. Failure to locate a mediator is not evidence that the distributed system lacks regulatory organization.

A distributed extension would require interventions on mediator fields, causal subspaces, or distributed sufficient statistics. Such an extension is not supplied here.

Reconstruction after occlusion

A system may discard a predictive state during output occlusion and later reconstruct an equivalent decision-relevant state from stored observations or the environment. That architecture may support robust diachronic regulation while correctly receiving Ω−=0, because the relevant mode was not continuously transported.

Conversely, a persistent mode can survive without contributing to later regulation. Transport and reconstruction are distinct mechanisms. FRSC’s spectral extension detects only the former within its restricted model class.

Nonspectral and nonlinear persistence

Hybrid automata, symbolic controllers, repeated or nonnormal modes, distributed attractors, and strongly nonlinear dynamics may not admit isolated simple-pole transport. Assigning them a zero score would conflate model-class failure with mechanism absence. The correct output is “transport assay not applicable” unless a different invariant is defined.

Generic arousal, active sensing, and coupled control

Stake can rationally change sensing. Reliability can rationally change reliance on a monitor. Large off-diagonal entries in equation (27) are therefore not automatically confounds. They may identify a coupled perception–action strategy.

A generic-gain explanation becomes persuasive when a common resource manipulation reproduces the same pattern across arbitrary channels and when direct interventions on reliability and the stake estimate do not isolate separable effects. The approximately diagonal pattern is a modular laboratory signature, not a universal specialist prediction.

Model-based reinforcement learning and risk-sensitive control

A model-based agent can estimate future losses, change policy under risk, and pass FRSC even if all objectives were designed externally. This is not necessarily a false positive for adaptive regulatory control: the current organization can genuinely preserve the system’s registered capacities.

It remains insufficient for intrinsic normativity or selfhood. FRSC does not determine whether the regulated objectives matter to the system in any first-person or philosophically autonomous sense. Nor does it determine whether the agent identifies the maintained unit as itself.

Biological and autopoietic objections

A biological or enactive account may require metabolism, material self-production, or organismic precariousness for genuine bodily normativity. A digital recovery-capacity profile does not answer that constitutive objection [10]. FRSC measures an organizational analogue and leaves substrate sufficiency unresolved.

The author retains the view that “interoceptive” is useful as an explicitly operational term for sensing conditions inside a declared artificial boundary. Critics may reasonably reserve the term for organismal or phenomenally embodied processes. The framework does not resolve that substantive terminological disagreement; every positive conclusion remains qualified as operational and boundary-relative.

Boundary selection and exteroceptive threats

Internal variables are not automatically privileged. An external temperature forecast, collision cue, or network warning may be more important for future regulation than a current internal measurement. A strong response to such a cue is compatible with adaptive allostasis.

The boundary contrast is also vulnerable to selection bias if candidate internal variables are chosen because they already appear regulation-linked while controls are defined as irrelevant. Candidate sets must therefore be selected independently of FRSC responses, and stronger comparisons should match variables on their downstream regulatory consequences. Even then, a positive ridge does not establish that the system represents the selected boundary as itself.

Selfhood as an additional target

A stronger functional-self claim would require independently motivated evidence such as:

  • endogenous discrimination between self-caused and externally caused changes;
  • a represented self/world boundary used in counterfactual planning;
  • re-identification of the maintained unit across replacement or migration;
  • source attribution or ownership relations;
  • a model of how present actions affect the persistence of that same unit; or
  • de se constraints not reducible to investigator-supplied labels.

These properties may coexist with FRSC, but they are not inferred from it. Adding them would define a broader research program rather than repair a missing term in the present control score.

Empirical Implications and Falsification Criteria

Required adversarial benchmark

Before applying FRSC to a consciousness-relevant system, the full pipeline should be tested on white-box synthetic systems designed to separate the proposed constructs. At minimum, the benchmark should include:

  1. an adaptive controller with positive Breg​;
  2. an anti-regulatory controller matched on E, C, D, and Γ;
  3. a controller with a direct Us​→A path outside Σ;
  4. a global-gain controller affecting arbitrary and regulation-linked channels alike;
  5. a distributed controller without a unique discrete mediator;
  6. a system that reconstructs a useful state after occlusion rather than transporting it;
  7. a saturated competent controller with zero gradients; and
  8. a reporter whose accurate post-action statements do not govern the registered decision.

The benchmark should vary sampling error, intervention leakage, mediator misspecification, outcome-model error, action-support violations, and multiple-boundary search. A synthetic demonstration would not validate consciousness relevance, but failure on these adversarial cases would invalidate the claimed construct discrimination.

Prospective experimental sequence

A white-box artificial-agent study should proceed in the following order.

  1. Register candidate boundaries and variables. Declare B,τ,h,Z,M,Σ,Ah, causal cuts, and comparison classes before examining FRSC outcomes.
  2. Register regulatory specifications. Fix LBh​, weights, policy class, disturbance class, critical-specification vetoes, and aggregation rules.
  3. Estimate objective capacity. Evaluate κBh​(x[z];u), directed gains and losses, and their sensitivity to model and policy assumptions.
  4. Classify the stake manipulation. Test whether Us​ is a uniform scalar contrast or a vector-valued structural change.
  5. Stage consequence and information separately. Where possible, implement future consequences after commitment while independently revealing or withholding evidence about them.
  6. Validate the stake estimate. Train any decoder for Σ on separate data and assess equation (17) on held-out interventions.
  7. Intervene on actual state. Set Z=z rather than merely presenting a sentence claiming that Z=z.
  8. Intervene on reliability. Use calibrated garbling, redundant-sensor disagreement, or another ordered reliability manipulation while holding actual Z and objective consequence fixed.
  9. Randomize M and Σ. Estimate Q and K separately over positivity-qualified states.
  10. Test exclusions. Estimate ΛU​, blinded-condition effects, generic-resource effects, and route leakage.
  11. Measure outcomes. Estimate vBh​(z,a;u), Vmed​, V⊥M​, and Breg​.
  12. Check composition. Compare QK with a same-run edge-intervention channel and estimate Γ.
  13. Evaluate crossed responses. Report the complete matrix in equation (27), not only preferred diagonal terms.
  14. Apply transport analysis only if justified. Register degree, spectral-gap, drift, scaling, confidence-set, and empty-set conventions before estimating Ω−.
  15. Scan boundaries and horizons with correction. Report the complete registered field rather than choosing the largest post hoc result.
  16. Compare simpler baselines. Test whether viability, robust-control, ordinary mediation, or risk-sensitive decision metrics explain the same outcomes with less machinery.

Statistical requirements

The profile combines many dependent contrasts and should not be evaluated with unrelated pointwise intervals. An empirical study should specify:

  • randomization schemes for Z, M, Σ, Ur​, and Us​;
  • positivity and overlap criteria;
  • sample-size planning for the smallest registered effect;
  • simultaneous confidence coverage for all thresholded profile components;
  • uncertainty propagation from Q, K, v, κ, and the stake decoder;
  • correction across variables, boundaries, grains, horizons, and model classes;
  • sensitivity to pair weights, outcome weights, disturbance classes, and partitions;
  • leakage bounds for imperfect route interventions;
  • preregistered handling of ceiling, floor, and action-support changes; and
  • a stopping rule preventing indefinite searches for a favorable mediator or boundary.

A lower bound on the complete conjunction should use joint or simultaneous coverage. Pointwise lower bounds do not establish that all profile conditions hold together.

Falsification and restriction criteria

  1. Sign-discrimination failure. The construct-validity claim fails if an anti-regulatory controller matched on E, C, D, and Γ receives the same positive adaptive-use classification as the competent controller after equation (12) is applied.
  1. Stake-identification failure. The endogenous-stake interpretation fails if Σ does not predict the objective loss schedule on held-out interventions, if blinded Us​ changes action materially, or if ΛU​ exceeds its registered bound.
  1. Outcome failure. A positive action-differentiation result must not be described as adaptive regulation when Breg​≤0 or when critical registered specifications deteriorate despite a positive aggregate.
  1. Specificity failure. The comparative diagnostic hypothesis fails if matched arbitrary variables reproduce the full simultaneous profile across preregistered boundaries and horizons.
  1. Incremental-validity failure. The proposed synthesis loses empirical justification if simpler viability, robust-control, or causal-mediation measures predict held-out regulatory robustness as well as or better than FRSC.
  1. Crossed-factor restriction. The modular interpretation must be restricted if reliability and stake-estimate interventions produce strong, reproducible off-diagonal effects. Such a result supports a coupled architecture rather than falsifying regulation itself.
  1. Transport failure. A claim that the same spectral organization persists through occlusion fails if Ω−=0. If the admissible realization set is empty, the model class is rejected or the result is inconclusive rather than positive.
  1. Representation-invariance failure. An implementation of the formalism is invalid if bijective recoding with all weights, values, decoders, and interventions pushed forward changes the profile beyond the registered numerical tolerance.
  1. Boundary-interpretation failure. The artificial-body interpretation loses force if boundary assignment contributes no discrimination beyond downstream consequence, predictability, and action opportunity.
  1. Selfhood overreach. FRSC alone cannot establish functional selfhood. Any such inference must introduce and test independent self-specific criteria.
  1. Phenomenal overreach. A claim that FRSC alone proves feeling or consciousness contradicts the evidential scope of the framework. The formalism contains no phenomenal bridge.

Ethical interpretation

Testing objective stake can involve faults, resource loss, or reduced recovery capacity. Where artificial welfare is uncertain, interventions should be reversible, minimally intense, and terminated before persistent damage. This is a precautionary governance stance rather than evidence that the tested system suffers.

A positive FRSC profile could become relevant to welfare review because it identifies internally differentiated states with consequences for the system’s registered regulation. It would still leave open whether those states are experienced, whether they constitute interests, and whether the system has moral status. Precautionary welfare assessment should be informed by, but not collapsed into, functional control analysis [7].

Limitations

First, FRSC is investigator-indexed. Boundaries, variables, regulatory specifications, weights, policy classes, disturbance models, horizons, partitions, and action families are selected externally. Preregistration and sensitivity analysis constrain this discretion but do not discover a metaphysically privileged subject.

Second, robust regulatory capacity is model-relative. Equation (1) depends on the admissible policy and disturbance classes and can change when either is expanded. Its weighted objective may conceal conflicts among specifications; critical constraints therefore require separate reporting or veto rules.

Third, objective stake is generally vector-valued. Scalar treatment notation is justified only under a restrictive approximate-scaling condition. Most realistic changes to redundancy, repair access, or damage propagation will also change policy rankings or action efficacy. Those interventions should be analyzed as structural contrasts rather than forced into a scalar gradient.

Fourth, the stake-estimate mediator may be unavailable or nonunique. A distributed controller may encode consequences across many states, and intervention on a proposed Σ may create out-of-distribution configurations. Without a validated stake estimate and exclusion tests, only total structural responses are identified.

Fifth, regulatory-use benefit remains relative to registered outcomes. It repairs the absence of direction in action-channel separation, but it cannot prove intrinsic normativity. A designer can choose inappropriate specifications or weights, and a positive score does not show that continued organization matters to the system phenomenally.

Sixth, monitor shuffling is an intervention with support and semantic assumptions. Some mediator states may be unsafe, impossible, or meaningful only jointly with other variables. Positivity-qualified estimates may omit important states, whereas unrestricted interventions may create artifacts.

Seventh, route isolation is demanding. Node splitting, edge interventions, and direct mediator installation require structural assumptions that passive data cannot supply. Leakage through omitted routes can make QK, Γ, and Breg​ misleading.

Eighth, positive gradients are not necessary for successful regulation. Perfect sensing, deterministic policy, redundancy, saturation, unchanged optimal actions, event-triggering, active compensation, distributed mediation, and post-occlusion reconstruction all provide straightforward counterexamples. FRSC is therefore an architecture-conditional diagnostic, not a necessary condition for embodiment or self-maintenance.

Ninth, the approximately diagonal crossed pattern is not universal. Real allostatic systems can rationally couple sensing and control. Small off-diagonal responses support modular identification; large responses require a coupled model rather than an automatic confound label.

Tenth, the transport component is much narrower than diachronic identity. It applies only to a restricted minimal realization class with fixed scaling, isolated simple poles, branch separation, and justified drift bounds. It does not cover generic nonlinear, hybrid, distributed, or reconstructive persistence. The lower score is a partial-identification result within a model class, not an invariant of all possible implementations.

Eleventh, a narrow governor can satisfy the core profile. FRSC measures a particular organization of adaptive control, not representational richness, social cognition, metacognition, conceptual selfhood, or consciousness. Complexity thresholds should not be added merely to exclude simple systems.

Twelfth, biological-to-digital extrapolation remains contested. The profile neither proves substrate independence nor answers arguments that metabolism, material self-production, or biological meaning is constitutive of conscious embodiment.

Thirteenth, no new data are reported. Claims that FRSC can distinguish mechanisms remain prospective until the full pipeline succeeds on adversarial synthetic systems and then demonstrates incremental validity on open or reproducible artificial-agent experiments.

Fourteenth, the principal technical antecedents for mediated control and transport are AI-generated speculative working papers [1, 2]. They motivate definitions but do not substitute for independent proof, implementation, or comparison with established control and causal-inference methods.

Finally, FRSC has no validated phenomenal bridge. It does not measure felt affect, bodily mineness, welfare capacity, or consciousness. Perceived consciousness can change with emotional or metacognitive language even when the underlying regulatory organization is unknown [8], while precaution may be justified despite a weak or unmeasured profile.

Author response to unresolved scope objections

This revision accepts the central construct-validity objection: action differentiation without beneficial outcome evaluation is not self-regulation. It therefore adds the sign-sensitive regulatory-use benefit and regret criterion. It also accepts that a structural intervention does not identify endogenous stake unless objective consequences, evidence, a stake-estimate mediator, and direct effects are separated.

The revision further withdraws the proposed necessary-condition conjecture and the claim that a positive profile by itself supports functional embodied selfhood. Perfect, saturated, distributed, and nonspectral counterexamples are genuine boundary cases, not measurement failures to be defined away.

A residual disagreement remains over terminology. The manuscript retains operational interoception for sensing and controlling conditions inside an investigator-declared artificial boundary. This does not imply organismal embodiment, intrinsic normativity, bodily mineness, or phenomenal feeling. Readers who reserve interoception for biological or experiential processes should interpret FRSC as a boundary-relative regulatory-control assay.

Conclusion

The revised framework addresses a narrower question than artificial selfhood or consciousness. It asks whether an artificial system uses information about a candidate internal condition, through an identified causal route and an endogenous estimate of regulatory consequences, to improve its future regulation.

That question requires more than report accuracy, architectural access, or differentiated action. Reliability-sensitive encoding is measured separately from monitor-mediated policy use. Objective regulatory stakes are represented as a vector over deviations rather than assumed to be a scalar label. The system’s estimate of those stakes is separated from the structural condition that creates them. Most importantly, monitor use is evaluated by whether preserving the state–monitor relation improves registered outcomes or reduces regret. The adaptive and anti-regulatory binary controllers show why this sign-sensitive block is indispensable: they have identical encoding, control, and mediated-channel separation but opposite regulatory consequences.

The resulting FRSC profile is prospective, boundary-relative, and architecture-conditional. It can fail in competent systems because of perfect sensing, saturation, redundancy, distributed control, fixed optimal actions, event triggering, reconstruction, or nonspectral dynamics. Its optional transport component is meaningful only within a narrow registered model class.
