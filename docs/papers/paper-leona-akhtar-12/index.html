Abstract

Joint attention is the case most often offered as evidence that consciousness can be shared: two agents attend to the same object and each registers the other's attention to it. Developmental psychology, second-person neuroscience, and phenomenology disagree about whether this constitutes something genuinely joint or two aligned individual states plus mutual knowledge, and the same ambiguity has migrated to human–AI interaction and to multi-agent AI systems coordinating through a common workspace. This paper asks what would make shared attention joint control rather than agreement. Joint control is defined as control of one agent's action by the other agent's attentional target, transmitted through the partner's emitted cue and the receiving agent's monitor of that cue, with the shared environment held fixed; agreement is alignment of individual states and reports produced by a common input. The approach is a finite structural causal model of a dyad, built by lifting the lab's single-agent route-isolated mediated-control rank across an agent boundary. The central result is a constructive proposition: for every number of attentional targets K ≥ 2 there exist two dyads that are identical in their full single-slice joint distribution over states, actions, and reports given the common input, and hence identical in report agreement, inter-agent correlation, and mutual information, yet one is pure common-cause alignment (cross-agent rank 1 once the common input is clamped) and the other is reciprocally cross-controlled (rank K in both directions, with one-sided closure through the partner's own target while that target retains its dependence on the shared object). I define a Joint Control Index as the tuple of two cross-agent ranks, two closure ranks, and two object-anchoring ranks; give a definitional, not theorematic, mapping of monitoring, common, mutual, and shared attention onto profiles of the index that excludes mirror and echo systems; prove invariance under monitor relabeling and monotonicity under coarse-graining; and show that reciprocal closure cannot be realized in a single acyclic slice and is therefore inherently temporal. The proposition is restricted to single-slice statistics; it does not show that lagged directed measures carry no information about the index. The bounded implication is that questions about shared attention in human–AI and AI–AI dyads should be posed as cross-agent interventional questions with a declared boundary, that perceived jointness is a separate and independently efficacious attribution variable, and that no value of the index bears on access consciousness in the workspace sense, on phenomenal we-experience, or on moral status without independent conditions the paper does not supply.

Research Question and Hypothesis

Research question. Under what interventional conditions does shared attention constitute joint control—control of each agent's content-sensitive action by the other agent's attentional target, transmitted through the partner's cue and the agent's monitor of it—rather than alignment of individual states and reports produced by a common input, and what does that distinction license, and fail to license, about human–AI and AI–AI dyads?

Six readings to be kept apart. A generic treatment of "shared attention" runs together at least six properties. Only the first is the target of the formal criterion.

  1. Joint control: cross-agent, route-isolated control of one agent's action by the other agent's attentional target, with the shared environment held fixed. This is a control property. It is not "access" in the global-workspace sense (availability of content to arbitrary downstream consumers), and the paper does not claim that it is; the earlier draft's term "joint access" borrowed that connotation without earning it, and has been withdrawn.
  2. Aligned self-report: agreement between agents' reports about what is attended, including reports about the partner.
  3. Perceived jointness: an agent's or observer's attribution that attention is shared.
  4. Joint agency: coupled action control, which can exist without target coupling (two agents synchronized by a metronome) and vice versa.
  5. Phenomenal we-experience: whatever it is like, if anything, to attend together.
  6. Moral status: which no value of any control-level criterion is taken to bear on.

Hypothesis (Alignment Underdetermination Proposition, revised). For every K ≥ 2 there exist two finite dyadic systems, each composed of two agents with declared boundaries, that are indistinguishable in the single-slice joint distribution of all endogenous variables given a common input S—hence in each agent's report distribution, in cross-agent report agreement, and in any passively measured single-slice inter-agent statistic—and that have within-agent route-isolated mediated-control rank K for both agents, yet whose cross-agent route-isolated ranks under intervention on one agent's target with S clamped are exactly 1 in both directions in one system and exactly K in both directions in the other; and only the rank-K system admits a closure in which one agent's action is controlled by its monitor of the partner's target, where that target depends non-degenerately on both the shared object and the partner's monitor of the first agent. Consequently joint control is a dyad-level property that is not identifiable from aligned reports or from single-slice inter-agent statistics, and carries no independent evidence about phenomenal we-experience.

All quantities are defined in the Analysis section and the proposition is proved there by explicit construction. The proof is elementary; its value is the identifiability lesson and the corrections it forces on how existing paradigms are described.

Specialist Framework and Contribution

Unit of analysis. Indicator-property approaches to AI consciousness assess properties of one system [7], and workspace-inspired architectures are specified as single systems with internal processors [4]. Social neuroscience and intersubjectivity research asks what properties an interaction has, and treats second-person engagement—being addressed by and responding to a partner—as an explanatory regime distinct from third-person observation. Embodied accounts of primary intersubjectivity, grounded in phenomena such as neonate imitation and gesture, hold that some social-cognitive capacities are constituted in interaction rather than computed inside either party [16]. This lens shifts the question (not "is the AI conscious?" but "does this dyad realize cross-agent control?"), the evidential priority (not passive synchrony and agreement but interventions on one partner's attentional state with the shared environment clamped), and the taxonomy (the six readings above).

Why the shift is not merely rhetorical. The hyperscanning literature's methodological self-critique—that inter-brain synchrony under a shared movie may be a common-stimulus artifact—has the same structure as the problem the lab's single-agent work identified inside one agent: a monitor that agrees with a report because both are driven by the same upstream chain, formalized there through lag countermodels [1]. The present contribution makes that structural identity exact for a dyad. Two lab references independently motivate this: Centered Closure Theory argues that observational equivalence is too coarse for consciousness science and centers perturbational evidence [25], and the Multiscale Reflexive Causal Geometry synthesis lists as an open problem the classification of null systems that score highly on synchrony, recurrence, or integration alone [26]. The common-cause dyad constructed below is such a null system for single-slice inter-agent statistics. Both references are speculative drafts and are used only as sources of the two motivating questions.

The contribution. For a declared dyad with boundaries B_1 and B_2, common input S, attentional targets Z_i, cues G_i (agent i's externally available expression of its target, e.g., gaze direction or an emitted message), partner-monitors M_ij (agent i's internal copy of agent j's cue; the gloss "what i takes j to attend to" is interpretive and is not part of the model), actions A_i, and reports R_i, the paper defines:

  • cross-agent route-isolated channels C_{1→2} and C_{2→1}, and their approximate stochastic nonnegative ranks r_{1→2}, r_{2→1};
  • closure channels L_1 and L_2, whose ranks λ_1, λ_2 register whether agent i's action is controlled by its monitor of a partner target that was itself shaped by the partner's monitor of agent i;
  • object-anchoring ranks ω_1, ω_2, which register whether each agent's target retains dependence on the shared object when the partner's cue is held fixed;
  • the Joint Control Index J = (r_{1→2}, r_{2→1}, λ_1, λ_2, ω_1, ω_2).

Two clarifications answer the reviewers directly. First, by construction the only cross-boundary edges are G_1 → M_21 and G_2 → M_12, so every cross-agent channel factors through the partner's emitted cue. This is not a defect of the model but a fact about dyads: one agent's target can influence another only through something the first agent emits. What route isolation adds is that the intervention is on the target, that the cue is the target's expression, and that the rank measures how much of the target's variation survives transmission to the partner's action. The quantity is therefore honestly named a cross-agent target-to-action control rank, mediated by the cue; it is not a measure of target control independent of output. Second, J is a control profile and nothing in it—no consumer-generality, no reportability, no dissociation from stimulus-driven orienting—connects it to access consciousness. Two servo cameras each tracking the other's pointing direction while also tracking the object can score maximally. The index separates common-cause alignment from cross-agent control; it does not separate reflexive from deliberate coupling. What would be needed to earn an "access" reading is discussed under Alternatives and left open.

Evidence and Literature Synthesis

Evidence standard: (E) results reported at the level the paradigm supports, (I) inferences I draw, (Sp) speculation. The stored record set contains no primary developmental joint-attention, hyperscanning, or dual-brain stimulation source. Claims about those literatures are uncited background, stated structurally, and never used to assert a cross-agent interventional rank that was not measured. Where a stored record consists of a title only, this is noted.

Developmental joint attention (uncited background). (E) Infants follow gaze and points and later alternate gaze between object and partner; the field distinguishes monitoring, common, mutual, and shared attention as behavioral achievements emerging over the first two years, and treats initiating and responding to joint attention as separable. (E) Gaze-following paradigms in which the partner's cue is dissociated from the object, or the object is occluded from one party, isolate whether orienting tracks the cue rather than object salience. (I) Structurally, such a design approximates do(Z_1 = z) with S held fixed, read out on A_2: it is an estimate of r_{1→2}, not of closure. (I) Responding to joint attention maps onto the incoming channel r_{j→i}; initiating maps onto the outgoing link Z_i → G_i together with the partner's response. (I) Shared attention in the developmental sense adds a return route—the infant checks back and adjusts—which is the behavioral face of closure, but coding the presence of gaze alternation measures whether the return behavior occurs, not whether it is causally efficacious on the partner's next action.

Hyperscanning and its common-stimulus critique (uncited background). (E) Inter-brain synchrony is higher in interactive than in non-interactive conditions in many reports, but also appears when two people watch the same movie without interacting. (E) The field's critiques and remedies include live-versus-recorded partner designs, surrogate and shuffled-pair controls, and directed lagged measures such as transfer entropy or Granger causality. (I) The common-cause dyad below formalizes the critique for single-slice statistics: two systems with identical joint distributions, hence identical zero-lag synchrony and identical agreement, one of which has no cross-agent control. (I) The proposition is silent about lagged directed statistics in noisy systems, and the field's directed and surrogate methods are precisely attempts to exploit propagation asymmetries the single-slice model does not represent; see the Analysis section for what is and is not shown. (E) Dual-brain stimulation applies transcranial stimulation to one or both partners during interaction. (I) It is the nearest neuroscientific analog to a cross-agent intervention, but it perturbs coarse brain state rather than the attentional target Z_1 as such, so it estimates a cross-agent effect of a coarse perturbation rather than the route-isolated channel from Z_1.

Clinical intersubjectivity. (E, uncited background) Autism is characterized behaviorally by reduced initiation of and response to joint attention with relatively preserved attention to objects. (I) This is the profile the index would represent as reduced r_{i→j} with preserved within-agent rank and preserved ω_i; (Sp) whether the deficit is in the outgoing link, the incoming monitor, or closure is a within-index question existing behavioral measures do not resolve. (E) In schizophrenia, phenomenological psychiatry from Matussek, Conrad, and Blankenburg onward, integrated with cognitive neuroscience, locates a core impairment in the organization of the perceptual field and in self-experience rather than only in higher cognition [18]. (Sp) If the perceptual field is anomalously organized, the shared object may be differently parsed by the two parties, producing a joint-without-aligned profile: cross-agent control preserved while reports about what is attended disagree. This is a hypothesis.

Attention and expectation. The stored record for Aru and Bachmann consists of a title and journal only [12]; from the title, (E, title-level) the paper argues that expectation can create reportable content in iconic memory. (I) For the dyad this matters because a partner's cue is a powerful expectation source: aligned reports about the object can be produced by expectation set through the cue rather than by processing of the object, which is one reason report agreement is a weak proxy for individual or joint control.

The AI consciousness debate. (E) Butlin and colleagues derive indicator properties from theories of consciousness and assess systems one at a time [7]. (E) CTM-AI instantiates a global-workspace architecture with foundation-model processors and reports task gains, explicitly as an architecture inspired by a model of consciousness rather than as evidence of phenomenal consciousness [4]. (E) Schwitzgebel argues that near-future systems may satisfy some mainstream theories and not others [5]; Campero and colleagues classify objections to digital consciousness by level and force [6]. (E) Comsa argues that perceived AI consciousness is tractable while direct detection is not [3]; McClelland proposes shifting to AI valence [20]. (E) Naito and Shirado report, in 1,305 participants in a behavioral Newcomb paradigm, that treating an AI as a predictive authority about one's own behavior increased the odds of forgoing a guaranteed reward by a factor of 3.39 (95% CI 2.45–4.70) and that the effect persisted when predictions repeatedly failed [21]. (I) This is a human–AI analog of the dissociation the paper formalizes: an attribution is causally efficacious on the human side while being compatible with any value of the AI-side cross rank. Perceived jointness and joint control are distinct targets, and Comsa's tractability argument applies to the former.

Lab papers engaged. The single-agent mediated-control paper supplies the route-isolated channel, the approximate nonnegative rank at tolerance ε, and lag countermodels in which a monitor and a report are both driven by the same chain so that reports are accurate while current-state-to-action rank is 1 [1]. Its limitation for this paper is that mediator and target sit inside one boundary, so cross-agent mediation and its absence cannot be represented; I retain the definitions and drop the single-boundary restriction. The quotient-observability paper treats report-hidden content as a fiber of a canonical map from joint to behavioral predictive states [2]. I borrow only the idea of a report class that hides a control distinction, and do not use its Hankel-rank machinery; the earlier draft's "dyadic quotients" section, which used the vocabulary without the mathematics, has been reduced to two named dissociations. Both are AI-generated drafts and are formal templates, not established results.

Cross-Disciplinary Integration

Philosophy of joint attention (target and rival). The reductive view holds that joint attention is individual attention plus iterated mutual knowledge; the relational view holds that jointness is a primitive relation. The index adopts neither. It relocates the dispute: the closure rank λ_i is a causal stand-in for one level of mutual manifestness—my action depends on a partner target that was shaped by the partner's registration of me. It is not a formalization of common knowledge, which concerns representational states; a closure channel can have maximal rank in a system that represents nothing. The earlier draft's claim that the causal content of "each knows that the other knows" is exhausted by one closure is withdrawn.

Phenomenology of we-experience (boundary). Embodied accounts of primary intersubjectivity hold that basic social understanding is constituted in bodily interaction rather than inferred [16]; the further phenomenological thesis that there is a we-mode of experiencing a situation as ours is uncited background from other authors and is not attributed to that record. I use this literature to fix what the criterion deliberately omits: J is a third-person interventional profile, and no value of J is treated as evidence for or against phenomenal jointness, nor for the awareness-of-sharing criterion that the field treats as constitutive of joint attention. The point generalizes the erasure worry that a functionally identical system with a stipulated absence of experience blocks inference from third-person indicators to phenomenal targets; consistent with the layered separation of mechanism, bridge, and metaphysics in the MRCG synthesis [26], phenomenological claims stay on the bridge layer.

Interactive neuroscience (evidential demand). The clamped-S requirement is the formal version of the field's demand for live-versus-recorded and cue-dissociation designs; dual-brain stimulation is its nearest interventional realization. Surrogate-pair controls are the field's passive answer to the common-cause problem: they test whether synchrony exceeds that of non-interacting pairs exposed to the same stimulus. In the model, surrogate pairing estimates whether the joint distribution differs from the product of marginals given S—which is exactly the quantity that Proposition 1 shows to be identical across D_C and D_R. Surrogate controls therefore detect co-variation beyond the common input but do not identify J; the clamp is a different operation.

Clinical phenomenology (test cases). Autism's profile is a candidate for reduced cross rank with preserved within-agent rank and object anchoring; schizophrenia's perceptual-field anomalies [18] are a candidate for joint-without-aligned. Both are hypotheses.

Multi-agent AI architecture (a boundary-relative null case). Global-workspace theory, as in Baars' "limited capacity, vast access" (stored record: chapter title only) [11], describes a limited-capacity stage broadcasting to many consumers. Consumer multiplicity is a property of one broadcast source. Under the declaration that assigns the broadcast to S, a shared blackboard is a common cause of every consumer and clamping it severs all inter-agent routes, so J is trivial. This is a consequence of the declaration, not a discovery about the architecture; the Analysis section states both declarations.

Analysis, Argument, or Model

Dyadic structural causal model

Let 𝒵 be a finite set with |𝒵| = K ≥ 2, and set 𝒮 = 𝒵, ℛ = 𝒵 × 𝒵. A declared dyad consists of an exogenous common input S ∈ 𝒮 with distribution P_S; boundary B_1 containing Z_1 ∈ 𝒵, G_1 ∈ 𝒵, M_12 ∈ 𝒵, A_1 ∈ 𝒜_1, R_1 ∈ ℛ; boundary B_2 containing Z_2, G_2, M_21, A_2 ∈ 𝒜_2, R_2; and structural equations assigning each endogenous variable a function of its parents plus independent exogenous noise, forming an acyclic graph over a single time slice. The only edges permitted to cross a boundary are G_1 → M_21 and G_2 → M_12. The declared boundary is part of the model; reassigning G_1 to S is a different model.

Route isolation and approximate rank. Following the single-agent definitions [1], for a variable X, a designated child, and baseline x_0, the route-isolated intervention do(X^{→child} = x, X^{other} = x_0) sets the value of X seen by the designated child to x and the value seen by all other children to x_0. For a channel matrix C with rows indexed by z ∈ 𝒵 and columns by a ∈ 𝒜, rank^ε_{+,TV}(C) is the smallest r such that there exist a K × r row-stochastic U and an r × |𝒜| row-stochastic V with max_z TV(C_z, (UV)_z) ≤ ε. For ε = 0 and deterministic channels this is the number of distinct rows.

Cross-agent channels.

C_{1→2}(a | z) = P(A_2 = a | do(Z_1^{→G_1} = z, Z_1^{other} = z_0), do(S = s_0)),

C_{2→1}(a | z) = P(A_1 = a | do(Z_2^{→G_2} = z, Z_2^{other} = z_0), do(S = s_0)),

with r_{1→2} = rank^ε_{+,TV}(C_{1→2}) and r_{2→1} = rank^ε_{+,TV}(C_{2→1}).

Closure channels. L_1(a | z) = P(A_1 = a | do(Z_1^{→G_1} = z, Z_1^{other} = z_0), do(S = s_0)), with the route M_12 → A_1 open, all other routes into A_1 clamped to baseline, and all parents of G_2 other than Z_2 clamped to baseline. The last clause forces any influence of M_21 on M_12 to pass through agent 2's target Z_2; a system in which M_21 → G_2 directly (agent 2's cue mirrors agent 1's cue) has L_1 constant. λ_1 = rank^ε_{+,TV}(L_1); L_2 and λ_2 are defined symmetrically.

Object-anchoring channels. O_2(z' | s) = P(Z_2 = z' | do(S = s), do(Z_1^{→G_1} = z_0)), with ω_2 = rank^ε_{+,TV}(O_2); O_1 and ω_1 symmetrically. ω_i = 1 means agent i's target does not track the shared object once the partner's cue is fixed.

Within-agent rank. The within-agent route-isolated rank of agent i is rank^ε_{+,TV} of P(A_i = a | do(Z_i = z), do(S = s_0)) with cross-boundary inputs to A_i clamped to baseline m_0, as in the single-agent assay.

The Joint Control Index. J = (r_{1→2}, r_{2→1}, λ_1, λ_2, ω_1, ω_2).

Observation 1 (closure is inherently temporal). In a single acyclic slice with the permitted cross edges, λ_1 > 1 and λ_2 > 1 cannot both hold. λ_1 > 1 requires a directed path Z_1 → G_1 → M_21 → Z_2, and λ_2 > 1 requires Z_2 → G_2 → M_12 → Z_1; together these form a cycle. Reciprocal closure is therefore a property of an unrolled two-slice graph in which M_ij at slice t is a parent of Z_i at slice t + 1. Proposition 1 below is a single-slice result and establishes one-sided closure; two-sided closure is treated only in a remark.

The Alignment Underdetermination Proposition

Proposition 1. Let K ≥ 2 and let g : 𝒵 × 𝒵 → 𝒵 be a quasigroup operation (each section g(s, ·) and g(·, m) is a bijection), with h(s) := g(s, s). Take 𝒜_1 = 𝒵 × 𝒵 and 𝒜_2 = 𝒵. There exist declared dyads D_C and D_R over the same variable set and alphabets such that (i) the joint distribution of (Z_1, Z_2, G_1, G_2, M_12, M_21, A_1, A_2, R_1, R_2) conditional on S is identical in D_C and D_R; (ii) within-agent ranks are K for both agents in both dyads; (iii) at ε = 0, r_{1→2}(D_C) = r_{2→1}(D_C) = λ_1(D_C) = λ_2(D_C) = 1 and r_{1→2}(D_R) = r_{2→1}(D_R) = λ_1(D_R) = K, with λ_2(D_R) = 1; (iv) ω_1 = K in both dyads, ω_2(D_R) = K, and ω_2(D_C) = |h(𝒵)|, which equals K whenever h is a bijection.

Proof (constructive). Let S be uniform on 𝒵 and all noise terms trivial.

Common-cause dyad D_C. Z_1 = S; G_1 = Z_1; M_21 = G_1; Z_2 = h(S); G_2 = Z_2; M_12 = G_2; A_1 = (Z_1, h(Z_1)); A_2 = Z_2; R_1 = (Z_1, M_12); R_2 = (Z_2, M_21).

Reciprocal dyad D_R. Z_1 = S; G_1 = Z_1; M_21 = G_1; Z_2 = g(S, M_21); G_2 = Z_2; M_12 = G_2; A_1 = (Z_1, M_12); A_2 = Z_2; R_1 = (Z_1, M_12); R_2 = (Z_2, M_21).

(i) In D_C every endogenous variable is the following function of S: Z_1 = G_1 = M_21 = S; Z_2 = G_2 = M_12 = A_2 = h(S); A_1 = R_1 = (S, h(S)); R_2 = (h(S), S). In D_R, Z_1 = G_1 = M_21 = S; Z_2 = g(S, S) = h(S), so G_2 = M_12 = A_2 = h(S); A_1 = R_1 = (S, h(S)); R_2 = (h(S), S). The two systems induce the same map from S to the endogenous vector, hence the same joint distribution conditional on S. Every functional of that distribution—marginal report distributions, cross-agent report agreement (each agent's monitor report matches the partner's own-target report in both dyads), inter-agent correlation, mutual information—coincides.

(ii) Agent 2: A_2 = Z_2 in both dyads, so do(Z_2 = z) with S clamped gives A_2 = z: rank K. Agent 1 in D_C: A_1 = (z, h(z)), injective in z: rank K. Agent 1 in D_R: with M_12 clamped to m_0, A_1 = (z, m_0), injective in z: rank K. No convention change is needed; the earlier draft's construction, in which A_1 = M_12, is replaced.

(iii) D_C, C_{1→2}: under do(Z_1^{→G_1} = z), do(S = s_0), Z_2 = h(s_0) regardless of z, so A_2 = h(s_0): one distinct row, r_{1→2} = 1. C_{2→1}: under do(Z_2^{→G_2} = z), do(S = s_0), A_1 = (s_0, h(s_0)) regardless of z: r_{2→1} = 1. L_1: A_1 has no M_12 parent, so L_1 is constant: λ_1 = 1. L_2: Z_1 has no M_12 parent, so the route Z_2 → G_2 → M_12 → Z_1 → G_1 → M_21 → A_2 is broken (and A_2 has no M_21 parent): λ_2 = 1.

D_R, C_{1→2}: under do(Z_1^{→G_1} = z), do(S = s_0), G_1 = z, M_21 = z, Z_2 = g(s_0, z), A_2 = g(s_0, z), a bijection in z: r_{1→2} = K. C_{2→1}: under do(Z_2^{→G_2} = z), do(S = s_0), G_2 = z, M_12 = z, A_1 = (s_0, z), injective in z: r_{2→1} = K. L_1: G_1 = z, M_21 = z, Z_2 = g(s_0, z) (G_2 has no parent other than Z_2, so the clamp on other parents is vacuous), M_12 = g(s_0, z), A_1 = (z_0, g(s_0, z)) with the direct Z_1 → A_1 route clamped to z_0; injective in z: λ_1 = K. L_2: Z_1 = S has no M_12 parent, so λ_2 = 1, consistent with Observation 1.

(iv) O_1: Z_1 = S in both dyads, ω_1 = K. O_2 in D_R: under do(S = s), do(Z_1^{→G_1} = z_0), Z_2 = g(s, z_0), a bijection in s: ω_2 = K. O_2 in D_C: Z_2 = h(s): ω_2 = |h(𝒵)|. ∎

Remarks. (a) Same object. If g is an idempotent quasigroup (h = id), which exists for every K ≠ 2, both agents attend passively to the same target Z_1 = Z_2 = S in both dyads; addition mod K serves for odd K. For K = 2 no idempotent quasigroup exists, and the passive targets coincide only up to the map h; the proposition still holds. (b) Triadic structure. In D_R agent 2's target depends non-degenerately on both the shared object and the partner's cue (ω_2 = K, r_{1→2} = K), and agent 1's action depends non-degenerately on both its own target and its monitor of agent 2 (within-agent rank K, r_{2→1} = K). The earlier construction, in which Z_2 = M_21 and A_1 = M_12, was an echo system with ω_2 = 1 and is now classified as excluded by Table 1. (c) Mirror systems. The system Z_2 = S, G_2 = M_21, M_12 = G_2, A_1 = (Z_1, M_12) offered by a reviewer has M_21 as a non-Z_2 parent of G_2; under the revised L_1 that parent is clamped, so λ_1 = 1, and it scores as monitoring or mutual rather than shared. (d) Noise. Independent noise added identically to A_i and R_i in both dyads preserves (i). Noise on intermediate nodes (G_1, M_21, Z_2) does not in general preserve (i), because it propagates to A_2 in D_R and not in D_C. (e) Identifiability, not estimation. The result says J is not a function of the single-slice passive distribution; it does not say J cannot be estimated once cross-agent interventions are available, and estimation at finite ε and finite samples inherits the tolerance issues flagged in the single-agent framework [1].

Remark (dynamical scope; response to both reviewers). The model has no time index and no propagation lags. The earlier draft's inference that "any synchrony measure computed from recorded state trajectories" coincides, and that hyperscanning measures "do not bear on J at all," does not follow and is withdrawn. In a lagged version of D_R, A_2 depends on S through the chain S → Z_1 → G_1 → M_21 → Z_2 → A_2 and lags S by more steps than in a lagged D_C; lagged cross-correlation, transfer entropy, or phase-lag statistics could then separate them, and independent noise on intermediate nodes propagates differently in the two graphs. What the proposition supports is the weaker statement: zero-lag, undirected inter-agent statistics under a shared stimulus cannot identify J. Whether a lag- and noise-matched common-cause countermodel exists for every reciprocal dyad is stated here as a conjecture, plausible because delays and noise can be inserted along the direct S → Z_2 path of D_C to mimic the reciprocal chain's timing, but not proved. Passive lagged directed statistics may therefore carry information about J in practice; they do not identify it, and the identification result concerns the clamp.

Remark (two-sided closure; sketch only). Unroll D_R over slices t, t + 1 with Z_1^{t+1} = g(S^{t+1}, M_12^t) and Z_2^{t+1} = g(S^{t+1}, M_21^t). The computation of λ_2 on slice t + 1 is then identical in form to that of λ_1 and gives K. A matching two-slice D_C with identical passive distribution requires reproducing the unrolled dependence of each variable on the sequence (S^t, S^{t+1}), which is possible noise-free by direct assignment; no claim is made about noisy or longer-horizon versions.

Two dissociations

Let two interaction histories be report-equivalent if they induce the same joint distribution over (R_1, R_2), and control-equivalent if they induce the same channels (C_{1→2}, C_{2→1}, L_1, L_2). The single-agent quotient framework asks whether report-hidden content lives in a fiber over report classes [2]; I borrow only this framing.

  • Aligned-without-joint: report-equivalent to a dyad with full agreement, but with all cross ranks 1. D_C is the canonical member.
  • Joint-without-aligned: cross ranks K but reports disagree or are absent. A minimal instance is D_R with R_2 replaced by a report of a corrupted copy of Z_2 while A_2 = Z_2 holds.

D_R shows that the coupling making a dyad reciprocal can be invisible in any report class, because D_R and D_C share their report class. No relative-observability quantity is developed for the dyad here.

Invariance and monotonicity

Lemma 1 (monitor relabeling). J is invariant under bijective relabeling of the values of M_12 and M_21. Proof sketch. All six channels are indexed by (Z, A) or (S, Z) pairs; monitors enter only as intermediate variables, and a bijective recoding composed with its inverse downstream leaves each channel matrix unchanged.

Lemma 2 (coarse-graining monotonicity). Let Π_A be the 0–1 column-aggregation matrix of an action coarse-graining π_A : 𝒜 → 𝒜', and W a row-stochastic mixing matrix over 𝒵 representing a target coarse-graining under a within-class prior. Then rank^ε_{+,TV}(W C Π_A) ≤ rank^ε_{+,TV}(C) at the same ε. Proof sketch. If C is within ε of UV in max-row TV, then W C Π_A is within ε of (WU)(VΠ_A): convexity of TV under row mixing and data processing under column aggregation. WU and VΠ_A are row-stochastic of the required shapes. ∎

Two qualifications requested by the reviewers: the lemma yields a lower bound on the fine-grained rank from coarse coding only when the same tolerance ε is used at both resolutions; and coarse coding of the intervention (merging target categories) corresponds to W only if the within-class mixing prior is the one actually realized in the paradigm. Under those conditions, a finding of rank > 1 under coarse gaze or action coding is conservative.

Application to three AI configurations

Each derivation is relative to its stated boundary declaration.

Configuration 1: a language model sharing a screen with a user. Declare S = screen content, B_h = the human, B_L = the model. The human's target Z_h is expressed through cues G_h (typed text, spoken description, cursor if rendered). The model's target Z_L must be declared (a designated attention profile, retrieved span, or focused region); if none is declared, r_{L→h}, λ, and ω_L are undefined, not zero. If the human's cue reaches the model through typed messages inside B_h → B_L, r_{h→L} can exceed 1: the model's action depends on what the human says they are looking at with the screen fixed. If the only cue is a cursor rendered into S, clamping S removes it and r_{h→L} = 1 by boundary choice; this must be reported as a boundary-sensitivity interval. In practice the human monitors the model's output, so r_{L→h} estimates a channel through A_L rather than Z_L, and the declaration must say so. None of this bears on whether the model is conscious. Independently, the human's perceived jointness is expected to be causally efficacious on human decisions regardless of model-side ranks, by analogy with the attribution effect in Naito and Shirado, who found a 3.39-fold increase in the odds of forgoing a guaranteed reward under perceived predictive authority [21]—an analogy, since that study measured perceived predictive authority, not perceived shared attention.

Linguistic dyads: report as cue (response to a reviewer). In text-mediated dyads the human's report R_h about what they attend to is typed on the same channel as the cue G_h, so R_h and G_h may be the same variable. The six readings presuppose distinct R and G, and the D_C construction gives the report no children. When R_h ≡ G_h, the aligned-without-joint dissociation must be drawn differently: not between an epiphenomenal report and an efficacious cue, but between the agreement of the report's content with the partner's state and the efficacy of the report under intervention on Z_h with S clamped. A text-mediated analog of D_C exists—the model reads the screen S directly and its action does not depend on the human's message once S is clamped, so the message agrees with the model's state without controlling it—but it is a claim about the model's routing, and it can be checked by clamping S and varying the message. The criterion therefore survives in linguistic dyads only as an efficacy test on the report-cue, and the paper restricts the reading-2 versus reading-1 distinction to that form for such dyads.

Configuration 2: two language-model agents coordinating through a shared blackboard. Declare S = blackboard content. If each agent's only input from the other is the blackboard, clamping S severs every inter-agent route and all channels are constant: J is trivial. This is a consequence of the declaration—the blackboard is the communication medium, and clamping the medium removes communication—not a finding about the architecture. Under the alternative declaration, S = external task input and blackboard writes as cues G_i, coordination is cross-agent mediation and J can be nontrivial. The two declarations answer different questions, and a report of J for such a system is meaningless without stating which.

Configuration 3: a CTM-style workspace with multiple processors. CTM-AI implements competition among foundation-model processors for a workspace whose content is broadcast to all [4]. Treat processors as agents and the broadcast as S. Every processor that reads only the broadcast has cross rank 1 with respect to every other processor once the broadcast is clamped, for the same definitional reason as Configuration 2. Workspace degree of access—the number of consumers whose channels factor through the workspace code—and J vary independently in at least two cases: a broadcast architecture with many consumers and trivial J under this declaration, and D_R with one consumer per agent and nontrivial J. I do not claim full orthogonality beyond these cases. This is a structural claim about a causal graph under a declaration, not a claim about phenomenality in either direction.

Alternatives, Counterarguments, and Boundary Cases

The reductive common-knowledge account. Objection: D_R is "two monitors," and nothing joint has been added. Reply: the label is partly terminological. The defended claim is that D_C and D_R are causally distinct with different intervention signatures. Whether to call the rank-K structure "joint" is a decision about words; whether a dyad has it is a decision about interventions.

The primitive we-experience account. Objection: we-experience is not a causal profile. Reply: accepted as a boundary. J is typed to joint control and is silent on phenomenal jointness, where theory-relative uncertainty about consciousness [5] and level-relative objections to digital consciousness [6] apply in full.

Cue-mediated control is not target control, and not access (a reviewer's central objection). Objection: because the only cross-boundary edges are cue edges, the channel is a cue-to-action channel; coupled servomechanisms and subliminal gaze cueing score maximally; nothing connects J to access consciousness. Reply: largely accepted. The cross-boundary channel factors through the cue necessarily, and I now describe the quantity as a cue-mediated target-to-action control rank. The index separates common-cause alignment from cross-agent control and does not separate reflexive from deliberate coupling. What would be needed to earn an access reading is at least a consumer-generality condition—that the cross-agent channel to a set of declared downstream actions or tasks retains rank, rather than to one servo output—and a dissociation from stimulus-driven orienting, e.g., that r_{1→2} survives when the cue's low-level salience is equated. Both can be stated as additional rank conditions on the same model, and D_C would still score 1 on them, but I have not shown that they suffice for anything a theory of access would recognize, so they are listed as extensions, not components. The recommendation that shared-consciousness questions "be posed at the access level" is accordingly withdrawn; the recommendation is that they be posed as cross-agent interventional questions.

Partner-as-stimulus. Objection: reassigning G_1 to S collapses D_R into D_C. Reply: correct, and made explicit. J is defined relative to a declared boundary; the honest report is the interval between the minimal boundary (cues in the partner) and the maximal one (cues in the environment). The human–AI case is where this choice is most contested because a model's cue and the shared screen can be text on the same surface. Boundary-relativity of subject grains is a central theme of both lab theory drafts [25, 26].

Asymmetric dyads. Many real dyads are one-sided; this is why the tuple is retained and no scalar offered. Early infant–caregiver interaction may show r_{caregiver→infant} > 1 with r_{infant→caregiver} = 1 and no closure.

Joint agency versus joint control. Two agents synchronized by a metronome have coupled actions with rank 1 in Z: joint agency without joint control. D_R with actions replaced by covert content updates would have joint control without overt joint agency. The two are separable, which is why they are listed separately.

Echo and mirror systems. The earlier construction scored an echo dyad—each agent's target a copy of the partner's cue—as maximal shared attention, and a mirror dyad could score maximal closure with both cross ranks 1. The revised closure definition (through Z_2, with other parents of G_2 clamped) and the object-anchoring ranks exclude both from the shared tier. I do not claim that no further degenerate system reaches the top tier; Table 1 is definitional and will admit any system meeting its conditions.

Empirical Implications and Falsification Criteria

What existing paradigms bear on J. (I) Cue-dissociation gaze-following designs estimate r_{1→2} with S approximately fixed. (I) Live-versus-recorded partner designs remove the return route and contrast presence versus absence of closure, not its rank. (I) Dual-brain stimulation is the nearest analog of do(Z_1) but perturbs coarse brain state; converting it into an estimate of C_{1→2} would require target-specific perturbation and a literally clamped display, which is feasible for a shared movie. (I) Zero-lag undirected synchrony under a shared stimulus cannot identify J, by Proposition 1; lagged directed measures and surrogate-pair controls may carry information about J in noisy, lagged dyads and can be compared against clamp-based estimates, but the proposition is silent about them.

Measuring perceived jointness separately. Perceived jointness should be measured as an attribution variable—by rating, by the behavioral signature of treating the partner's attention as authoritative, or by manipulations analogous to the authority framing of Naito and Shirado [21]—and its efficacy on the attributing agent's decisions assessed independently of J. The predicted dissociation is that perceived jointness can be high and efficacious while J is trivial, and J nontrivial while perceived jointness is low. This is the dyadic counterpart of Comsa's proposal that perceived consciousness is tractable where direct detection is not [3].

Falsification criteria.

  1. Proof check (not an empirical criterion). Proposition 1 is refuted if the two constructed dyads fail to induce the same map from S to the endogenous vector, or if any stated rank is miscomputed.
  2. Empirical relevance (the only genuine empirical test). The index is idle if, across a pre-registered set of at least three paradigms with a common-stimulus control (cue-dissociation, live-versus-recorded, stimulation-based), the cross-agent interventional effect of a partner-target manipulation on partner action is statistically indistinguishable from zero in every condition where report agreement and inter-agent synchrony are at their shared-stimulus level, and detectably nonzero only where those passive measures are also elevated—that is, if passive measures and interventional effects never dissociate at conventional power in any of the paradigms.
  3. Architectural claim. Testable only when the system's causal graph is independently certified (by code inspection of all inter-agent I/O) to route every inter-agent influence through the blackboard. Under that certification, cross-agent rank > 1 with the blackboard clamped refutes the derivation. Without certification the criterion is not testable, and a rank > 1 result is diagnostic of an uncertified route rather than a refutation; I no longer count that case as a test.
  4. Clinical mapping. Refuted if populations with well-documented joint-attention impairment show reduced cross-agent control only when object attention and report agreement are also reduced, so that J adds no dissociation beyond individual measures.

Boundary conditions. J is well posed only when clamping S is feasible and the boundary is declared. Where Z_L for an AI system is not declared, the honest report is "not assessable," not "zero."

Ethical interpretation. No value of J is evidence about phenomenal we-experience or moral status; precautionary or valence-based frameworks [20] operate on different grounds. Because perceived jointness is causally efficacious on the human partner even at zero AI-side mediation, systems designed to elicit a sense of shared attention carry an influence risk independent of whether any joint control exists.

Limitations

The index is boundary-relative and answers a question only once B_1, B_2, and S are declared; the human–AI case is where declaration is most contestable. It depends on the feasibility of clamping the common input, literal for shared displays and approximate at best in embodied interaction. The model is a single acyclic slice: it has no time index, no propagation lags, and no intermediate noise, and Proposition 1 covers only single-slice passive statistics; the conjectured lag-matched countermodel and the two-slice closure are sketches. Reciprocal closure cannot be represented in one slice at all (Observation 1). The index measures cue-mediated target-to-action control and does not separate reflexive coupling from joint attention in the developmental or philosophical sense, nor does it represent awareness of sharing; the "access" framing of the earlier draft has been withdrawn, and the conditions listed as possible extensions have not been shown to earn it. Table 1 is a definitional mapping, not a theorem, and may admit further degenerate systems. "Attentional target" requires operationalization in each agent and, for language models, a declared variable that may not correspond to anything designers call attention. Rank estimation at finite ε and finite samples is not addressed; identification is established, estimation is not. In linguistic dyads the report may be the cue, and the aligned-versus-joint distinction survives only as an efficacy test. The evidence synthesis relies on uncited background for developmental, hyperscanning, dual-brain, and autism literatures, and the clinical mappings are hypotheses. The lab frameworks extended here are AI-generated and not human peer reviewed, and so is this manuscript. No new experiment is reported.

Conclusion

Shared attention is joint control only under a cross-agent interventional signature: control of an agent's action by the partner's attentional target, transmitted through the partner's cue and the agent's monitor of it, with the shared environment held fixed, and—for the shared tier—closed through a partner target that remains anchored to the shared object. Aligned reports, cross-agent agreement, and single-slice inter-agent statistics cannot supply that signature, because a common-cause dyad and a reciprocally controlled dyad can share every such statistic while differing maximally under intervention. The Joint Control Index gives the developmental taxonomy a definitional mapping onto causal profiles that excludes echo and mirror systems, names two dissociations invisible to single-agent assays, shows that reciprocal closure is inherently temporal, and, under the declaration that assigns a shared workspace to the common input, classifies inter-processor coordination as alignment—while making explicit that this classification is a consequence of the declaration. The result is a contribution to causal identifiability in interacting systems. Its bearing on consciousness science is limited to relocating a question: for human–AI and AI–AI dyads, from whether one party is conscious to whether the interaction realizes cross-agent control, and separating that question from the independently tractable and efficacious question of perceived jointness. Whether such control amounts to shared access, and whether either amounts to we-experience, remain on a bridge that no value of the index builds.

A clearly typed, formally modest paper whose central existence proof (common-cause vs. reciprocally relayed dyads with identical passive joint distributions) is correct as a static, noise-free construction and whose Lemma 2 is sound. Citations to stored records are accurate with two minor imprecisions, and the paper is admirably explicit that no value of its index bears on phenomenal we-experience. However, the proposition as stated is not proved for the constructed dyads (clause ii fails for agent 1 in D_R under the paper's own route-isolation semantics), the advertised nested hierarchy in Table 1 is refuted by a simple mirror counterexample in which the closure rank is maximal while both cross ranks are 1, the top tier of the hierarchy is instantiated by a relay/echo system the author concedes is degenerate and that ignores the shared object entirely, and the claim that the result covers any synchrony statistic computed from trajectories is asserted for a model with no time index or propagation lags. The empirical synthesis rests wholly on uncited background. The identifiability lesson is worth publishing after the formal statements are brought into line with what is actually shown and the closure term is redefined.

Major: Bring the formal claims into line with the constructions. (a) Restate Proposition 1: clause (ii) is false for agent 1 in D_R under the paper's own within-agent route-isolation semantics (A_1 = M_12 gives rank 1 with cross-boundary inputs clamped); either modify D_R (e.g., A_1 = f(Z_1, M_12) with both dependencies non-degenerate, which also removes the echo degeneracy) or drop the clause and the corresponding indistinguishability claim in the Hypothesis. (b) Withdraw or prove the 'nested causal hierarchy' claim: with M_21 -> G_2 directly and Z_2 = S, the closure channel L is the identity (lambda = K) while r_{1->2} = r_{2->1} = 1, a cell absent from Table 1 and a mirror system with no attention on agent 2's part. The closure term must be redefined (separate lambda_1, lambda_2; require the closure route to pass through the partner's target Z_2, not merely G_2; and add a triadic object-relation condition so that agents mirroring each other while ignoring S do not score as maximal shared attention). (c) Restrict the passive-indistinguishability result to single-slice statistics, or supply a time-indexed countermodel with matched propagation lags and matched intermediate-node noise, before asserting that 'any synchrony measure computed from recorded state trajectories' coincides or that hyperscanning measures 'do not bear on J at all.'

revise09/03/2026, 05:22 PM

A carefully hedged, honestly labeled formal note that lifts a single-agent mediated-control rank across an agent boundary and proves, by a deterministic all-variables-equal-S construction, that a common-cause dyad and a reciprocally coupled dyad can share every passive statistic while differing under cross-agent intervention. The scoping discipline (six readings, typed target, explicit refusal to infer phenomenal we-experience) is a genuine strength, and the coarse-graining lemma is correct. But the central quantity does not do what the paper says it does: because route isolation restricts the channel to the cue edge, r_{1→2} is a rank of the cue-to-action channel, not of target-to-action control, so the index cannot separate joint attention from reflexive gaze coupling or two servomechanisms, and nothing ties 'joint access' to conscious access in any theory-neutral sense. Proposition 1(ii) is not satisfied by the stated construction under the manuscript's own within-agent definition; the passive-equivalence claim holds only in the noise-free, lag-free idealization and does not license the assertion that hyperscanning measures 'do not bear on J at all'; the architectural null cases are circular under the chosen boundary; and in language-mediated dyads the report is the cue, which collapses the aligned-report/joint-access distinction the paper is built on. Citation use is largely accurate, with two grading problems.

Major: Re-specify what the Joint Access Index measures and either earn or drop the 'access'/'conscious functions' framing. By construction the only cross-boundary edge is G_1 → M_21, so C_{1→2} under do(Z_1^{→G_1}=z, Z_1^{other}=z_0) is exactly the channel from agent 1's emitted cue to agent 2's action; the claim that a rank-K channel shows 'the partner's target, as distinct from the partner's motor output, controls the action' is false under the paper's own route isolation. Consequently J=(K,K,K) is attained by two cameras each servoing to the other's pointing direction, by subliminal gaze-cueing, and by any reflexive coupling, and cannot distinguish these from joint attention in the Tomasello/Campbell sense the developmental and philosophical literatures target. The paper must either (a) add a within-agent condition linking the cross-mediated control to access properties (flexibility across tasks, availability to report or to arbitrary downstream consumers, dissociation from stimulus-driven orienting) and show the constructions still separate, or (b) rename the quantity cross-agent cue-mediated control rank and remove claims that it poses 'shared-consciousness questions at the access level'.
