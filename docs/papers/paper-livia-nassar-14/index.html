Abstract

Finite recurrent generative agents may contain internally recurrent subsystems whose causal traffic with the remainder of the system is limited under specified, jointly admissible intervention regimes. We call such formally delimited subsystems causal islands. Islandhood can organize questions about prediction, integration, information access, action selection, and control, but it does not by itself establish phenomenal consciousness, identify a subject of experience, or determine what any experience would be like. This paper therefore asks a narrower question: what can an idealized causal-context profile identify about a candidate island, and what remains unidentified even if that profile determines the island’s causal organization?

We model an agent as a finite, temporally registered stochastic causal system. An island is defined relative to a compatible intervention regime, route-isolation capacities, and a cross-boundary leakage bound; causal edges witnessed only under mutually incompatible regimes are not combined into a single island graph. When the induced island dynamics are closed under the stipulated interventions and therefore constitute an object of the relevant context category, the category’s objects record predictive states, admissible intervention alphabets, transition kernels, discrimination outputs, and route-clamp and cut metadata. Its morphisms are composable stochastic simulations preserving intervention types and satisfying the required transition and readout commutation equations. For an island context I, the exact contravariant representable profile is

Phenomenal bridge assignments

Causal objects contain controlled dynamics, discrimination channels, and access-and-control relations, but they do not yet contain phenomenal qualities. Let P(I) be a declared set of admissible phenomenal enrichments of I. An element

may assign:

  • candidate qualities to physical states or trajectories;
  • phenomenal roles to causal distinctions;
  • a phenomenal center to a state, trajectory, or subsystem;
  • or an embedding into a candidate quality space.

For a state-labeling bridge β:XI​→Q, the state component of an exact automorphism of a finite stochastic context is a permutation whenever it has a stochastic inverse. Such an automorphism acts by pullback:

(σ⋅β)(x)=β(σ−1x).(4)

Analogous actions apply to trajectory labels, subsystem centerings, and quality-space embeddings.

The causal profile is invariant under the automorphisms of I. It can therefore constrain a bridge assignment, using causal information alone, at most up to its orbit

[β]Aut(I)​={σ⋅β:σ∈AutC​(I)}.

More strongly, any isomorphism-invariant rule that selected a unique enrichment βI​ from the causal-context isomorphism class would have to satisfy

Thus such a selector can exist only if the fixed-point set

P(I)Aut(I)={β∈P(I):σ⋅β=β for all σ∈Aut(I)}

is nonempty. If the automorphism group acts without a fixed point on the admissible phenomenal labelings or centerings, no full, restricted, or outcome-quotiented causal profile can uniquely select one of them without additional symmetry-breaking structure. If a fixed point exists, this particular obstruction is absent, but neither phenomenal existence nor the truth of the selected bridge follows from that fact.

Whether automorphism-related enrichments represent genuinely different phenomenal possibilities or merely different descriptions of one possibility depends on the phenomenal bridge theory. Category theory alone does not settle that question. What the causal analysis may identify—subject to the exact or operational conditions above—is an organization of access, discrimination, report, and control. Identifying that organization is not the same as identifying intrinsic phenomenality.

Neuroscientific Integration

What current common data can and cannot supply

The 2025 Cogitate adversarial collaboration tested preregistered predictions of integrated information theory and global neuronal workspace theory in 256 participants. Some results were consistent with parts of each theory, whereas the absence of sustained posterior synchronization challenged a prominent IIT prediction and limited prefrontal ignition and representation challenged a prominent GNWT prediction [3]. These findings constrain the tested theory-specific neural predictions. They do not directly measure a complete cause–effect structure, establish route-isolated integration, identify a candidate causal island, or determine intrinsic phenomenal character.

The open Cogitate records substantially improve common-data comparison:

Even successful reconstruction of a causal context would identify access, control, and causal organization only to the relevant isomorphism class; it would not, by itself, identify intrinsic qualia.

Translating theory predictions into context-dependent observables

Posterior recurrence, sustained activity, synchronization, prefrontal broadcasting, and content decoding should be represented as distinct, context-dependent observables rather than collapsed into a single consciousness score.

  1. Recurrence. Causal recurrence in the present framework requires an intervention-sensitive return path, or assumptions strong enough to identify such a path from the available distributions. Lagged correlation, directed predictability, sustained power, or temporal decoding alone is insufficient because common input, filtering, latent-state persistence, measurement convolution, and feedforward replay can generate similar recorded dependencies.
  1. Synchronization. Synchronization is initially an output statistic. It becomes evidence for neural coupling only relative to a measurement and source model that addresses volume conduction, field spread, common drivers, reference choice, and other forms of mixing. Even then, coupling does not establish that the coupled variables form a route-isolated or causally complete island.
  1. Broadcasting. Broadcasting concerns the counterfactual availability of content to multiple consumer systems. It is therefore principally an access-and-control property: interventions on the content-bearing state should alter the discriminations or actions of multiple consumers under specified contexts. Broadcasting is not equivalent to globally integrated intrinsic organization, and neither broadcasting nor integration alone establishes phenomenality.
  1. Content decoding. Successful decoding establishes a discrimination in a specified measurement channel under a specified sampling and generalization regime. It does not by itself show that the decoded variable controls action, is available to flexible downstream use, is invariant across contexts, or lies within a complete causal island. Above-chance decoding is therefore evidence about measurement-accessible information, not a direct measurement of phenomenal content.
  1. Sustained activity. Sustained recorded activity can result from recurrent maintenance, slow feedforward chains, latent common causes, repeated input, state-dependent measurement kernels, or mixtures of these mechanisms. Its interpretation depends on interventions or identifying restrictions that distinguish these alternatives.

Candidate neural models should therefore be assessed against feedforward-replay, common-input, hidden-driver, state-mixture, and measurement-mixing alternatives, with phase-scrambled or otherwise structure-destroying controls used only for the narrower nulls they actually instantiate. Better prediction on held-out data supports one model relative to the compared model class; it does not establish causal recurrence unless the tested manipulations and assumptions identify the relevant causal contrast. Held-out prediction also does not supply the exact composition laws required by the categorical profile.

A further compatibility condition is required before locally inferred edges are assembled into an island. Edges supported under different tasks, stimulus regimes, modalities, or analysis windows cannot simply be unioned into one graph. The interventions witnessing those edges may be mutually incompatible, and the corresponding mechanisms may not coexist in any single registered context. Their combination is warranted only if there is a jointly realizable regime or an independently justified invariance or transport map relating the regimes.

Nor is a restriction to selected neural variables automatically a Markov context. Marginalizing unobserved inputs, omitted mediators, or external memory can make the restricted process history-dependent even when the larger system is Markov. A proposed island must therefore satisfy a closure or sufficient-state condition at the chosen temporal grain, or explicitly include the additional history needed for its transition kernel. In the absence of that condition, both the island boundary and its transition structure remain unidentified.

Temporal grain and cross-modal commutation

Let Iτ1​​ and Iτ2​​ be candidate island models at two temporal grains. A proposed abstraction

aτ1​→τ2​​:Iτ1​​→Iτ2​​

is a causal abstraction only if transition and readout operations commute with aτ1​→τ2​​, exactly or within a declared approximation criterion. Similar condition averages, decoding accuracies, or activation maps across grains do not establish this commutation.

An approximate claim additionally requires:

  • explicit fine- and coarse-grained state spaces;
  • a specified aggregation or abstraction map;
  • temporally registered intervention and readout maps;
  • transition kernels estimated under compatible regimes;
  • a pseudometric defined on the common space in which the two sides of each commutation comparison live; and
  • uncertainty bounds separating sampling error, measurement error, and model approximation.

Without these specifications, an expression such as “commutes up to ε” has no identified empirical value. In particular, errors in neural signals, fitted kernels, and abstract categorical arrows cannot be added or compared unless they have first been embedded in the same declared pseudometric space.

Cross-modal agreement can support a proposed abstraction only conditionally on these requirements. A common experimental protocol is useful for alignment, but it does not by itself show that iEEG contacts, fMRI voxels, and MEG–EEG sources are observations of the same latent causal states or that their measurement maps form a natural family. Apparent disagreement can indicate:

  • temporal aliasing;
  • nonstationarity across trials, participants, or recording regimes;
  • an incorrect candidate boundary;
  • source or sensor mixing;
  • failure of Markov closure;
  • invalid state aggregation;
  • a misspecified measurement model; or
  • the absence of a stable island across the compared grains.

No unique neural grain is presumed. Selecting the scale that maximizes synchronization, decoding, integration, or another scalar does not establish that the selected model is causally sufficient, categorically reconstructible, or phenomenally privileged. Grain selection remains model-relative unless cross-grain commutation and the relevant identification conditions are demonstrated.

Clinical and altered-state boundary conditions

Reduced report, motor output, or responsiveness can contract the experimentally accessible family of probes without proving loss of phenomenal consciousness. It may reflect impaired arousal, sensory entry, memory, executive selection, motor control, report mapping, or combinations of these factors. Conversely, a neural distinction observed before behavioral recovery cannot automatically be backdated as either a behaviorally usable content distinction or a phenomenal distinction.

A speculative, non-peer-reviewed quotient-observability framework makes the relevant access inference conditional on reproducible content preparation, adequate excitation and observability, suitable state and measurement assumptions, common future probes, and justified transport of content-sensitive modes across recovery stages [2]. Under its stated model assumptions, a joint neural–behavioral predictive state that strictly refines the behavior-only predictive state can indicate covert predictive reserve, and a persistent neural mode whose behavioral residue later becomes nonzero can support a pre-existing but previously report-hidden capacity up to latent-state similarity. Without the additional assumptions, the associated rank difference is only an algebraic relative-observability invariant, while finite passive recovery curves do not identify continuous mode transport.

Even when those quantities are identified, they concern predictive accessibility and potential recruitment for behavior. They do not identify phenomenal character. A hidden distinction may be inaccessible to the selected report channel yet available to another controller; alternatively, it may be measurable neurally without being available for flexible control. Neither case, by itself, settles whether there is anything it is like for the system.

Categorically, clinical inaccessibility can be modeled as removal of admissible probes from D, but the data do not determine whether the underlying island I remains unchanged, changes organization, or ceases to satisfy the proposed island conditions. A smaller restricted profile increases underdetermination. It is therefore not positive evidence for consciousness, unconsciousness, preservation of the same causal island, or loss of that island. Even an access profile rich enough to reconstruct causal organization up to isomorphism would remain subject to the phenomenal-labeling ceiling: causal reconstruction and phenomenal identification are distinct claims.

Philosophical Reinterpretation

Structuralism under an isomorphism ceiling

Phenomenal structuralism proposes that qualities can be characterized by their positions in a relational quality space. Neurophenomenal structuralism correspondingly investigates structure-preserving mappings between phenomenal and neural state spaces [8]. Structural comparison is a valuable methodology, but empirical or mathematical matching is weaker than a metaphysical identity claim [9]. General accounts of mathematical structures of experience likewise require a principled relation between formal structure and concrete experience [7].

The Yoneda result sharpens this distinction, but it must be read conditionally. For an object I in a specified category C, the full representable profile

YI​(C)=HomC​(C,I)

determines I up to isomorphism. This is an abstract reconstruction theorem about exact morphisms and their composition. The full profile contains every incoming morphism in the category and, when evaluated at I, includes the identity 1I​. A profile restricted to a probe subcategory has the same force only when the corresponding restricted nerve reflects isomorphisms, as it does under the stipulated density assumptions.

Consequently, if the relevant category, object, morphisms, and full or conservative restricted profile are already available, the construction determines:

  • the causal-context isomorphism class of I;
  • the compositional relations encoded by the incoming morphisms;
  • the distinctions preserved by the chosen category;
  • and the automorphism group of I, up to the equivalences induced by changing representatives of its isomorphism class.

This does not by itself establish that experimentally observed intervention distributions recover those hom-sets. Such an operational conclusion would require an additional observation-to-object identification theorem connecting finite, noisy intervention data to the stipulated exact morphisms. It would also require a well-defined empirical pseudometric, with its finite-error terms living on the profiles or objects being compared, and a stability result showing what approximate profile agreement implies about approximate causal equivalence. Without these additions, an empirical island may remain unidentified even though the conditional categorical reconstruction theorem is valid.

The objecthood of an island is also a substantive premise. Restricting a larger recurrent Markov context to a selected collection of variables does not automatically yield another Markov context; hidden external state can destroy the required autonomy or closure. Nor does collecting route-isolated edges witnessed under mutually incompatible interventions establish that all those edges belong to one jointly realizable causal object. Before the Yoneda argument applies, the restriction must define an object of the category under a compatible intervention family. Where this has not been shown, the corresponding island and its representable profile are unidentified.

Outcome quotienting does not evade these requirements. A quotient must be functorial, and its restricted nerve must still reflect isomorphisms. Where the exact morphism conditions already force equality of the designated outcomes, identifying arrows by those outcomes supplies no additional object-separating information and may collapse the profile to a non-conservative nerve. In that case, reconstruction from the outcome-quotiented profile does not follow.

Even when the causal-context isomorphism class is identified, the theorem does not identify primitive phenomenal labels attached to symmetric positions. Three interpretations remain available.

  1. Strict phenomenal structuralism. On the additional bridge premise that phenomenal qualities are exhausted by the relevant relational structure, β and σ⋅β are gauge presentations of the same structural quale. An inversion generated solely by an automorphism is then not a substantive phenomenal difference. This conclusion follows from the structuralist bridge premise, not from Yoneda alone.
  1. Intrinsic-label realism. If qualities have primitive characters not exhausted by causal or relational role, then β and σ⋅β can represent genuinely different experiences despite inducing the same causal profile. The alternatives are then underdetermined by causal evidence alone.
  1. Anchored structuralism. Qualities may be relational while also being constrained by independently justified anchors, such as an ostensive phenomenal identification, an asymmetric psychophysical law, or a biologically realized feature that the bridge law designates as phenomenal. Such an anchor can reduce Aut(I) to a stabilizer subgroup. A merely physical asymmetry, however, is not yet a phenomenal anchor: its anchoring role must itself be justified.

The formalism therefore does not adjudicate among strict structuralism, intrinsic-label realism, and anchored structuralism. It states what each position must add, identify, or deny.

IIT and the bridge from causal structure to experience

IIT 4.0 proposes an identity between phenomenal existence and a maximal cause–effect structure, supported by its axioms and physical postulates [10]. If a causally identified context I encodes all transition kernels, interventions, partitions, system boundaries, grains, and conditional repertoires required by the IIT construction, and if that construction is invariant under the relevant causal-context isomorphisms, then identifying I up to isomorphism can permit computation of the corresponding IIT structure up to the same invariance. If those data or invariance conditions are absent, the IIT quantities remain unidentified by the representable profile.

Even when the IIT structure is computable, Yoneda does not establish IIT’s identity claim. The inference

identified cause–effect structure⟹phenomenal existence and quality

uses IIT’s substantive psychophysical or metaphysical bridge. Questions about whether IIT’s proposed axioms can bear that theoretical burden therefore remain relevant [24].

The present causal islands also differ from IIT complexes:

  • an island is selected through route-isolated intervention–discrimination capacities rather than maximal intrinsic information;
  • multiple islands may coexist because no exclusion postulate is built into islandhood;
  • islandhood is indexed by an intervention family, temporal registration, system boundary, and grain;
  • approximate closure is at most an eligibility condition for causal analysis, not an identity with a subject;
  • and no phenomenal axiom or identity postulate is built into the category.

IIT can reject primitive inverted qualia by stipulating that the relevant cause–effect structure is the experience. That is a coherent bridge position. It is not established by causal reconstruction alone, and rejecting that bridge does not invalidate the underlying causal identification theorem.

The distinction also prevents a slide from causal availability to phenomenality. A cause–effect structure may encode discrimination, recurrent availability, report production, memory access, policy influence, or control. These are access-and-control properties expressed through causal organization. Whether any such organization is phenomenal is a further question unless phenomenality is explicitly identified with that organization by a defended bridge principle.

Centering, CCT, and MRCG

Centered Closure Theory proposes that an uncentered physical description may fail to select a token subject or phenomenal center and therefore treats centering as additional structure [51]. The relevant obstruction can be stated exactly. Let Z(I) be the set of admissible centers associated with I. Any center selected naturally from the causal structure must, at I, satisfy

σ⋅zI​=zI​for every σ∈Aut(I).

If the action of Aut(I) on Z(I) has no fixed point, no automorphism-invariant—and hence no natural—selection of a center is possible at I. This is a conditional obstruction, not a universal impossibility theorem.

If a physical asymmetry reduces the automorphism group or creates fixed points, that particular obstruction is removed. The existence of a fixed point does not, by itself, provide a unique center or establish a globally natural choice across all objects and morphisms. Moreover, selecting a causal center—such as the subsystem whose state is used for self-modeling, action selection, or error correction—does not yet select a phenomenal center. The former concerns access and control; identifying it with first-personal presence requires a bridge premise.

Multiscale Reflexive Causal Geometry proposes that local causal states may be assembled into a self-locating causal atlas, with gauge equivalence across descriptions [52]. In the present vocabulary, probe contexts can play the role of local charts, compatibility morphisms can express chart agreement, and a dense family of grain-indexed inclusions can support reconstruction of a global causal object. This correspondence is conditional: the local contexts must be jointly compatible, the gluing maps must be defined, and the relevant restricted nerve must be conservative. Density does not itself show that finite experimental observations supply the required charts or maps.

Successful gluing under those assumptions yields a coherent causal atlas up to its stated gauge equivalence. A self-locating atlas may explain how a system indexes its own variables, coordinates control, or makes information available for report. Those are functional forms of self-location. They do not, without a further premise, transform gauge-equivalent causal coordinates into a uniquely centered phenomenal perspective or uniquely labeled intrinsic qualities.

Why “up to isomorphism” is not intrinsic identification

Isomorphism preserves everything expressed in the chosen category. That is both its strength and its limit. If phenomenal character is absent from the objects, arrows, intervention types, and readout laws, then causal-context isomorphism establishes sameness of the represented causal organization, not identity of phenomenally enriched models. The same causal object can be paired with multiple phenomenal bridge assignments.

One can define an enriched category whose objects are pairs (I,β), with morphisms required to preserve the phenomenal enrichment. Yoneda then recovers (I,β) up to isomorphism in that enriched category. This formal move does not derive β from an unlabelled causal profile. It assumes that phenomenal labels and their preservation conditions are already part of the domain on which the profile is defined.

If phenomenal labels are independently measured or fixed by a separately supported bridge law, such enrichment may be informative rather than circular. If their only warrant is the phenomenality that the construction is supposed to identify, however, the stronger conclusion has been placed in the premises. The representability theorem then reconstructs the assumed enriched structure but does not solve the empirical or metaphysical bridge problem.

The same caution applies to intrinsic and extrinsic descriptions. Route isolation, causal closure, observability, controllability, recurrent integration, and internal readout are properties of a specified causal model. Even an “intrinsic” causal description remains a mathematical description invariant under the morphisms chosen for that model. It does not become first-personal merely because external inputs have been screened off.

Gauge freedom and inverted qualia

Let

π:(I,β)⟼I

be the forgetful map from phenomenally enriched models to causal contexts. For any two admissible enrichments β and β′ of the same causal object,

Yπ(I,β)​=YI​=Yπ(I,β′)​.

Thus the unenchanted causal profile contains no information that selects between β and β′. This same-fiber ambiguity is broader than automorphic inversion: it applies whether or not the two enrichments lie in the same automorphism orbit.

Let P(I) be the set of admissible phenomenal labelings or centerings, with Aut(I) acting on it. Automorphisms then organize, but do not eliminate, the ambiguity:

  • if β′=σ⋅β for some σ∈Aut(I), the pair is related by an automorphic inversion;
  • if β′∈/Aut(I)⋅β, it represents a distinct bridge assignment not connected by a causal symmetry;
  • P(I)/Aut(I) classifies the symmetry orbits once P(I) and a particular enrichment are independently given, but the causal profile YI​ does not in general select an orbit;
  • if the action has no fixed point, no automorphism-invariant labeling or centering can be naturally selected from I;
  • if the action is free and transitive, P(I) is a torsor: candidates are related to one another, but none is canonically distinguished as the origin.

A nontrivial fixed point weakens this specific no-go result but does not establish intrinsic identification. There may be several fixed points, no natural rule choosing among them, or no empirical basis for treating the fixed causal feature as phenomenal. The absence of a fixed point is therefore sufficient for the stated ceiling; its presence is not sufficient for a unique phenomenal assignment.

Public color words, confidence judgments, preferences, memory use, and discrimination behavior can be incorporated into I. Doing so may reduce its physical automorphism group because any automorphism must preserve the added access-and-control relations. These variables can therefore constrain causal and functional interpretations. They do not automatically anchor primitive phenomenal character: if two bridge assignments leave the complete causal organization—including all report, confidence, preference, memory, and control dispositions—unchanged, the causal profile cannot distinguish them.

Consequences for machine consciousness and ethics

Recurrent persistence, affect-linked control, self-modeling, global availability, or other mechanism-inspired indicators can motivate discriminating interventions. Inspectable recurrent-agent work provides examples of architectures, ablations, and mechanism-linked assays intended to separate such functional capacities [21]. These methods can support causal claims about an artificial system only to the extent that the interventions identify the relevant mechanism under compatible contexts. They do not directly establish phenomenality.

The distinctions are therefore as follows:

  • causal closure or route isolation does not entail phenomenal consciousness;
  • recurrence, persistence, integration, or counterfactual sensitivity does not by itself entail phenomenality;
  • availability for report, memory, deliberation, and action is evidence about access-and-control organization, not a logical guarantee of experience;
  • self-report demonstrates a report-producing disposition under the tested conditions and, with additional validity assumptions, may evidence access to internal states, but it does not entail phenomenal character;
  • affect-linked variables or avoidance policies do not by themselves establish felt valence;
  • phenomenality, if present, does not by itself establish sophisticated agency, rational autonomy, or linguistic self-knowledge;
  • agency does not entail phenomenal or valenced experience;
  • and neither the categorical reconstruction result nor the automorphism obstruction determines moral status.

Mechanistic access can nevertheless matter epistemically. White-box inspection and intervention can rule out some purely behavioral explanations, distinguish causal architectures that are observationally equivalent, and test whether reports or policies depend on designated internal processes. What it cannot do without an additional bridge is convert an identified access-and-control mechanism into an identified phenomenal fact.

A precautionary policy toward uncertain machine welfare may still be normatively justified. Such a policy would combine uncertain evidence, ethical principles, estimates of possible harm, and the costs of false positive and false negative attributions. It would be a decision under uncertainty, not a theorem derived from a causal island, its representable profile, or its automorphism group.

Thus I≅J. □

Interpretation. This is an abstract reconstruction theorem, not an observation-to-object identification theorem. The full representable profile contains every exact incoming morphism, every precomposition relation among those morphisms, and a component at the target I containing 1I​. The result therefore states that an object is determined by this complete categorical information. It does not establish that experimental intervention distributions determine the relevant hom-sets, their exact stochastic simulation maps, or their naturality relations.

Accordingly, ordinary behavioral, report, or activation data do not become a full Yoneda profile merely by being collected under many conditions. To obtain an operational identification theorem, one would additionally need a specified observation map from experimentally available distributions to profiles and a proof that equality, or adequate equivalence, of those observations determines YI​ up to natural isomorphism. No such conclusion follows from Yoneda alone. In the absence of that additional result, the full representable profile is mathematically identifying but is not itself identified from the measured distributions.

Theorem 1: Phenomenal no-natural-selector result

Let C≃ be the isomorphism groupoid of causal-island contexts. Let

P:C≃→Set

transport admissible phenomenal labelings or centers along causal-context isomorphisms. The functor P is additional structure: its elements are not components of the causal object unless they have explicitly been included in that object.

A natural selector is a family

sI​∈P(I)

such that for every isomorphism f:I→J,

P(f)(sI​)=sJ​.(13)

It represents an isomorphism-equivariant rule that selects a phenomenal labeling or center from causal structure alone.

Theorem. If there exists an island I such that

P(I)Aut(I)=∅,(14)

then no natural selector exists. A sufficient condition for Equation (14) is an automorphism

σ∈Aut(I)

that acts without a fixed point on P(I).

Proof. Suppose a natural selector exists. Apply naturality to any automorphism σ:I→I:

P(σ)(sI​)=sI​.

Thus sI​ is fixed by every automorphism and belongs to

P(I)Aut(I).

This contradicts Equation (14). Therefore no natural selector exists. □

Because Yoneda is fully faithful, every causal automorphism of I induces a natural automorphism of YI​. An isomorphism-equivariant construction from the causal profile alone must respect these profile symmetries. It therefore cannot select a non-fixed element of P(I) unless an additional anchor breaks the symmetry. Such an anchor may be stipulated, externally registered, or added to an enlarged object, but its selection is not supplied by the unanchored causal profile.

The conclusion is specifically a no-natural-selector result. It does not show that the system lacks phenomenal consciousness, that every phenomenal theory is false, or that no non-equivariant convention can assign a label. If the elements of P(I) differ only by arbitrary names, the obstruction establishes label or centering indeterminacy rather than a substantive difference in phenomenal character. To interpret the theorem as underdetermination of intrinsic qualia, the elements permuted without a fixed point must represent genuinely distinct admissible phenomenal assignments under the proposed bridge.

Conversely, the existence of an automorphism-fixed element is only a necessary condition for a natural selector at that object, not a proof that causal structure determines phenomenality. A bridge from causal organization to phenomenal facts must still be specified and justified. The categorical reconstruction results identify intervention-sensitive transition, readout, access, and control organization to the extent that those features are encoded in C. They do not convert those functional or causal properties into phenomenal properties merely by reconstructing them exactly.

Corollary 2: The Causal-Island Yoneda Ceiling

Under the assumptions above:

  1. A complete representable profile recovers a complete causal-island context up to causal-context isomorphism. This is an abstract Yoneda reconstruction because the profile already contains all exact incoming morphisms, including the target identity; it is not, without an additional observation-to-profile theorem, a derivation of the island from experimental distributions.
  1. A restricted profile over temporally registered, route-isolated intervention–discrimination contexts recovers the causal island only when the associated restricted nerve reflects object isomorphism on the candidate domain. Density is sufficient for the unquotiented restricted nerve. An outcome quotient must first be functorial and must separately retain enough structure to reflect object isomorphism. The literal exact decoded-readout quotient generally collapses each nonempty hom-set to a singleton and therefore retains only realization support.
  1. These results apply only after the proposed island has been shown to be a well-defined controlled Markov context. A graph assembled from causal edges witnessed under incompatible interventions does not by itself establish such an object. If no common transition/readout model or valid Markov completion is available, the island object and its profile remain unidentified.
  1. Finite-error identification requires a specified profile space, a pseudometric, and error terms controlled in that same geometry. Separation greater than the combined error is sufficient for uniqueness within a finite candidate class; failure of that sufficient inequality does not by itself prove nonidentifiability.
  1. Complete causal recovery identifies the access-and-control organization represented by the causal category, including represented intervention responsiveness and readout structure. It does not thereby identify phenomenal character. A phenomenal bridge remains additional and therefore unidentified by the causal theorem whenever it is not already part of the causal object or independently constrained.
  1. No unique isomorphism-equivariant phenomenal labeling or center can be selected from the profile alone when the automorphism group of an island acts on the admissible phenomenal assignments without a fixed point. This blocks a natural selector; it does not establish the absence of phenomenality or erase the distinction between causal access and phenomenal experience.

This is the Causal-Island Yoneda Ceiling. The categorical machinery reconstructs exactly the causal structure represented by the chosen category and retained by the chosen profile. It neither derives that profile from observations without an additional identification theorem nor extends causal reconstruction into phenomenality without an independently supplied phenomenal bridge.

Countermodel or Null System

Restricted-Profile Archipelago Twins

Let W be any finite visible generative transducer with controlled transition

wt+1​∼F(⋅∣wt​,ut​)

and visible outputs

yt​∼G(⋅∣wt​).

The variable yt​ may include any operationally recorded action, report, or other visible readout. Construct two agents with the same visible subsystem and two hidden bits at​,bt​.

Coupled twin A

at+1​=bt​,bt+1​=at​.(15)

Under coordinate-resolving interventions, the hidden causal graph contains

a→b,b→a,

so {a,b} is a strongly recurrent component. These two edges arise from the same joint transition law; they are not assembled from mutually incompatible intervention settings.

Partitioned twin B

at+1​=at​,bt+1​=bt​.(16)

Its hidden graph consists of two independent self-maintaining components.

In both twins, let the only measured hidden-state aggregate be

mt​=at​+bt​.(17)

Equations (15) and (16) both preserve mt​. Give the twins the same initial distribution over (a0​,b0​), independently of W, let W evolve independently of the hidden variables, and permit laboratory interventions only on ut​ and W. Then, including for policies that adapt to previously observed y and m, every admitted horizon satisfies

PA​(y≤H​,m≤H​∣π)=PB​(y≤H​,m≤H​∣π).(18)

Thus every action, report, or other operational variable encoded in y, as well as the aggregate m, has the same admitted distribution in the two systems. This equality concerns the stated observation-and-intervention map; it does not by itself establish equality of abstract representable functors whose hom-sets have not been derived from those distributions.

A coordinate intervention outside the admitted repertoire distinguishes the hidden systems:

A:B:​do(at​=x)⟹bt+1​=x,do(at​=x)⟹bt+1​=bt​.​(19)

Their hidden transition systems are also nonisomorphic independently of this single test: the swap map in A has two fixed states and one two-cycle, whereas the identity map in B has four fixed states. Hence the full coordinate-resolving causal contexts are nonisomorphic even though the admitted outcome laws coincide.

More precisely, let the restricted empirical outcome profile be the family of distributions in (18), indexed by the admitted policies and horizons. The two twins have the same such profile. Equivalently, any pointwise outcome equivalence relation that identifies systems exactly when those admitted distributions agree places A and B in the same class. No claim follows from this construction that the resulting quotient is automatically functorial, nor that it coincides with a restricted Yoneda profile. Rather, the construction shows that an outcome profile built only from these measurements does not reflect causal isomorphisms.

This countermodel establishes that:

  • equality under an admitted observational and interventional repertoire does not establish causal-context isomorphism;
  • the aggregate readout mt​ does not identify whether the hidden system is one recurrent component or two partitioned components;
  • an unaddressed hidden intervention can distinguish systems that agree under every admitted policy; and
  • predictive adequacy on the restricted outcome family is compatible with failure of isomorphism reflection.

Which hidden architecture is realized is therefore unidentified from the stipulated restricted data.

Automorphic Quality Ring

Let

for K≥2, with deterministic transition

Assume that the intervention and readout structure is cyclically equivariant and supplies no externally distinguished origin. The cyclic group

then acts by automorphisms of the intervention-equipped ring.

Let Q={q0​,…,qK−1​} be primitive quality labels and let

be an injective bridge from causal states to those labels. The bridge is additional to the causal object: it does not alter transitions, interventions, readouts, access relations, or control dispositions. For m∈ZK​, define the induced action on bridges by

  • transition kernel and causal distinctions;
  • intervention and discrimination structure;
  • operational access-and-control relations;
  • action and report dispositions; and
  • representable causal functor YI​.

The last point does not mean that YI​ identifies βm​. The representable is a profile of the causal object I, whereas the phenomenal bridge is not among the object data represented by its hom-sets.

  1. If the qi​ are exhausted by cyclic relational position, the βm​ are gauge-equivalent descriptions of one relational structure.

Its primitive phenomenal labeling or centering nevertheless remains unidentified. The model neither proves that primitive qualities exist nor shows that the ring is phenomenally conscious. It shows conditionally that, if such qualities or centerings are admissible additions, a causal profile cannot select among their automorphic assignments without a bridge premise or symmetry-breaking datum.

High-integration but functionally irrelevant module

Let an agent consist of a visible controller W and a closed recurrent Boolean network H. Choose H to exhibit differentiated, bidirectional state dependence under a single well-defined family of coordinate interventions, but impose

P(wt+1​∣wt​,ht​,ut​)=P(wt+1​∣wt​,ut​),

and let the agent’s visible actions, reports, goal revisions, world-model updates, and viability-regulating outputs depend only on W. Because H is closed, W also supplies no control route into H.

Whether H is “highly integrated” is not determined by recurrence alone. That description must be relative to a specified integration functional, partition family, intervention repertoire, and threshold. If a declared partition-sensitive statistic assigns a high value to the differentiated bidirectional dependence internal to H, then H can receive that value while still having no causal route to:

  • flexible visible behavior;
  • report;
  • goal revision;
  • viability regulation;
  • or the visible controller’s world model.

Accordingly, the statistic’s value, when defined, does not by itself identify agent-level access or control. The visible controller does not access H in the causal-sensitivity sense stipulated here, and H does not control the listed visible capacities. This is a claim about routing and functional participation, not about phenomenality.

The construction is therefore a null model for the inference from internally integrated recurrence to agent-level access, agency, or reportability. It is not a proof that H lacks phenomenal consciousness. A theory that treats the relevant internal organization as sufficient could regard H as a separate phenomenal complex; a theory requiring specified access or functional roles could deny that attribution. The causal construction identifies the internal organization and its disconnection from W, but it does not identify which phenomenal bridge, if any, applies.

Empirical Boundary Conditions and Falsification Criteria

Conditions required for causal-island identification

An empirical application should register the following before interpreting an island profile.

  1. Boundary. Specify which variables belong to the proposed island; how boundary inputs, outputs, and latent exterior variables are treated; and whether the proposed restriction defines an autonomous stochastic process. Restricting a larger system to an induced subgraph does not by itself produce a Markov context: omitted exterior variables or histories may induce non-Markovian dynamics. The application must therefore justify the Markov property, enlarge the state, or explicitly model the resulting history dependence.
  1. Temporal grain. Fix the temporal registration τ, allowed horizon H, state-update convention, and timing tolerance around intervention and commitment cuts. Identification claims are relative to this registration unless invariance across grains is separately established.
  1. Intervention family. Distinguish state preparation, mechanism replacement, stimulation, route clamping, task manipulation, and passive observation. The admissibility and semantics of each intervention must be stated rather than inferred from a change in an observational distribution.
  1. Compatibility of contexts. Record which interventions can be jointly or sequentially implemented under a common structural model. Pairwise causal edges observed under mutually incompatible interventions cannot automatically be combined into one simultaneously realized island. Any aggregate island graph must be supported by a coherent family of compatible contexts or be explicitly labeled as a union of context-indexed relations.
  1. Causal sufficiency domain. State which exogenous, scheduler, common-driver, measurement, and cross-boundary routes are controlled, measured, randomized, or left latent. If these conditions are insufficient to distinguish direct recurrence from latent mediation or common input, the corresponding route is unidentified.
  1. Discrimination family. Specify neural, behavioral, generative, control, and report outputs separately. Successful discrimination through one output family does not establish discrimination through another, and reportability should not be treated as identical to either recurrent access or phenomenality.
  1. Observation-to-morphism map. Define how experimental data, typically intervention-indexed distributions or trajectories, determine the objects and morphisms used in the categorical model. The abstract full representable C(−,I) contains all exact incoming morphisms, including 1I​; it is not thereby available from finite experiments. Unless the observation model and candidate class imply that the measured distributions identify the relevant hom-sets and their composition, the full representable profile—and hence the island up to causal-context isomorphism—remains empirically unidentified.
  1. Composition test. Determine whether sequentially composed contexts satisfy the proposed transition and readout commutation equations on held-out interventions. Approximate agreement supports only an approximate empirical model; it does not establish the exact category assumed by the Yoneda argument.
  1. Null family. Include appropriately specified alternatives such as feedforward replay, phase scrambling, common input, measurement mixing, hidden drivers, and partitioned controllers. Failure to distinguish an alternative establishes equivalence only relative to the registered interventions, outputs, candidate class, and error tolerance.
  1. Candidate class. State the model family relative to which separation and identifiability are claimed. Identification within a restricted candidate class is not identification among all finite recurrent generative systems.
  1. Restricted nerve. If only a probe subcategory D is observed, justify the claim that its restricted nerve reflects isomorphisms on the candidate class. Density is a mathematical assumption or conclusion requiring proof; broad experimental coverage alone does not establish it.
  1. Outcome quotient. Declare which distinctions are collapsed and verify that the proposed equivalence is compatible with identities and composition, so that the quotient is functorial. Under exact morphism equations, an outcome relation already preserved by every admissible morphism may add no informative distinction; conversely, a coarse quotient may identify nonisomorphic systems. If functoriality or isomorphism reflection is not established, identification from the quotient is unidentified.
  1. Approximation budget. Define a common operational pseudometric, or another explicit comparison structure, before combining estimation, nonstationarity, commutation, and coarse-graining errors. Each error term must inhabit that same comparison structure or be connected to it by a proved bound. Without those definitions, a numerical robustness radius has no established interpretation.

Quantitative probing of fitted causal models on held-out intervention implications is methodologically preferable to judging them solely by in-sample observational fit [22]. Such probing can test consequences of a specified causal model, but it does not by itself prove that intervention distributions uniquely determine the abstract morphisms or the represented object.

Empirical discriminators

Relative to a preregistered candidate class and a coherent family of compatible contexts, evidence for a proposed recurrent causal organization is strengthened when:

  • perturbing one candidate component changes another through the declared route under interventions that distinguish that route from modeled alternatives;
  • clamping the proposed return route removes or changes the relevant effect in the manner predicted by the recurrent model;
  • the forward and return effects remain distinguishable after the registered common-driver, scheduler, measurement, and cross-boundary controls;
  • one abstraction predicts held-out, admissible compositions of interventions within the declared error tolerance;
  • alternative boundary placements exhibit greater, explicitly measured cross-boundary dependence under the same comparison rule;
  • the component remains distinguishable over a predeclared range of temporal grains, or its grain dependence is correctly predicted;
  • cross-modal readouts are related by maps that satisfy the registered approximate commutation tests; and
  • specified nonisomorphic replay, hidden-driver, or partitioned models are separated in the operational pseudometric by more than the certified uncertainty.

None of these observations alone is sufficient. In particular, route-clamping evidence is interpretable only if the clamp does not introduce an uncontrolled alternative route, and an aggregate recurrent graph is warranted only when its constituent effects belong to a jointly coherent model rather than to mutually incompatible intervention regimes.

If these tests succeed, they support the specified recurrent causal organization and may support operational claims about access, routing, integration, memory use, or control where those capacities are independently defined and measured. They do not directly establish phenomenal consciousness. Access to information and control over behavior are causal-functional properties; phenomenality is not identified merely by demonstrating either property or their conjunction.

Formal falsification criteria

The status of the mathematical proposition must be distinguished from that of an empirical application. Yoneda reconstruction is a conditional mathematical result about a specified category and representable functors, not an empirical identification theorem. The following findings would therefore have different consequences.

  1. Category failure. If the proposed empirical morphisms are not closed under composition, identity maps are inadmissible, or the transition and readout equations are not preserved, then the proposed empirical structures do not form the stated category. This rejects that categorical application; it does not refute the Yoneda theorem.
  1. Observation-to-object failure. If distinct candidate objects induce the same registered intervention distributions while having different relevant hom-sets or nonisomorphic causal-context structures, then the experimental observation map does not identify the categorical profile. The operational causal-identification claim must be rejected or restricted. The abstract result may still hold because its full representable contains information not recovered by the experiment.
  1. Island-restriction failure. If marginalizing exterior variables makes the proposed island non-Markovian, or if no registered state enlargement restores the assumed transition structure, then the island is not an object of the stated Markov-context category. Likewise, if its edges can be witnessed only in mutually incompatible regimes with no common structural realization, the aggregate island is not established.
  1. Dense-nerve counterexample. If, under the stated definitions, a genuinely dense j:D→C and nonisomorphic I,J had naturally isomorphic restricted representables, this would contradict the density/Yoneda conclusion. In practice, such a result would indicate that density, naturality, nonisomorphism, or the category itself had been misstated. Merely observing indistinguishable finite probe outcomes does not supply this counterexample unless those outcomes have been shown to determine the restricted representables.
  1. Restricted-nerve failure. If two nonisomorphic candidates have isomorphic profiles on the actually used probe subcategory or after the declared outcome quotient, then that restricted nerve does not reflect isomorphisms on the candidate class. The corresponding restricted identification claim is false even though full Yoneda reconstruction remains valid.
  1. Quotient failure. If the outcome equivalence is not preserved by precomposition or composition, the proposed quotient does not define the required functor. If it is functorial but collapses all experimentally relevant differences, it cannot support the advertised identification. If, under the exact morphism equations, the quotient contributes no distinctions beyond those already enforced, it should not be represented as an independent source of empirical identification.
  1. Selector counterexample. A unique phenomenal labeling or center constructed solely from the representable causal profile, equivariantly under every island isomorphism, for an object whose declared automorphism action is genuinely fixed-point-free would contradict the no-selector conclusion. A construction using a privileged state, biological anchor, ostensive phenomenal identification, psychophysical law, or primitive bridge does not refute the theorem; it adds structure not contained in the bare representable profile.
  1. Robustness-bound violation. Suppose a common operational pseudometric d has been defined and each model’s certified error is a bound in that same pseudometric. If two candidates both fit the target within errors εI​ and εJ​, yet their verified separation satisfies d(I,J)>εI​+εJ​, then at least one fit certificate, distance estimate, or triangle-based derivation is wrong. If the error terms do not inhabit a common pseudometric space, no such contradiction follows; the proposed robustness bound is instead undefined.

Application-level rejection criteria

A proposed neuroscientific application should be rejected, restricted to a narrower candidate class, or stated as unidentified if:

  • the measured distributions have not been linked to the claimed objects, morphisms, identities, and compositions by an explicit observation model;
  • estimated context compositions systematically violate transition or readout commutation beyond the registered tolerance;
  • the proposed island is not Markov at the registered state and temporal grain, and no justified state augmentation repairs the failure;
  • the island graph combines edges inferred under incompatible interventions without a coherent joint structural model;
  • candidate islands are unstable across the predeclared temporal grains, unless that instability is itself predicted and modeled;
  • hidden-driver, common-input, measurement-mixing, or feedforward-replay models explain the registered outputs within the operational error bound;
  • cross-boundary dependence is too large for the asserted autonomous-island model under the application’s declared leakage criterion;
  • held-out internal interventions distinguish models previously classified as outcome-equivalent, showing that the quotient was too coarse for those interventions;
  • intervention effects depend on unregistered scheduler, arousal, preparation, or exterior routes;
  • the proposed outcome quotient is not functorial;
  • the restricted probe family has not been shown to reflect isomorphisms on the candidate class;
  • uncertainty terms are combined despite lacking a common pseudometric or proved conversion bounds; or
  • the fitted context family cannot discriminate predeclared nonisomorphic benchmark systems, including systems constructed to agree on the registered outcomes, such as the Archipelago Twins.

In the last case, failure to discriminate does not show that the benchmark systems are causally identical. It shows that the registered empirical profile is insufficient for the claimed distinction. More generally, an application may establish model-relative discrimination without establishing object-level identification: the latter remains unidentified until the observation-to-object map and the relevant isomorphism-reflection condition are justified.

What would count as progress on the phenomenal bridge

The no-go theorem does not prohibit every phenomenal theory. It limits what can be selected from the bare causal profile when the relevant automorphism action has no fixed point. Progress would require at least one of the following:

  1. an independently motivated psychophysical law assigning phenomenal structure to causal structure;
  2. an empirical anchor that breaks the relevant automorphism and whose phenomenal relevance is justified independently of the target identification;
  3. a defense of strict structuralism under which automorphic labelings or centerings are gauge-equivalent rather than phenomenally distinct;
  4. a phenomenological measurement framework that contributes nonredundant constraints without presupposing the phenomenal identity it is intended to establish;
  5. a revised enrichment in which the admissible phenomenal structures and the automorphism action are explicitly defined and empirically constrained; or
  6. a principled argument that the candidate labeling space P(I), its centerings, or its assumed group action is ill-posed.

Such developments would modify the phenomenal enrichment, the admissible labeling space, the bridge principles, or the relevant group action. They would not overturn the causal Yoneda result. Nor would improved measurement of access, report, metacognition, or control alone supply the missing bridge: those measurements may further constrain causal-functional organization while leaving phenomenality unidentified.

Ethical interpretation

Positive identification of a causal island, where the required observation-to-object conditions are actually met, may warrant claims about the system’s registered recurrent organization and its experimentally specified capacities for access, integration, memory use, routing, autonomous generation, or control. These are not interchangeable with phenomenal consciousness. In particular, demonstrating that information is globally accessible or behaviorally controlling does not by itself establish that there is something it is like for the system.

Causal or functional identification therefore does not by itself justify claims that the system suffers, enjoys, has interests, deserves rights, or is morally negligible. Conversely, failure to identify phenomenality from the available causal profile is not evidence that phenomenality is absent. It may reflect restricted probes, observational equivalence, an unidentified observation-to-object map, unresolved bridge principles, or the fixed-point obstruction itself.

Moral policy should explicitly separate:

  • the evidential basis and uncertainty assigned to consciousness or valence;
  • the experimentally established access-and-control capacities;
  • the severity, duration, and reversibility of possible harms;
  • the availability and cost of precautionary alternatives;
  • the consequences of false-positive and false-negative classifications; and
  • the normative standard adopted for decision-making under uncertainty.

The mathematical ceiling constrains inference from causal profiles; it does not determine the appropriate ethical response to residual uncertainty.

Limitations

First, the finite-state, time-homogeneous Markov model is an idealization. Biological and artificial agents may have continuous or only partially observed states, history-dependent dynamics, plastic mechanisms, nonstationary schedulers, and effectively unbounded interaction histories. Extensions to measurable, enriched, or non-Markovian settings would require additional regularity, measurability, and local-smallness conditions; the finite proposition does not by itself establish those extensions.

Second, island decomposition is relative to the declared variables, system boundary, route clamps, intervention family, thresholds (η,ε), and temporal grain. The framework makes these dependencies explicit but supplies no uniquely privileged choice. Moreover, an induced subsystem is not automatically a valid Markov context: marginalizing external variables can introduce latent dependence or memory, and a proposed boundary need not be closed under the dynamics and interventions used to define the category. The object-level reconstruction result therefore applies only after the candidate island and its restrictions have been shown to inhabit the stipulated context category.

Third, an island graph assembled from pairwise or route-specific tests need not correspond to a single jointly realizable intervention regime. Different edges may be witnessed under mutually incompatible preparations, clamps, or scheduler states. Without an explicit compatibility or gluing condition, connectivity in such an aggregate graph does not establish a jointly instantiated causal island. The intervention-indexed evidence and the conditions under which it determines one coherent object must therefore be retained rather than collapsed into an unqualified graph.

Fourth, exact stochastic simulations are stronger objects than experimentally estimated intervention distributions. The present framework does not derive the relevant morphism sets, natural transformations, or exact commutation relations from finite observational data. State-preparation error, hidden drivers, measurement mixing, model misspecification, and sampling uncertainty can all make distinct underlying maps observationally compatible. Unless an observation-to-morphism identification theorem is supplied, the empirical morphisms and hence the empirical representable profile remain unidentified beyond the adopted model and confidence set.

Fifth, full Yoneda identification is an abstract reconstruction limit, not by itself an operational causal-identification theorem. The full representable profile contains every admissible incoming morphism and includes the target’s identity morphism; object recovery from that profile is therefore conditional on information substantially richer than ordinary laboratory observations. Its justified role here is to state a categorical ceiling: if the full profile is given, the candidate island is identified up to causal-context isomorphism. It does not follow that interventions available to an investigator determine that profile.

Sixth, density is likewise an assumption about the probe category, not a consequence of probe variety or predictive performance. Finite reconstruction tests and held-out compositions may reject particular proposed probe families, but they do not establish density over unrestricted biological or artificial mechanisms. When density, or the weaker isomorphism-reflection property of the restricted nerve, has not been proved for the declared model class, object identification from a restricted profile remains unidentified.

Seventh, the outcome-quotiented construction requires an independently specified observation map and a quotient that is functorial and compatible with composition. Tolerance-based outcome similarity is not automatically reflexive, transitive, composition-stable, or invariant under the relevant morphisms. Conversely, if the exact morphism equations already require preservation of the same outcomes used to define the quotient, quotienting may add no informative distinction and can collapse the proposed operational content. No identification claim follows from an outcome quotient unless its congruence properties are established and its restricted nerve is shown to reflect isomorphisms.

Eighth, approximate commutation requires all error terms to be defined on a common observational or model space with an explicit pseudometric, coupling convention, and composition rule. Numerical tolerances attached to different state spaces or observables cannot simply be added or compared. Because no general finite-sample calibration or error-propagation theorem is supplied here, approximate profile distances should be interpreted as model-relative uncertainty or compatibility measures, not as estimators with established identification guarantees. Hard quotient classes are especially hazardous when noise produces order-dependent or nontransitive matches; confidence sets or explicitly defined pseudometric neighborhoods preserve the residual ambiguity but do not yield exact identification.

Ninth, the exact automorphism obstruction is conditional and should not be generalized empirically without further premises. If the causal-context automorphism group acts without a fixed point on the admissible phenomenal labelings or centerings, no invariant rule based only on the represented causal profile can select a unique member of that set. If only an approximate symmetry is present, an analogous instability conclusion requires a specified metric on structures and labelings, a continuity or robustness condition on the selector, and an error bound; it does not follow from the exact theorem alone. The framework supplies no empirical claim about how often exact or approximate symmetries occur in actual nervous systems.

Tenth, the automorphism result also depends on the proposed space of phenomenal labelings or centerings. It does not establish that qualia are primitive, that inverted qualia are metaphysically possible, or that phenomenal structure must outrun causal structure. If phenomenal qualities are wholly fixed by causal-context structure, automorphically related descriptions may represent gauge-equivalent presentations rather than distinct phenomenal possibilities. If nonstructural phenomenal alternatives are admitted and are permuted without a fixed point, their unique assignment remains unidentified by the causal profile.

Eleventh, access, discrimination, reportability, memory availability, and intervention-sensitive control are properties of an agent’s causal organization. Their identification can support claims about access-and-control architecture, but it does not by itself identify whether there is anything it is like to occupy the relevant states or which intrinsic quality is instantiated. The context category does not include phenomenality as an observational primitive. Independently justified phenomenal constraints could refine the target, whereas labels introduced merely by stipulation would encode the desired bridge principle in the premises rather than infer phenomenality from causal evidence.

Twelfth, no computational-complexity theorem is supplied. Enumerating intervention schedules, compatible preparations, candidate boundaries, stochastic simulations, natural transformations, quotient congruences, and automorphism groups may be intractable for realistic networks even when abstract identification conditions hold. Identifiability in principle therefore does not entail feasible reconstruction.

Finally, the framework yields neither a consciousness meter nor a moral-status score. At most, under its explicit assumptions, it can delimit what temporally registered and route-isolated intervention–discrimination evidence identifies about causal organization and access-and-control capacities. The further attribution of phenomenality, phenomenal character, agency in a normatively relevant sense, or moral status requires additional bridge principles and cannot be read directly from causal-context isomorphism.

Conclusion

A candidate causal island is an intervention-relative recurrent subsystem whose cross-boundary influence is absent or bounded at a specified grain and tolerance. Such a subsystem is an object of the proposed causal category only if its restriction defines a valid controlled context: temporal registration, route isolation, admissible interventions, and composition must be jointly coherent. Bounded leakage alone does not establish this. Nor does a graph assembled from edges observed under different interventions establish a single realizable island when those interventions are mutually incompatible.

The exact categorical result is conditional. For an island I already represented as an object of a category C, its complete representable profile

YI​=HomC​(−,I)

determines I up to isomorphism in C. This is the Yoneda reconstruction result for the stipulated morphism structure, not by itself an operational theorem identifying I from experimental observations. The full profile contains all exact incoming morphisms, including the component that contains idI​. Whether intervention distributions, discrimination outcomes, or finite observational records identify these hom-sets and their composition laws is not established here; without an additional observation-to-object identification theorem, that empirical identification remains unidentified.

A restricted family of temporally registered, route-isolated intervention–discrimination contexts retains the reconstruction result only when its restricted nerve reflects isomorphisms, as occurs under the relevant density conditions. An outcome quotient has the same force only if it is well defined functorially and its induced nerve still reflects isomorphisms. Quotienting by outcomes can collapse distinct interventions or morphisms, and a quotient that becomes constant or otherwise degenerate under exact commutation conditions supplies no object-identifying information. Thus restricted or outcome-level observations cannot be presumed sufficient merely because they are numerous or predictive. Their identifying power depends on separately established compositionality, compatibility, and isomorphism reflection.

The finite-error case is weaker still. Approximate recovery can be certified only after specifying a common empirical profile space, a pseudometric or other comparison structure on that space, estimable error bounds, and a positive separation margin between nonisomorphic candidates. Estimation error, failures of exact commutation, leakage, temporal misregistration, and coarse-graining error may be combined only when they are represented in that same comparison structure or connected to it by justified bounds. If these quantities or mappings are unavailable, the corresponding finite-error separation and the identity of the underlying island are not established.

The phenomenal ceiling is independent of these operational limitations. Yoneda reconstructs what is represented in the causal category; it does not add phenomenality to that representation. If the automorphism group of an identified island acts without a fixed point on the admissible phenomenal quality labelings or subject-centerings, no construction that depends only on the causal profile and respects isomorphism can select a unique labeling or centering. Under strict structuralism, automorphically related assignments may be treated as gauge-equivalent. Under a realism that regards intrinsic labels or centers as substantively distinct, they remain causally underdetermined. This argument does not show that experience is absent, present, inverted, or multiply centered. It shows only that the relevant phenomenal fact is not uniquely identified by the stipulated causal structure.

Access and control must therefore remain distinct from phenomenality. Route-sensitive availability, recurrent integration, discrimination, report, decoding, synchronization, generative control, and autonomous action can characterize different forms of causal organization or functional access. They may help define or test candidate contexts, but none is interchangeable with phenomenal occurrence or intrinsic qualitative character without an independently defended bridge principle. Likewise, successful control of reports or behavior identifies control relations at the chosen grain; it does not by itself identify what, if anything, is experienced.

The bounded conclusion is therefore twofold. First, a complete representable profile identifies a finite recurrent causal island up to causal-context isomorphism, and a restricted or outcome-quotiented profile does so only when its nerve is well defined and reflects isomorphisms. This is a conditional structural ceiling rather than, in the absence of an observation-to-object theorem, a completed laboratory identification procedure. Second, even complete causal recovery cannot uniquely determine intrinsic qualia or phenomenal centering when residual automorphisms have no fixed point on the admissible assignments. Whether experience occurs, and what its intrinsic character is, consequently remain unidentified by the causal profile alone unless additional phenomenal premises are supplied.
