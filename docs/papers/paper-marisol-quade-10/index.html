Abstract

When overt report or content-appropriate action returns after a nonresponsive stage, later success does not determine whether a relevant internal component persisted, was recreated, or was newly constructed. This is a retrodictive problem distinct from diagnosis and prognosis. The paper develops Recovery-Linked Causal Recruitment (RLCR) as a conservative audit with two targets. Signature-linked recruitment means that an early, content-reachable component was neurally or internally visible but equivalent to practical absence in prespecified behavior; a later component matched its interventionally measured causal signature within registered drift bounds; and the later component made a nontrivial, precommitment route-specific contribution to action. This is type-level continuity relative to a declared signature and does not identify token or material persistence. Lineage-linked recruitment additionally requires a joint longitudinal model representing persistence, birth, death, copying, splitting, merging, and replacement, together with interstage provenance evidence that excludes an interventionally indistinguishable de novo replacement.

The linear early-stage quantity is first interpreted conservatively as an algebraic relative-observability invariant. It equals the dimension of a joint-over-behavioral predictive quotient only under explicit reachability, horizon-saturation, state-preparation, attainable-set, common-probe, and state-independent translation-noise assumptions. Reserve eligibility is assigned to an isolated invariant component, not inferred from a global rank difference. At the recovered stage, route interventions are defined in an edge-split structural causal model. A distance from the rank-1 channel set supplies effect-sized contribution and equivalence criteria. Bypass equivalence, stale-state equivalence, and agreement between separately randomized and same-run channels are reported as stronger diagnostics of necessity, temporal exclusivity, and modularity; they are not necessary for route-specific contribution.

The principal impossibility result is that endpoint intervention distributions and cross-stage signature matching cannot distinguish persistence from perfect replacement in any model class allowing unobserved birth with matching late dynamics. Accordingly, the framework uses Boolean lower and upper certificates over a simultaneously covered set of joint models, boundaries, commitment cuts, component decompositions, and lineage graphs. A positive signature lower certificate supports cross-stage causal-signature equivalence followed by later recruitment. A positive lineage lower certificate supports the stronger continuity claim only under the registered provenance model. Neither result establishes phenomenal consciousness, global workspace access, broad agency, report veracity, welfare, or moral status.

Research Question and Hypothesis

Research question

When a patient or artificial agent moves from absent overt content-appropriate behavior to recovered action or report, what can longitudinal evidence establish about the earlier mechanism?

Four questions must be separated:

  1. Did a randomized contrast already affect an internal predictive component at the early stage?
  2. Was that effect practically absent from the complete prespecified behavioral output set?
  3. Did a later component with the same registered causal signature contribute to recovered action before commitment?
  4. Was the later component genealogically descended from the early component, rather than an interventionally indistinguishable replacement?

The fourth question is stronger than the first three. Organism continuity, checkpoint naming, endpoint waveform similarity, or endpoint transfer equivalence does not by itself establish submechanism lineage.

Throughout, c denotes a prespecified exogenous contrast. It may be called a content contrast only when its sensory or semantic interpretation is adequately anchored across stages. If stable interpretation cannot be established, the warranted term is stimulus-contrast-sensitive processing. Reusing the same command or image does not alone establish that the endogenous content is the same at both stages.

Independent causal targets

RLCR distinguishes two model-level targets.

Signature-linked recruitment holds when a joint model contains:

  • an early intervention-reachable component with a registered internal response above a minimum effect size and selected behavioral response within an equivalence margin;
  • a later component whose common-probe and transfer signature matches the early component within independently registered drift bounds; and
  • a nontrivial controlled route-specific effect from current contrast to action through the later component before the registered commitment cut.

This target concerns intervention-relative component type. It remains true in a model where the early component is destroyed and replaced by a causally indistinguishable component.

Lineage-linked recruitment holds when a joint longitudinal model instead contains:

  • the same early reserve-bearing component;
  • an admissible genealogical path from that component to the later mediator, under a registered definition of strict or ancestral continuity; and
  • the same late precommitment route-specific contribution.

Lineage is defined independently of endpoint similarity. A replacement event remains a replacement even if the new component has identical dynamics, transfer residues, probe responses, and action effects.

The late target is controlled route contribution, not necessity or exclusivity. It asks whether varying current contrast along the candidate route can change the action distribution while alternative contrast routes are held at a registered baseline. It does not require the absence of parallel routes, dependence on earlier content, or mediator-context interactions.

Case-level retained-recruitment hypothesis

For a particular registered recovery episode, the substantive hypothesis is:

Hlin​:the true joint longitudinal model contains lineage-linked recruitment.

A weaker hypothesis is:

Hsig​:the true model contains signature-linked recruitment.

These hypotheses are not defined by whether an estimator returns a positive result. They are properties of a joint causal model. The proposed lower certificates are conservative sufficient evidence for them when the model class, intervention semantics, and simultaneous coverage assumptions hold. No converse is claimed: genuine recruitment may remain uncertified because measurement, intervention, timing, semantics, or provenance is inadequate.

The manuscript does not assert that Hlin​ or Hsig​ holds universally in disorders of consciousness, recovered AI systems, or any specified population. It provides a case-level audit and a prospectively testable protocol.

Neither hypothesis contains the bridge principle

Hsig​ or Hlin​⟹phenomenal consciousness.

An early reserve-bearing component and a later causal route could implement unconscious discrimination, priming, sensorimotor preparation, salience processing, or another nonphenomenal function.

Revision of the original formal ambition

The framework draws critically on two AI-generated, non-peer-reviewed laboratory manuscripts. Recovery-Linked Quotient Observability proposed an early joint-versus-behavioral rank difference and longitudinal component matching, while expressly noting that its strongest predictive-quotient interpretation required assumptions beyond finite Hankel rank [2]. The present manuscript retains the useful algebraic distinction but restates the required assumptions and makes reserve eligibility component-specific.

A second speculative manuscript distinguished separately randomized mediator composition from a same-run route-isolated channel and supplied lagged countermodels in which passive accuracy and post-action reporting coexist with rank-1 synchronized control [1]. The present paper retains the registered-cut and same-run intervention ideas. It does not treat low disagreement between the two assays as necessary for genuine mediation, because the source itself characterizes that agreement as a diagnostic rather than proof of mediator completeness.

The strongest earlier claim—that cross-stage causal-signature matching identifies persistence—is withdrawn. Endpoint matching can identify only type-level signature equivalence. A separate lineage tier is introduced, and its lower certificate must remain zero whenever a de novo but interventionally indistinguishable replacement model is compatible with the data.

Why the output is Boolean rather than a branch count

Exact counts of hidden branches are unstable under observationally equivalent state splitting, merging, duplication, nonminimal augmentation, and decomposition of repeated or nonnormal spectral blocks. Similarity invariance does not resolve those ambiguities. RLCR therefore reports only Boolean existence:

  • present in every admissible model;
  • present in at least one but not every admissible model; or
  • absent from every admissible model.

Linear components are restricted to registered isolated invariant clusters in minimal realizations. Repeated or defective eigenvalues inside a cluster are not counted separately. Finite-state components are defined through minimal interventionally distinguishable predictive equivalence classes rather than arbitrary hidden-state labels.

Evidence ladder

The resulting evidence ladder is:

  1. Outcome association: an early variable predicts later recovery.
  2. Stage-local contrast sensitivity: randomized c changes an early internal measurement.
  3. Component-specific covert reserve: a prespecified invariant component has an internal effect above a registered margin while its selected behavioral effect is within an equivalence margin.
  4. Signature-linked recruitment: a later matched component has a precommitment controlled route effect.
  5. Lineage-linked recruitment: interstage evidence excludes death-and-replacement and supports a registered genealogical path.
  6. Multi-consumer availability: the same transported contrast is available to several independent consumer systems through a common causal organization.

Levels 4 and 5 concern route-specific control. Level 6 is a possible functional extension toward workspace-like availability. None is by itself a phenomenal-consciousness criterion.

Evidence and Literature Synthesis

Evidence standard

This is not a systematic review or meta-analysis. The supplied records are used under the following evidential constraints:

  • empirical studies support only the associations or interventions described in their records;
  • preprints and reports provide provisional evidence;
  • conceptual articles provide arguments rather than experimental proof;
  • title-only records establish topic or title-level claims, not detailed findings;
  • the shared laboratory papers and working theories are speculative prompts, not evidential authority;
  • no cited record supplies an existing positive RLCR certificate.

Prognosis and unstable recovery in disorders of consciousness

A resting-state functional-network and clinical model reportedly achieved approximately 90% discrimination of one-year outcomes across three datasets from two centers [17]. This bears on prognosis. A variable measuring network integrity can predict later recovery while the content-sensitive mechanism responsible for later action is absent at the earlier stage. Outcome prediction therefore does not establish early reserve or lineage.

Behavioral stage labels can also fluctuate. An acute-brain-injury preprint reported that, among patients who recovered command-following, first recovery was transient in 22.2%, 62.4% crossed the threshold repeatedly, and command non-following was strongly associated with concurrent sedation; similar transient-recovery estimates were reported in an external database [28]. These results motivate repeated stage preparation, trial-level state variables, and sensitivity to sedation. They do not identify a hidden content component.

The supplied record for a paper on cognitive motor dissociation establishes that the article addresses unresponsiveness and possible dissociation between cognition and motor expression, but it contains no abstract from which a more specific empirical conclusion can be checked [26]. Likewise, the title of a healthy-subject fMRI study explicitly distinguishes command following from communication, but the title-only record does not support a detailed statement about the size, mechanism, or generality of that dissociation [25].

A conference record is titled “Reappearance of command-following is associated to recovery of language and consciousness networks: A longitudinal multiple-cases report” [27]. It is appropriately treated as preliminary title-level motivation for longitudinal study, not as proof that one neural component persisted and later controlled behavior.

A healthy-participant preprint reported randomized, double-blind, polarity-controlled tDCS experiments and polarity-specific changes in Dynamic Causal Modelling estimates of thalamocortical dynamics during command following [35]. This suggests that perturbational studies of command-following circuitry are feasible in controlled settings. It does not show that tDCS isolates a route, that a DCM connection is a literal mediator path, or that the same intervention identifies recruitment in an injured brain.

The supplied clinical records therefore motivate the distinction among prognosis, stage-local processing, fluctuation, and route-specific control. They do not contain the synchronized path interventions, complete output measurements, interstage provenance, or commitment-time identification required for a positive lineage certificate.

The content anchor is itself an identification problem

A randomized command or image is an intervention on a physical stimulus. It becomes an intervention on a stable representational content only under additional evidence. At the early stage, sensory impairment, changed language processing, sedation, or task reinterpretation may make the same physical input causally different from its later counterpart. If this cannot be constrained, RLCR must be reported as stimulus-contrast recruitment rather than semantic-content recruitment.

Cross-modal generalization, nuisance-matched controls, preserved low-level sensory responses, and independently supported task comprehension can narrow this ambiguity. None of these establishes phenomenality, and no single test is assumed sufficient in all patients.

Artificial systems: architecture, behavior, and attribution

Theory-derived computational indicators can organize evidence without settling phenomenal consciousness. A major report on consciousness in AI expressly presents indicators as theory-relative and does not treat satisfaction as decisive proof [9]. RLCR is narrower still: it concerns the longitudinal history and causal use of one registered contrast-sensitive component.

CTM-AI reports task gains from an architecture inspired by a global-workspace model [4]. Such performance supports architectural and functional claims. It neither establishes phenomenality nor makes a one-route content-to-action assay a global-workspace test.

Theory-relative uncertainty remains substantial. Some near-future systems may satisfy some theories of consciousness and not others [5]. Objections to artificial consciousness also operate at different levels, including practical implementation constraints, challenges to computational functionalism, and strict impossibility claims [6]. A lineage-linked AI mechanism can address a functional implementation question without resolving a biological-substrate objection.

Porebski and Figura defend a categorical negative position using considerations of embodiment, biological organization, meaning, and organism–algorithm differences [10]. That is a peer-reviewed conceptual argument, not an experimental demonstration that no artificial substrate can instantiate consciousness. RLCR neither assumes nor refutes its metaphysical conclusion.

Observer attribution is a separate target. Comsa recommends shifting some research attention from direct phenomenal claims to perceived consciousness and its social effects [3]. Kang and colleagues report that metacognitive self-reflection and emotional expression increase perceived consciousness in language-model-based systems [8]. These results show why fluent retrospective narrative cannot serve as mechanistic evidence: discourse can alter observers’ judgments without establishing how an earlier action was controlled.

Welfare policy likewise operates under a different decision rule. Work on AI welfare argues for assessment under uncertainty without claiming that current systems are conscious [7], while another proposal focuses on states that would be valenced if a system were conscious [15]. Such precautionary reasoning can be appropriate without being entered into a causal-mechanism estimator.

Cross-Disciplinary Integration

What is borrowed, and what is not

The framework uses systems analysis and causal inference only where they constrain a consciousness-research inference:

  • Reachability asks whether the registered contrast can affect a component.
  • Observability asks whether the component affects a declared output family.
  • Predictive quotienting distinguishes histories that have the same selected behavioral future but different joint neural–behavioral futures.
  • Signature matching compares interventionally measured component types across stages.
  • Genealogy records whether a component persisted, died, split, copied, merged, or was replaced.
  • Route intervention asks whether current contrast affects action through a designated precommitment path.

These objects are not interchangeable. An early component can be neurally observable without controlling action. A late mediator can control action without having existed earlier. Two components can have identical endpoint signatures without sharing lineage. A genuine mediator can contribute despite a parallel bypass, dependence on recent history, or disagreement between separately randomized and same-run mediation assays.

Clinical neurology and white-box AI

Clinical and AI applications share the logical structure but not the same intervention possibilities.

For patients, direct mediator installation may be unsafe or undefined, neural observation operators may change with injury or stage, and the action commitment time may be known only within a broad interval. Lesions, natural changes, stimulation, and model-based connectivity estimates can restrict an admissible causal model, but they are not substitutes for an edge intervention unless an explicit identification design supplies the necessary exclusion and consistency assumptions. Clinical RLCR will therefore often be most useful as an audit showing why a positive claim is not identified.

For white-box AI, activation replacement, route ablation, checkpoint comparison, and tool restriction may make route assays more feasible. Yet lineage is especially vulnerable to hidden replacement. A fresh instance receiving the same transcript, a copied checkpoint, a retrained module with the same input–output behavior, or an external cache can reproduce endpoint signatures without strict persistence. White-box access improves provenance only when the relevant weights, processes, memories, tools, and event logs are included in the registered boundary.

Route-specific control is not global access

A nontrivial channel from one contrast through one mediator to one action can be local, unconscious, and sensorimotor. It does not establish global availability in the sense relevant to a global-workspace architecture.

A workspace-oriented extension would require the same matched or lineage-linked component to affect several independently controlled consumer systems—for example, flexible report, working memory, planning, error correction, and context-dependent action—under interventions showing a common gate or broadcast organization rather than parallel local routes. The consumer set and action alphabets would have to be registered, and local recurrent and feedforward alternatives retained in the model class. Even that would be a functional workspace assay, not a proof of phenomenal consciousness. Workspace-inspired task gains in CTM-AI do not change this distinction [4].

Relation to speculative consciousness frameworks

Centered Closure Theory proposes approximately closed grains and a closure landscape, and it emphasizes perturbational evidence over behavioral equivalence [23]. It therefore does offer a candidate substrate-selection program. The narrower criticism is that it does not uniquely or task-independently select the boundary, component, content contrast, or genealogical relation relevant to RLCR.

Multiscale Reflexive Causal Geometry separates empirical mechanism, phenomenal bridge, and metaphysical interpretation and treats consciousness research as a family of coupled inverse problems [24]. That methodological layering is useful. Its gluing and phenomenal-geometry proposals are not assumed here. RLCR replaces informal continuity with an explicit lineage graph and treats any phenomenal bridge as an additional hypothesis.

Phenomenal and normative bridges

Let Φ denote phenomenal consciousness and D the RLCR evidence. Any update from D to Φ remains theory-dependent:

Pr(¬Φ∣D,T)Pr(Φ∣D,T)​=Pr(D∣¬Φ,T)Pr(D∣Φ,T)​Pr(¬Φ∣T)Pr(Φ∣T)​.

RLCR constrains the mechanistic content of D. It does not supply the likelihood ratio. Because unconscious systems can instantiate contrast-sensitive reserve and route-specific control, Pr(D∣¬Φ,T) cannot simply be assumed negligible.

Normative policy additionally depends on consequences, uncertainty, interests, and asymmetric error costs. Those factors can support precaution while leaving the mechanistic certificate unchanged.

2.4 Conditions for a distributional predictive-quotient interpretation

The stronger interpretation is available only under additional conditions. A sufficient version is:

  1. the system is deterministic linear or belongs to an additive innovations family in which state and input affect the future-output law only through an identifiable translation of a state-independent noise law;
  2. initial states are reproducibly prepared as point states, or the initial-state mixture and translation-noise family are separately identifiable;
  3. process and measurement noise do not have content-dependent covariance or other content-dependent higher-order structure;
  4. common future probes are available under both behavioral and joint observation;
  5. the attainable history set spans the contrast-reachable subspace relevant to the quotient;
  6. the probe policy class is rich enough to generate the required linear input histories;
  7. the horizons saturate reachability and observability;
  8. the realization is minimal on the relevant predictive quotient.

Under these assumptions, equality of translated future-output laws is equivalent to equality of their translation parameters, so the algebraic kernel coincides with the kernel of the canonical joint-to-behavioral predictive-state map. Linear-Gaussian systems with state-independent covariance can satisfy this condition; models with content-dependent noise covariance or higher-order output effects generally do not.

Without these assumptions, qcalg​ must be reported only as an algebraic relative-transfer invariant. The speculative RLQO antecedent made the same distinction, although its stronger assumptions were not fully carried into the original manuscript [2].

3. Genealogical continuity

3.1 Lineage event graph

A joint model contains a directed acyclic event graph Lm​ whose nodes are interventionally distinguishable components at intermediate times. Edges or events are labeled:

  • persistence: one component continues through an allowed dynamical transformation;
  • birth: a component appears without a component parent;
  • death: a component ceases to exist;
  • copy: one component generates an additional component with inherited state or parameters;
  • split: one component becomes multiple descendants;
  • merge: multiple components contribute to one descendant;
  • replacement: death of one component followed by birth of another, whether or not their endpoint signatures agree.

The application must register a lineage relation ⇝ℓ​.

  • Strict continuity admits only persistence edges and excludes a copied or newly instantiated token.
  • Ancestral mechanism continuity may admit registered split, copy, or merge edges when interventionally tagged material, parameter, or state ancestry remains traceable.
  • Neither convention admits an unconnected birth as persistence.

A copied AI checkpoint can therefore possess ancestral parameter continuity without being the same running process. The reported claim must specify which relation is used.

3.2 Endpoint nonidentification theorem

Theorem 1: Endpoint signatures do not identify strict lineage. Suppose:

  1. observations and interventions are confined to an early interval and a later interval;
  2. no variable is continuously observed, tagged, or intervened on across the gap;
  3. the model class permits death and birth during the gap;
  4. a birth mechanism may initialize a late component according to any conditional distribution allowed by the observed pre-late history.

Then no collection of stage-local output distributions, common endpoint probe responses, spectral blocks, transfer residues, or late route-intervention distributions can identify strict persistence against an interventionally indistinguishable replacement.

Proof. Consider any admissible model mP​ in which early component E0​ persists to late component E1​. Construct mR​ as follows. Keep the complete early model unchanged. Immediately after the final early observation, terminate E0​. Before the first late observation, instantiate a new component E1′​. Give E1′​ the same late structural equations, observation kernels, route connections, and response to all registered late interventions as E1​. Initialize E1′​, conditional on the available history, with the same distribution that E1​ has under mP​. All early and late interventional distributions are then identical in mP​ and mR​, including every registered signature component and late action effect. Yet mP​ contains a strict persistence edge and mR​ contains death followed by birth. Therefore strict lineage is not identified. □

The construction also defeats minimization over endpoint matchings: the replacement genealogy is absent from a stage-local matching object unless birth and death are represented in the joint model.

3.3 Evidence capable of constraining lineage

The theorem does not make lineage unknowable in principle. It shows what endpoint data cannot do. Stronger evidence may include:

  • continuous or sufficiently dense observation of the candidate component;
  • a persistent causal tag or perturbation maintained across the interval;
  • anatomical or material tracing with a model of possible reorganization;
  • tamper-evident process, parameter, checkpoint, and memory provenance;
  • documented absence of retraining, reinstantiation, or replacement;
  • conserved parameter ancestry combined with a complete event log;
  • interventions during recovery that distinguish continued evolution from reinitialization.

Each item is useful only relative to a model of how replacement could occur. If an undetected constructor can reproduce the same tag or state, the replacement countermodel remains admissible.

σ(E,ρ)=(λE​,{COFℓPE​Gcdc​}O,ℓ​,{KE,u⋆​}u⋆∈U⋆​,CE​),

where:

  • λE​ denotes the spectral cluster or another invariant dynamical descriptor;
  • the transfer terms are component-specific observable residues;
  • KE,u⋆​ contains responses to common calibration interventions;
  • CE​ contains registered measurement-calibration quantities.

Let dΣ​ be a prespecified metric on these observable and interventional objects. Components E0​ and E1​ are signature-matched when

dΣ​(σ(E0​,ρ0​),σ(E1​,ρ1​))≤κ,

with κ derived from independently justified biological, computational, and measurement-drift bounds.

This relation is meaningful as type-level causal equivalence. It is not called persistence. A perfect replacement can satisfy it exactly. Ambiguous clusters, model orders, or matchings remain in the admissible set rather than being resolved by post hoc similarity.

5. Registered-cut route intervention

5.1 Edge-split structural causal model

At a candidate late cut τ, let:

  • Hτ​ be the pretreatment history;
  • Zτ​∈Z be the current randomized contrast;
  • Mτ​ be the candidate mediator, possibly continuous and multidimensional;
  • Rτ​ collect complementary contrast routes;
  • Aτ​∈A be a finite registered action alphabet.

Introduce two treatment versions, ZτM​ and ZτMˉ​, which share the same natural value when no route intervention is performed. A minimal structural representation is

Mτ​=fM​(Hτ​,ZτM​,UM​),
Rτ​=fR​(Hτ​,ZτMˉ​,UR​),
Aτ​=fA​(Hτ​,Mτ​,Rτ​,UA​).

Every open path assigned to the candidate route must pass through Mτ​. Paths not passing through Mτ​ belong to Rτ​. Interactions between Mτ​ and Rτ​ in fA​ are allowed.

The edge intervention

do(ZτM​=z,ZτMˉ​=z0​)

is defined only when:

  1. outgoing edges are partitioned before analysis;
  2. treatment versions are consistent under natural exposure;
  3. no shared descendant receives conflicting treatment versions unless a valid node-splitting model is specified;
  4. the intervention does not create unmodeled recanting-witness conflicts;
  5. mediator installation has no direct path to action outside the modeled mediator state, or that direct effect is separately represented;
  6. installed states lie within a registered support or intervention envelope;
  7. the intervention preserves the action mechanism except for the declared route change.

If these conditions fail, the route channel is undefined rather than merely noisy.

5.2 Same-run and separately randomized channels

The content-to-mediator kernel is

Qz​(dm)=Pr(Mτ​∈dm∣do(Zτ​=z)).

For a separately installed mediator value, define

Km,z0​​(a)=Pr(Aτ​=a∣do(Mτ​=m),do(ZτMˉ​=z0​)).

The separately randomized composition is

Rrand​(z,a)=∫M​Km,z0​​(a)Qz​(dm).

The same-run route-isolated channel is

Siso​(z,a)=Pr(Aτ​=a∣do(ZτM​=z,ZτMˉ​=z0​)).

The mediator space M need not be finite. The integral, not a finite matrix product, is the general definition. Any finite nonnegative-rank factorization of the resulting Z-to-A channel describes an equivalent hypothetical finite mediator; it does not recover the cardinality, dimensionality, or geometry of the actual neural or artificial component.

The channels Rrand​ and Siso​ may differ because separate mediator randomization can break same-run dependence, alter support, or remove mediator-context interactions. Such disagreement does not by itself refute genuine route contribution.

5.3 Effect-sized distance from content independence

For a row-stochastic channel S, define its distance from the rank-1 channel set:

Δ1​(S)=q∈P(A)inf​z∈Zmax​TV(Sz​,q).

Then

Δ1​(S)=0

if and only if all rows of S are identical.

A nontrivial controlled route contribution requires

Δ1​(Siso​)≥δA​

for a registered, scientifically meaningful effect margin δA​>0. This establishes that changing current Zτ​ along the candidate route changes the action distribution under the declared baseline and cut. It does not establish natural-use frequency, indispensability, or phenomenality.

For comparison, define approximate stochastic nonnegative rank

r+,TVε​(S)=min{r:∃ row-stochastic U,V, zmax​TV(Sz​,(UV)z​)≤ε}.

The correct rank-1 interpretation is

r+,TVε​(S)=1⟺Δ1​(S)≤ε.

For ε>0, this does not mean that the channel rows are equal. It means that every row lies within ε total variation of one common distribution, implying pairwise row distances of at most 2ε.

Because exact and approximate nonnegative rank can be unstable, the primary certificate uses confidence bounds on Δ1​, not an exact estimated rank.

5.4 Assay-agreement diagnostic

Define

Γ=zmax​TV(Rrand,z​,Siso,z​).

A small Γ is a modularity and assay-agreement diagnostic. It is not a core requirement for controlled route contribution.

The distance Δ1​ is Lipschitz under the maximum row-wise total-variation metric:

∣Δ1​(Rrand​)−Δ1​(Siso​)∣≤Γ.

Therefore, if

Δ1​(Siso​)≥δA​andΓ≤γ,

then

Δ1​(Rrand​)≥δA​−γ.

A claim that both assays show a meaningful effect must register a positive separation margin with δA​>γ. Low disagreement alone is uninformative when both channels are practically rank 1.

6. Stronger optional properties

6.1 Bypass equivalence

For a registered mediator value m, define

Sbypm​(z,a)=Pr(Aτ​=a∣do(Mτ​=m),do(ZτMˉ​=z)).

An equivalence certificate

m∈M0​sup​Δ1​(Sbypm​)≤εbyp​

over a registered support M0​ supports the stronger claim that no practically meaningful content-specific bypass remains under those clamps. Testing one baseline m0​ is generally insufficient. The clamp must also preserve relevant action capacity, assessed through a prespecified positive-control input, so that a globally disabled controller is not mistaken for absence of a bypass.

Bypass equivalence concerns content-specific necessity or exclusivity under the intervention. A mediator can genuinely contribute while a parallel bypass remains active.

6.2 Lag equivalence

Let Mτ−d​ be a forced stale mediator state. A lag channel is meaningful only when current Zτ​ is randomized independently of the relevant history, or when the estimand is explicitly conditional on that history. Define

Slagd​(z,a)=Pr(Aτ​=a∣do(Zτ​=z),install(Mτ−d​)).

An upper bound

Δ1​(Slagd​)≤εlag​

shows practical absence of a current-contrast effect through the forced stale state in that design. It does not show that the natural controller never uses earlier content. A temporally thick system may genuinely depend on both current and recent inputs.

6.3 Temporal and modular claims

RLCR therefore distinguishes:

  • current controlled contribution: Δ1​(Siso​)≥δA​;
  • content-specific necessity under clamps: bypass equivalence;
  • temporal exclusivity: lag equivalence over a registered lag set;
  • assay modularity: Γ≤γ;
  • sufficiency: a separately defined intervention showing that the mediator route alone produces the target action effect.

These properties should not be silently bundled under the word “recruitment.”

7. Formal target definitions

For a joint model m, boundary g, and cut τ, define

Tsig​(m,g,τ)=1

if there exist early and late components E0​,E1​ such that:

  1. E0​ is contrast-reachable;
  2. E0​ satisfies the registered early reserve margins;
  3. E0​ and E1​ satisfy the registered signature distance;
  4. E1​ is the mediator in a valid edge-split SCM at cut τ;
  5. Δ1​(SisoE1​,τ​)≥δA​.

Otherwise Tsig​=0.

Define

Tlin​(m,g,τ)=1

if there exist E0​,E1​ such that:

  1. E0​ satisfies the registered early reserve margins;
  2. E0​⇝ℓ​E1​ in the joint lineage graph;
  3. E1​ satisfies the valid late controlled-route condition.

Signature similarity may be used as evidence for locating the lineage candidate, but it is not the definition of lineage.

Optional stronger targets are formed by conjoining bypass, lag, modularity, or sufficiency conditions. No claim is made that these conjunctions are logically minimal.

8. Admissible model set and assumptions

Let Aα​(D) be the set of joint models, boundaries, cuts, component resolutions, signature matchings, and lineage graphs compatible with the registered design and a simultaneous 1−α confidence construction.

The core assumptions are:

A1. Contrast validity. The physical intervention rule is stable across stages. Semantic-content language is used only when stable interpretation is independently constrained.

A2. Current randomization. The late contrast is randomized after the history relevant to stale-state alternatives, or the route effect is explicitly conditioned on that history.

A3. Model adequacy and no outcome leakage. The joint model family includes the relevant nonlinearities, environmental routes, and stage variation to the declared approximation. Later outcomes are not used to select early branches, horizons, drift bounds, boundaries, or probe families.

A4. Early-assay conditions. Reachability and observability horizons are saturated for the algebraic claim. The stronger distributional interpretation is used only under the state-preparation, common-probe, attainable-set, and translation-noise assumptions stated above.

A5. Component identifiability. Linear components are minimal isolated invariant clusters at a resolution supported by the data. Nonnormality and spectral crowding are represented in the confidence set. Finite-state components are quotiented by interventional predictive equivalence.

A6. Calibrated cross-stage drift. Signature metrics and drift bounds are registered independently of the desired match, and measurement-operator changes are modeled rather than absorbed into component identity.

A7. Explicit genealogy. Birth, death, copy, split, merge, and replacement are represented. A lineage lower certificate is permitted only when the provenance model is sufficiently complete to exclude the replacement countermodel.

A8. Valid route intervention. Edge splitting, consistency, support, intervention modularity, action timing, and absence or modeling of direct installation effects are adequate for the stated route estimand.

A9. Registered boundary, cut, and outputs. The system boundary, action alphabet, behavioral output family, and cut window are declared before confirmatory analysis.

A10. Simultaneous inference. Uncertainty covers model order, horizon, component selection, signature matching, drift, route channels, boundaries, cuts, optional lags, and any multiplicity induced by those choices.

If an assumption fails, the appropriate output is nonidentification or an undefined model audit—not a positive mechanistic conclusion.

9. Boolean lower and upper certificates

For x∈{sig,lin}, define

Cx−​=(m,g,τ)∈Aα​(D)min​Tx​(m,g,τ),
Cx+​=(m,g,τ)∈Aα​(D)max​Tx​(m,g,τ).

If Aα​(D)=∅, both certificates are undefined. Empty-set conventions for ordinary infima and suprema are not used.

For a nonempty admissible set:

CertificateInterpretation
Cx−​=1Every admissible model contains target x
Cx−​=0, Cx+​=1Target x is possible but not identified
Cx+​=0Target x is excluded within the declared class
UndefinedModel, design, or assumption failure

A common and informative outcome is

Csig−​=1,Clin−​=0,Clin+​=1.

This means that signature-linked recruitment is identified but persistence remains ambiguous because both continuation and replacement genealogies fit the evidence.

10. Simultaneous confidence construction

No universal estimator is appropriate for all clinical and AI designs. A valid application must nevertheless provide a concrete simultaneous coverage statement:

m⋆∈Minf​m⋆Pr​[m⋆∈Aα​(D)]≥1−α.

A defensible workflow is:

  1. use a discovery split or external data to select candidate orders, horizons, clusters, output scaling, signature metrics, and intervention timing;
  2. freeze those choices before confirmatory estimation;
  3. on the confirmatory data, estimate the joint finite collection of Markov parameters, probe kernels, route channels, calibration quantities, and provenance observations;
  4. construct a joint confidence region by a design-appropriate likelihood inversion, calibrated bootstrap, randomization procedure, or conservative familywise allocation;
  5. retain the union of all model orders, clusters, boundaries, and cuts not excluded by that region;
  6. propagate the joint region through every target inequality and matching relation.

If sample splitting is unavailable, all data-dependent choices must remain inside the model union. Selecting the most favorable early decoder, spectral cluster, boundary, or cut and then treating it as fixed invalidates the lower certificate.

For practical hiding and exclusion claims, use equivalence bounds:

Uα​[βB​(E)]≤εB​,
Uα​[Δ1​(Sbyp​)]≤εbyp​,
Uα​[Δ1​(Slagd​)]≤εlag​.

Failure to reject an effect is not an equivalence certificate.

For positive effects, use lower bounds:

Lα​[ηN​(E)]≥δN​,
Lα​[Δ1​(Siso​)]≥δA​.

The thresholds εB​,δN​,δA​,εbyp​,εlag​,κ,γ must be registered in scientifically interpretable units. Their uncertainty cannot be tuned after observing whether the certificate is positive.

11. Statistical soundness

Theorem 2: Confidence propagation to Boolean certificates. Suppose the true joint model m⋆, true boundary g⋆, and true cut τ⋆ belong to Aα​(D) with probability at least 1−α. Then, for x∈{sig,lin},

Pr(Cx−​=1 and Tx​(m⋆,g⋆,τ⋆)=0)≤α,

and

Pr(Cx+​=0 and Tx​(m⋆,g⋆,τ⋆)=1)≤α.

Proof. On the event that (m⋆,g⋆,τ⋆)∈Aα​(D), a lower certificate of one implies that the target is one for every admissible tuple, including the true tuple. Likewise, an upper certificate of zero implies that the target is zero for every admissible tuple. Either erroneous conclusion can therefore occur only when the true tuple is not covered, an event of probability at most α. □

This theorem propagates simultaneous coverage. It does not validate the model class, semantic anchor, lineage completeness, or route-intervention interpretation. Those remain substantive assumptions.

12. Coordinate invariance

For an invertible state transformation x′=Sx,

F′=SFS−1,G′=SG,C′=CS−1.

Therefore,

C′(F′)kG′=CFkG,

so Markov parameters and Hankel matrices are unchanged. For an isolated cluster,

PE′​=SPE​S−1,

and hence

C′(F′)kPE′​G′=CFkPE​G.

The algebraic reserve, component transfer signature, and signature distance are therefore similarity-invariant when the confidence set is formulated in these observable quantities.

For a relabeling or measurable reparameterization of M, the kernels Q and K change coordinates, but the induced channels Rrand​ and Siso​, their total-variation distances, and the Boolean target do not.

This invariance is limited. It does not cover:

  • nonminimal hidden-state augmentation;
  • arbitrary splitting of an invariant cluster;
  • changes in the behavioral output family;
  • changes in action coarse-graining;
  • changes in system boundary;
  • movement of the commitment cut;
  • coordinate-dependent regularization used during model selection.

Those changes alter either the model class or the estimand and must remain inside the sensitivity analysis.

13. Synthetic illustration

Consider a binary contrast Zt​∈{−1,+1}. At the early stage,

xt+1​=axt​+Zt​,∣a∣<1,
Nt+1​=xt+1​+νt+1​,Bt+1​∼Bernoulli(1/2),

with B independent of Z. The isolated component E is contrast-reachable, has nonzero neural transfer, and has zero behavioral transfer in the oracle model.

At the later stage, let the matched component generate mediator Mτ​=Zτ​, and define

Pr(Aτ​=1∣Mτ​=+1)=0.9,
Pr(Aτ​=1∣Mτ​=−1)=0.1.

With alternative contrast routes clamped, the two rows of Siso​ are Bernoulli distributions with parameters 0.9 and 0.1. The distance to the nearest common row is

Δ1​(Siso​)=0.4.

Suppose the registered thresholds are

εB​=0.05,δN​=0.5,δA​=0.25,κ=0.10.

For illustration, assume a simultaneous 95% confidence region yields:

U[βB​(E)]=0.03,
L[ηN​(E)]=0.92,
U[dΣ​(E0​,E1​)]=0.04,
L[Δ1​(Siso​)]=0.34.

Every endpoint-compatible model then contains an early reserve component, a signature-matched late component, and a meaningful route contribution. Thus

Csig−​=1.

Now compare two joint models:

  • mP​: the early component persists throughout the interval;
  • mR​: the early component dies, and an identical component is instantiated before the later stage.

They have the same displayed confidence region and every endpoint intervention distribution. Therefore, if both genealogies remain admissible,

Clin−​=0,Clin+​=1.

Only additional interstage evidence capable of excluding mR​ can raise the lineage lower certificate. The example shows why improved signature precision does not, by itself, repair the persistence problem.

Alternatives, Counterarguments, and Boundary Cases

Prognostic integrity without early reserve

Let R be an early tissue- or architecture-integrity variable independent of randomized contrast Z. Suppose

Nρ0​​=R+ϵ,Bρ0​​=0,

and later recovery is likely when R=1. At ρ1​, a new controller is created:

V=Z,A=V.

The early variable can predict recovery accurately while no early contrast-sensitive component exists. This is why the prognostic performance reported in disorders-of-consciousness work cannot be converted into early-content evidence [17].

Early reserve plus stale control

Let Zt​ be autocorrelated, with Mt​=Zt​ represented internally at both stages, but let recovered action satisfy

At​=Zt−1​.

Passive action accuracy can be high, and a later report can correctly state Zt​. Under a synchronized intervention on current Zt​, however, the stale state and action remain unchanged. The current route channel is rank 1. This reproduces the core lesson of the speculative mediated-control countermodel: observational accuracy and post-action report do not lower-bound current causal control [1].

A lag equivalence test is especially informative when current Zt​ is a randomized, nonrepeating nonce. It is not a universal requirement for genuine controllers that integrate current and past content.

Unaligned early reserve and de novo late control

An early component U may respond to Z and remain neurally visible while a new late component V controls action:

Uρ0​​=f(Z),Bρ0​​=0,
Uρ1​​=f(Z),Vρ1​​=Z,A=Vρ1​​.

A global early reserve statistic and a global late mediation statistic are both positive, but they concern different components. Component-specific reserve and late route alignment prevent this false conjunction.

Perfect replacement

A stronger alternative destroys U and creates U′ with exactly the same dynamics, transfer residues, probe responses, and action role. Signature-linked recruitment remains true; strict lineage-linked recruitment is false. No reduction in signature tolerance can distinguish the two if their signatures are identical. This is not residual statistical uncertainty but structural nonidentification.

Parallel bypasses

Suppose the transported mediator M and a direct route R both carry current Z to action. Varying Z only along the M route changes action, so M genuinely contributes. Clamping M nevertheless leaves a content-sensitive bypass. This system satisfies contribution but not exclusivity.

A bypass-equivalence requirement would reject it only because that requirement defines a stronger target. Parallel redundancy should not be redescribed as absence of recruitment.

Genuine temporally thick control

A controller may use

At​=f(Zt​,Zt−1​,Mt​).

Both current and lagged content can have nontrivial effects. The current synchronized route effect remains genuine. Requiring all lag channels to be practically rank 1 would impose temporal exclusivity rather than causal contribution and could create false negatives.

Genuine mediation with noncommuting assays

If latent variables jointly affect mediator generation and downstream response, or if the action depends on a mediator-context interaction, then separately sampling M and installing it in another run can break dependencies present in the same-run route intervention. Consequently,

Rrand​=Siso​

even when Siso​ shows a genuine precommitment route effect. A small commutation defect is therefore a useful modularity result, not a necessary truth about mediation.

Post-decision monitoring

Let action be fixed before the monitor update:

Aτ​=Zτ​,
Mτ+1​=Aτ​,Yτ+1​=Mτ+1​.

Monitor–action association and later report are perfect, but intervening on Mτ+1​ cannot change the committed action. The route assay must therefore occur at every retained precommitment cut. Widening the cut window after observing the report is not permitted.

A new decoder attached to a preserved component

Recovery may add a new downstream decoder to a genuinely continuous early component. This is the intended recruitment case:

early reserve component+new downstream route.

Lineage is claimed only for the component, not for the entire route. The framework does not require late action connectivity to have existed earlier.

Semantic drift

The same acoustic command may function as one physical contrast at ρ0​ and a comprehended instruction at ρ1​. In that case, a cross-stage semantic-content claim is unsupported even if low-level transfer signatures match. The analysis can still concern physical-stimulus sensitivity, but it must not silently upgrade that target to preserved meaning.

Measurement drift and apparent mode identity

Changes in sensor placement, preprocessing, neurovascular coupling, noise, arousal, or observation sensitivity can alter apparent component signatures without changes in underlying dynamics. Conversely, compensating changes in dynamics and observation operators can preserve the measured signature. Calibration narrows but does not eliminate this ambiguity. Domain-specific measurement models are therefore part of the admissible set, not nuisance terms assumed away.

Fluctuating stage mixtures

Repeated transitions across a behavioral threshold can make ρ0​ and ρ1​ mixtures rather than reproducible stages. The reported instability and sedation dependence of command-following motivate trial-level state modeling rather than a single charted stage label [28]. If a mixture of responsive and nonresponsive trials explains the apparent reserve, the lower certificate must remain zero.

Incomplete behavioral outputs

Behavioral hiding is relative to B. If eye movement, electromyography, autonomic response, assistive communication, or tool action is omitted, an apparently covert component may be visible through an unmeasured route. Adding a valid output can reduce early reserve. That is correction of the estimand, not failure of invariance.

Action coarse-graining presents the converse risk. Merging clinically or functionally distinct actions can reduce Δ1​ and approximate mediated rank. Sensitivity to prespecified meaningful action alphabets should be reported.

Boundary dependence

For a patient, plausible boundaries may include brain only, brain and body, or patient plus an assistive device. For an AI system, they may include weights, running activations, context, retrieval memory, tools, orchestration software, or human operators.

RLCR does not solve the metaphysical subject-boundary problem. It asks whether the target survives a registered family of scientifically defensible boundaries. The family should be constrained by task ownership, causal coupling, intervention access, and lineage—not enlarged arbitrarily to force a negative lower bound or narrowed after analysis to force a positive one.

AI copying and personal identity

A copied checkpoint can preserve parameter ancestry and causal organization while failing strict process continuity. A fresh instance given an old transcript can reproduce behavioral memory without retaining the earlier activation token. RLCR can report strict process lineage, ancestral parameter lineage, or signature equivalence, but it cannot infer metaphysical personal identity across duplication.

Unconscious processing

Feedforward discrimination, local recurrence, priming, salience encoding, and sensorimotor preparation can satisfy the mechanistic target. Increasing report fluency or adding self-reflective language does not remove this alternative. Observer studies show that such surface features can increase perceived consciousness [8], which makes their exclusion from the mechanistic certificate especially important.

Biological-substrate objections

A substrate-essentialist may accept every RLCR result while denying artificial phenomenality. A computational functionalist may treat lineage-linked multi-consumer availability as relevant but still insufficient. These positions concern different inferential levels, as emphasized by taxonomies distinguishing implementation constraints, functionalist objections, and strict impossibility claims [6]. RLCR does not resolve that dispute.

Empirical Implications and Falsification Criteria

Prospective registration protocol

A confirmatory study should perform the following steps.

  1. Declare the contrast. Specify its levels, randomization, timing, physical anchor, claimed semantic status, and nuisance-matched controls.
  1. Declare outputs. Prespecify neural or internal measurements, overt behavior, covert motor channels, assistive outputs, tools, and action coarse-grainings.
  1. Declare stages. Use repeated measurements rather than a single behavior where possible. Record variables relevant to stage fluctuation, including sedation or computational configuration.
  1. Declare boundaries and cuts. Define G and cut windows Wg​ before examining target effects.
  1. Declare lineage semantics. State whether the target is strict process continuity, material continuity, or ancestral parameter/mechanism continuity. Register which event types preserve that relation.
  1. Separate discovery and confirmation. Select model orders, horizons, clusters, signature metrics, drift bounds, and probes without using confirmatory recovery outcomes.
  1. Estimate component-specific early reserve. Obtain simultaneous upper bounds on behavioral transfer and lower bounds on internal transfer. Do not infer component eligibility from a global rank difference.
  1. Collect interstage provenance. Measure or log events capable of distinguishing continuation, birth, replacement, copy, split, or merge.
  1. Estimate signature bounds. Apply common probes and calibration interventions, retaining all matches consistent with uncertainty.
  1. Specify the route SCM. Draw the edge-split graph, list candidate and bypass edges, identify possible shared descendants, and justify intervention support and consistency.
  1. Estimate current route contribution. Obtain a lower confidence bound on Δ1​(Siso​) at every retained cut.
  1. Run only target-relevant stronger assays. Use bypass, lag, commutation, or sufficiency tests when making the corresponding stronger claim.
  1. Construct a simultaneous admissible set. Propagate uncertainty across models, branches, boundaries, cuts, lineage graphs, and all registered channels.
  1. Report both tiers. Report
[Csig−​,Csig+​]and[Clin−​,Clin+​].
  1. Hold out a perturbation. Reserve a safe or technically feasible route perturbation for prospective testing.

Held-out perturbation requirements

A held-out test should preregister:

  • the direction and minimum size of the predicted content-specific action change;
  • the cut at which the perturbation occurs;
  • which nuisance capacities must remain preserved;
  • the action and content specificity of the effect;
  • the alternative route expected not to change;
  • a precision or power requirement sufficient to distinguish the predicted effect from the equivalence margin.

A held-out failure is informative only if the perturbation engaged the intended component, occurred before commitment, preserved relevant action capacity, and achieved the registered precision.

Clinical implementation

A realistic clinical study may combine randomized sensory contrasts, serial EEG or fMRI, electromyography, eye tracking, repeated behavioral assessments, and clinically justified changes in treatment or stimulation. These measurements can constrain the admissible set.

They do not automatically define a path intervention. Lesion structure may exclude some routes but rarely all unmeasured alternatives. DCM effective-connectivity estimates are model-dependent. Broad stimulation can alter multiple components and background state. The healthy-participant tDCS study is therefore best treated as methodological motivation for perturbing command-following dynamics, not as validation of patient-level route isolation [35].

If a mediator route cannot be intervened on, a study may support an upper signature certificate or narrow the set of possible mechanisms. It should not report a positive route-contribution lower certificate from longitudinal decoding alone. In many clinical applications, the most defensible result may be a formal demonstration of nonidentification.

White-box AI implementation

A stronger AI design can:

  1. generate a randomized, nonrepeating contrast after context reset;
  2. identify an early component while temporarily suppressing output without retraining;
  3. log weights, activations, process identity, context, retrieval state, tools, and orchestration changes across the interval;
  4. distinguish route restoration from retraining or module replacement;
  5. use common causal probes and activation interventions for signature estimation;
  6. perform a same-run edge or route intervention before action commitment;
  7. restrict installed activations to a registered support and include sham installations;
  8. test stale activations when current contrast is independent of history;
  9. test bypasses through context, retrieval, external memory, tools, and scaffold code;
  10. include a deliberate replacement condition as a positive control for the endpoint lineage ambiguity;
  11. repeat the analysis across registered boundaries;
  12. reserve a held-out content-specific perturbation.

Tamper-evident provenance can support a lineage lower certificate only relative to an explicit model of what events the log would detect. Identical weights or hashes alone cannot rule out copying.

Formal falsification and failure criteria

The framework contains mathematical, design-level, and case-level claims. They fail in different ways.

Mathematical falsification

  1. Algebraic counterexample: a saturated finite-dimensional linear system satisfying Lemma 1 in which the displayed Hankel-rank difference does not equal the stated quotient dimension.
  1. Lineage theorem counterexample: an endpoint-only method that distinguishes the persistent and replacement constructions while both remain in the stated model class and induce identical registered interventional distributions.
  1. Confidence-propagation counterexample: a simultaneously covered true model for which a Boolean lower certificate is one while the independently defined target is false.
  1. Coordinate failure: a change in a certificate caused solely by an invertible latent similarity or mediator relabeling, with the confidence construction transformed equivariantly.

Design invalidation

  1. The route intervention creates an off-support mediator state or has a direct action effect outside the declared route.
  2. A shared descendant or recanting witness makes the edge intervention inconsistent.
  3. Later outcomes influence early branch, horizon, drift, boundary, or cut selection.
  4. A practical-absence claim is based on nonsignificance rather than an upper equivalence bound.
  5. The confidence region does not cover all model and selection uncertainty.
  6. A semantic-content claim is made although only physical stimulus identity is established.
  7. A lineage lower certificate is reported while a replacement genealogy remains compatible with the provenance data.

Case-level refutation

For a registered case, Hlin​ is refuted within the declared class if adequate evidence establishes any of the following:

  • no early reserve-bearing component meets the registered effect margins;
  • the late candidate has no route effect above δA​;
  • the lineage graph contains death and de novo birth rather than an admissible continuation;
  • the action effect occurs only after commitment;
  • the effect tracks a registered nuisance rather than the contrast;
  • a held-out perturbation produces an adequately precise result incompatible with the predicted route-specific effect.

Sparse data, unsafe intervention, or an empty admissible set do not refute retained recruitment. They show that the target was not identified by that design.

Ethical interpretation

RLCR is not a clinical decision rule. A zero lower certificate can result from missing interventions, uncertain semantics, broad cut windows, incomplete measurements, or unresolved replacement. It should not independently determine treatment, communication effort, or withdrawal of care.

For AI systems, welfare policy may rationally use precautionary thresholds different from mechanistic-identification thresholds [7, 15]. Such policies should state their normative assumptions and error costs rather than treating fluent self-report or observer attribution as a causal certificate.

Limitations

First, endpoint causal-signature matching does not identify token persistence. This objection is not resolved by tighter matching, more spectral features, or minimization over matchings. The manuscript accepts the replacement countermodel and weakens the endpoint conclusion to signature-linked recruitment. The retained disagreement is terminological and practical: type-level causal-signature equivalence remains useful for auditing recovery claims, but it should not be described as strict persistence.

Second, lineage identification depends on a restrictive joint model of possible events and the completeness of interstage provenance. A positive lineage lower certificate means that every model left in that declared class contains a lineage path. It does not prove that the class contains every physically possible replacement mechanism.

Third, the linear reserve theorem concerns mean-transfer structure unless the stronger distributional assumptions hold. Content-dependent covariance, higher-order effects, hidden initial mixtures, inadequate excitation, or insufficient policy support can separate Hankel rank from all-policy predictive equivalence.

Fourth, component identification remains difficult. Isolated Riesz projectors are well defined for registered spectral clusters, including repeated or defective eigenvalues inside the cluster, but nonnormality and crowded spectra can make them statistically unstable. Components not separable at the supported resolution cannot receive a positive lower certificate.

Fifth, the conservative component-specific criterion may miss genuine reserve distributed across mixed or nonseparable components. This is an accepted false-negative risk. A more permissive decomposition would require a proved invariant quotient assignment rather than post hoc branch selection.

Sixth, path-specific neural interventions may be infeasible, unsafe, or conceptually undefined. Broad stimulation, lesions, natural recovery, and effective-connectivity models can constrain causal accounts without implementing the edge intervention used here. Clinical applications may therefore remain at the audit or upper-bound level.

Seventh, commitment time is rarely observed directly. Minimization over a broad cut window is conservative but can erase a genuine effect. Narrowing the window using a downstream correlate can place the cut after the causal decision. The paper supplies no universal solution to that timing problem.

Eighth, semantic identity across stages may be unidentifiable. When command comprehension cannot be independently constrained, the result must remain about physical-stimulus sensitivity. This limitation is especially serious when language recovery is part of the recovery process being explained.

Ninth, behavioral hiding depends on the output set, and route contribution depends on action coarse-graining. Unmeasured effectors can create apparent reserve, while coarse actions can conceal real control. The framework requires sensitivity analyses but cannot guarantee measurement completeness.

Tenth, simultaneous confidence regions for ranks, spectral clusters, matching, edge-intervention channels, and lineage graphs can be computationally demanding. Approximate nonnegative rank is unstable, which is why the primary procedure uses distance from the rank-1 set. Even that distance requires adequate support across contrast and action levels.

Eleventh, a small commutation defect, bypass equivalence, or lag equivalence is not necessary for route contribution. These diagnostics support stronger modularity, exclusivity, or temporal claims. Their interpretation remains intervention-specific and can fail under legitimate mediator-context interaction or temporal integration.

Twelfth, a one-route result is not a global-workspace result. Multi-consumer causal availability and common broadcast organization require additional interventions and alternatives. Neither local recruitment nor functional broadcast establishes phenomenal consciousness.

Thirteenth, clinical and AI lineage are not interchangeable. Brain recovery includes injury, plasticity, bodily regulation, and changing measurement operators. Artificial recovery may consist of route restoration, copying, reloading, or software replacement. Shared formal vocabulary does not imply shared biology, identity, or phenomenology.

Fourteenth, the supplied literature archive is insufficient for a comprehensive novelty comparison with established methods in realization theory, predictive-state modeling, longitudinal identification, causal mediation, edge interventions, and equivalence testing. The local formal sources are AI-generated and non-peer-reviewed [1, 2]. The paper therefore presents a self-contained proposal and does not claim that each mathematical ingredient is new to its broader field.

Finally, RLCR supplies no validated bridge to phenomenal consciousness, valence, interests, welfare, or moral standing. It improves the specification of one retrodictive mechanistic question while leaving those larger questions open.

Conclusion

Recovery does not retrospectively prove that the mechanism supporting later report or action was present during an earlier nonresponsive stage. Accurate prognosis can coexist with absence of early contrast-sensitive processing. Early neural sensitivity can coexist with stale or behaviorally irrelevant dynamics. A late controller can be newly constructed. Most importantly, an early component can be destroyed and replaced by a component with identical endpoint dynamics, probe responses, and route effects.

Recovery-Linked Causal Recruitment therefore separates two conclusions. A signature-linked lower certificate supports an early component-specific reserve, cross-stage causal-signature equivalence, and later precommitment route contribution. It is a type-level result. A lineage-linked lower certificate additionally requires a joint genealogy and interstage evidence that excludes replacement under a registered continuity convention. Endpoint matching alone cannot supply that conclusion.

The revised framework also separates route contribution from stronger properties. A genuine contributor may coexist with bypasses, lagged inputs, or noncommuting randomized and same-run mediation assays. Bypass equivalence supports necessity or exclusivity; lag equivalence supports a design-specific temporal exclusion; low commutation defect supports modularity. None is silently treated as necessary for recruitment.

For disorders of consciousness, the likely near-term value is an evidence audit: it shows why prognosis, fluctuating command-following, longitudinal activation, and broad stimulation usually do not identify retained control. For white-box AI, stronger route interventions and provenance may be possible, but copying, checkpoint replacement, external memory, tools, and process resets create explicit lineage alternatives.

A positive signature certificate would justify the restrained statement that an early contrast-sensitive component had an interventionally equivalent later counterpart that contributed to recovered action. Only a positive lineage certificate would justify the stronger, model-relative statement that the later contributor descended from the earlier component. Neither conclusion establishes phenomenal consciousness, global access, report truth, personal identity, welfare, or moral status. That limit is not incidental: it is the central condition under which recovery can inform consciousness research without being retrospectively overinterpreted.
